
Operation Thunderbolt, also known as Operation Entebbe, was a 1976 Israeli commando rescue mission in Uganda to save hostages from a hijacked Air France plane.

## What happened?

In June 1976, Air France Flight 139 was hijacked by militants from the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine – External Operations and the German Revolutionary Cells.

The plane was flying from Tel Aviv to Paris, but it was hijacked after a stop in Athens and taken to Entebbe Airport in Uganda.

There were about 248 passengers and 12 crew members on the plane. Later, many non-Israeli and non-Jewish passengers were released and sent to Paris. About 94 mainly Israeli and Jewish hostages, along with 12 Air France crew members, remained in Entebbe.

The hijackers separated many Israeli and Jewish passengers from others and kept them as hostages.

## Who helped the hijackers?

Uganda was ruled by dictator Idi Amin at that time. His government supported the hijackers and allowed them to keep the hostages at Entebbe Airport.

## What did Israel do?

Israel planned a secret military rescue mission. Israeli commandos flew thousands of kilometers from Israel to Uganda.

On the night of 3–4 July 1976, Israeli commandos landed at Entebbe Airport and attacked the hostage area. The operation happened at night, around midnight / early morning.

The commandos rescued most of the hostages.

## Result

102 hostages were rescued.

Three hostages were killed during the rescue operation. One hostage, Dora Bloch, was later killed in Uganda.

Israeli commander Yonatan Netanyahu was also killed during the operation. He was the older brother of Benjamin Netanyahu(first elected Prime Minister in 1996 at age 46, becoming the youngest person to hold the office).

Several hijackers and some Ugandan soldiers were also killed.

## Why is it famous?

Operation Thunderbolt is famous because it was a very bold and risky rescue mission. Israel rescued hostages from another country, thousands of kilometers away.

It is considered one of the most successful hostage-rescue operations in modern history.

## Very short exam note

Operation Thunderbolt, also known as Operation Entebbe, was a 1976 Israeli commando rescue mission in Uganda to save hostages from a hijacked Air France plane. Israeli commandos rescued 102 hostages, while Yonatan Netanyahu, the commander of the mission, was killed.

## What Kenya did

Israel had to fly a very long distance from Israel to Uganda. For the mission to succeed, the Israeli planes needed a safe place for **refueling and support**.

Kenya helped by allowing Israeli aircraft to use **Nairobi Airport** after the rescue. This Kenyan support was very important for the commandos to return safely. Some reports also mention Kenyan intelligence/logistical help.

## What Uganda did after that

Uganda’s dictator **Idi Amin** became angry because Kenya helped Israel. After the raid, Uganda-Kenya tension increased, and Amin threatened or considered retaliation against Kenya. A U.S. diplomatic document from July 1976 says Amin might order a military strike against Kenya to avenge the Entebbe raid.

Many accounts say Amin also ordered the killing of **hundreds of Kenyans living in Uganda** in retaliation for Kenya’s assistance. One commonly mentioned figure is about **245 Kenyans killed**, and thousands fled Uganda as refugees.
