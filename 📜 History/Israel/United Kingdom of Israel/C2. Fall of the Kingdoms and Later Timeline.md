## 1.  Division After King Solomon

After the death of King Solomon, the United Kingdom of Israel divided into two kingdoms:

- Northern Kingdom of Israel
- Southern Kingdom of Judah
  
This division happened around **931 BCE**.

## 2. Fall of the Two Kingdoms

## 3. Fall of the Northern Kingdom of Israel

The northern Kingdom of Israel was conquered by the **Assyrian Empire** in **722 BCE**.
Many Israelites were deported by the Assyrians. 
This weakened the northern tribes of Israel. 
  
## 4. Fall of the Southern Kingdom of Judah 

The southern Kingdom of Judah was conquered by the **Babylonian Empire** in **586 BCE**. 
Jerusalem was captured. 
The **First Temple / Solomon’s Temple** was destroyed.
Many Jews were taken into exile in Babylon. 
This period is called the **Babylonian Exile** or **Babylonian Captivity**.

## 5. Babylonian Exile

In 586 BCE, the Babylonian Empire conquered the Kingdom of Judah.

Jerusalem was captured, and the First Temple / Solomon’s Temple was destroyed.

Many Jews were taken into exile in Babylon.

This period is called the **Babylonian Exile** or **Babylonian Captivity**.

This was one of the most important events in Jewish history.

## 7. King Cyrus and Return of the Jews

In **539 BCE**, Persian King **Cyrus the Great** defeated Babylon. After this, he allowed many Jews to return to Jerusalem. 
The Jews rebuilt the Temple in Jerusalem. This rebuilt temple is known as the **Second Temple**.

## 8. Second Temple

The Second Temple was built on the same Temple Mount area where the First Temple had stood.
The Second Temple was completed around **516 BCE**. 
It was built on the same **Temple Mount** area where the First Temple had stood. 
The Second Temple became the central place of Jewish worship.
## 9. Greek Rule After Alexander the Great

 Around **330 BCE**, **Alexander the Great** defeated the Persian Empire. 
 After this, the region of Judah/Judea came under Greek influence.
Greek language, culture, and politics affected the region. This period is called the **Hellenistic period**.

## 10. Roman Control of Jerusalem and Judea

In **63 BCE**, the Roman general **Pompey the Great** captured Jerusalem. 
After this, Judea came under Roman influence.
Rome did not immediately rule Judea as a direct province.
At first, Rome controlled Judea indirectly through local rulers and client kings.
	
## 11. Julius Caesar and Judaism 

Julius Caesar later gave certain legal protections and privileges to the Jews. 
He allowed Jewish religious practices to continue under Roman authority. 
Julius Caesar was assassinated in **44 BCE**.

## 12. Herod the Great 

In **40 BCE**, the Roman Senate appointed **Herod** as **King of the Jews**. 
Herod later captured Jerusalem in **37 BCE** and began ruling Judea. 
He ruled as a **Roman client king**.
A client king means a local king who rules under the authority and support of a larger empire.
Herod is famous for expanding and renovating the **Second Temple** in Jerusalem.

## 13. Judea Becomes a Roman Province 

After Herod’s death, his kingdom was divided among his sons. 
Later, in **6 CE**, Judea became a direct Roman province. 
This means Rome began ruling Judea more directly through Roman governors.

## 14. Destruction of the Second Temple 

In **70 CE**, the Romans destroyed the Second Temple in Jerusalem. 
This happened during the Jewish revolt against Roman rule. The destruction of the Second Temple was a major turning point in Jewish history.
