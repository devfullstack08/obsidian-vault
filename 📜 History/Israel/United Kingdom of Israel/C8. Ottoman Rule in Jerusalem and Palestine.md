## Basic Introduction

After the Mamluk period, Jerusalem and Palestine came under the control of the **Ottoman Empire**.

The Ottomans captured Jerusalem in **1517 CE**.

Ottoman rule lasted(ટકી રહેવું) for about **400 years**, from **1517 CE to 1917 CE**.

This was one of the longest periods of rule in the history of Jerusalem and Palestine.

During this period, Jerusalem remained sacred to:

- Muslims
- Christians
- Jews

The city was not usually a major political capital, but it remained very important religiously.

---

## Who Were the Ottomans?

The **Ottomans** were a Muslim Turkish empire.

Their empire was founded in Anatolia and later expanded into Europe, Asia, and Africa.

Their capital was **Constantinople**, later known as **Istanbul**.

The Ottoman Empire became one of the most powerful empires in the world.

---

## Ottoman Conquest of Jerusalem - 1517 CE

In **1517 CE**, Ottoman Sultan **Selim I** defeated the Mamluks.

After this victory, Palestine and Jerusalem came under Ottoman control.

This ended Mamluk rule and began the Ottoman period.

## Important Point

1517 CE → Ottoman control began in Jerusalem and Palestine.

---

## Selim I

**Sultan Selim I** was the Ottoman ruler who defeated the Mamluks.

During his rule, the Ottomans gained control over:

- Syria
- Palestine
- Egypt
- Hijaz

This made the Ottoman Empire the main Muslim power in the region.

---

## Jerusalem Under Early Ottoman Rule

Under Ottoman rule, Jerusalem remained a holy city.

The Ottomans protected and maintained Islamic holy places, especially:

- Al-Aqsa Mosque
- Dome of the Rock
- Al-Haram al-Sharif

Christians and Jews also continued to live in Jerusalem.

Different religious communities were allowed to manage many of their own religious affairs.

---

## Suleiman the Magnificent

The most important Ottoman ruler connected with Jerusalem was **Suleiman the Magnificent**.

He ruled from **1520 CE to 1566 CE**.

He carried out major building and restoration work in Jerusalem.

---

## Walls of Jerusalem

One of Suleiman’s most important works was rebuilding the walls of Jerusalem.

The present walls around the Old City of Jerusalem were rebuilt under Sultan Suleiman in the **16th century CE**.

These walls still stand today.

## Memory Line

Suleiman the Magnificent → Rebuilt the walls of Jerusalem

---

## Ottoman Development of Jerusalem

During Ottoman rule, Jerusalem saw many developments.

The Ottomans built and repaired:

- City walls
- Gates
- Mosques
- Markets
- Roads
- Water systems
- Religious buildings
- Public fountains

Jerusalem remained a pilgrimage city for Muslims, Christians, and Jews.

---

## Religious Communities Under Ottoman Rule

The Ottoman Empire ruled many different religious groups.

In Jerusalem, the main communities were:

- Muslims
- Christians
- Jews

Each community had its own religious institutions and leaders.

The Ottoman system allowed many communities to manage religious and personal matters through their own authorities.

---

## Millet System

The **Millet System** was an Ottoman system for managing religious communities.

Under this system, non-Muslim religious communities were allowed to manage many of their internal affairs.

This included:

- Religious practices
- Marriage matters
- Community leadership
- Education
- Charity institutions

## Simple Meaning

Millet System → Ottoman system of religious community management

---

## Status of Christians

Christians continued to live in Jerusalem under Ottoman rule.

Christian holy places remained important.

Different Christian groups competed for control over sacred sites such as:

- Church of the Holy Sepulchre
- Bethlehem Church of the Nativity
- Mount of Olives
- Via Dolorosa

European Christian powers later became more interested in protecting Christian communities and holy places.

---

## Status of Jews

Jewish communities also continued in Jerusalem and Palestine.

After the Ottoman conquest, some Jewish migration to the region increased, especially after Jews were expelled from Spain in **1492 CE**.

Many Sephardic Jews moved into Ottoman lands because the Ottoman Empire gave them refuge(આશ્રય).

Some settled in cities such as:

- Jerusalem
- Safed
- Tiberias
- Hebron

These four cities became important Jewish religious centers.

---

## Four Holy Cities of Judaism

During the Ottoman period, four cities were especially important for Jewish religious life.

| City | Importance |
|---|---|
| Jerusalem | Holiest city; Temple Mount and Jewish prayer tradition |
| Hebron | Connected with Abraham and patriarchs |
| Safed | Center of Jewish mysticism / Kabbalah |
| Tiberias | Important center of Jewish learning |

---

## Safed and Jewish Learning

Safed became an important Jewish center during the Ottoman period.

It became especially famous for **Kabbalah**, or Jewish mysticism.

Many Jewish scholars and mystics lived in Safed.

---

## Jerusalem’s Political Status

During most of the Ottoman period, Jerusalem was not the capital of a large province.

It was usually administered as part of larger Ottoman provinces.

However, because of its religious importance, Jerusalem remained symbolically very important.

Later, in the 19th century, Jerusalem’s administrative importance increased.

---

## Palestine Under Ottoman Rule

The word **Palestine** was used geographically, but it was not usually a separate independent country under Ottoman administration.

The region was divided into different Ottoman administrative units.

Areas of Palestine were connected with provinces such as:

- Damascus
- Sidon
- Beirut
- Jerusalem district

---

## Economy and Society

Most people in Palestine during Ottoman rule lived in:

- Villages
- Towns
- Agricultural communities

Important activities included:

- Farming
- Olive cultivation
- Trade
- Pilgrimage services
- Religious endowments
- Local crafts

Jerusalem’s economy was strongly connected with pilgrimage and religious institutions.

---

## Waqf System Under Ottomans

The **waqf** system continued under Ottoman rule.

A waqf was a religious endowment used to support:

- Mosques
- Schools
- Fountains
- Poor people
- Religious scholars
- Pilgrims
- Public services

Waqf played an important role in Jerusalem’s religious and social life.

---

## 19th Century Changes

In the 19th century, Jerusalem and Palestine began to change more rapidly.

Important changes included:

- Growth of European influence
- More Christian missionary activity
- More foreign consulates
- Increased Jewish immigration
- Modern schools and hospitals
- Better roads and communication
- Growth of Jerusalem outside the Old City walls

---

## European Influence

European powers became more interested in Jerusalem because of religious, political, and strategic reasons.

Countries like Britain, France, Russia, Germany, and Austria opened consulates or supported religious institutions.

They often claimed to protect different Christian communities.

---

## Growth Outside the Old City

For many centuries, most people lived inside the walls of Jerusalem.

In the 19th century, new neighborhoods began to develop outside the Old City walls.

This marked the beginning of modern urban growth in Jerusalem.

---

## Jewish Immigration and Zionism

In the late 19th century, Jewish immigration to Palestine increased.

This was connected with:

- Anti-Jewish persecution in Europe
- Religious attachment to the land
- Rise of modern Zionism
- Hope for Jewish national revival

## Zionism

**Zionism** was a modern Jewish nationalist movement.

It aimed to establish a homeland for the Jewish people in Palestine.

The movement became more organized in the late 19th century.

---

## Theodor Herzl

**Theodor Herzl** was one of the main founders of modern political Zionism.

In **1897 CE**, the First Zionist Congress was held in Basel, Switzerland.

This increased international attention on Palestine.

---

## Ottoman View of Zionism

The Ottoman rulers were cautious about political Zionism.

They allowed some Jewish settlement but were concerned about political demands and foreign influence.

Land purchases and immigration became sensitive issues.

---

## Late Ottoman Jerusalem

By the late Ottoman period, Jerusalem had become more international.

It had:

- Muslim population
- Christian communities
- Jewish communities
- Foreign consulates
- Missionary institutions
- Schools and hospitals
- Pilgrims from many countries

Jerusalem became a city of local religious life and international political interest.

---

## First World War and End of Ottoman Rule

The Ottoman Empire joined the First World War on the side of Germany and Austria-Hungary.

During the war, British forces advanced into Palestine.

In **1917 CE**, British General **Edmund Allenby** captured Jerusalem.

This ended Ottoman rule in Jerusalem.

After this, Palestine came under British control.

---

## Balfour Declaration - 1917 CE

In **1917 CE**, Britain issued the **Balfour Declaration**.

It supported the idea of establishing a “national home for the Jewish people” in Palestine.

At the same time, it said that the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities should not be harmed.

This declaration became very important in the later history of Palestine and Israel.

---

## End of Ottoman Period

Ottoman rule in Jerusalem ended in **1917 CE**.

After the First World War, Palestine came under the **British Mandate**.

This started a new modern political period in Palestine.

# Short Summary

Jerusalem and Palestine came under Ottoman rule in 1517 CE after Sultan Selim I defeated the Mamluks. Ottoman rule lasted about 400 years, until 1917 CE. During this period, Jerusalem remained sacred to Muslims, Christians, and Jews. Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilt the walls of Jerusalem in the 16th century. Religious communities continued under the Ottoman millet system. In the 19th century, European influence, Jewish immigration, missionary activity, and modern institutions increased. Modern Zionism developed in the late 19th century. Ottoman rule ended in 1917 when British forces captured Jerusalem during the First World War, beginning the British period in Palestine.
