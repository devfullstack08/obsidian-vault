## Basic Introduction

After the early Islamic rule of Jerusalem, the city remained under different Muslim dynasties for several centuries.

These included:

- Rashidun Caliphate
- Umayyad Caliphate
- Abbasid Caliphate
- Fatimid Caliphate

By the 11th century CE, political instability increased in the region.

At the same time, Christian Europe became more interested in controlling Jerusalem and the Holy Land.

This led to the **Crusades**.

The First Crusade began in **1095 CE**, and in **1099 CE**, the Crusaders captured Jerusalem.

This started the period of **Crusader rule in Jerusalem**.

---

## Meaning of Crusades(ધર્મયુદ્ધો)

The **Crusades** were a series of religious and military campaigns launched mainly by Western European Christians.

Their main aim was to capture or control the Holy Land, especially **Jerusalem**.

The Crusades were fought mainly between Christian Crusader forces and Muslim powers.

## Simple Meaning

Crusades → Christian military campaigns to control Jerusalem and the Holy Land

---

## Why Jerusalem Was Important to Christians

Jerusalem was sacred to Christians because it was connected with the final events of Jesus Christ’s life.

Christians connected Jerusalem with:

- Last Supper
- Prayer in Gethsemane
- Trial of Jesus
- Crucifixion
- Burial
- Resurrection belief
- Ascension tradition

Because of this, many Christians wanted free access to Jerusalem and its holy places.

---

## Background Before the First Crusade

Before the Crusades, Jerusalem was under Muslim rule.

For many centuries, Christian pilgrims were still able to visit holy places, though conditions changed under different rulers.

In the 11th century, political changes in the region created tension.

Important background factors included:

- Decline of Abbasid central power
- Fatimid control of Jerusalem
- Seljuk Turkish expansion
- Byzantine Empire facing pressure from Seljuks
- Christian concern about pilgrimage routes
- Religious enthusiasm in Europe
- Papal desire to unite Christians under church leadership

---

## Seljuk Turks and Byzantine Appeal

The **Seljuk Turks** were a Muslim Turkish power that expanded into the Middle East and Anatolia.

The Byzantine Empire was weakened by Seljuk pressure.

The Byzantine emperor asked Western Europe for military help.

This request became one of the background causes of the First Crusade.

---

## Pope Urban II and the Call for Crusade

In **1095 CE**, **Pope Urban II** called for the First Crusade at the **Council of Clermont**.

He encouraged Western European Christians to go to the East and fight for the Holy Land.

The Crusaders believed that fighting in the Crusade was a religious duty.

Many also hoped for land, wealth, forgiveness of sins, and honor.

---

| Papal authority    | Pope wanted to strengthen church leadership            |
| Political ambition | Nobles wanted land and power                           |
| Economic reason    | Some hoped for wealth and trade opportunities          |
| Spiritual promise  | Crusaders were promised religious reward               |

---

## First Crusade

The First Crusade began after Pope Urban II’s call in **1095 CE**.

Crusader armies from Western Europe travelled toward the eastern Mediterranean.

They captured several important places before reaching Jerusalem.

In **1099 CE**, the Crusaders captured Jerusalem from the Fatimids.

This was a major turning point in the history of Jerusalem.

---

## Capture of Jerusalem in 1099 CE

In **1099 CE**, Crusader forces captured Jerusalem.

The city was taken after a siege(ઘેરો).

Many Muslim and Jewish inhabitants were killed during the capture.

The Crusaders then established Christian rule in Jerusalem.

This created the **Kingdom of Jerusalem**, also called the **Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem**.

---

## Kingdom of Jerusalem

After capturing Jerusalem, the Crusaders created a Christian kingdom.

This kingdom is known as the **Kingdom of Jerusalem**.

It was ruled by Latin Christian rulers from Western Europe.

The kingdom became one of the major Crusader states in the eastern Mediterranean.

---

## Crusader States

After the First Crusade, Crusaders established several states in the eastern Mediterranean.

These are called **Crusader States**.

| Crusader State | Importance |
|---|---|
| Kingdom of Jerusalem | Most important Crusader state; centered on Jerusalem |
| County of Edessa | First Crusader state established |
| Principality of Antioch | Important northern Crusader state |
| County of Tripoli | Crusader state on the Mediterranean coast |

---

## Jerusalem Under Crusader Rule

Under Crusader rule, Jerusalem became a Christian-ruled city.

Christian churches and institutions became powerful.

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre became central to Christian religious life.

Muslims and Jews faced restrictions and hardship.

Many Muslims and Jews were removed from the city or prevented from living freely there.

---

## Religious Importance Under Crusaders

The Crusaders saw Jerusalem as the center of Christian sacred history.

Important Christian sites became strongly controlled by Latin Christians.

These included:

- Church of the Holy Sepulchre
- Mount Zion
- Via Dolorosa
- Mount of Olives
- Bethlehem
- Other pilgrimage sites

---

## Treatment of Muslims and Jews

After the Crusader capture of Jerusalem, Muslims and Jews suffered greatly.

Many were killed during the conquest.

Under Crusader rule, Muslim and Jewish presence in Jerusalem was strongly restricted.

The city became mainly a Latin Christian religious and political center.

---

## Al-Aqsa and Dome of the Rock Under Crusaders

During Crusader rule, Muslim holy sites were taken over and repurposed.

| Site | Crusader Use |
|---|---|
| Dome of the Rock | Turned into a Christian church called Templum Domini |
| Al-Aqsa Mosque | Used by Crusaders and later associated with the Knights Templar |

This shows how religious control of Jerusalem changed during Crusader rule.

---

## Knights Templar

The **Knights Templar** were a Christian military order founded during the Crusader period.

They were originally created to protect Christian pilgrims.

They became one of the most powerful military orders of the Crusades.

Their name is connected with the Temple Mount area because they were based near Al-Aqsa Mosque.

---

## Important Christian Military Orders

| Order | Role |
|---|---|
| Knights Templar | Protected pilgrims and fought in Crusades |
| Knights Hospitaller | Cared for pilgrims and later became military order |
| Teutonic Knights | German crusading military order |

---

## Muslim Response to Crusader Rule

Muslim rulers gradually began efforts to resist and remove Crusader rule.

At first, Muslim powers were divided.

Over time, Muslim leaders began to unite against the Crusaders.

Important Muslim figures included:

- Imad ad-Din Zengi
- Nur ad-Din
- Salah ad-Din / Saladin

---

## Zengi and Nur ad-Din

**Zengi** was an important Muslim ruler who fought against the Crusader states.

In **1144 CE**, Zengi captured Edessa.

This was a major Muslim victory and weakened the Crusaders.

After Zengi, **Nur ad-Din** continued the struggle against Crusader power.

These leaders prepared the background for the rise of Salah ad-Din.

---

## Salah ad-Din / Saladin

**Salah ad-Din**, known in the West as **Saladin**, was a Muslim ruler and military leader.

He united Egypt and Syria and became the most important Muslim leader against the Crusaders.

He is famous for recapturing Jerusalem in **1187 CE**.

---

## Battle of Hattin - 1187 CE

The **Battle of Hattin** was fought in **1187 CE**.

Salah ad-Din defeated the Crusader army.

This victory opened the way for the Muslim recapture of Jerusalem.

---

## Recapture of Jerusalem by Salah ad-Din

After the Battle of Hattin, Salah ad-Din captured Jerusalem in **1187 CE**.

Unlike the Crusader conquest of 1099, the Muslim recapture is often remembered as more controlled and less destructive.

Christians were generally allowed to leave after paying ransom, and Eastern Christian communities remained.

Jerusalem returned to Muslim rule.

---

## Importance of 1187 CE

The year **1187 CE** is very important because:

- Salah ad-Din defeated the Crusaders at Hattin.
- Muslims recaptured Jerusalem.
- Crusader control of Jerusalem ended.
- This led to the Third Crusade.

---

## Third Crusade

The fall of Jerusalem to Salah ad-Din shocked Christian Europe.

This led to the **Third Crusade**.

Important European rulers joined the Third Crusade, including:

- Richard the Lionheart of England
- Philip II of France
- Frederick Barbarossa of the Holy Roman Empire

The Third Crusade failed to recapture Jerusalem, but it secured some coastal areas and pilgrimage access for Christians.

## Short Summary

The Crusades were Christian military campaigns launched mainly by Western European Christians to control Jerusalem and the Holy Land. In 1095 CE, Pope Urban II called for the First Crusade. In 1099 CE, Crusaders captured Jerusalem from the Fatimids and established the Kingdom of Jerusalem. Under Crusader rule, Jerusalem became a Latin Christian city, while Muslims and Jews faced severe restrictions. Muslim resistance gradually increased under leaders like Zengi and Nur ad-Din. In 1187 CE, Salah ad-Din defeated the Crusaders at the Battle of Hattin and recaptured Jerusalem, returning the city to Muslim rule.
