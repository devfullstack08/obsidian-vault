
## Basic Introduction

After the Roman destruction of the Second Temple in **70 CE** and the suppression of the Bar Kokhba Revolt in **135 CE**, Jerusalem remained under Roman control.

In the 4th century CE, the Roman Empire began changing from a pagan empire into a Christian empire.

This change became very important for Jerusalem because the city was now developed as a major Christian sacred center.

The period from Constantine onward is often called the **Christian Roman period** or **Byzantine period** in Palestine.

---

## Background Before Constantine

| Date           | Event                              | Importance                                              |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 70 CE          | Romans destroyed the Second Temple | Jerusalem lost its central Jewish Temple                |
| 132–135 CE     | Bar Kokhba Revolt                  | Major Jewish revolt against Rome                        |
| After 135 CE   | Jerusalem renamed Aelia Capitolina | Jews were heavily restricted from entering Jerusalem    |
| 3rd century CE | Roman pagan control continued      | Jerusalem was not yet a major Christian imperial center |

After the Bar Kokhba Revolt, the Romans rebuilt Jerusalem as a Roman-style city called **Aelia Capitolina**.

Many Jews were banned or restricted from living in Jerusalem.

---

# Constantine and Jerusalem

## Constantine the Great

**Constantine the Great** was Roman Emperor from **306 CE to 337 CE**.

He was the first Roman emperor to strongly support Christianity.

His rule changed the history of Christianity and Jerusalem.

---

## Edict of Milan - 313 CE

In **313 CE**, Constantine and Licinius issued the **Edict of Milan**.

This gave religious tolerance to Christianity in the Roman Empire.

It did not make Christianity the official state religion yet.

It allowed Christians to worship openly and legally.

## Important Correction

Christianity was legalised/tolerated in **313 CE**.

Christianity became the official state religion later in **380 CE**, under Emperor Theodosius I.

---

## Jerusalem Becomes a Christian Sacred Center

Under Constantine, Jerusalem became a major Christian pilgrimage center.

Christians believed that many important events in the life of Jesus happened in and around Jerusalem, including:

- Crucifixion
- Burial
- Resurrection
- Ascension

Because of this, Constantine supported the construction of churches and Christian holy sites in Jerusalem.

---

## Helena and the True Cross

Constantine’s mother was **Helena**.

According to Christian tradition, Helena visited Jerusalem and identified several holy places connected with Jesus.

She is traditionally said to have discovered the **True Cross**, meaning the cross on which Jesus was crucified.

This tradition made Jerusalem even more important for Christian pilgrimage.

## Important Note

The discovery of the True Cross is a Christian tradition. Historians discuss it as a religious tradition, not as a fully verifiable historical fact.

---

## Church of the Holy Sepulchre

One of the most important Christian buildings built under Constantine was the **Church of the Holy Sepulchre**.

It was built in Jerusalem in the 4th century CE.

Christian tradition connects this church with:

- Crucifixion of Jesus
- Burial of Jesus
- Resurrection of Jesus

The Church of the Holy Sepulchre became one of the most sacred places in Christianity.

---

## Constantine and Constantinople

In **330 CE**, Constantine shifted the capital of the Roman Empire to **Constantinople**.

Constantinople was also called **New Rome**.

Today, Constantinople is known as **Istanbul** in Turkey.

This shift made the eastern part of the Roman Empire more powerful.

---

## Death of Constantine

Constantine died in **337 CE**.

By the time of his death, Christianity had become powerful and legally protected in the Roman Empire.

However, Christianity had not yet become the official state religion.

---

# Christianity Becomes State Religion

## Theodosius I and 380 CE

In **380 CE**, Emperor **Theodosius I** issued the **Edict of Thessalonica**.

This made Nicene Christianity the official state religion of the Roman Empire.

After this, Christianity became strongly connected with imperial power.

---

## Effect on Jerusalem and Palestine

After Christianity became the state religion:

- Jerusalem became a major Christian pilgrimage city.
- Many churches and monasteries were built.
- Christian festivals and rituals became important.
- Christian bishops became powerful.
- Jewish restrictions increased in many periods.
- Pagan temples and practices declined.

---

# Treatment of Jews During Christian Roman Rule

## Jewish Restrictions

During the Christian Roman / Byzantine period, Jews faced many restrictions.

They were often restricted from living in or entering Jerusalem.

In many periods, Jews were allowed to enter Jerusalem only on specific occasions, such as mourning the destruction of the Temple.

## Important Point

The strongest Roman ban on Jews from Jerusalem began after the **Bar Kokhba Revolt** in **135 CE**.

During the Christian Roman / Byzantine period, these restrictions generally continued and sometimes increased.

---

## Persecution and Discrimination

Jews faced discrimination under Christian Roman rule.

They faced:

- Restrictions on entering Jerusalem
- Religious limitations
- Social and legal discrimination
- Loss of political power
- Occasional violence and persecution

However, Jewish communities continued to exist in Palestine and nearby regions.

Many Jews lived outside Jerusalem, especially in Galilee and other areas.

---

# Byzantine Empire

## Meaning of Byzantine Empire

The **Byzantine Empire** means the eastern part of the Roman Empire.

It was Christian and Greek-speaking.

Its capital was **Constantinople**.

Historians usually use the term “Byzantine Empire” for the Eastern Roman Empire, especially after the western Roman Empire declined.

---

## 395 CE Division of Roman Empire

In **395 CE**, after the death of Emperor Theodosius I, the Roman Empire was permanently divided into:

| Part | Capital | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Western Roman Empire | Rome / Ravenna | Later collapsed in 476 CE |
| Eastern Roman Empire / Byzantine Empire | Constantinople | Continued for many centuries |

Palestine and Jerusalem came under the **Eastern Roman / Byzantine Empire**.

---

# Jerusalem Under Byzantine Rule

## Christian Character of Jerusalem

During the Byzantine period, Jerusalem became a strongly Christian city.

It was filled with:

- Churches
- Monasteries
- Pilgrimage routes
- Christian clergy
- Christian festivals
- Sacred sites connected with Jesus

The city became one of the most important pilgrimage centers in the Christian world.

---

## Important Churches and Sites

| Site | Importance |
|---|---|
| Church of the Holy Sepulchre | Crucifixion, burial, and resurrection tradition |
| Mount of Olives | Ascension tradition |
| Bethlehem Church of the Nativity | Birth of Jesus |
| Gethsemane | Prayer before arrest |
| Via Dolorosa tradition | Path of suffering before crucifixion |

---

# Persian Conquest of Jerusalem

## Sasanian Persian Rule - 614 CE

In **614 CE**, the Sasanian Persian Empire captured Jerusalem from the Byzantines.

This was a major shock to the Christian Byzantine Empire.

The Persians damaged Christian sites and took control of the city.

Some Jewish groups supported the Persians because they hoped for relief from Byzantine Christian restrictions.

---

## Byzantine Recovery - 629 CE

The Byzantine emperor **Heraclius** later defeated the Persians.

In **629 CE**, the Byzantines recovered Jerusalem.

After Byzantine recovery, Christian rule returned.

Jewish hopes for greater freedom were reduced again.

---

# Muslim Conquest of Jerusalem

## 638 CE

In **638 CE**, Jerusalem came under Muslim rule during the time of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab.

This ended the long period of Byzantine Christian rule over Jerusalem.

The Muslim conquest opened a new phase in the history of Jerusalem and Palestine.

# Short Summary

During the rule of Constantine the Great, Christianity became legally protected in the Roman Empire through the Edict of Milan in 313 CE. Constantine and his mother Helena helped develop Jerusalem as a major Christian sacred center. The Church of the Holy Sepulchre was built, and Jerusalem became an important pilgrimage city. In 380 CE, Theodosius I made Christianity the official state religion of the Roman Empire. After 395 CE, Palestine and Jerusalem came under the Eastern Roman or Byzantine Empire. During this Christian Roman period, Jews faced restrictions and discrimination, while Jerusalem became strongly Christian. In 614 CE, the Persians captured Jerusalem, but the Byzantines recovered it in 629 CE. Byzantine Christian rule ended when Muslims captured Jerusalem in 638 CE.
