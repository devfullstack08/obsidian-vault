## Basic Introduction

After the Crusaders captured Jerusalem in **1099 CE**, the city remained under Crusader Christian control for almost 88 years.

In **1187 CE**, the Muslim ruler **Salah ad-Din**, also known as **Saladin**, defeated the Crusaders at the **Battle of Hattin** and recaptured Jerusalem.

This ended Crusader rule in Jerusalem and began the **Ayyubid period**.

After the Ayyubids, Jerusalem came under the control of the **Mamluks**.

The Ayyubid and Mamluk periods were important because Jerusalem again became a major Muslim city, while Christian and Jewish communities also continued to exist there.

---

# Ayyubid Rule in Jerusalem

## Who Were the Ayyubids?

The **Ayyubids** were a Muslim dynasty founded by **Salah ad-Din**.

They ruled parts of:

- Egypt
- Syria
- Palestine
- Yemen
- Hijaz

Their rule began in the late 12th century.

Jerusalem came under Ayyubid control after Salah ad-Din recaptured it in **1187 CE**.

---

## Salah ad-Din / Saladin

**Salah ad-Din** was a Muslim ruler and military leader.

He is famous for:

- Uniting Egypt and Syria
- Fighting the Crusaders
- Defeating the Crusaders at the Battle of Hattin
- Recapturing Jerusalem in 1187 CE

In Western history, he is commonly known as **Saladin**.

---

## Battle of Hattin - 1187 CE

The **Battle of Hattin** was fought in **1187 CE**.

Salah ad-Din defeated the Crusader army.

This victory opened the way for the Muslim recapture of Jerusalem.

## Importance

| Point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Major Muslim victory | Crusader power was weakened |
| Opened road to Jerusalem | Salah ad-Din could move toward Jerusalem |
| Turning point | Muslim rule returned to Jerusalem |

---

## Recapture of Jerusalem - 1187 CE

After the Battle of Hattin, Salah ad-Din captured Jerusalem in **1187 CE**.

This ended Crusader control of the city.

Unlike the Crusader conquest of 1099, the Muslim recapture under Salah ad-Din is often remembered as more controlled.

Many Christians were allowed to leave after paying ransom.

Eastern Christian communities were allowed to remain.

Jerusalem returned to Muslim political control.

---

## Treatment of Christians

After Salah ad-Din recaptured Jerusalem:

- Many Latin Christians left the city.
- Some Christians were allowed to remain.
- Eastern Christian communities continued.
- Christian holy places were not completely destroyed.
- Christian pilgrims were later allowed access under agreements.

This was different from the Crusader capture of 1099, when many Muslims and Jews were killed.

---

## Treatment of Jews

Under Ayyubid rule, Jews were again allowed to live in Jerusalem in many periods.

This was important because Jews had faced restrictions under both Byzantine Christian rule and Crusader rule.

Jerusalem again became a city where Muslims, Christians, and Jews had a presence, though under Muslim political authority.

---

## Al-Aqsa Mosque and Dome of the Rock Restored

During Crusader rule, Islamic holy places had been repurposed.

After Salah ad-Din recaptured Jerusalem:

| Site             | During Crusader Rule                | After Salah ad-Din                 |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Dome of the Rock | Converted into a Christian church   | Restored as an Islamic sacred site |
| Al-Aqsa Mosque   | Used by Crusaders / Knights Templar | Restored as a mosque               |

This restoration was one of the most important religious acts after the Muslim recapture.

---

## Third Crusade

The fall of Jerusalem to Salah ad-Din shocked Christian Europe.

This led to the **Third Crusade**.

Important leaders of the Third Crusade included:

- Richard the Lionheart of England
- Philip II of France
- Frederick Barbarossa of the Holy Roman Empire

The Third Crusade did not recapture Jerusalem.

However, it secured some coastal areas and allowed Christian pilgrims to access Jerusalem.

---

## Treaty of Jaffa - 1192 CE

The **Treaty of Jaffa** was made in **1192 CE** between Salah ad-Din and Richard the Lionheart.

According to this agreement:

- Jerusalem remained under Muslim control.
- Christian pilgrims were allowed access to holy sites.
- Crusaders kept some coastal cities.

## Importance

The Treaty of Jaffa shows that Jerusalem remained Muslim-controlled, but Christian pilgrimage access was allowed.

---

# Jerusalem After Salah ad-Din

## Political Situation

After Salah ad-Din’s death in **1193 CE**, his empire was divided among his relatives.

The Ayyubid rulers controlled different regions such as Egypt, Syria, and Palestine.

Jerusalem remained important, but it was not always the main political capital.

Cairo and Damascus were more important political centers.

---

## Temporary Return of Jerusalem to Crusaders - 1229 CE

In **1229 CE**, Jerusalem temporarily returned to Crusader control through diplomacy, not a major battle.

This happened during the **Sixth Crusade**.

The Holy Roman Emperor **Frederick II** made a treaty with the Ayyubid ruler **Al-Kamil**.

Through this treaty(સંધિ), Christians gained control of Jerusalem for a period.

However, Muslims kept control of important Islamic holy sites such as Al-Haram al-Sharif.

---

## Muslim Recovery of Jerusalem - 1244 CE

In **1244 CE**, Jerusalem was again taken from Crusader control.

After this, Crusader control over Jerusalem effectively ended.

Jerusalem returned to Muslim control and later came under the Mamluks.

---

# Mamluk Rule in Jerusalem

## Who Were the Mamluks?

The **Mamluks** were a military ruling class.

They were originally slave-soldiers who rose to power.

They ruled Egypt, Syria, Palestine, and the Hijaz from **1250 CE to 1517 CE**.

Their capital was **Cairo**.

Jerusalem came under Mamluk rule after the decline of the Ayyubids.

---

## Mamluk Rule Over Jerusalem

During the Mamluk period, Jerusalem was not a major political capital.

However, it was very important religiously.

The Mamluks developed Jerusalem as a center of:

- Islamic learning
- Religious endowments
- Mosques
- Madrasas
- Sufi lodges
- Pilgrimage

---

## Mamluk Building Activity

The Mamluks built many religious and educational buildings in Jerusalem.

They especially developed areas around **Al-Haram al-Sharif**.

Important Mamluk constructions included:

- Madrasas
- Mosques
- Khanqahs
- Sufi lodges
- Markets
- Public fountains
- Religious endowment buildings

---

## Madrasas in Jerusalem

A **madrasa** is an Islamic educational institution.

During the Mamluk period, many madrasas were built in Jerusalem.

These madrasas helped make Jerusalem a center of Islamic learning.

Students and scholars came to study religion, law, Arabic, and Islamic sciences.

---

## Waqf System

The **waqf** system was very important during the Mamluk period.

A waqf is a religious endowment.

It means property or money donated for religious, educational, or charitable purposes.

Waqf supported:

- Mosques
- Schools
- Scholars
- Poor people
- Pilgrims
- Public fountains
- Religious buildings

---

## Jerusalem Under Mamluks: Society

During Mamluk rule, Jerusalem had Muslim, Christian, and Jewish communities.

Muslims were politically dominant.

Christians continued to visit and live in the city.

Jewish communities also existed in Jerusalem, though their numbers changed over time.

The city remained religiously important for all three Abrahamic religions.

---

## End of Crusader Presence

During the Mamluk period, the remaining Crusader coastal strongholds were gradually removed.

In **1291 CE**, the Mamluks captured **Acre**, the last major Crusader stronghold in the region.

This effectively ended Crusader rule in the Holy Land.

---

## Importance of 1291 CE

The capture of Acre in **1291 CE** was important because:

- It ended major Crusader power in the region.
- Mamluk control became stronger.
- Muslim rule over Palestine and Jerusalem became more stable.
- Crusader states disappeared from the mainland Levant.

---

## Jerusalem Under Late Mamluks

In the late Mamluk period, Jerusalem remained a sacred city but not a major political capital.

It was governed from Cairo and Damascus.

The city was religiously important, but economically and politically it was less powerful than cities like Cairo, Damascus, and Aleppo.

---

## End of Mamluk Rule

Mamluk rule ended when the **Ottoman Empire** defeated the Mamluks.

In **1517 CE**, the Ottomans took control of Jerusalem and Palestine.

This began the long **Ottoman period**, which lasted until the First World War.

# Short Summary

After the Crusader period, Salah ad-Din recaptured Jerusalem in 1187 CE after defeating the Crusaders at the Battle of Hattin. This began the Ayyubid period in Jerusalem. Under Salah ad-Din, Islamic holy sites such as Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock were restored, while many Christian communities and pilgrims were allowed access. Jerusalem temporarily returned to Crusader control in 1229 through a treaty during the Sixth Crusade, but Muslims recovered it again in 1244. Later, Jerusalem came under Mamluk rule. The Mamluks developed the city as an Islamic religious and educational center, building madrasas, mosques, and waqf institutions. Mamluk rule continued until the Ottomans captured Jerusalem in 1517.
