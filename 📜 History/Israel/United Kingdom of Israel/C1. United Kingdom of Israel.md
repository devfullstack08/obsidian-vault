
# United Kingdom of Israel and Division into Israel and Judah

## Basic Introduction

The **United Kingdom of Israel** refers to the early united monarchy of the Israelites.

It is called “United” because the Israelite tribes were ruled together under one kingdom before the kingdom later divided into two parts.

## Main Kings of the United Kingdom of Israel

| No. | King    | Importance                                                                         |
| --- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Saul    | First king of Israel                                                               |
| 2   | David   | Famous king; made Jerusalem an important political and religious center            |
| 3   | Solomon | Son of David; known for wisdom, wealth, and building the First Temple in Jerusalem |

## Simple Timeline

| Period         | Event                                                |

## King Saul

Saul was the first king of Israel.

He united the Israelite tribes under monarchy.

His rule marks the beginning of kingship among the Israelites.

## King David

David was one of the most famous kings of Israel.

He made Jerusalem an important center.

He is remembered as a warrior king, ruler, and important religious figure in Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions.

In Islam, David is known as **Prophet Dawud**.
## King Solomon / Prophet Sulaiman

Solomon was the son of David.

In Islam, Solomon is known as **Prophet Sulaiman**, and David is known as **Prophet Dawud**.

Solomon is known for wisdom, wealth, strong rule, and justice.

He is traditionally connected with the building of the **First Temple in Jerusalem**, also called **Solomon’s Temple**.

## First Temple

In Biblical tradition, the First Temple was built in Jerusalem as the central place of worship for the Israelites.

The First Temple is traditionally believed to have been built on the **Temple Mount** in Jerusalem.

The Temple became the most sacred religious center of ancient Israelite worship.

## Holy of Holies

The most sacred inner part of the Temple was called the **Holy of Holies**.

Only selected priests could enter this sacred area, and it was considered the closest symbolic place to the presence of God in the Temple.

## Ark of the Covenant

The **Ark of the Covenant** was kept inside the Holy of Holies.

The Ark of the Covenant was a sacred chest.

It contained the stone tablets of the **Ten Commandments**, which were given to Prophet Musa / Moses.

## Important Chain

Prophet Musa / Moses → Ten Commandments → Stone Tablets → Ark of the Covenant → Holy of Holies → First Temple / Solomon’s Temple → Temple Mount
## Division of the Kingdom

After Solomon’s death, the United Kingdom of Israel divided into two kingdoms.

The division happened because of political, tribal, and economic tensions.

## Two Kingdoms After Division

| Kingdom           | Location         | Capital   | Main Tribes         |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | --------- | ------------------- |
| Kingdom of Israel | Northern kingdom | Samaria   | Ten northern tribes |
| Kingdom of Judah  | Southern kingdom | Jerusalem | Judah and Benjamin  |

## Northern Kingdom: Israel

The northern kingdom was called the **Kingdom of Israel**.

It included most of the northern tribes of Bani Israel.

Its capital later became **Samaria**.

The Kingdom of Israel was conquered by the Assyrian Empire in 722 BCE.

## Southern Kingdom: Judah

The southern kingdom was called the **Kingdom of Judah**.

It was centered around Jerusalem.

It mainly included the tribes of Judah and Benjamin.

The word **Jew / Jewish / Yahudi** is historically connected with **Judah / Yehudah**.

The Kingdom of Judah was conquered by the Babylonian Empire in 586 BCE.

## Important Chain

Prophet Ibrahim → Prophet Ishaq → Prophet Yaqub / Israel → Bani Israel → United Kingdom of Israel → Israel and Judah

## Fall of the Two Kingdoms

| Kingdom           | Conquered By      | Approx. Date | Important Point                                                                  |

## Fall of the Northern Kingdom of Israel

The northern Kingdom of Israel was conquered by the **Assyrian Empire** in **722 BCE**.
Many Israelites were deported by the Assyrians. 
This weakened the northern tribes of Israel. 
  
## Fall of the Southern Kingdom of Judah 

The southern Kingdom of Judah was conquered by the **Babylonian Empire** in **586 BCE**. 
Jerusalem was captured. 
The **First Temple / Solomon’s Temple** was destroyed.
Many Jews were taken into exile in Babylon. 
This period is called the **Babylonian Exile** or **Babylonian Captivity**.

## Babylonian Exile

In 586 BCE, the Babylonian Empire conquered the Kingdom of Judah.

Jerusalem was captured, and the First Temple / Solomon’s Temple was destroyed.

Many Jews were taken into exile in Babylon.

This period is called the **Babylonian Exile** or **Babylonian Captivity**.

This was one of the most important events in Jewish history.
## Persian Rule and Return

## King Cyrus and Return of the Jews

In **539 BCE**, Persian King **Cyrus the Great** defeated Babylon. After this, he allowed many Jews to return to Jerusalem. 
The Jews rebuilt the Temple in Jerusalem. This rebuilt temple is known as the **Second Temple**.

## Second Temple

The Second Temple was built on the same Temple Mount area where the First Temple had stood.
The Second Temple was completed around **516 BCE**. 
It was built on the same **Temple Mount** area where the First Temple had stood. 
The Second Temple became the central place of Jewish worship.

## Short Summary

The United Kingdom of Israel was the early united monarchy of the Israelites under Saul, David, and Solomon. After Solomon’s death around 931 BCE, the kingdom divided into two parts: the northern Kingdom of Israel and the southern Kingdom of Judah. The northern kingdom was later conquered by Assyria, while the southern kingdom was conquered by Babylon.
