## Basic Introduction

After the Byzantine Christian period, Jerusalem came under Muslim rule in **638 CE**.

This happened during the rule of **Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab**, the second Rashidun Caliph.

The Muslim conquest of Jerusalem ended the long period of Byzantine Christian control and opened a new Islamic phase in the history of Jerusalem and Palestine.

Jerusalem became important in Islam because it was connected with:

- Masjid al-Aqsa
- First Qibla
- Isra and Mi‘raj
- Earlier prophets
- Abrahamic religious history

---

## Background Before Muslim Conquest

Before the Muslim conquest, Jerusalem was under the control of the **Byzantine Empire**.

The Byzantine Empire was Christian and ruled from **Constantinople**.

During Byzantine rule:

- Jerusalem became a major Christian pilgrimage center.
- Churches and monasteries were built.
- Jews faced restrictions from entering or living in Jerusalem.
- The city was strongly connected with Christian sacred history.

In **614 CE**, the Sasanian Persians captured Jerusalem from the Byzantines.

In **629 CE**, the Byzantines recovered Jerusalem.

In **638 CE**, Muslims captured Jerusalem from the Byzantines.

---

## Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab

**Umar ibn al-Khattab** was the second caliph of Islam.

He ruled from **634 CE to 644 CE**.

During his rule, Muslim armies expanded into Syria, Palestine, Egypt, Iraq, and Persia.

Jerusalem came under Muslim rule during his caliphate.

## Important Point

Muslim rule over Jerusalem began under **Caliph Umar**, not during the lifetime of Prophet Muhammad.

Prophet Muhammad died in **632 CE**, and Jerusalem came under Muslim rule in **638 CE**.

---

## Surrender of Jerusalem

When Muslim forces surrounded Jerusalem, the city was under Byzantine Christian control.

According to Islamic historical tradition, the Christian patriarch of Jerusalem, **Sophronius**, agreed to surrender the city to Caliph Umar.

Caliph Umar personally came to Jerusalem and accepted the surrender.

This event is remembered as a relatively peaceful transfer of power compared with many earlier conquests.

---

## Pact of Umar

After entering Jerusalem, Caliph Umar gave assurances to the people of the city.

This agreement is often called the **Pact of Umar** or **Covenant of Umar**.

It gave protection to the Christian population, their churches, and their property.

## Main Ideas of the Pact

| Point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Protection | Christians and their churches were protected |
| Religious freedom | Christians were allowed to continue worship |
| Safety | People’s lives and property were protected |
| Tax | Non-Muslims paid jizya under Islamic rule |
| Order | The city came under Muslim political authority |

---

## Caliph Umar and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre

According to tradition, Caliph Umar visited the **Church of the Holy Sepulchre**.

When prayer time came, he did not pray inside the church.

He prayed outside.

This was done so that Muslims in the future would not claim the church as a mosque.

This story is often used to show Umar’s respect for Christian holy places.

---

## Status of Christians Under Muslim Rule

After the Muslim conquest, Christians continued to live in Jerusalem.

Their churches and religious institutions remained important.

Christians became **dhimmis**, meaning protected non-Muslim communities under Islamic rule.

They were allowed to practice their religion but had to accept Muslim political authority and pay **jizya**.

---

## Status of Jews Under Muslim Rule

Under Byzantine Christian rule, Jews had faced restrictions in Jerusalem.

After the Muslim conquest, Jewish access to Jerusalem improved in many periods.

Some Jews were allowed to return and live in or near Jerusalem.

This was an important change from the earlier Byzantine period.

## Important Point

Muslim rule did not make Jerusalem only Islamic.

Jerusalem remained a city sacred to:

- Judaism
- Christianity
- Islam

---

## Jerusalem in Islam

Jerusalem became important in Islam because of its connection with **Masjid al-Aqsa**.

In Islamic belief, Prophet Muhammad’s **Isra and Mi‘raj** is connected with Masjid al-Aqsa.

Jerusalem was also the **first Qibla** of Muslims before the Qibla was changed to the Kaaba in Makkah.

---

## Temple Mount / Al-Haram al-Sharif

The sacred compound in Jerusalem is known by different names in different religions.

| Religion | Name / Importance |
|---|---|
| Judaism | Temple Mount; site of First and Second Temples |
| Christianity | Connected with Biblical and Jesus-era Jerusalem |
| Islam | Al-Haram al-Sharif; site of Masjid al-Aqsa and Dome of the Rock |

This area became one of the most important religious places in the world.

---

# Umayyad Period in Jerusalem

## Umayyad Rule

After the Rashidun Caliphate, the **Umayyad Caliphate** ruled much of the Islamic world.

The Umayyads ruled from **661 CE to 750 CE**.

Their capital was **Damascus** in Syria.

During the Umayyad period, Jerusalem became a major Islamic sacred city.

---

## Dome of the Rock

The **Dome of the Rock** was built in Jerusalem during the Umayyad period.

It was built by Caliph **Abd al-Malik** around **691–692 CE**.

It is located on **Al-Haram al-Sharif / Temple Mount**.

## Important Correction

The Dome of the Rock was not built by Prophet Muhammad.

It was built after his death, during the Umayyad period.

---

## Importance of Dome of the Rock

The Dome of the Rock became one of the most famous Islamic monuments.

It is connected with the sacred rock on Al-Haram al-Sharif.

Islamic tradition connects the area with Prophet Muhammad’s Mi‘raj.

The golden dome became a major symbol of Jerusalem.

---

## Al-Aqsa Mosque

The **Al-Aqsa Mosque** is also located in the same sacred compound.

It is connected with the Islamic tradition of Isra and Mi‘raj.

The present mosque structure developed over time under early Muslim rulers.

## Difference Between Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque

| Point | Dome of the Rock | Al-Aqsa Mosque |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Shrine / Islamic monument | Mosque |
| Main feature | Golden dome | Congregational prayer mosque |
| Built | Umayyad period under Abd al-Malik | Developed under early Muslim rulers |
| Location | Al-Haram al-Sharif | Al-Haram al-Sharif |
| Common confusion | Often mistaken as Al-Aqsa Mosque | Actual mosque for prayer |

---

# Abbasid Period

## Abbasid Rule

The **Abbasid Caliphate** replaced the Umayyads in **750 CE**.

The Abbasids ruled from **Baghdad**.

Jerusalem remained under Muslim rule, but Baghdad became the main political center of the Islamic world.

During the Abbasid period:

- Jerusalem remained religiously important.
- Islamic scholarship and administration developed.
- Earthquakes damaged some buildings.
- Repairs were made to religious sites.

---

# Fatimid Period

## Fatimid Rule

Later, Jerusalem came under the **Fatimid Caliphate**.

The Fatimids ruled from Egypt.

During the Fatimid period, Jerusalem remained religiously important for Muslims, Christians, and Jews.

However, political instability and religious tensions sometimes increased.

One important event was the destruction of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre by the Fatimid ruler **Al-Hakim** in **1009 CE**.

Later, the church was rebuilt.

This event became one of the background causes that increased Christian concern about access to holy places.

---

# Road to Crusades

By the 11th century, Jerusalem had passed through different Muslim rulers.

Political instability increased in the region.

Christian pilgrims from Europe still wanted access to Jerusalem and Christian holy sites.

Tensions between Christian Europe and Muslim powers increased.

In **1095 CE**, Pope Urban II called for the First Crusade.

In **1099 CE**, Crusaders captured Jerusalem.

This ended the first long phase of Muslim rule in Jerusalem and began the Crusader period.

# Short Summary

After the Byzantine period, Jerusalem came under Muslim rule in 638 CE during the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab. Caliph Umar accepted the surrender of the city and gave protection to its Christian population and holy places. Under Muslim rule, Jerusalem remained sacred to Christians and Jews, but it also became an important Islamic city because of Masjid al-Aqsa, the first Qibla, and the tradition of Isra and Mi‘raj. During the Umayyad period, the Dome of the Rock was built by Caliph Abd al-Malik around 691–692 CE. Later, Jerusalem came under Abbasid and Fatimid rule. In 1099 CE, Crusaders captured Jerusalem, beginning the Crusader period.
