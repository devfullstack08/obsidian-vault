
Jerusalem was the religious center of the Jews because the **Second Temple** was located there.

The region around Jerusalem was called **Judea**.

After Greek influence in the region, Rome slowly became powerful and started controlling Judea and Jerusalem.

## Roman Conquest of Jerusalem

In **63 BCE**, the Roman general **Pompey the Great(Pompus Magnus)** captured Jerusalem.

This event marked the beginning of Roman influence over Judea.

Rome did not immediately make Judea a direct Roman province. At first, Rome controlled the region indirectly through local rulers and client kings.

## Pompey the Great

| Person           | Role                                           |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Pompey the Great | Roman general who captured Jerusalem in 63 BCE |

Pompey entered Jerusalem during an internal Jewish political conflict.

After this, Judea came under Roman influence.

## Julius Caesar and Jewish Rights

Later, **Julius Caesar** gave certain legal protections and privileges to the Jews.

He allowed Jewish religious practices to continue under Roman authority.

Julius Caesar was assassinated in **44 BCE**.

## Herod the Great

In **40 BCE**, the Roman Senate declared 'Judea' as Roman province and appointed **Herod** as **King of the Jews**.

Herod later captured Jerusalem in **37 BCE** and began ruling Judea.

He ruled as a **Roman client king**.

A client king means a local king who rules under the authority and support of a larger empire.

Herod is famous for expanding and renovating the **Second Temple** in Jerusalem

He also expanded the **Temple Mount** area around the Second Temple.

Herod constructed various buildings:

## Judea Becomes a Roman Province

After Herod’s death, his kingdom was divided among his sons.

Later, in **6 CE**, Judea became a direct Roman province.

This means Rome began ruling Judea directly through Roman governors.

## Roman Governors

After Judea became a Roman province, Roman governors controlled the region.

One famous Roman governor was **Pontius Pilate**.

In Christian tradition, Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor involved in the trial of Jesus / Prophet Isa.

## Jewish Revolt(Roman-Jew conflict) and Destruction of the Second Temple & expulsion of Jews

Jewish people became unhappy with Roman rule.

This led to a major Jewish revolt against Rome.

In **70 CE**, the Romans captured Jerusalem and destroyed the **Second Temple**.

This was one of the most important events in Jewish history.

After this destruction, the Temple was not rebuilt again.

## Jewish Revolts, Roman-Jewish Conflict, and Destruction of the Second Temple

Jewish people became unhappy with Roman rule because of political control, heavy taxation, religious tensions, and Roman interference in Jewish life.

This led to a major Jewish revolt against Rome, known as the **First Jewish-Roman War**.

The revolt began in **66 CE**.

In **70 CE**, the Romans captured Jerusalem.

The Roman army was led by **Titus**, the son of Emperor Vespasian.

Titus destroyed the **Second Temple** in Jerusalem.

The destruction of the Second Temple was one of the most important events in Jewish history.

After this destruction, the Temple was not rebuilt again.

Many Jews were killed, enslaved, or displaced, and the Jewish diaspora increased.

Later, Titus became Roman Emperor.

However, the major Roman action of banning many Jews from Jerusalem is more closely connected with the later **Bar Kokhba Revolt** in **132–135 CE**.

After the Bar Kokhba Revolt, the Romans crushed the rebellion, renamed Judea as **Syria Palaestina**, and Jerusalem as **Aelia Capitolina**.

## Simple Flow

Persian Rule → Greek Rule → Roman Influence → Herod as Client King → Judea as Roman Province → Second Temple Destroyed

## Short Summary

In 63 BCE, the Roman general Pompey the Great captured Jerusalem and brought Judea under Roman influence. Later, the Roman Senate appointed Herod as King of the Jews in 40 BCE. Herod ruled as a Roman client king and renovated the Second Temple. In 6 CE, Judea became a direct Roman province. In 70 CE, the Romans destroyed the Second Temple during the Jewish revolt.
