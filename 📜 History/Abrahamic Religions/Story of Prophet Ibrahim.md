
## Basic Introduction

Prophet Ibrahim, also known as Abraham, is one of the most important prophets in the Abrahamic religions: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.(Around 2000 BCE to 1800 BCE, summer death - hebron)

Also called it as **Father of faith**
	
In Islamic tradition, Prophet Ibrahim is called **Khalilullah**, meaning **the friend of Allah**.

He is remembered for his strong faith, rejection of idol worship, obedience to God, and sacrifice.

## Main Message

Prophet Ibrahim taught people to worship **one God** and reject idol worship.

His main message was:

> There is only one God, and people should not worship idols, stars, sun, moon, or any created thing.

## Early Life

Prophet Ibrahim was born in a society where people worshipped idols(મૂર્તિઓ).

His father, often mentioned as **Azar** in Islamic tradition, was connected with idol-making or idol worship.

From a young age, Ibrahim questioned idol worship. He understood that idols made by human hands could not be real gods.

## Search for Truth

Prophet Ibrahim observed the stars, moon, and sun.

At first, he looked at these powerful natural objects and thought about whether they could be God.

But when the star disappeared, the moon disappeared, and the sun also set, he understood:

> Anything that rises and sets cannot be God.

So he concluded that the true God must be the Creator of the stars, moon, sun, and the whole universe.

## Breaking the Idols

One day, Prophet Ibrahim broke the idols of his people, except the biggest idol.

When the people asked who had broken the idols, Ibrahim pointed toward the biggest idol and told them to ask it.

The people replied that idols cannot speak.

Then Ibrahim showed them their mistake:

> If idols cannot speak, help, or protect themselves, how can they be gods?

## Thrown into Fire

The people became angry with Prophet Ibrahim and decided to burn him in a huge fire.

But God protected him.

In Islamic tradition, Allah commanded the fire to become cool and safe for Ibrahim.

This event shows Ibrahim’s strong faith and God’s protection.

## Migration

Prophet Ibrahim left his homeland because his people rejected the message of one God.

He migrated for the sake of God and continued spreading the message of monotheism.

## Hajar and Ismail in Makkah

According to Islamic tradition, Prophet Ibrahim left Hajar and baby Ismail in the desert valley of Makkah by God’s command.

Hajar searched for water between the hills of **Safa** and **Marwah** for her baby Ismail.

God provided water through the spring of **Zamzam**.

This event is remembered during Hajj and Umrah through the ritual of **Sa’i**.

## Sa’i

Sa’i means walking between the hills of Safa and Marwah.

In Sa’i, pilgrims complete seven walks between Safa and Marwah.

The first walk starts from Safa and ends at Marwah. The seventh walk ends at Marwah.

This ritual remembers Hajar’s struggle, patience, and trust in God while searching for water for Ismail.

## Hajj and Umrah

Hajj is performed during the special Islamic month of Dhul Hijjah.
Umrah can be performed at almost any time of the year.

## Building of the Kaaba

Prophet Ibrahim and his son Ismail built or rebuilt the **Kaaba** in Makkah.

The Kaaba became the central place of worship for one God.

In Islamic tradition, Ibrahim prayed that Makkah should become a peaceful place and that his descendants should remain guided.

## Tawaf

Tawaf means walking around the Kaaba.
During Tawaf, pilgrims complete seven rounds around the Kaaba.

## Test of Sacrifice

One of the greatest tests of Prophet Ibrahim was the command to sacrifice his beloved son.

Ibrahim was ready to obey God’s command.

But before the sacrifice happened, God replaced his son with a ram.

This event shows complete obedience, faith, and surrender to God.

It is remembered by Muslims during **Eid al-Adha**.

## Short Summary

Prophet Ibrahim was a great prophet who rejected idol worship and taught people to worship one God. He faced many tests, including being thrown into fire, migration, leaving his family in Makkah, building the Kaaba, and the test of sacrifice. He is remembered as a symbol of faith, obedience, courage, and sacrifice.
