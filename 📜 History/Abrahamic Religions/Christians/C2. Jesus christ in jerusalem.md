# Jesus and the Second Temple

After entering Jerusalem, Jesus went to the Temple.

According to Christian tradition, Jesus criticized the misuse of the Temple area.

He objected to money-changing and commercial activities connected with the Temple.

This event is often called the **Cleansing of the Temple**.

## Meaning

Jesus taught that the Temple should be a place of sincere(નિષ્ઠાવાન) worship, not greed or corruption.

This action increased conflict between Jesus and the Temple authorities.
# Conflict with Religious Authorities

In Jerusalem, Jesus faced increasing opposition from some religious leaders.

They questioned his authority, teachings, and popularity.

Jesus criticized hypocrisy, pride, and empty religious practices.

He emphasized:
- Sincere worship
- Mercy
- Justice
- Faith
- Inner purity
- Love of God
- Love of neighbor

## Main Reason for Conflict

| Reason              | Explanation                                                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Popularity          | Many people followed Jesus                                  |
| Temple criticism    | Jesus criticized misuse of the Temple                       |
| Authority           | Religious leaders questioned his authority                  |
| Messiah expectation | Some people believed Jesus was the Messiah                  |
| Roman fear          | Any popular movement could be seen as politically dangerous |
# Teachings of Jesus in Jerusalem

In Jerusalem, Jesus continued teaching people.

He taught about faith, judgment, hypocrisy, love, and the Kingdom of God.

## Important Teachings

| Teaching             | Meaning                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Love God             | God should be loved with full devotion            |
| Love your neighbor   | People should care for others                     |
| Beware of hypocrisy  | Religion should be sincere, not only outward show |
| Faith and prayer     | True faith should be strong and sincere           |
| Moral responsibility | People are responsible for their actions          |
| Kingdom of God       | God’s moral and spiritual rule                    |
# Last Supper

The Last Supper was the final meal Jesus shared with his disciples before his arrest.

In Christian tradition, this meal is very important.

It is connected with:
- Passover
- Bread and wine
- New covenant
- Jesus’ warning about betrayal
- Beginning of the Christian Eucharist / Holy Communion tradition

## Judas Iscariot

According to Christian tradition, Judas Iscariot, one of the disciples, betrayed Jesus.

He agreed to hand Jesus over to the authorities.

## Importance

The Last Supper is remembered by Christians as a sacred event connected with sacrifice, faith, and remembrance of Jesus.

The Last Supper was the final meal Jesus shared with his disciples before his arrest. In Christian tradition, it is connected with Passover, the betrayal by Judas Iscariot, and the origin of the Eucharist or Holy Communion.
# Garden of Gethsemane

After the Last Supper, Jesus went with his disciples to the Garden of Gethsemane.

There, he prayed deeply before his arrest.

According to Christian tradition, Jesus knew that suffering was coming.

His disciples became tired and slept while he prayed.

## Importance

This event shows:
- Prayer
- Emotional struggle
- Obedience to God
- Preparation for suffering
# Arrest of Jesus

Jesus was arrested in the Garden of Gethsemane.

According to Christian tradition, Judas Iscariot identified Jesus to the arresting group.

Jesus was taken to the Jewish religious authorities for questioning.

## Important People

| Person / Group     | Role                        |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Jesus              | Main figure                 |
| Judas Iscariot     | Disciple who betrayed Jesus |
| Temple authorities | Opposed Jesus               |
| Disciples          | Followers of Jesus          |
# Trial Before Jewish Authorities

After his arrest, Jesus was questioned by Jewish religious authorities.

The high priest and council questioned him about his teachings and identity.

In Christian tradition, this trial focused on religious accusations.

The authorities considered Jesus’ claims and influence dangerous.

## Important Term

| Term        | Meaning                                                     |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sanhedrin   | Jewish council connected with religious and legal authority |
| High Priest | Senior religious leader connected with the Temple           |
# Trial Before Pontius Pilate(Governor)

Because Judea was under Roman rule, the Jewish authorities could not carry out major political punishment without Roman approval.

Jesus was taken to **Pontius Pilate**, the Roman governor of Judea.

Pilate questioned Jesus.

In Christian tradition, Jesus was accused of claiming to be **King of the Jews**.

This accusation was politically serious because Rome did not tolerate rival kings or rebellion.

## Important Point

The Roman trial made the issue political.

The title “King of the Jews” could be seen by Rome as a challenge to Roman authority.
# Herod Antipas

In some Gospel accounts, Jesus was also sent to Herod Antipas.

Herod Antipas was the ruler of Galilee.

He was the son of Herod the Great.

Jesus had spent much of his ministry in Galilee, so Herod Antipas was connected with his regional background.

## Important Difference

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Herod the Great | Roman client king; ruled near the time of Jesus’ birth |
| Herod Antipas | Son of Herod the Great; ruler of Galilee during Jesus’ ministry |
# Crucifixion of Jesus

In Christian tradition, Jesus was sentenced to death by crucifixion under Roman authority.

Crucifixion was a Roman method of execution used for criminals, rebels, and people considered threats to Roman power.

After the trial, Jesus was mocked, humiliated, beaten, and insulted.

According to Christian tradition, Roman soldiers placed a crown of thorns on his head and mocked him as “King of the Jews.”

Jesus was then led through the streets of Jerusalem toward the place of crucifixion.

This path is traditionally known as the **Via Dolorosa**, meaning **Way of Suffering** or **Sorrowful Way**.

Christian tradition says that Jesus was forced to carry the cross.

During this journey, he faced public humiliation, insults, and suffering.

The suffering of Jesus from his arrest to his crucifixion is called the **Passion of Christ**.

Jesus was crucified at a place called **Golgotha** or **Calvary**, outside Jerusalem.

According to Christian belief, Jesus died on the cross.

In Christianity, the crucifixion is central because Christians believe Jesus suffered and died for the sins of humanity.

## Importance in Christianity

The crucifixion is central to Christian theology.

Christians believe Jesus died for the sins of humanity.

# Burial of Jesus

According to Christian tradition, after Jesus died, his body was taken down from the cross.

He was buried in a tomb(કબર).

A man named **Joseph of Arimathea** is connected with the burial of Jesus in the Gospels.

## Importance

The burial is important because it connects with the later Christian belief in resurrection(પુનરુત્થાન,મરેલાઓમાંથી ઊભા થવું,કબરમાંથી ઊભા થવું).
# Resurrection of Jesus

In Christianity, the resurrection means that Jesus rose from the dead.

Christians believe Jesus rose on the **third day** after his crucifixion.

This belief is central to Christianity.

The resurrection is described in the **Gospels** of the New Testament.

## Empty Tomb

According to Christian tradition, after Jesus was buried, some of his followers visited the tomb.

They found the tomb empty.

This event is known as the **Empty Tomb**.

The empty tomb became an important sign of the resurrection in Christian belief.

## Appearances After Resurrection

According to the New Testament, Jesus appeared to his disciples and followers after the resurrection.

These appearances strengthened the faith of his followers.

They believed that Jesus had truly risen from the dead.

## Forty Days

According to Christian tradition, Jesus appeared to his disciples for **forty days** after his resurrection.

During this period, he taught them and prepared them to spread his message.

## Ascension of Jesus

After forty days, Jesus ascended to heaven.

This event is called the **Ascension of Jesus**.

In Christian belief, the Ascension means that Jesus was taken up to heaven after completing his earthly mission.

## Easter

The resurrection of Jesus is remembered during **Easter**.

Easter is the most important and oldest festival of the Christian Church.

It celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

In the Western Church, Easter is usually celebrated on the first Sunday after the first full moon following the northern spring equinox.

## Importance in Christianity

The resurrection is central to Christian faith because Christians believe it shows:
- Victory over death
- Hope of eternal life
- Confirmation of Jesus as the Messiah
- Foundation of Christian preaching
- God’s power and salvation

# Islamic View of the Crucifixion

In Islam, Jesus is known as Prophet Isa.

Islam respects Isa as a great prophet and Messiah.

However, Islam does not accept the Christian belief that Jesus was crucified and died on the cross in the same way.

The Qur’an teaches that they did not kill him nor crucify him, but it appeared so to them.

Islam teaches that Allah raised Prophet Isa.

## Important Difference

| Topic           | Christianity                 | Islam                                   |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Status of Jesus | Son of God, Messiah, Savior  | Prophet and Messiah, not Son of God     |
| Crucifixion     | Jesus was crucified and died | Jesus was not truly killed or crucified |
| Resurrection    | Jesus rose from the dead     | Allah raised Isa                        |

# Short Summary

Jesus entered Jerusalem before Passover and was welcomed by many people. He visited the Second Temple and criticized misuse of the Temple area, which increased conflict with religious authorities. During the Last Supper, he shared a final meal with his disciples, and Judas Iscariot later betrayed him. Jesus was arrested in the Garden of Gethsemane, questioned by Jewish authorities, and taken to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor. In Christian tradition, Jesus was crucified at Golgotha, buried, and rose from the dead on the third day. In Islam, Prophet Isa is respected as a great prophet, but Islam does not accept that he was truly killed or crucified in the Christian sense.
