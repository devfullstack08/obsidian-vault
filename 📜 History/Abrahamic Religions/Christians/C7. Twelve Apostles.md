Apostle → From the Greek apostolos, meaning "one who is sent," "messenger," or "envoy."

In Christianity, the Twelve Apostles were disciples chosen by Jesus Christ to spread his teachings.

| Name	Key Distinction    | Background                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simon Peter (Cephas)    | A fisherman who became the leader of the Apostles and a foundational pillar of the early Church.<br>                |
| Andrew                  | Simon Peter's brother and a fellow fisherman; he was originally a disciple of John the Baptist.                     |
| James (Son of Zebedee)  | Known as "James the Greater," he was a fisherman and part of Jesus' inner circle alongside Peter and John.          |
| John (Son of Zebedee)   | James' brother, traditionally identified as "the disciple whom Jesus loved" and the author of the Gospel of John.   |
| Philip                  | A Galilean from Bethsaida who helped recruit Nathanael (Bartholomew) to follow Jesus.                               |
| Bartholomew (Nathanael) | Traditionally identified with Nathanael; later Christian traditions associate his preaching with India and Armenia. |
| Matthew (Levi)          | A former tax collector for the Roman Empire who authored the Gospel of Matthew.                                     |
| Thomas (Didymus)        | Famously dubbed "Doubting Thomas" for questioning Jesus' resurrection until he saw the wounds firsthand.            |
| James (Son of Alphaeus) | Often called "James the Less" to distinguish him from Zebedee's son; likely younger or shorter in stature.          |
| Thaddaeus (Jude)        | Also known as Jude the son of James; traditionally invoked as the patron saint of lost causes.                      |
| Simon the Zealot        | A member of the political "Zealots" faction, a group that strictly opposed Roman occupation.                        |
| Judas Iscariot          | The treasurer of the group who famously betrayed Jesus to the Roman authorities for 30 pieces of silver.            |

Note: Following Judas Iscariot's death, Matthias was chosen by the remaining eleven to replace him, maintaining the traditional number of twelve.

- Disciple = A student or follower.
- Apostle = A messenger sent with a mission.
- Matthias = Replaced Judas Iscariot.
- Paul = An important apostle, but not one of the original Twelve.
