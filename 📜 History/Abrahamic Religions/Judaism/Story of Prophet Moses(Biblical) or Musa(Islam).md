
## Basic Introduction

Prophet Musa, known as Moses in Judaism and Christianity, is one of the most important prophets in the Abrahamic religions.

In Islam, he is one of the most frequently mentioned prophets in the Qur’an and is commonly counted as being mentioned around 136 times.

He is especially connected with:
- Bani Israel (**People rescued from Egypt**)/ Israelites
- Pharaoh of Egypt / Fir‘awn
- Exodus from Egypt
- Mount Tur / Mount Sinai
- Tawrat / Torah
- Miracles of God(God supported Musa with signs)
- Struggle against tyranny

## Title

Prophet Musa is also known as **Kalim Allah**, meaning **the one to whom God spoke**, because God spoke to him directly at Mount Tur / Sinai.

## Family Line

Prophet Musa belonged to Bani Israel, the descendants of Prophet Yaqub / Israel.

Simple chain:

Prophet Ibrahim → Prophet Ishaq → Prophet Yaqub / Israel → Bani Israel → Prophet Musa

## Historical Background

At the time of Prophet Musa, Bani Israel were living in Egypt.

They were oppressed and enslaved by the Egyptian ruler known as Pharaoh / Fir‘awn.

Pharaoh was cruel and arrogant. He feared that a boy from Bani Israel would challenge his rule, so he ordered the killing of newborn Israelite boys.

## Birth of Prophet Musa

Prophet Musa was born during this dangerous period.

God inspired Musa’s mother to place baby Musa in a basket and put him into the River Nile.

In Biblical tradition, Musa’s mother is known as **Yokhebed / Yukabid**.

## Musa in Pharaoh’s Palace

The basket reached Pharaoh’s palace.

Pharaoh’s wife found the baby and loved him. She requested Pharaoh to keep the child.

So Prophet Musa grew up in Pharaoh’s palace, even though Pharaoh was the enemy of Bani Israel.

## Musa’s Mother Reunited

Baby Musa refused to drink milk from other women.

His sister followed him and suggested his own mother as a nurse.

In this way, God returned Musa to his mother safely while he was still protected in Pharaoh’s palace.

## Musa Leaves Egypt

When Musa became young, he saw an Egyptian man fighting with a man from Bani Israel.

Musa tried to help, but the Egyptian man died accidentally.

In Biblical tradition, this Egyptian man is described as a taskmaster who was beating a Hebrew slave.

Because of this, Musa became afraid and left Egypt.

He fled to Madyan / Midian, where he lived for several years.

## Musa in Madyan

In Madyan, Musa helped two women draw water for their animals.

Their father, often identified in Islamic tradition as Prophet Shu‘ayb, invited Musa.

Musa later married one of the daughters and lived in Madyan for several years, commonly mentioned as ten years in traditional accounts.

## Revelation at Mount Tur / Sinai

After many years, Musa left Madyan with his family.

On the way, he saw a fire near Mount Horeb, also known as Mount Sinai or Mount Tur.

When Musa reached there, God spoke to him directly and chose him as a prophet.
This was the beginning of his prophethood(Musa was chosen by God).

| Tradition              | Description                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Islam                  | Allah spoke directly to Prophet Musa near Mount Tur / Sinai and chose him as a prophet.               |
| Judaism / Christianity | The Angel of the Lord appeared to Moses in a burning bush, and God/Yahweh spoke to him from the bush. |

This event marked the beginning of Musa’s prophethood and mission.

God gave Musa signs and commanded him to return to Egypt, confront Pharaoh, and call him to worship one God.

## Miracles Given to Musa

| Miracle | Meaning |

| Splitting of the sea | God opened a path through the sea for Bani Israel |
| Manna and quails | Food provided to Bani Israel in the wilderness |
| Water from rock | Water came from a rock by God’s command |

## Musa and Harun

Prophet Musa asked God to make his brother Harun his helper.

Prophet Harun was also a prophet.

Harun helped Musa speak to Pharaoh and guide Bani Israel.

## Musa Confronts(face to face) Pharaoh

Musa and Harun went to Pharaoh and invited him to believe in one God and free Bani Israel.

Pharaoh rejected the message.

He accused Musa of sorcery and challenged him with his magicians.

## Contest with Magicians

Pharaoh gathered magicians to challenge Musa.

The magicians threw ropes and sticks, which appeared like snakes.

By God’s command, Musa threw his staff, and it became a real serpent(સાપ) that swallowed their false magic.

The magicians understood that Musa’s miracle was from God.

They believed in the God of Musa and Harun.

Pharaoh became angry and threatened them, but they remained firm in faith.

## Plagues / Signs Against Egypt

In Biblical tradition, Egypt faced several plagues before Pharaoh allowed the Israelites to leave.

In Islamic tradition also, several signs are mentioned, such as:
- Flood
- Locusts
- Lice
- Frogs
- Blood

These signs showed God’s power and warned Pharaoh.

## Exodus(Departure of Israelites from Egypt) from Egypt

God commanded Musa to leave Egypt with Bani Israel.

They escaped at night.

Pharaoh and his army followed them.

When Bani Israel reached the sea(Red Sea), they feared they would be captured.

By God’s command, Musa struck the sea with his staff, and the sea(Red Sea) split.

Bani Israel crossed safely.

When Pharaoh and his army entered the sea, the water returned and they drowned.

## Drowning of Pharaoh

Pharaoh drowned while chasing Musa and Bani Israel.

In Islamic tradition, Pharaoh declared belief at the moment of drowning, but it was too late.

This event shows the end of arrogance(Pharaoh’s pride led to destruction), tyranny, and oppression(Pharaoh’s cruelty toward Bani Israel).

## Mount Sinai and the Torah

After leaving Egypt, Prophet Musa went to Mount Sinai / Mount Tur.

There he received divine guidance(God guided Musa and Bani Israel).

In Islamic tradition, Musa was given the **Tawrat / Torah**( the law of God as revealed to Moses and recorded in the **first five books of the Hebrew** scriptures).

In Jewish and Christian tradition, Moses received the **Ten Commandments**.

## Sabbath

The Sabbath is a holy day of rest and worship.
In Judaism, the Sabbath is observed from Friday evening to Saturday evening.
Most Christians observe Sunday as their main day of worship.

## The Golden Calf

When Musa went to receive revelation, some people from Bani Israel became misguided.

They worshipped a golden calf.

Musa returned and became angry because they had turned away from worshipping one God.

In Islamic tradition, Prophet Harun is not blamed as the creator of the calf. He tried to guide the people, but they disobeyed.

This event teaches the danger of idol worship, weak faith, and disobedience.

## Journey in the Wilderness

After leaving Egypt, Bani Israel wandered in the wilderness for many years.

They often complained, tested Musa’s patience, and disobeyed divine guidance(God guided Musa and Bani Israel).

God provided them food and water, but many among them still showed ingratitude.

## Death of Prophet Musa

In Biblical tradition, Moses’ death is connected with Mount Nebo(current jordan).

In Islamic tradition, the Qur’an does not give a detailed death-place story.

So for exam notes, write carefully:

Prophet Musa continued to guide Bani Israel after the Exodus. Biblical tradition connects his death with Mount Nebo, while Islamic tradition focuses more on his mission, struggle against Pharaoh, and guidance of Bani Israel.

## Promised Land

After the Exodus from Egypt, Bani Israel were commanded to move toward the Promised Land.

The Promised Land is generally understood as the land of Canaan, connected with the present-day Israel-Palestine region and nearby areas.

In Biblical tradition, this land was promised by God to the descendants of Prophet Ibrahim, Prophet Ishaq, and Prophet Yaqub.

Prophet Musa led Bani Israel out of Egypt, but due to their disobedience and lack of courage, they wandered in the wilderness for many years.

In Biblical tradition, Prophet Musa did not enter the Promised Land. His death is connected with Mount Nebo, from where he saw the land from a distance.

In Islamic tradition, the Qur’an also mentions the Holy Land and the refusal of Bani Israel to enter it because they feared the powerful people living there.

## Importance in Islam

In Islam, Prophet Musa is respected as:
- A great prophet and messenger
- Kalim Allah
- Receiver of Tawrat
- Leader of Bani Israel
- Opponent of Pharaoh’s tyranny
- Symbol of courage, patience, and faith

## Importance in Judaism

In Judaism, Moses is one of the greatest figures.

He is considered:
- The lawgiver
- Leader of the Exodus
- Receiver of the Torah
- Central prophet of the Jewish tradition

## Importance in Christianity

In Christianity, Moses is respected as:
- A major prophet
- Lawgiver
- Leader of Israelites
- Important Old Testament figure

## Short Summary

Prophet Musa, known as Moses in Judaism and Christianity, was born among Bani Israel during the cruel rule of Pharaoh in Egypt. To save him, his mother placed him in a basket in the River Nile, and he was raised in Pharaoh’s palace. Later, he went to Madyan, received prophethood at Mount Tur / Sinai, and was commanded by God to confront Pharaoh. With the help of his brother Harun and by God’s miracles, Musa challenged Pharaoh, led Bani Israel out of Egypt, crossed the sea(Red sea) by God’s command, and received the Tawrat / Torah at Mount Sinai.
