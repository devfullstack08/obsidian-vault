
Eid-ul-Adha is one of the two major Islamic festivals.

It is also called:
- Eid-ul-Azha
- Bakrid
- Festival of Sacrifice

## Time

Eid-ul-Adha is celebrated in the Islamic month of Dhul Hijjah.

It is observed on the 10th day of Dhul Hijjah, after the main Hajj ritual of Arafat.

The background of Eid-ul-Adha is connected with Prophet Ibrahim and his son Ismail.

According to Islamic tradition, Prophet Ibrahim saw a command from God in a dream to sacrifice his beloved son.

Prophet Ibrahim was ready to obey God’s command, and his son Ismail also accepted it with patience and faith.

But before the sacrifice took place, God replaced Ismail with a ram(uncastrated male sheep).

This event showed Prophet Ibrahim’s complete faith, obedience, and willingness to sacrifice for God.

## Why is Eid-ul-Adha celebrated?

Eid-ul-Adha is celebrated to remember:
- Prophet Ibrahim’s obedience to God
- Prophet Ismail’s patience and submission
- The value of sacrifice
- Faith and trust in God
- Helping poor and needy people

## Qurbani / Sacrifice

On Eid-ul-Adha, Muslims perform Qurbani, which means sacrificing an animal such as a goat, sheep, cow, or camel according to Islamic rules.

The meat is usually divided into three parts:

| Part     | Given To                           |
| -------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 1st part | Family                             |
| 2nd part | Relatives, friends, and neighbours |
| 3rd part | Poor and needy people              |

## Connection with Hajj

Eid-ul-Adha is closely connected with Hajj.

Hajj pilgrims perform important rituals in Makkah during Dhul Hijjah, and Muslims around the world celebrate Eid-ul-Adha at the same time.

## Main Message

The main message of Eid-ul-Adha is not only animal sacrifice.

Its deeper meaning is:
- Obedience to God
- Sacrifice of ego and selfishness
- Charity
- Compassion
- Sharing with others
- Helping the poor
