# From Ghazni to Ghor to Delhi

Previous: [[05. Abbasids and the Rise of Regional Powers]] · Next: [[07. Geography and Visual Revision]]

## 1. Mahmud of Ghazni

**Mahmud ruled from 998 to 1030.** Ghazni, in present-day Afghanistan, was his centre. His expeditions into India are conventionally counted as **17** in exam preparation, usually placed between **1000 and 1027**; the count depends on how campaigns are grouped.

Important associations:

- **Jayapala:** defeated in **1001**.
- **Anandapala:** defeated in **1008**, often dated **1008–1009** in historical works.
- **Somnath:** campaign in **1025–1026**; **1025** is the common exam shorthand.
- **Al-Biruni:** scholar associated with Mahmud’s period; author of a major study of India, commonly called *Kitab al-Hind*.

His campaigns involved plunder, territorial control, dynastic prestige and religious claims. He annexed territory in the northwest; calling him a raider who acquired no territory is misleading. He did not establish the Delhi Sultanate. [Iranica: Mahmud](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mahmud-b-sebuktegin/); [Ikram, chapter 2](https://franpritchett.com/00islamlinks/ikram/part1_02.html).

**Name trap:** Mahmud’s son **Muhammad of Ghazni** was a different ruler. The famous Indian campaigns belong to **Mahmud**.

## 2. Why the Ghurids appear next

**Ghor** is a mountainous region of present-day Afghanistan; **Ghazni** is a city. The Ghurids and Ghaznavids were separate, sometimes hostile dynasties.

The Ghurid brothers **Ghiyath al-Din** and **Muizz al-Din Muhammad**, commonly called **Muhammad Ghori**, expanded in different directions. Ghori led campaigns into India. The dynasty is generally connected with an eastern Iranian background; it should not simply be labelled another Arab dynasty. His forces included prominent Turkic commanders. [Iranica: Ghurids](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ghurids/).

## 3. The milestones to learn

- **1175:** Ghori takes Multan.
- **1178:** defeated in Gujarat; the Chaulukya/Solanki resistance matters, not only his later victories.
- **1186:** takes Lahore, ending Ghaznavid rule there.
- **1191 — First Battle of Tarain:** Prithviraj Chauhan defeats Ghori.
- **1192 — Second Battle of Tarain:** Ghori defeats Prithviraj.
- **1194 — Chandawar:** Ghori defeats Jayachandra of the Gahadavala kingdom.
- **1206:** Ghori dies; Aibak emerges as an independent ruler in the Indian territories.

For battle revision see [IGNOU, establishment of the Delhi Sultanate](https://www.egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/20180/1/Unit-14.pdf) and [INFLIBNET, Indian campaigns](https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp01/chapter/muslim-invasions-on-india-arabs-ghazni-and-ghor/). Use the latter for the campaign sequence; some of its early-caliphate claims conflict with specialist histories.

## 4. What changed after Tarain?

Ghurid commanders held forts, collected revenue and expanded control. **Qutb-ud-din Aibak** administered and enlarged conquests in northern India; other commanders pursued separate campaigns farther east.

After Ghori’s death the empire fragmented. Aibak’s rise in **1206** is conventionally used to begin the **Mamluk/Slave dynasty** and the Delhi Sultanate. **Iltutmish** subsequently consolidated the state. Calling Ghori the direct founder of the Delhi Sultanate loses the distinction between laying its foundations and becoming its first sultan. [Ikram, chapter 3](https://franpritchett.com/00islamlinks/ikram/part1_03.html).

## 5. Why did these conquests succeed?

Study mobile cavalry, military organization, access to horses and resources, leadership, alliances and rivalries among regional powers. Outcomes varied: Ghori lost both in Gujarat and at First Tarain before later victories. Avoid a single explanation such as “all Indians were weak” or “religion alone decided the result.”

Likewise, conquest did not instantly convert the population. Religious change also involved merchants, migration, Sufi networks, patronage and local social developments over centuries.

**Memory:** Qasim → Sindh. Mahmud → Ghazni and repeated campaigns. Ghori → Tarain and foundations of northern rule. Aibak → Delhi Sultanate, 1206.
