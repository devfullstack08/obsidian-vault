# Who led after Muhammad? 632–661 CE

Back: [[02. From Muhammad to Medieval India]] · Next: [[04. Umayyads and the Road to Sindh]]

## First understand the offices

**Prophet:** a religious messenger. Muslims regard Muhammad as the final prophet.

**Caliph / khalifa:** a successor in leadership of the Muslim community. **Caliphate** means the institution or state under a caliph. It is not a continuation of prophethood.

**Ummah:** the Muslim community. **Dynasty:** a ruling family. **Sultan:** a title associated with ruling authority; later sultans could acknowledge a caliph while governing their own states.

## The succession question in 632

After Muhammad’s death, leadership was contested. Abu Bakr received allegiance at the meeting associated with **Saqifa** and became caliph. Supporters of Ali emphasized his relationship to Muhammad and his claim to leadership.

Sunni and Shia traditions interpret this succession differently. In Sunni tradition the first four caliphs are the **Rashidun**, or “Rightly Guided.” Shia traditions give Ali a distinctive status as the rightful successor and first Imam. The communities and their doctrines developed over time; they were not fully formed political parties on a single day. [Background: CFR, historical origins of the Sunni–Shia division](https://www.cfr.org/articles/sunni-shia-divide-2).

## The four caliphs

1. **Abu Bakr, 632–634:** Muhammad’s companion and father-in-law. Faced the **Ridda wars**, involving rebellions and challenges to Medina’s authority after Muhammad’s death.
2. **Umar, 634–644:** presided over major expansion into Byzantine and Sasanian lands. Remember Syria, Egypt and Iraq, with expansion into Iran.
3. **Uthman, 644–656:** associated in Islamic tradition with standardizing the written Qur’anic text. Continued expansion; his assassination in 656 intensified the political crisis.
4. **Ali, 656–661:** Muhammad’s cousin and son-in-law. Based his government at **Kufa** in Iraq; his reign was dominated by civil conflict. Assassinated in 661.

**Memory:** Abu Bakr → Umar → Uthman → Ali. [Historical overview: UC Press, *A History of Islam in America*, opening background chapter](https://content.ucpress.edu/chapters/11556.ch01.pdf).

## Why Persia matters for India

The **Byzantine Empire** was the eastern Roman Empire. The **Sasanian Empire** ruled Iran and neighbouring lands. Both had been weakened by prolonged warfare.

Arab victories in Iraq and Iran broke Sasanian imperial power. The last Sasanian king, Yazdegerd III, died in **651**. Conquest did not instantly make the entire population Muslim or erase Persian language and culture. It created political connections that later helped link the Islamic world with the approaches to India. [Iranica: Arab conquest of Iran](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/arab-ii/).

## Civil war and the transition to dynasty

The **First Fitna**, beginning in 656, was a civil war within the Muslim community. Ali faced rivals, including Muawiya, governor of Syria. After Ali’s death and Hasan’s agreement with Muawiya, Muawiya consolidated power in **661**.

The next phase is the **Umayyad dynasty**, based in Damascus. Muhammad bin Qasim’s campaign belongs to that later phase, about eighty years after Muhammad’s death.

**Recall:** First caliph? Abu Bakr. Fourth? Ali. Does “caliph” mean “next prophet”? No.
