# Umayyads: the connection to Qasim

Previous: [[03. Succession and the Rashidun Caliphs]] · Next: [[05. Abbasids and the Rise of Regional Powers]]

## Damascus becomes the centre

**Muawiya I** established the Umayyad dynasty in **661**. Its central caliphate lasted until **750**, with Damascus in Syria as its capital. Dynastic succession became established, although succession could still be contested.

The empire expanded west towards North Africa and Iberia, and east towards Central Asia and the Indus. **Qasim’s Sindh expedition was one part of this wider expansion.** [The Met: Umayyad period](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-umayyad-period-661-750).

Karbala, **680**, belongs to the schism, not to this campaign. It is in [[01. Origins of the Schism]].

Fictional scene of the arrival at a river port, the Debal moment. It is not a portrait of Qasim, and it is not the whole march. The army also came along the Makran coast. The chain above is the command; this picture is only the situation at the water.

**Qasim was a commander, not a khalifa.** The caliph during the conquest was **al-Walid I**. Al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf, governor in Iraq, sent him.

The geographical sequence was **Iran → Makran → Debal → interior Sindh → Multan**. Debal was a port in the lower Indus coastal region; its precise ancient location is debated. Multan lies farther north in Punjab.

Remember **711–712** for the campaign and **712** for Dahir’s defeat. This established Arab rule in Sindh and parts of the lower Indus region, not throughout India. [S. M. Ikram, *Muslim Civilization in India*, chapter 1, formerly hosted at Columbia](https://franpritchett.com/00islamlinks/ikram/part1_01.html).

## Why did the campaign happen?

Chronicles describe an attack on shipping and the taking of captives near Debal as an immediate cause. Put that story within the wider struggle for frontier control, trade and imperial expansion. Some detailed stories come from much later texts; avoid treating every dramatic episode as an eyewitness account.

Arab maritime trade with India preceded the conquest. **The arrival of Muslim traders and the establishment of Muslim political rule are different questions.**

## What followed?

Qasim was recalled after the change of caliph in **715**. Accounts of his death differ. His removal did not erase the Arab foothold. Later governors attempted further expansion, but regional Indian resistance and problems of maintaining distant power limited it.

In **750**, the Abbasids displaced the Umayyads in the central caliphate. There was no immediate jump from Qasim to Ghazni: almost three centuries separate **712** from Mahmud’s accession in **998**.
