## Geography map

![[arabia-india-geography.svg]]

The coastline uses public-domain **Natural Earth** data. Dots mark approximate locations. Coloured connectors explain geographical relationships; they are **not reconstructed march itineraries or empire boundaries**. No modern political borders are drawn.

## Read the map west to east

1. **Mecca and Medina:** western Arabia; Muhammad’s life and the early community.
2. **Damascus:** Syria; Umayyad capital.
3. **Kufa and Baghdad:** Iraq; Ali’s political base and the later Abbasid capital respectively.
4. **Iran and Khurasan:** the broad bridge towards Central Asia and Afghanistan.
5. **Bukhara:** a major Samanid centre, north of Afghanistan.
6. **Ghor and Ghazni:** different places in Afghanistan; bases of different dynasties.
7. **Makran and Sindh:** the southwestern approach relevant to Qasim.
8. **Punjab, Tarain and Delhi:** the northwestern approach and the later advance towards the Ganga plain.

**Debal:** the plotted point represents the approximate lower-Indus coastal area, not a settled archaeological identification. **Ghor** is plotted as a region, not a single capital.

## Two approaches to remember

**Qasim:** Iranian/Makran coastal frontier → Sindh → Multan.

**Ghaznavid and Ghurid campaigns:** Afghan bases → northwestern approaches → Punjab; later Ghurid expansion towards Delhi. Individual expeditions took different routes. Do not assume every army used the Khyber Pass.

The Arabian Sea also linked trading communities. The map’s military connectors do not represent all the routes through which Islam, goods or ideas reached India.
