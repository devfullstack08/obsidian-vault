**Study question:** Muhammad died in Arabia in **632 CE**. How do we reach Qasim in Sindh, Mahmud at Ghazni, and the Delhi Sultanate in 1206?

The connection is **successive political changes across Arabia, Iran, Central Asia and Afghanistan**. These rulers belonged to different families and states. They were not a single line of Muhammad’s descendants.

## The story in six steps

**632:** Muhammad dies at Medina. Abu Bakr becomes the first caliph. A caliph succeeds to leadership of the community; he does not become another prophet.

**632–661:** The first four caliphs govern. Arab armies expand beyond Arabia, taking major Byzantine territories and overthrowing Sasanian rule in Iran.

**661–750:** The Umayyad dynasty rules from Damascus. During this period, Muhammad bin Qasim conquers Sindh in **711–712**.

**750 onward:** The Abbasids replace the Umayyads in the central caliphate. Baghdad is founded in **762**. Over time, regional dynasties gain autonomy; the caliph’s prestige and a ruler’s actual military power increasingly become separate things.

**998–1030:** Mahmud rules at Ghazni. His Indian expeditions bring wealth and territorial gains, particularly in the northwest.

**Late 1100s–1206:** Ghurid victories establish a stronger base in northern India. After Muhammad Ghori’s death in **1206**, his commander Aibak emerges as a ruler; this is the conventional beginning of the Delhi Sultanate.

## Three names to keep separate

- **Muhammad bin Qasim:** Arab commander; Umayyads; Sindh; **712**.
- **Mahmud of Ghazni:** Ghaznavid ruler; Ghazni; campaigns in the early 1000s.
- **Muhammad Ghori:** Ghurid ruler; **Second Tarain, 1192**; connection to Aibak and **1206**.

The dates are CE. “India” in this story often means the **historical subcontinent**, including regions now in Pakistan. Modern national boundaries did not exist in this form.

## Keep the wider Indian story alongside this one

This is one essential connection, not the whole of medieval India. Study the Pratiharas, Palas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas and other regional states alongside it. Military conquest, migration, trade, scholarship and religious change happened at different speeds.
