## Basic Introduction

Jerusalem is an important city in Islam.

Although Prophet Muhammad was born in **Makkah** and later migrated to **Madinah**, Jerusalem has a special spiritual connection with his life and mission.

In Islam, Jerusalem is connected with:

- Earlier prophets
- Masjid al-Aqsa
- The first Qibla
- Isra and Mi‘raj
- Link between Islam, Judaism, and Christianity
- Continuity of Abrahamic tradition

Muslims believe that Prophet Muhammad was the final prophet, and his message continued the same monotheistic tradition of earlier prophets such as Ibrahim, Musa, Dawud, Sulaiman, and Isa.

---

## Jerusalem in Islam

Jerusalem is known in Islamic tradition as:

| Name           | Meaning                       |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Al-Quds        | The Holy City                 |
| Bayt al-Maqdis | The Holy House / Sacred House |
| Masjid al-Aqsa | The Farthest Mosque           |

Jerusalem is considered one of the most sacred cities in Islam after **Makkah** and **Madinah**.

---

## Why Jerusalem Is Important in Islam

Jerusalem is important in Islam because it is connected with many prophets.

It is associated with:

- Prophet Ibrahim / Abraham
- Prophet Dawud / David
- Prophet Sulaiman / Solomon
- Prophet Zakariya
- Prophet Yahya / John the Baptist
- Prophet Isa / Jesus
- Prophet Muhammad through Isra and Mi‘raj

This makes Jerusalem a bridge between earlier Abrahamic traditions and Islam.

---

## Masjid al-Aqsa

**Masjid al-Aqsa** means “the Farthest Mosque.”

It is located in Jerusalem.

It is one of the most important sacred places in Islam.

Masjid al-Aqsa is connected with the night journey of Prophet Muhammad.

## Important Point

Masjid al-Aqsa is part of the larger sacred area known as **Al-Haram al-Sharif** or the **Temple Mount**.

---

## Temple Mount / Al-Haram al-Sharif

The same sacred area is important in different religions.

| Religion | Importance of the Area |
|---|---|
| Judaism | Temple Mount, site of First and Second Temples |
| Christianity | Connected with Biblical and Jesus-era Jerusalem |
| Islam | Al-Haram al-Sharif, Masjid al-Aqsa, Dome of the Rock |

This is why Jerusalem is sacred and sensitive for all three Abrahamic religions.

---

# First Qibla

## Meaning of Qibla

**Qibla** means the direction Muslims face during prayer.

Today Muslims face the **Kaaba in Makkah** while praying.

But in the early period of Islam, Muslims initially prayed facing **Jerusalem**.

Because of this, Jerusalem is called the **first Qibla** in Islam.

---

## Change of Qibla

After the Hijrah to Madinah, Muslims prayed facing Jerusalem for a period of time.

Later, the Qibla was changed from Jerusalem to the Kaaba in Makkah.

This change made the Kaaba the permanent direction of Muslim prayer.

## Simple Memory

First Qibla → Jerusalem  
Final Qibla → Kaaba in Makkah

---

## Importance of First Qibla

The first Qibla shows that Islam is connected with earlier prophets and earlier monotheistic traditions.

It also shows that Jerusalem had an important place in early Islamic worship.

---

# Isra and Mi‘raj

## Basic Meaning

The most important connection between Prophet Muhammad and Jerusalem is **Isra and Mi‘raj**.

Isra and Mi‘raj refers to the miraculous night journey and ascension of Prophet Muhammad.

It has two parts:

| Term   | Meaning                                                  |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Isra   | Night journey from Makkah to Masjid al-Aqsa in Jerusalem |
| Mi‘raj | Ascension(જીવનમુક્તિ) from Jerusalem to the heavens      |

## Qur’an and Hadith Difference 

The Qur’an mentions the Night Journey in **Surah Al-Isra 17:1**. 

It says that Allah took His servant by night from **Masjid al-Haram** to **Masjid al-Aqsa**. 

However, the Qur’an does not directly mention the word **Jerusalem** in that verse. 

In Islamic understanding, **Masjid al-Aqsa** is identified with Jerusalem. 

The detailed story of **Buraq**, the journey through the **seven heavens**, meeting earlier prophets, and receiving the command of **five daily prayers** comes mainly from **Hadith** and Islamic tradition.

---

## Isra: Night Journey

According to Islamic belief, Prophet Muhammad was taken by night from **Masjid al-Haram** in Makkah to **Masjid al-Aqsa** in Jerusalem.

This journey is called **Isra**.

## Route

Makkah → Jerusalem

## Important Point

This made Jerusalem directly connected with the life of Prophet Muhammad.

---
## Jibril and Buraq 

According to Hadith and Islamic tradition, Angel **Jibril** brought a heavenly mount called **Buraq** for Prophet Muhammad. 

Buraq carried Prophet Muhammad from **Makkah** to **Masjid al-Aqsa**. 

Buraq is described as a special heavenly creature, smaller than a mule and larger than a donkey. 

In popular imagination, Buraq is sometimes shown like a winged horse, but the main idea is that it was a miraculous heavenly mount. 

Some Islamic traditions also say that Buraq was used by earlier prophets.

 --- 
 
## Prophet Muhammad Leading Earlier Prophets 

According to Islamic tradition, Prophet Muhammad led earlier prophets in prayer at Masjid al-Aqsa. 

This event symbolizes: 
- Unity of all prophets 
- Continuity of divine message 
- Islam as continuation of earlier monotheistic tradition 
- Prophet Muhammad’s status as the final prophet
 
 --- 
 
## Mi‘raj: Ascension to the Heavens 

After reaching Masjid al-Aqsa, Prophet Muhammad ascended to the heavens. 

This second part is called **Mi‘raj**. 

According to Hadith, Prophet Muhammad passed through the **seven heavens** and met earlier prophets. 

He then returned to Makkah in the same night. 

--- 
## Prophets Met During Mi‘raj 

Islamic tradition mentions that Prophet Muhammad met earlier prophets during Mi‘raj. 

These include: 
- Adam 
- Isa / Jesus 
- Yahya / John the Baptist 
- Yusuf / Joseph 
- Idris 
- Harun / Aaron 
- Musa / Moses 
- Ibrahim / Abraham 
  
This shows the connection between Prophet Muhammad and earlier prophets.
 
 ---

## Seven Heavens
 
 According to Islamic tradition, Prophet Muhammad ascended through the **seven heavens** during Mi‘raj. 
 
 At different levels, he met different prophets. 
 
 This event shows: 
- Unity of all prophets 
- Continuity of divine guidance 
- Special status of Prophet Muhammad 
- Connection between Islam and earlier Abrahamic traditions
  
 --- 

## Five Daily Prayers 
 
 During Mi‘raj, the command of **five daily prayers** was given to Muslims. 
 
 This is why Isra and Mi‘raj is very important in Islam. 
 
 The event connects Jerusalem not only with Prophet Muhammad’s journey, but also with the practice of daily prayer in Islam.

# Jerusalem and Earlier Prophets

## Connection with Prophet Dawud and Sulaiman

Jerusalem is connected with Prophet Dawud and Prophet Sulaiman.

In Jewish tradition, Solomon built the First Temple in Jerusalem.

In Islam, Dawud and Sulaiman are respected as prophets.

This connects Jerusalem with Islamic prophetic history.

---

## Connection with Prophet Isa

Jerusalem is also connected with Prophet Isa.

Christians connect Jerusalem with the final events of Jesus’ life.

Islam respects Isa as a prophet and Messiah, though Islam differs from Christianity about his crucifixion and divinity.

Because of Isa’s connection with Jerusalem, the city also has importance in Islamic understanding of earlier prophets.

---

## Abrahamic Connection

Jerusalem connects Islam with Judaism and Christianity because all three religions honor many of the same prophets.

| Tradition    | Important Connection with Jerusalem                      |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Judaism      | Temple, prophets, King David, Solomon                    |
| Christianity | Jesus’ final events, crucifixion and resurrection belief |
| Islam        | First Qibla, Isra and Mi‘raj, Masjid al-Aqsa             |

# Jerusalem During Prophet Muhammad’s Lifetime

## Did Prophet Muhammad Live in Jerusalem?

Prophet Muhammad did not live in Jerusalem.

He was born in Makkah and later migrated to Madinah.

However, Jerusalem was spiritually important in his life because of:

- First Qibla
- Isra and Mi‘raj
- Masjid al-Aqsa
- Connection with earlier prophets

---

## Was Jerusalem Under Muslim Rule During His Lifetime?

No.

During Prophet Muhammad’s lifetime, Jerusalem was not under Muslim rule.

Jerusalem came under Muslim rule later, in **638 CE**, during the rule of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab.

---

# Muslim Conquest of Jerusalem

## 638 CE

After the death of Prophet Muhammad, Islam spread outside Arabia.

In **638 CE**, Jerusalem came under Muslim rule during the time of Caliph Umar.

This happened after the Byzantine period.

Caliph Umar accepted the surrender of Jerusalem and gave assurances to its people.

This began the Islamic period of Jerusalem.

---

## Importance of Caliph Umar

Caliph Umar is important in the history of Jerusalem because he entered the city peacefully and respected its religious importance.

Muslim rule of Jerusalem began under him, not during the lifetime of Prophet Muhammad.

---

# Dome of the Rock

## Basic Meaning

The **Dome of the Rock** is an important Islamic monument in Jerusalem.

It is located on Al-Haram al-Sharif / Temple Mount.

It was built later, after the time of Prophet Muhammad, during the Umayyad period.

## Important Correction

The Dome of the Rock was not built by Prophet Muhammad.

It was built in the late 7th century CE under the Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik.

---

## Difference Between Masjid al-Aqsa and Dome of the Rock

| Point | Masjid al-Aqsa | Dome of the Rock |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Mosque | Shrine / Islamic monument |
| Location | Al-Haram al-Sharif / Temple Mount | Same sacred compound |
| Importance | Connected with Isra and first Qibla tradition | Connected with the sacred rock and Islamic tradition |
| Built | Later Islamic period | Umayyad period |
| Common confusion | People sometimes call the whole compound “Al-Aqsa” | Golden dome building often confused with Al-Aqsa Mosque |

## Short Summary

Prophet Muhammad’s relationship with Jerusalem is mainly spiritual and religious. He did not live in Jerusalem, and the city was not under Muslim rule during his lifetime. However, Jerusalem is very important in Islam because it was the first Qibla of Muslims and the destination of the Isra, the night journey from Makkah to Masjid al-Aqsa. From Jerusalem, Prophet Muhammad ascended to the heavens during Mi‘raj. Jerusalem is also connected with many earlier prophets respected in Islam, such as Ibrahim, Musa, Dawud, Sulaiman, Zakariya, Yahya, and Isa. Muslim rule in Jerusalem began later in 638 CE under Caliph Umar.
