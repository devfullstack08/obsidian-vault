The Islamic year has **12 months**. Learn them in order, the way you learn January to December. The year always opens on **1 Muharram** and always ends with **Dhul Hijjah**.

The calendar’s name and first day are in [[Islamic Calendar]]. The festivals are in [[Festivals and Holy Days]].

## Read the year as one story

Say each month, then the one thing that happens in it.

1. **Muharram.** The year starts. The 1st is the Islamic New Year. The 10th is **Ashura**.
2. **Safar.** A quiet month. No big festival.
3. **Rabi' al-Awwal.** The 12th is **Mawlid**, the birth of Prophet Muhammad. The Hijrah also reaches Madinah in this month.
4. **Rabi' al-Thani.** Quiet. No big festival.
5. **Jumada al-Awwal.** Quiet. No big festival.
6. **Jumada al-Thani.** Quiet. No big festival.
7. **Rajab.** A sacred month. The night journey, **Isra and Mi'raj**, is marked on the 27th.
8. **Sha'ban.** The month just before the fast. The 15th is **Shab-e-Barat**.
9. **Ramadan.** The fasting month. People fast from dawn to sunset. **Laylat al-Qadr** is in the last ten nights.
10. **Shawwal.** The 1st is **Eid al-Fitr**, also called Ramzan Eid. The fast is over.
11. **Dhul Qa'dah.** A sacred quiet month. People prepare for Hajj.
12. **Dhul Hijjah.** The Hajj month. The 9th is the Day of **Arafah**. The 10th is **Eid al-Adha**, also called Bakrid.

Four of these months are sacred: **Muharram, Rajab, Dhul Qa'dah, Dhul Hijjah**.

Only two months contain an Eid: **Shawwal** and **Dhul Hijjah**.

## Why the names sound like seasons

These names are older than Islam. They described the weather of an Arabian year that still stayed near the seasons.

| Name sounds like | Because, in old Arabia |
| --- | --- |
| Rabi' | It fell in a spring grazing season |
| Jumada | The ground was dry, or water was frozen |
| Ramadan | The heat was severe |
| Shawwal | Camels raised their tails |
| Dhul Hijjah | The pilgrimage was held then |

The Qur'an fixed twelve months and rejected *nasī'*, the shifting of sacred months to suit the season or a tribe's plans. From then on, the lunar year slipped about 11 days earlier every solar year. Ramadan still carries a name of heat, and it now arrives in every season.

The picture of that slip is in [[Islamic Calendar]].

## Month by month

### 1. Muharram

Muharram opens the year. The **1st of Muharram** is the Islamic New Year, often called Ra's as-Sanah al-Hijriyyah.

The **10th of Muharram** is **Ashura**. Many Sunni Muslims fast on this day. Shia Muslims mourn the killing of Husayn ibn Ali at Karbala, which happened on 10 Muharram 61 AH (10 October 680 CE). The full note is in [[Festivals and Holy Days]].

Muharram is the first of the four sacred months.

### 2. Safar

The name is commonly explained as "empty": after the sacred months, people left home for travel, and the houses were empty. Some folk customs treat Safar as an unlucky month. That idea is a custom. It is not a teaching of the Qur'an.

Safar has no festival on the level of the two Eids.

### 3. Rabi' al-Awwal

Two great memories sit in this month.

| Date | Memory |
| --- | --- |
| The Hijrah, 1 AH | Prophet Muhammad arrives in Madinah. The traditional day is **12 Rabi' al-Awwal**. |
| **12 Rabi' al-Awwal** | The date most Sunni communities use for the **Mawlid**, the birth of Prophet Muhammad, in about 570 CE. |
| **17 Rabi' al-Awwal** | The birth date many Shia communities observe. |

Many Sunni histories also place the Prophet's death on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH (632 CE). Communities that celebrate the Mawlid and communities that remember the death are often looking at the same calendar day for different reasons.

### 4. Rabi' al-Thani

The second of the two months named "spring". It is also written Rabi' al-Akhir, "the last Rabi'". It has no universal festival.

### 5 and 6. The two Jumadas

**Jumada al-Awwal** and **Jumada al-Thani** take their name from dry or hard ground. Like Rabi', the name is a fossil of an old season. These two months have no universal festival.

### 7. Rajab

Rajab is a sacred month, standing alone between Jumada al-Thani and Sha'ban.

The **27th of Rajab** is the traditional date of **Isra and Mi'raj**, the Night Journey and the Ascension. The Qur'an describes the journey from the Sacred Mosque to al-Aqsa (17:1). It does not give the calendar date. The 27th is a later tradition, widely marked and not universally agreed as a fixed historical day.

### 8. Sha'ban

Sha'ban comes just before Ramadan. The Prophet is reported to have fasted in Sha'ban more than in other months outside Ramadan.

The **15th of Sha'ban** is **Laylat al-Bara'ah**, called **Shab-e-Barat** in South Asia. Many families spend the night in prayer and visit graves. Scholars differ on how strongly this night is established in the earliest sources. The detail is in [[Festivals and Holy Days]].

### 9. Ramadan

Ramadan is the ninth month and the month of the obligatory fast, from dawn to sunset. Fasting in Ramadan is one of the Five Pillars.

Muslims believe the Qur'an began to be revealed in this month, on **Laylat al-Qadr**, the Night of Power. That night is sought in the last ten nights, especially the odd nights.

The long note is [[Month of Ramdan(Islamic holy month)]].

Ramadan ends when the Shawwal crescent is seen. The next morning is Eid.

### 10. Shawwal

The **1st of Shawwal** is **Eid al-Fitr**, the festival of breaking the fast. In India it is also called Ramzan Eid or Meethi Eid.

After Eid, a voluntary fast of six days in Shawwal is recommended in a well-known hadith. Those six days can be kept any time in the month after the 1st. They are separate from the Eid celebration. Eid day itself is a day of eating, not a fasting day.

### 11. Dhul Qa'dah

Dhul Qa'dah is a sacred month. Its name means the month of sitting, because warfare stopped and people could travel toward Makkah in safety. It is the quiet month before Hajj.

### 12. Dhul Hijjah

Dhul Hijjah is the month of the pilgrimage, and it is sacred.

| Day | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1st–7th | Pilgrims are arriving in Makkah. Many enter the state of ihram. |
| 8th | Yawm al-Tarwiyah. Pilgrims go toward Mina. |
| 9th | **Yawm Arafah**, the Day of Arafah. The standing at Arafat is the heart of Hajj. |
| 10th | **Eid al-Adha**. Sacrifice, and the pilgrims' rites at Mina and the Kaaba. |
| 11th–13th | Ayyam al-Tashriq, the days of drying meat and of remaining rites. |

The background of the sacrifice is in [[Background of  Eid-ul-Adha or Eid-ul-Azha or bakrid]] and [[Story of Prophet Ibrahim]].

In a leap year of the calculated calendar, this month has 30 days. In a common year it has 29.

## The year in one strip

Muharram (New Year, Ashura) → Safar → Rabi' al-Awwal (Mawlid, Hijrah) → Rabi' al-Thani → Jumada al-Awwal → Jumada al-Thani → Rajab (Isra and Mi'raj) → Sha'ban (mid-Sha'ban) → **Ramadan** → **Shawwal (Eid al-Fitr)** → Dhul Qa'dah → **Dhul Hijjah (Hajj, Eid al-Adha)** → the next Muharram.
