Islam has **two Eids**. The other dates below are fasts, nights of worship, or days of remembrance. Communities differ on some of them. The month each one belongs to is in [[The Twelve Months]]. The calendar's name and first day are in [[Islamic Calendar]].

---

## The two Eids

| Eid             | Arabic meaning                | Date               | Indian names                   | What it marks                                            |
| --------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Eid al-Fitr** | Festival of breaking the fast | **1 Shawwal**      | Eid, Ramzan Eid, Meethi Eid    | The end of the Ramadan fast                              |
| **Eid al-Adha** | Festival of sacrifice         | **10 Dhul Hijjah** | Bakrid, Bakra Eid, Eid-ul-Azha | Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, and the Hajj |

Eid al-Fitr comes first in the year, after Ramadan. Eid al-Adha comes in the last month, during Hajj.

On both mornings, Muslims gather for the Eid prayer. They greet one another, visit family, and give in charity.

| | Eid al-Fitr | Eid al-Adha |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charity before the prayer | **Zakat al-Fitr**, a food gift for the poor, due before the Eid prayer | The meat of the sacrifice is shared |
| Food | People eat. The fast is over. | A sacrifice, **qurbani**: camel, cow, sheep, or goat, for those who are able |
| Place in worship | It closes Ramadan | It falls on the main day of the Hajj rites |
| Longer note | [[Month of Ramdan(Islamic holy month)]] | [[Background of  Eid-ul-Adha or Eid-ul-Azha or bakrid]] |

The story behind Eid al-Adha is the story of Prophet Ibrahim. In Islamic tradition the son is Ismail. Judaism and Christianity tell the test of Abraham with Isaac. That comparison is already written in the Eid note and in [[Story of Prophet Ibrahim]].

## 1 Muharram — Islamic New Year

The first day of Muharram opens the new Hijri year. People remember that the whole count of years begins from the Hijrah.

It is a day of reflection. It does not have the Eid prayer, the new clothes, and the feast that belong to the two Eids. In some countries it is a public holiday. In others the month passes into Ashura with little public notice of the 1st.

---

## 10 Muharram — Ashura

Ashura means "the tenth".

| Community          | What the day means in practice                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Many Sunni Muslims | A day of **fasting**. A hadith connects it with Prophet Musa and with gratitude for deliverance. The fast is often kept on the 9th and the 10th, or the 10th and the 11th.          |
| Shia Muslims       | A day of **mourning**. Husayn ibn Ali, grandson of Prophet Muhammad, was killed at **Karbala** on 10 Muharram **61 AH** (10 October 680 CE) with many of his family and companions. |

Both of these meanings are attached to the same date. They are different acts. A Sunni fast and a Shia mourning gathering can fall on one civil day in the same city.

Karbala is in Iraq. Husayn was the son of Ali and Fatima. The political road to that day is in [[01. Origins of the Schism]].

## 12 Rabi' al-Awwal — Mawlid

**Mawlid al-Nabi** is the birth of Prophet Muhammad. In India and Pakistan it is called **Eid-e-Milad-un-Nabi** or **Barawafat** (*barah* = twelve, *wafat* is sometimes used in the name because the same date is also linked with his death).

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Usual Sunni date | 12 Rabi' al-Awwal |
| Usual Shia date | 17 Rabi' al-Awwal |
| Year of the birth | About 570 CE, in Makkah, before the Hijrah |
| What people do | Recitation of the Qur'an, poetry in praise of the Prophet, food for the poor, processions in some cities |

This date is also the traditional date of his arrival in Madinah in 1 AH, and many Sunni reports place his death on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH.

Scholars who observe the Mawlid treat it as love for the Prophet and a retelling of his life. Scholars who avoid it, especially in the Salafi tradition, treat a fixed birthday celebration as a later custom, absent from the practice of the earliest community. Both positions exist inside Islam. The calendar date itself is the 12th of the third month.

The birth and the first revelation are in [[C1. Initial Life of Prophet Muhammad]] and [[C2. Foundation of Islam]].

## 27 Rajab — Isra and Mi'raj

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Isra | The night journey |
| Mi'raj | The ascent |

Muslims believe Prophet Muhammad was taken by night from the Sacred Mosque in Makkah to **al-Aqsa** in Jerusalem, and from there ascended through the heavens. The five daily prayers are connected with this ascent in Islamic tradition.

The Qur'an speaks of the night journey in the first verse of Surah al-Isra. It does not say "27 Rajab". That date became the common night on which mosques hold the commemoration. The relationship of the Prophet with Jerusalem is in [[C3. Prophet Muhammad’s Relationship with Jerusalem]].

---

## 15 Sha'ban — Shab-e-Barat

The Arabic name often used is **Laylat al-Bara'ah**, the Night of Freedom from sin, or the night of the middle of Sha'ban.

In India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh it is **Shab-e-Barat**. Families pray, ask forgiveness, and many visit graveyards the night before or on the day.

The practice is old and popular across much of the Muslim world. A number of hadith scholars consider the special reports about this particular night to be weak, so some communities give it no extra status. The note to remember is: it is the **15th of Sha'ban**, the month just before Ramadan, and it is not one of the two Eids.

## Ramadan and Laylat al-Qadr

Ramadan is the ninth month. From dawn (**fajr**) to sunset (**maghrib**), a fasting person leaves food, drink, and marital relations. The month is also the month of the Qur'an, of night prayer (**tarawih**), and of charity.

**Laylat al-Qadr**, the Night of Power, is the night Muslims believe the revelation began. The Qur'an says it is better than a thousand months. It sits in the last ten nights. People especially keep the odd nights:

| Night of Ramadan | Why it is watched |
| --- | --- |
| 21 | Odd night of the last ten |
| 23 | Odd night of the last ten |
| 25 | Odd night of the last ten |
| 27 | The night most widely hoped for |
| 29 | Odd night of the last ten |

The full month, the fast, and the comparison with Lent and Yom Kippur are in [[Month of Ramdan(Islamic holy month)]].

---

## 1 Shawwal — Eid al-Fitr

The Shawwal crescent ends Ramadan. The next day is Eid al-Fitr.

The morning moves in a set order: a small sweet food, the prayer, the sermon, then visits. Before the prayer, **Zakat al-Fitr** is given so that poor people can eat on Eid as well.

The six voluntary fasts of Shawwal come after this day. They are a separate act of worship inside the same month.

---

## 9 and 10 Dhul Hijjah — Arafah and Eid al-Adha

Hajj is the pilgrimage to Makkah, in the last month. It is one of the Five Pillars for every Muslim who is able to make the journey once in a lifetime.

The plain of **Arafat** is the centre of it. On the **9th of Dhul Hijjah**, pilgrims stand there in prayer from after noon until sunset. This photograph is **Jabal al-Rahmah**, the Mount of Mercy, on that plain.

![[mount-arafat.jpg]]

People who are not on Hajj often fast on the Day of Arafah. Pilgrims on the plain do not fast, because the day is for standing and supplication.

The **10th** is Eid al-Adha everywhere, including in countries far from Makkah. Those who can afford it offer a sacrifice and divide the meat among family, neighbours, and the poor. The division is set out in [[Background of  Eid-ul-Adha or Eid-ul-Azha or bakrid]].

This picture is the **Kaaba** in the middle of the Hajj, from an 1886 print. The Kaaba is the building toward which Muslims pray, and the building pilgrims circle.

![[kaaba-hajj-1886.jpg]]


The days that follow, the **11th, 12th, and 13th**, are the Days of Tashriq. Pilgrims complete the remaining rites. These three days are days of eating and of remembering God. They are not extra Eids.
