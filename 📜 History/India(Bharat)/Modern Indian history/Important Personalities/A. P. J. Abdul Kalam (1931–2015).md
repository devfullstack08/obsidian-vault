## Early life and education

Abdul Kalam was born into a modest family in Rameswaram. As a young boy, he helped distribute newspapers.

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and later studied aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT), Chennai.

He subsequently worked with two major Indian scientific organisations:

- **DRDO:** Defence Research and Development Organisation.

- **ISRO:** Indian Space Research Organisation.

## Why is he called the Missile Man of India?


Kalam played an important leadership role in India's space-launch vehicle development and indigenous guided-missile programmes.

His major contributions included:

**SLV-III:** He served as project director for India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle. In 1980, SLV-III successfully placed the Rohini satellite into orbit.

**Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP):** Launched in 1983, this programme developed India's missile capabilities, including the Agni and Prithvi systems.

**Pokhran-II (1998):** He played a significant coordinating role in India's nuclear tests alongside other scientists and government organisations.
### **Five missiles associated with IGMDP**

|Missile|Type|
|---|---|
|Agni|Ballistic missile|
|Prithvi|Surface-to-surface ballistic missile|
|Akash|Surface-to-air missile|
|Trishul|Short-range surface-to-air missile|
|Nag|Anti-tank guided missile|

**Exam point:** Kalam did not personally invent every missile. His significance lies in scientific leadership and programme development.

## Death and legacy

On **27 July 2015**, Dr. Kalam collapsed while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management (IIM), Shillong, and died later that day.

He is remembered for advocating scientific education, innovation, national development and youth empowerment.

His birth anniversary, **15 October**, is widely observed in India as a day celebrating students and education. However, be careful about claims that the United Nations officially designated it as World Students' Day.
