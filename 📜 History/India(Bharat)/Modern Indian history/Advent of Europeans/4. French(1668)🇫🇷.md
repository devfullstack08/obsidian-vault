## 1. Arrival and Establishment

- **1664:** French East India Company (_Compagnie des Indes Orientales_) established by **Jean-Baptiste Colbert**, finance minister of King **Louis XIV** of France.
    
- **1668:** First French factory established at **Surat (Gujarat)** by **François Caron**.
    
- **1669:** Second French factory established at **Masulipatnam (Andhra Pradesh)** by **Marcara**, with permission from the Sultan of Golconda.
    
- **1673:** French presence established at Pondicherry with permission from local authorities under the Bijapur Sultanate.
    
- **1674:** **François Martin** developed Pondicherry (now Puducherry), which became the principal French headquarters in India.

## 4. Anglo-French Rivalry: Three Carnatic Wars

The French initially came to India for trade, but later attempted to gain political influence. This brought them into conflict with the British East India Company.

| War                                 | Major events                                                                                                   | Result                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **First Carnatic War (1746–1748)**  | French captured Madras in 1746 under La Bourdonnais                                                            | Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748); Madras returned to British |
| **Second Carnatic War (1749–1754)** | French and British supported rival claimants in Hyderabad and the Carnatic; Robert Clive captured Arcot (1751) | Dupleix recalled; Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)               |
| **Third Carnatic War (1756–1763)**  | Battle of Wandiwash (1760); British captured Pondicherry (1761)                                                | Treaty of Paris (1763); French political                     |
Most important battle: Battle of Wandiwash (22 January 1760)

- **British commander:** Sir Eyre Coote
- **French commander:** Count de Lally
- **Winner:** British
- **Significance:** Decisively weakened French military and political ambitions in India.

## 5. Decline of French Power

**Main reasons:**
1. British naval superiority.
2. Greater British financial and commercial resources.
3. Inadequate support from the French government.
4. Differences and poor coordination among French commanders.
5. Defeat at Wandiwash in 1760.

**1763 – Treaty of Paris:** French trading settlements were restored, but France could no longer effectively challenge British political supremacy in India.

**1954 – French withdrawal:** The remaining French possessions were transferred _de facto_ to independent India on **1 November 1954**.
