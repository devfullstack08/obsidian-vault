# The Dutch in India

The Dutch are the people of the **Netherlands**. Holland was the richest province, so the country is often called Holland. Their real empire was **Indonesia**, the Spice Islands and Java. India was the shop where they bought the **cotton cloth** that paid for those spices. They cracked the Portuguese hold on the sea, and they never became the rulers of India.

## 1595 — Lisbon shuts the door

From 1580 to 1640 Portugal was joined to Spain, and Spain was at war with the Dutch. Dutch merchants had been buying pepper at Lisbon. That market closed.

**Cornelis de Houtman** sailed in **1595**. He reached **Bantam**, in Java, in **1596**. The voyage was aimed at the Spice Islands, not at an Indian province. A Dutchman who had served the Portuguese at Goa, **Jan Huyghen van Linschoten**, had already published their sailing directions. The route was no longer a Portuguese secret.

## 1602 — a company with the powers of a state

![[voc-house.jpg]]

*A house of the Dutch East India Company in the Netherlands. The company was chartered in 1602.*

On **20 March 1602** the **States-General**, the Dutch assembly, chartered the **VOC** — the **Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie**, the United East India Company. People call it the Dutch East India Company.

It was a government company. It held the monopoly of Dutch trade east of the Cape of Good Hope. It could make war, sign treaties, and coin money. In **1619** **Jan Pieterszoon Coen** founded **Batavia** (today's Jakarta). That city, not any town in India, was the capital of the Dutch East.

## India was the cloth that bought the spice

Cloves, nutmeg and pepper sat in the islands. The islanders would take Indian cotton in exchange. So the Dutch settlements in India gathered **cotton cloth from the Coromandel**, the east coast, and also silk, indigo, saltpetre and, later, opium from Bengal. The cloth went east. The spices went to Europe.

That is why their Indian posts hug the coasts and the rivers, and why Indonesia, not India, filled their colonies.

## The factories, in the order they matter

A factory was a trading post. The first one in India was **Masulipatnam (Machilipatnam), in 1605**, on the Coromandel, in the territory of Golconda.

**Pulicat, in 1610**, became the headquarters. They built **Fort Geldria** there. **Nagapattinam**, taken from the Portuguese in **1658**, later replaced Pulicat as the headquarters on that coast.

## They push the Portuguese aside, and the English toward India

The same company that was buying cloth was taking the Portuguese gates.

**Malacca fell to the Dutch in 1641.** **Cochin fell in 1663.** With those two harbours, and with the Spice Islands already in their hands, the Portuguese sea monopoly was finished.

In **1623**, at **Amboyna** in the Spice Islands, the Dutch executed the English merchants settled there. After that the English put their strength into India. The Dutch kept the islands. One massacre drew the line between the two companies.

## 1741 and 1759 — the two defeats that end the bid

On the Malabar, **Marthanda Varma of Travancore** defeated the Dutch at **Colachel in 1741**. Their expansion down that coast stopped.

In Bengal they tried once to match the English, after Plassey. On **25 November 1759**, at **Bedara (Biderra)**, near Chinsurah, **Colonel Forde** destroyed the Dutch force. The English won. After Bedara the Dutch remained traders in Bengal. They were no longer a rival power.

## 1824 — India is handed over, Indonesia is kept

During the wars with France the English occupied the Dutch posts in India. The **Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824** made the division permanent. The Dutch ceded their Indian settlements to the English. They kept the East Indies.

The Dutch were the first Europeans to break the Portuguese spice monopoly. Their monument in India is a line of factories, a cemetery at Chinsurah, and a palace at Cochin. Their empire was in Indonesia.

| --- | --- |

| The VOC was founded in 1600 | **1600** is the English company. The Dutch company is **1602** |
| Their capital in the East was Goa or Pulicat | **Batavia**, in Java, was the capital. Pulicat and later Nagapattinam were only the Coromandel headquarters |
| The first Dutch factory in India was Nagapattinam | The first was **Masulipatnam, 1605**. Nagapattinam was taken in **1658** and became the later headquarters |

| Bedara was a Dutch victory | **Bedara, 1759**, was an English victory under **Forde** |
