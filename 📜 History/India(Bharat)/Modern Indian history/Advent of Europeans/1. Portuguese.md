# The Portuguese in India

A small Atlantic kingdom reached India before any other European power. It did not conquer the country. It took the harbours, sold a pass to every other ship, and, through Goa, sold horses to Vijayanagara. When other Europeans brought the same kind of ships, and Indian rulers took the ports back, Goa, Daman and Diu were all that remained, until 1961.

## 1488 — the cape that opened the road

![[cape-of-good-hope.jpg]]

**Bartolomeu Dias rounded this cape in 1488.**

Europe wanted Indian pepper. The old road ran through Venetian and Ottoman hands, and after the Ottomans held the eastern Mediterranean that road grew costly. Portugal faced the Atlantic, so its sailors worked down the coast of Africa. Once Dias had turned this corner, India was one more voyage.

## The ship that could make that voyage

![[caravel-boa-esperanca.jpg]]

**The caravel was light, with lateen sails, and could sail closer to the wind.** That is how they worked down Africa and back.

The **carrack**, the nau, was the large ship. It carried the cargo and the cannon. The **compass** and the **astrolabe** gave direction and latitude out of sight of land.

The gun decided the later battles. Indian and Arab fleets still fought by boarding. A Portuguese ship fired from the deck, so a smaller fleet could break a larger one.

## 20 May 1498 — Calicut

![[vasco-da-gama-portrait.jpg]]

*A painting in Lisbon called a portrait of Vasco da Gama. It is a painting, not a photograph from his voyage. Public domain.*

![[vasco-da-gama’s-route-to-india.png]]

![[kozhikode-beach.jpg]]

*The beach at Kozhikode, the Calicut where he landed. Sulyab Thottungal, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

**Vasco da Gama reached Calicut (Kozhikode) on 20 May 1498.** A Gujarati pilot brought the ships across from the East African coast. The ruler was the **Zamorin (Samoothiri)**.

Arab merchants already sat in that trade. Da Gama was a new buyer with little to sell. He sailed home with the only thing that mattered: the Cape route worked.

## 1500 — Cochin keeps them

![[st-francis-cochin.jpg]]

*St Francis Church, Fort Kochi. The Portuguese built it. Ingo Mehling, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

**Pedro Álvares Cabral came in 1500** with a larger fleet and set up a factory at Calicut. A factory, the **feitoria**, was a trading post. The post was destroyed in the clash with the Arab merchants.

**Cochin**, the Zamorin's rival down the coast, took the Portuguese in. Cochin became the first lasting base. They had come to buy pepper. From here they stayed to decide who else might buy it.

Da Gama came back and died at Cochin in **1524**. His body was buried in this church, and later taken to Portugal.

## 1509 — Almeida takes the sea

![[diu-fort-sea.jpg]]

*Diu. The wall on the cliff is the Portuguese fort of 1535. The battle of 1509 was fought by ships on this water.*

**Francisco de Almeida**, the first Portuguese **Viceroy (1505–1509)**, wanted the sea and not the provinces. That is the **Blue Water Policy**.

His son **Lourenço** was killed at **Chaul in 1508**, fighting a fleet of the Mamluks of Egypt, Gujarat, and the Zamorin. Almeida answered at **Diu on 3 February 1509** and destroyed that fleet.

After that day the Portuguese held the Indian Ocean. Remember the two Dius. **1509 is the sea battle. 1535 is the fort.**

## 1510 — Goa, because Vijayanagara wanted horses

![[hampi-platform.jpg]]

*The Mahanavami Dibba at Hampi, built for Krishnadevaraya. Horses are carved on this platform. The living war-horses were imported, and Goa was the market. Wingedtree, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

**Afonso de Albuquerque** (the books also spell him Alfonso) governed from **1509 to 1515**. A fleet needs harbours. He is the man who turned the victory at sea into a state.

**Goa** belonged to the **Adil Shahi** ruler of Bijapur. It was also the landing place of the horses **Vijayanagara** bought for its cavalry. Under **Krishnadevaraya** that cavalry could not live on the few horses bred in the south. The horses came from Arabia and Persia.

**Timoja**, a Hindu chief tied to that horse trade and to Vijayanagara, brought Albuquerque in. The Portuguese took Goa early in **1510**, lost it when the Adil Shah's army returned, and took it for good on **25 November 1510**. Vijayanagara had not conquered the city. It wanted the horses, and a power at Goa that was not Bijapur. After 1510 the Portuguese sold those horses to Vijayanagara, and the sale became one of their steadiest incomes.

**Blue Water is Almeida. Goa, and the real start of Portuguese power in India, is Albuquerque.**

## 1511 and 1515 — the other two doors of the same sea

![[a-famosa-malacca.jpg]]

*A Famosa, Malacca. Albuquerque took the city in 1511. This gate is what remains. The Dutch took Malacca in 1641. CEphoto, Uwe Aranas, CC BY-SA 3.0.*

**Malacca** was the gate to the spice islands.

![[hormuz-fort.jpg]]

*The Portuguese fort on Hormuz island, at the mouth of the Persian Gulf. Taken in 1515. Iman Fakhri, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

**Hormuz** was the gate of the Gulf, and the road by which the horses reached India. He failed at **Aden**.

At Goa he had h**is soldiers marry local women**, so families, and not only a garrison(સૈન્યદળ), would hold the city. In the Portuguese towns he stopped **sati**.

## The state, the pass, and Bassein

![[vasai-fort.jpg]]

*The citadel gate at Bassein (Vasai). Bahadur Shah gave the port in 1534. Chimaji Appa took it for the Marathas in 1739. Gladson Machado, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

What Albuquerque had built was the **Estado da Índia**, the Portuguese State of India. The King of Portugal ruled it through the Viceroy at Goa. It was a line of forts and harbours: East Africa, Hormuz, Diu, Daman, Bassein, Goa, Cochin, Ceylon, Malacca, and later Macau. It was never a realm of inland provinces.

The capital was Cochin. **Nuno da Cunha** moved it to **Goa in 1530**.

**1510 is the capture. 1530 is the capital.**

The fleet that stayed in these harbours was the **Armada**. The paper it enforced was the **cartaz**. Any ship that wanted to trade bought this pass. A ship without it could be seized. The spice monopoly was this pass, backed by cannon. It was not a tax on land.

**Bahadur Shah of Gujarat**, with Humayun behind him, gave the Portuguese **Bassein (Vasai) in 1534** and **Diu in 1535**. This Treaty of Bassein is with the Sultan of Gujarat. The Treaty of Bassein of **1802** is a different treaty, between the English and the Peshwa.

They also held Daman, Chaul, Cannanore, a settlement at Hooghly in Bengal, and San Thome at Mylapore.

## What you still see in the towns

![[se-cathedral-goa.jpg]]

*The Sé Cathedral, Old Goa. This is the Portuguese-Gothic church. iMahesh, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

![[bom-jesus-goa.jpg]]

*The Basilica of Bom Jesus, Old Goa. Baroque. The body of Francis Xavier lies here. iMahesh, CC BY-SA 4.0.*

**Francis Xavier** reached Goa in **1542** and died in **1552**. The first printing press in India was set up at **Goa in 1556**.

The **Goa Inquisition** began in **1560**. In the Portuguese towns, conversion was pressed and Hindu and Muslim practice was punished. The same state that had been welcomed at Goa as a horse-dealer now made itself hated by the people it ruled. This is the turn in the story.

## How the ports were lost

They had won with ships. They lost when other people had the same ships, and when Indian rulers wanted their own harbours back.

In **1580** Portugal was joined to Spain, and stayed joined until **1640**. The Dutch and the English were already Spain's enemies, so Portuguese ports became their targets.

**Shah Jahan** destroyed the Hooghly settlement in **1632**. The Dutch took **Malacca in 1641** — the gate in the picture above — and **Cochin in 1663**.

In **1661** Bombay, which the Portuguese held, went to Charles II of England as the dowry of **Catherine of Braganza**. He handed it to the English Company in **1668**. A Portuguese harbour had become the English capital-to-be, without a battle.

In **1739** the Marathas under **Chimaji Appa** took **Bassein**. That is the ruined gate. Goa was not taken.

Brazil was pulling Portuguese men and money across the Atlantic. Officials traded for themselves, so the King's monopoly leaked. There were never enough Portuguese to turn a line of forts into the kind of state the English later built.

Goa, Daman and Diu were what remained. India took them in **1961**.

The Portuguese were the first Europeans to arrive, and the last to leave.
