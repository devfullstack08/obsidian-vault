**Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917)** was an Indian freedom fighter, economist, educator, social reformer, and political leader. Known as the **“Grand Old Man of India,”** he was among the earliest Indian nationalists to expose the economic exploitation of India under British colonial rule through his famous **Drain of Wealth Theory**.
## Why was he so important?

1. Drain of Wealth Theory

Naoroji argued that the British were transferring India's wealth to Britain through colonial salaries, pensions, profits, and other payments.

His central argument was that India's poverty was not simply natural or accidental; British colonial economic policies were contributing to it.

2. First Indian elected to the British Parliament

In 1892, he became the first Indian elected to the British House of Commons, representing **Finsbury** Central in London.

3. One of the founders of the Indian National Congress

He helped establish the Indian National Congress in 1885 and served as its president three times: 1886, 1893, and 1906.

4. Author of Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901)

This influential book explained how colonial economic arrangements contributed to Indian poverty.

Remember: Dadabhai Naoroji helped transform criticism of British rule from a political grievance into an evidence-based economic argument.

His Drain of Wealth Theory became one of the intellectual foundations of India's freedom movement.
