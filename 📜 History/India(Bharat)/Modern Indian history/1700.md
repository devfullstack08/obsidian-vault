# 11. Aurangzeb dies — 1707

This is a very important turning point.

Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb died in 1707**.

After his death, Mughal authority gradually weakened.

Regional powers became stronger, including:

Marathas, Bengal Nawabs, Awadh, Hyderabad, Mysore, Sikhs and others.

The East India Company took advantage of this political fragmentation.

This is one major reason why a trading company was eventually able to become a political power.

---
# 12. Farrukhsiyar's Farman — 1717

Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted important trading privileges to the East India Company in **1717**.

This farman allowed significant concessions, particularly in Bengal.

It helped the Company's trade expand enormously.

The British later misused some of these commercial privileges, contributing to tensions with Bengal's rulers.

----
# 13. British vs French

The British were not the only European power trying to dominate India.

The **French East India Company** was also powerful.

The British and French fought the: **Carnatic Wars**

These wars were fought mainly in southern India.

There were three Carnatic Wars between roughly **1746 and 1763**.

The decisive event was the: **Battle of Wandiwash — 1760**

The British defeated the French.

After this, French political ambitions in India were severely weakened.

They retained a few trading settlements, but the British became the dominant European power.

----
# 14. Bengal becomes the key battleground

Bengal was one of India's richest provinces.

The ruler of Bengal was:
**Siraj-ud-Daulah**

He became Nawab in **1756**.

He opposed growing British interference and the strengthening of British fortifications.

Conflict followed.

---
# 15. Battle of Plassey — 1757

This is one of the most important dates in modern Indian history.

**23 June 1757**

Battle:

**Battle of Plassey**

Between:

**Siraj-ud-Daulah**  
vs  
**British East India Company under Robert Clive**

But the British victory was not simply because their army was stronger.

**Robert Clive** secretly made agreements with people inside Siraj's administration, especially:

**Mir Jafar**

Mir Jafar and some others did not properly support Siraj during the battle.

Siraj-ud-Daulah was defeated.

Mir Jafar was made Nawab.

### Why Plassey matters

Plassey gave the Company enormous political influence in Bengal.

This marks the beginning of the Company's transformation:

**Trading company → political power**

Remember:
**1757 = Plassey = beginning of British political dominance in India**

----
# 16. Mir Jafar and Mir Qasim

The British installed **Mir Jafar** as Nawab.

But when he could no longer satisfy British financial demands, the Company replaced him with:

**Mir Qasim**

Mir Qasim tried to become independent of British control.

Conflict developed.

Eventually, Mir Qasim joined forces with:

**Shuja-ud-Daula**, Nawab of Awadh
and
**Shah Alam II**, Mughal emperor.

This led to another decisive battle.

---

# 17. Battle of Buxar — 1764

**22 October 1764**

The British East India Company defeated the combined forces of:

Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-Daula + Shah Alam II.

The British forces were led by: **Hector Munro**

This battle is arguably even more important politically than Plassey.

Why?

Plassey gave the Company influence.

Buxar confirmed its **military and political supremacy** in eastern India.

The political result comes the next year, at Allahabad.

---

# 18. Treaty of Allahabad — 1765

**12 August 1765**

**Robert Clive** settled the victory of Buxar with two agreements at Allahabad.

**Shah Alam II** granted the Company the **Diwani** of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.

Diwani means the right to collect the land revenue and to decide the revenue cases.

In return the Company agreed to pay the emperor **Rs 26 lakh a year**, and gave him the districts of **Kora and Allahabad**.

**Shuja-ud-Daula** got Awadh back, except those two districts.

He paid a war indemnity of **Rs 50 lakh**.

Mir Jafar had died in 1765. His son **Najm-ud-Daula** was the new Nawab of Bengal. Defence of the province was already in the Company's hands.

Remember:

**1765 = Diwani = the revenue of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**
(People of Bengal usually paid 1/10th of their produces tax to mugal emperor then after few year company increase it 10% to 50%. also collect the land as well as tariffs )

The "Orissa" of this grant is the small western tract the Mughals then called Orissa, around Midnapore. It is not the whole of modern Odisha.

---

# 19. Dual Government of Bengal — 1765–1772

Clive did not take the administration into the Company's own offices.

He split the government.

| Power                                     | Who held it        |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Diwani** — revenue                      | The Company        |
| **Nizamat** — police and criminal justice | The Nawab, in name |

The Company collected the money. The Nawab was left with the duty of keeping order, and with too little money to do it.

The work on the ground was done by Indian deputies: **Muhammad Reza Khan** in Bengal and **Raja Shitab Rai** in Bihar.

This is Clive's **Dual System**, or Dual Government.

The line to write:

**The Company had power without responsibility. The Nawab had responsibility without power.**

It lasted from **1765 to 1772**.

The First Mysore War, the Treaty of Madras and the famine of 1770 fall inside these years. Hastings closes the system in 1772.

---
# 20. First Anglo-Mysore War — 1767–1769

This war was fought in the south, by the **Madras** government, while Bengal was under the Dual System.

**Haidar Ali** held Mysore.

The English and the **Nizam** began as allies. The Nizam then joined Haidar. The Marathas took money from the English and stood aside.

In **March 1769** Haidar stood at the gates of Madras. The council there made peace.

![[first-anglo-mysore-war-Infographic.png]]

---

# 21. Treaty of Madras — 1769

**April 1769.** Haidar Ali and the English.
- Each side gave back the places it had taken, and the prisoners.
- Each promised to help the other if attacked.

In **1771** the Marathas attacked Haidar. The English sent no help. Haidar treated that as a broken promise. It is the grievance he carries into the second war.

Remember:
**1769 = Treaty of Madras = conquests restored, and a promise of help.**

---

# 22. Great Bengal Famine — 1770
(imperial genocide)

The rains failed in **1769**. The famine(દુષ્કાળ) struck Bengal and Bihar in **1770**.

In Bengal it is remembered as the famine of the Bengali year 1176, **Chhiyattarer Manvantar**.

The governor in Bengal was **John Cartier**. Hastings took charge only in **1772**, after the worst year.

**Warren Hastings** later reported that about **one-third** of the inhabitants had died. The figure used in the books is about **one crore** people.

The Dual System was in force. The Company went on collecting the land revenue. The collection in **1771** was higher than in **1768**. **Muhammad Reza Khan** was the deputy who collected in Bengal. The Nawab had the duty of relief, and no money for it.

This famine is one reason Parliament passed the Regulating Act in 1773.

Remember:
**1770 = Bengal famine = inside the Dual System.**

---

# 23. Warren Hastings ends the Dual System — 1772

In **1772** **Warren Hastings** became Governor of Bengal.

He abolished the Dual System.

The Company now collected the revenue itself, and took over the civil administration.

The treasury was moved from **Murshidabad** to **Calcutta**.

# 25. Supreme Court at Calcutta — 1774

The Regulating Act set up a **Supreme Court** at Fort William, Calcutta, in **1774**.

The first Chief Justice was **Sir Elijah Impey**.

The court used English law. Its reach was not clearly drawn. It sat over British subjects and the Company's servants, and it clashed with the Company's own courts.

The case to remember is **Nandakumar**, **1775**.

![[📜 History/India(Bharat)/Modern Indian history/Assets/the-nandakumar-case-colonial-justice-infographic.png]]

The Act that drew a line around this court comes in **1781**, after the next war has begun.

---
# 26. First Anglo-Maratha War — 1775–1779

This war was fought while **Warren Hastings** was Governor-General. The peace, at Salbai, comes in **1782**.

In **1773** Peshwa **Narayan Rao** was murdered. His uncle **Raghunath Rao** claimed the seat. The ministers at Pune, with **Nana Fadnavis**, set up Narayan Rao's infant son, **Madhav Rao II**.

Bombay promised Raghunath Rao the Peshwaship by the **Treaty of Surat, 1775**, and took **Salsette** and **Bassein** as the price.

The council at Calcutta rejected Surat and made its own **Treaty of Purandar, 1776**, with the government at Pune.

Bombay fought on. In **1779** its army was surrounded and signed the **Convention of Wadgaon**. Hastings rejected that convention and carried the war on.

---
# 27. Second Anglo-Mysore War — 1780–1781

Haidar opened this war because the English had given him no help against the Marathas in **1771**, after the Treaty of Madras.

He invaded the Carnatic. The Marathas and the Nizam were with him.

In **1780** Haidar and his son **Tipu** defeated Colonel Baillie at **Pollilur**.

In **1781** **Sir Eyre Coote** defeated Haidar at **Porto Novo**.

---

Parliament passed this Act, also called the Act of Settlement, to limit the Supreme Court of 1774.

# 29. Treaty of Salbai — 1782

**17 May 1782.** **Mahadji Scindia** signed for the Marathas and stood as guarantor. This ends the first Maratha war.

- **Salsette** stayed with the Company. **Bassein**, and the other places taken since Purandar, went back to the Peshwa.
- **Madhav Rao II** was accepted as Peshwa.
- The Company withdrew its support from **Raghunath Rao**. He was left with a pension.
- The Marathas left Haidar's war. They were to press him to give back the territory he had taken from the Company.

**Haidar Ali** died in **December 1782**. Tipu carried the Mysore war on.

Remember:

**1782 = Salbai = end of the first Maratha war.**

The **Treaty of Bassein of 1802** is a later treaty, with a different Peshwa, and it opens the second Maratha war.

---

# 30. Treaty of Mangalore — 1784

**March 1784.** Tipu and the English.

Each side gave back the places it had taken, and the prisoners. The peace was made on equal terms.

Remember:

**Mangalore 1784 — Tipu keeps his land.**

### 32. [[1. The Company Rule (1773–1858)#4. Act of 1786|Company Rule: Act of 1786]]

**Lord Cornwallis** came out as Governor-General under this Act. The third Mysore war and the Permanent Settlement are his.

# 33. Third Anglo-Mysore War — 1790–1792

Tipu attacked **Travancore** in **1789**. Travancore was an ally of the Company.

**Cornwallis** fought the war with the Nizam and the Marathas.

**Treaty of Seringapatam, 18 March 1792.** Tipu gave up about half his territory, paid **3 crore rupees**, and sent two sons as hostages.

That half was shared by the Company, the Nizam and the Marathas.

Remember:

**Seringapatam 1792 — Tipu loses half, and keeps the throne.**

---

# 34. Permanent Settlement — 1793

Cornwallis fixed the land revenue of **Bengal, Bihar, and the Orissa districts then under Bengal**.

Cuttack was still with the Marathas. It was taken only in **1803**, so it is outside this settlement. The "Orissa" here is the same small western tract as in the Diwani of 1765.

A ten-year settlement had been tried from **1789**. **John Shore** wanted it kept for a term of years. In **1793** Cornwallis made the demand **permanent**.

The zamindar was recognised as the owner of the estate.

- The sum he paid the Company was fixed for ever: **ten-elevenths** of the rental.
- He kept **one-eleventh**.
- If the money was not paid by sunset on the due day, the estate could be sold. This is the **Sunset Law**.

The Company gained a fixed income, and a class of landlords. Any later rise in rent stayed with the zamindar. The cultivator's rent could still be raised.

The same John Shore was Governor-General from **1793 to 1798**. He made no fresh alliances. Wellesley reversed that.

The other two revenue systems, so the names stay apart:

| System | Where | Associated with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Permanent**, with the zamindar | Bengal, Bihar, the Orissa districts | Cornwallis, **1793** |
| **Ryotwari**, with the cultivator | Madras, and later Bombay | **Thomas Munro** and **Alexander Read** |
| **Mahalwari**, with the village estate | North-Western Provinces, later Punjab | **Holt Mackenzie**, **1822** |

# 36. Wellesley — Subsidiary Alliance and the Fourth Mysore War

**Lord Wellesley** was Governor-General from **1798 to 1805**.

He turned an offer of troops into a system. An Indian ruler who accepted a **subsidiary alliance**:

- kept a British force in his territory and paid for it
- left his foreign relations to the Company
- employed no European other than the English
- received a British Resident at his court

The Company took charge of the defence of the state.

The first to accept was the **Nizam of Hyderabad**, **Nizam Ali Khan**, in **1798**. The French-officered corps at Hyderabad was disbanded.

**Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, 1799.** Wellesley named Tipu's dealings with the French as the cause. Tipu had not accepted the alliance.

**Seringapatam** was taken on **4 May 1799**. Tipu was killed.

Mysore was restored to the **Wodeyar** family and placed under a subsidiary alliance. The Company kept the coastal districts. The Nizam took a share. The Marathas were offered a share and refused it.

The same system was then accepted by:

- **Awadh, 1801.** The Nawab ceded the Doab, Rohilkhand and Gorakhpur in place of a cash payment.
- The **Peshwa**, by the **Treaty of Bassein, 31 December 1802**.

Remember:

**1792 — Tipu loses half his land and lives.**

**1799 — Tipu is killed. The Wodeyars return.**

**1798 — the Nizam is the first subsidiary ally.**

**1802 — Bassein. That treaty opens the second Maratha war.**
