# 11. Aurangzeb dies — 1707

This is a very important turning point.

Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb died in 1707**.

After his death, Mughal authority gradually weakened.

Regional powers became stronger, including:

Marathas, Bengal Nawabs, Awadh, Hyderabad, Mysore, Sikhs and others.

The East India Company took advantage of this political fragmentation.

This is one major reason why a trading company was eventually able to become a political power.

---
# 12. Farrukhsiyar's Farman — 1717

Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted important trading privileges to the East India Company in **1717**.

This farman allowed significant concessions, particularly in Bengal.

It helped the Company's trade expand enormously.

The British later misused some of these commercial privileges, contributing to tensions with Bengal's rulers.

----

# 13. British vs French

The British were not the only European power trying to dominate India.

The **French East India Company** was also powerful.

The British and French fought the:

**Carnatic Wars**

These wars were fought mainly in southern India.

There were three Carnatic Wars between roughly **1746 and 1763**.

The decisive event was the:

**Battle of Wandiwash — 1760**

The British defeated the French.

After this, French political ambitions in India were severely weakened.

They retained a few trading settlements, but the British became the dominant European power.

----
# 14. Bengal becomes the key battleground

Bengal was one of India's richest provinces.

The ruler of Bengal was:
**Siraj-ud-Daulah**

He became Nawab in **1756**.

He opposed growing British interference and the strengthening of British fortifications.

Conflict followed.

---
# 15. Battle of Plassey — 1757

This is one of the most important dates in modern Indian history.

**23 June 1757**

Battle:

**Battle of Plassey**

Between:

**Siraj-ud-Daulah**  
vs  
**British East India Company under Robert Clive**

But the British victory was not simply because their army was stronger.

Robert Clive secretly made agreements with people inside Siraj's administration, especially:

**Mir Jafar**

Mir Jafar and some others did not properly support Siraj during the battle.

Siraj-ud-Daulah was defeated.

Mir Jafar was made Nawab.

### Why Plassey matters

Plassey gave the Company enormous political influence in Bengal.

This marks the beginning of the Company's transformation:

**Trading company → political power**

Remember:

**1757 = Plassey = beginning of British political dominance in India**

----
# 16. Mir Jafar and Mir Qasim

The British installed **Mir Jafar** as Nawab.

But when he could no longer satisfy British financial demands, the Company replaced him with:

**Mir Qasim**

Mir Qasim tried to become independent of British control.

Conflict developed.

Eventually, Mir Qasim joined forces with:

**Shuja-ud-Daula**, Nawab of Awadh
and
**Shah Alam II**, Mughal emperor.

This led to another decisive battle.

---

# 17. Battle of Buxar — 1764

**22 October 1764**

The British East India Company defeated the combined forces of:

Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-Daula + Shah Alam II.

The British forces were led by:

**Hector Munro**

This battle is arguably even more important politically than Plassey.

Why?

Plassey gave the Company influence.

Buxar confirmed its **military and political supremacy** in eastern India.

The political result comes the next year, at Allahabad.

---

# 18. Treaty of Allahabad — 1765

**12 August 1765**

**Robert Clive** settled the victory of Buxar with two agreements at Allahabad.

**Shah Alam II** granted the Company the **Diwani** of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.

Diwani means the right to collect the land revenue and to decide the revenue cases.

In return the Company agreed to pay the emperor **Rs 26 lakh a year**, and gave him the districts of **Kora and Allahabad**.

**Shuja-ud-Daula** got Awadh back, except those two districts.

He paid a war indemnity of **Rs 50 lakh**.

Mir Jafar had died in 1765. His son **Najm-ud-Daula** was the new Nawab of Bengal. Defence of the province was already in the Company's hands.

Remember:

**1765 = Diwani = the revenue of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**

The "Orissa" of this grant is the small western tract the Mughals then called Orissa, around Midnapore. It is not the whole of modern Odisha.

---

# 19. Dual Government of Bengal — 1765–1772

Clive did not take the administration into the Company's own offices.

He split the government.

| Power                                     | Who held it        |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Diwani** — revenue                      | The Company        |
| **Nizamat** — police and criminal justice | The Nawab, in name |

The Company collected the money. The Nawab was left with the duty of keeping order, and with too little money to do it.

The work on the ground was done by Indian deputies: **Muhammad Reza Khan** in Bengal and **Raja Shitab Rai** in Bihar.

This is Clive's **Dual System**, or Dual Government.

The line to write:

**The Company had power without responsibility. The Nawab had responsibility without power.**

It lasted from **1765 to 1772**.

---

# 20. Warren Hastings ends the Dual System — 1772

In **1772** **Warren Hastings** became Governor of Bengal.

He abolished the Dual System.

The Company now collected the revenue itself, and took over the civil administration.

The treasury was moved from **Murshidabad** to **Calcutta**.

Remember the chain:
**1757 Plassey** — the Company becomes a political power
**1764 Buxar** — that power is confirmed in the field
**1765 Allahabad** — the revenue is legally granted
**1765–1772 Dual System** — Clive collects, and does not administer
**1772** — Hastings takes the administration too
