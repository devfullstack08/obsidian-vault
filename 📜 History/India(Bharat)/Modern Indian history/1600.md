# 1. Before the British: Europeans were already coming to India

The British were **not the first Europeans** to reach India.

The sequence you should remember is:

**Portuguese → Dutch → English/British → Danish → French**

In **1498**, Portuguese explorer **Vasco da Gama** reached **Calicut (Kozhikode)** on the Malabar Coast(Kerala). This opened the sea route from Europe to India around the Cape of Good Hope.

European powers wanted Indian spices, cotton, silk, indigo and other valuable goods.

----
# 2. English East India Company was created — 1600

On **31 December 1600**, Queen **[[01. Elizabeth I(1533-1603)| Elizabeth I]]** granted a royal charter to a group of English merchants.

This created the:
**English East India Company (EIC)**
(John Watts + George White)

Its original purpose was **trade**, not ruling India.
The company received permission to trade with countries east of the Cape of Good Hope.
So remember:

**1600 = East India Company established in England.**

---
# 3. British arrive in India — 1608

In **1608**, the English ship **Hector** reached **Surat**.
Its captain was:
**[[ 03. Captain William Hawkins | Captain William Hawkins]]**

Hawkins went to the Mughal emperor **Jahangir's court** to seek permission for English trade.

Jahangir initially did not give the English all the privileges they wanted, partly because the Portuguese already had influence.

---
# 4. Battle of Swally — 1612

The English and Portuguese were competitors.
In **1612**, the English defeated the Portuguese in the **Battle of Swally**, near Surat.

On 22 September 1612  Captain Best(British) decided to send an emissary(દૂત) to the Emperor asking for permission to trade and settle a factory at Surat. if refused he planned to quite the country.

On 30 September 1612 Captain Best got news that two of his men, Mr. Cunning and William Chambers were arrested while on shore. 
On 10 October Captain Best and his ships sailed to Suvali, a small town about 12 miles(19km) North of Surat, A skirmish took place between the two navies(British and Portueguese).

English ships alight,  a bark was send toward them as a fire ship. But the English watch was alert, and the bark was sunk by cannon fire with the loss of eight lives . A standoff remained until 5 December, when captain Best sailed for the port of Diu.
  
This increased English prestige.
The Mughal authorities now realized that the English could challenge Portuguese naval power.
After this, the English received greater permission to trade.

This event sufficiently impressed the Sardar(Governor) of Gujarat, who reported it to the Emperor. Thereafter the Emperor was more favourable towards the english than the Portuguese.

The English victory at Swally Hole in 1612 over the Portuguese, whose control of the pilgrim sea route to Mecca was resented by the Mughals, brought a dramatic change.

***The embassy of Sir Thomas Roe(1615-18) to the Mughal court secured an accord(In the form of a forman, or grant of privileged) by which the English secured the right to trade.**

----
# 5. First important English factory — Surat

In **1613**, the English established a permanent factory at **Surat**.

Here, "factory" does **not** mean a modern manufacturing factory.

It meant a:
**trading post / commercial establishment**
The people working there were called factors.

----

# 6. Sir Thomas Roe comes to India — 1615

In **1615**, King James I sent **Sir Thomas Roe** as ambassador to Mughal emperor **Jahangir**.

Roe stayed in India until around **1619**.

He successfully obtained trading privileges for the English.

This was extremely important because now the British could gradually expand their commercial operations.

Remember the distinction:

| Person          | Importance                                                 |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| William Hawkins | Came to Jahangir's court earlier                           |
| Sir Thomas Roe  | Official ambassador of James I; secured trading privileges |

---

# 7. British trading settlements expand

During the 17th century, the East India Company established settlements in different parts of India.

Three cities eventually became extremely important:

**Madras, Bombay and Calcutta.**

These became the three major British **Presidencies**.

----
# 8. Madras — 1639

In **1639**, the English acquired land at **Madras**.

They constructed:

**Fort St. George**

Madras became an important British base in southern India.

---
# 9. Bombay comes to the British

Bombay was originally controlled by the Portuguese.

In **1661**, Portuguese princess **Catherine of Braganza** married English king **Charles II**.

Bombay was given to Charles II as part of her marriage dowry(દહેજ).

Then in **1668**, Charles II transferred Bombay to the East India Company.

Bombay later became one of Britain's most important Indian settlements.

---

# 10. Calcutta — 1690

The English expanded into Bengal.

**Job Charnock** has traditionally been associated with the establishment of the English settlement at Calcutta around **1690**.

The British later built:

**Fort William**

Calcutta became one of the most important British political and commercial centres.

For many years it would serve as the capital of British India.

---
# 11. Aurangzeb dies — 1707

This is a very important turning point.

Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb died in 1707**.

After his death, Mughal authority gradually weakened.

Regional powers became stronger, including:

Marathas, Bengal Nawabs, Awadh, Hyderabad, Mysore, Sikhs and others.

The East India Company took advantage of this political fragmentation.

This is one major reason why a trading company was eventually able to become a political power.

---
# 12. Farrukhsiyar's Farman — 1717

Mughal emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted important trading privileges to the East India Company in **1717**.

This farman allowed significant concessions, particularly in Bengal.

It helped the Company's trade expand enormously.

The British later misused some of these commercial privileges, contributing to tensions with Bengal's rulers.

----

# 13. British vs French

The British were not the only European power trying to dominate India.

The **French East India Company** was also powerful.

The British and French fought the:

**Carnatic Wars**

These wars were fought mainly in southern India.

There were three Carnatic Wars between roughly **1746 and 1763**.

The decisive event was the:

**Battle of Wandiwash — 1760**

The British defeated the French.

After this, French political ambitions in India were severely weakened.

They retained a few trading settlements, but the British became the dominant European power.

----
# 14. Bengal becomes the key battleground

Bengal was one of India's richest provinces.

The ruler of Bengal was:
**Siraj-ud-Daulah**

He became Nawab in **1756**.

He opposed growing British interference and the strengthening of British fortifications.

Conflict followed.

---
# 15. Battle of Plassey — 1757

This is one of the most important dates in modern Indian history.

**23 June 1757**

Battle:

**Battle of Plassey**

Between:

**Siraj-ud-Daulah**  
vs  
**British East India Company under Robert Clive**

But the British victory was not simply because their army was stronger.

Robert Clive secretly made agreements with people inside Siraj's administration, especially:

**Mir Jafar**

Mir Jafar and some others did not properly support Siraj during the battle.

Siraj-ud-Daulah was defeated.

Mir Jafar was made Nawab.

### Why Plassey matters

Plassey gave the Company enormous political influence in Bengal.

This marks the beginning of the Company's transformation:

**Trading company → political power**

Remember:

**1757 = Plassey = beginning of British political dominance in India**

----
# 16. Mir Jafar and Mir Qasim

The British installed **Mir Jafar** as Nawab.

But when he could no longer satisfy British financial demands, the Company replaced him with:

**Mir Qasim**

Mir Qasim tried to become independent of British control.

Conflict developed.

Eventually, Mir Qasim joined forces with:

**Shuja-ud-Daula**, Nawab of Awadh
and
**Shah Alam II**, Mughal emperor.

This led to another decisive battle.

---

# 17. Battle of Buxar — 1764

**22 October 1764**

The British East India Company defeated the combined forces of:

Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-Daula + Shah Alam II.

The British forces were led by:

**Hector Munro**

This battle is arguably even more important politically than Plassey.

Why?

Plassey gave the Company influence.

Buxar confirmed its **military and political supremacy** in eastern India.
