 # India-Pakistan Wars & Conflicts

Since the partition of British India in 1947, India and Pakistan have engaged in four major wars and several border standoffs. The root cause of most of these conflicts has been the dispute over the princely state of Jammu & Kashmir, combined with geopolitical rivalries and state-sponsored cross-border militancy.

## Detailed Study of Major Conflicts

### 1. First Kashmir War (1947–1948)

* **Background:** Under the Indian Independence Act, J&K was a princely state ruled by Maharaja Hari Singh, who initially chose to remain independent.
* **The Trigger:** In October 1947, armed Pashtun tribal militias (*Lashkars*), aided and directed by the Pakistani Army, invaded J&K. Fearing the fall of Srinagar, Maharaja Hari Singh signed the **Instrument of Accession** on October 26, 1947, making J&K an integral part of India.
* **The War:** Indian troops were immediately airlifted to Srinagar. Major battlefields included Badgam, Naushera, and Uri. 
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Brigadier Rajinder Singh (India):** Known as the *"Savior of Kashmir,"* he delayed the Pakistani advance with a tiny force at Uri, sacrificing his life to allow J&K to sign the accession.
  * **Brigadier Mohammad Usman (India):** Known as the *"Hero of Naushera,"* he successfully defended Naushera against overwhelming odds.
  * **Major Somnath Sharma (India):** Posthumously awarded India's first Param Vir Chakra for defending the Srinagar airfield at Badgam.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** Prime Minister Nehru referred the dispute to the United Nations. A ceasefire was declared on January 1, 1949. J&K was divided: India retained about two-thirds of the territory, while Pakistan occupied one-third (Pakistan-Occupied Kashmir/POK and Gilgit-Baltistan). This created a permanent border issue.

### 2. Second Kashmir War (1965)

* **Background:** Encouraged by India’s defeat in the 1962 Sino-Indian War and hoping that the Kashmiri population would revolt against India, Pakistan planned a secret attack. This was preceded by border skirmishes in the **Rann of Kutch** in April 1965, which were defused by a tribunal but made Pakistan confident in India's military hesitance.
* **The Trigger:** Pakistan launched **Operation Gibraltar** in August 1965, sending thousands of covert soldiers disguised as local Kashmiris into the valley.
* **The War:** The Kashmiri locals did not rebel but instead reported the infiltrators. India retaliated by capturing the strategic **Haji Pir Pass** and launching a full-scale offensive across the international border toward Lahore (*Operation Riddle*). The conflict saw the largest tank battles since World War II (e.g., the **Battle of Asal Uttar**).
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Lal Bahadur Shastri (PM, India):** Gave decisive political leadership and coined the legendary slogan *"Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan"* to boost public morale.
  * **Company Quartermaster Havildar (CQMH) Abdul Hamid (India):** Awarded the Param Vir Chakra for single-handedly destroying seven Pakistani Patton tanks with a recoilless gun.
  * **Air Marshal Arjan Singh (India):** Led the Indian Air Force with distinction during the air warfare.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** The Soviet Union mediated a peace agreement in January 1966, leading to the **Tashkent Declaration** signed by PM Shastri and Pakistani President Ayub Khan. Tragically, Shastri died in Tashkent hours after signing the deal. Both countries agreed to withdraw to their pre-August 1965 positions, resulting in a military stalemate but a strategic victory for India as it foiled Pakistan's plan to capture Kashmir.

### 3. Bangladesh Liberation War (1971)

* **Background:** East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) was politically and culturally marginalized by West Pakistan. After Sheikh Mujibur Rahman’s Awami League won the 1970 elections, West Pakistan refused to hand over power. The Pakistani army launched **Operation Searchlight**, committing mass genocide and raping millions of Bengalis. This forced nearly 10 million refugees into India.
* **The Trigger:** India supported the Bengali resistance (*Mukti Bahini*). On December 3, 1971, Pakistan launched preemptive airstrikes on Indian airbases (*Operation Chengiz Khan*).
* **The War:** India launched a lightning, multi-pronged campaign on both the Western and Eastern fronts. The Indian Navy blockaded Pakistani ports and crippled Karachi's naval capabilities (**Operation Trident** and **Operation Python**), while the Indian Air Force gained complete air superiority. 
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Indira Gandhi (PM, India):** Resisted intense international pressure (including from the USA, which sent its Seventh Fleet) and took the bold decision to support the creation of Bangladesh.
  * **Field Marshal Sam Manekshaw (Army Chief, India):** Strategically planned the war and refused to rush the invasion, waiting for winter when the Himalayan passes would be closed, preventing Chinese intervention.
  * **Lieutenant General Jagjit Singh Aurora (India):** Led the Eastern Command and accepted the surrender of Pakistan's forces.
  * **Major General J.F.R. Jacob (India):** Negotiated the unconditional public surrender of the Pakistani Army.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** On December 16, 1971, Pakistani commander Lt. Gen. A.A.K. Niazi surrendered to Gen. Aurora in Dhaka. **Over 93,000 Pakistani soldiers surrendered**, the largest surrender of troops in a single conflict since WWII. East Pakistan became the independent nation of **Bangladesh**. In 1972, India and Pakistan signed the **Simla Agreement**, agreeing to resolve all future disputes bilaterally.

### 4. Kargil War (1999)

* **Background:** In early 1999, the Prime Ministers of India and Pakistan (Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Nawaz Sharif) signed the historic *Lahore Declaration* for peace. However, behind Sharif's back, the Pakistani military mastermind Gen. Pervez Musharraf planned a covert invasion.
* **The Trigger:** During the winter, both armies traditionally vacated their high-altitude peaks in the Kargil-Drass region. In early 1999, Pakistani soldiers disguised as Kashmiri militants occupied these vacant Indian posts. The goal was to cut off the **NH 1D Highway** (Srinagar-Leh), cutting off Ladakh from Kashmir.
* **The War:** India launched **Operation Vijay**. Facing uphill combat against entrenched enemy positions, the Indian Army fought heroic battles at extreme altitudes (18,000+ feet). The Indian Air Force launched **Operation Safed Sagar** to destroy enemy supply lines.
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Atal Bihari Vajpayee (PM, India):** Maintained strict orders that Indian forces must not cross the Line of Control, earning India massive international support while diplomatically isolating Pakistan.
  * **Captain Vikram Batra (India):** Posthumously awarded the Param Vir Chakra for his legendary bravery in recapturing Point 5140 and Point 4875. His catchphrase was *"Yeh Dil Maange More!"*
  * **Lieutenant Manoj Kumar Pandey (India):** Posthumously awarded the Param Vir Chakra for clearing enemy bunkers on Khalubar Hill.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** India successfully recaptured all occupied peaks (Tololing, Tiger Hill, etc.). Under diplomatic pressure from US President Bill Clinton, Pakistan withdrew its remaining forces. Vajpayee declared victory on **July 26, 1999** (*Kargil Vijay Diwas*). The war highlighted major intelligence failures on the Indian side, leading to the creation of the *Kargil Review Committee* and subsequent defense reforms (like the creation of the Chief of Defence Staff role).

### 5. 2016 Surgical Strikes

* **Background:** In September 2016, four heavily armed terrorists launched a pre-dawn attack on an Indian Army brigade headquarters in **Uri**, J&K, killing 19 Indian soldiers. This caused widespread public outrage and demands for action.
* **The Action:** On the night of September 28–29, 2016, the Indian Army's Special Forces (Para SF) crossed the Line of Control (LoC) and conducted coordinated, precision surgical strikes against multiple terrorist launchpads in Pakistan-Occupied Kashmir (POK), inflicting heavy casualties on terrorists and their handlers.
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Narendra Modi (PM, India):** Provided strong political will to order a direct military response across the LoC.
  * **Ajit Doval (NSA, India):** Monitored and planned the operation along with Army leadership.
  * **Lieutenant General Ranbir Singh (DGMO, India):** Announced the strikes to the media, asserting India's right to defend itself.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** The operation was a massive tactical and public relations success. It marked a critical policy shift for India: abandoning the traditional policy of **"strategic restraint"** and establishing a new normal where India will cross the LoC to retaliate against state-sponsored terror attacks.

### 6. Operation Sindoor (2025)

* **Background:** In late April 2025, terrorists from "The Resistance Front" (TRF), an offshoot of Lashkar-e-Taiba, launched a deadly attack on tourists in Pahalgam, Jammu & Kashmir, killing 26 civilians after separating them based on religion.
* **The Trigger:** The mass civilian casualties created intense public outrage and a security challenge. In response, the Indian Armed Forces planned a swift, calibrated strike to dismantle the terrorist infrastructure without triggering an all-out conventional war.
* **The Conflict:** Launched on the night of May 6–7, 2025, Indian forces conducted multi-domain precision airstrikes and artillery actions against 9 major terrorist training camps across the Line of Control (LoC) and within Pakistan. Pakistan retaliated with drone and missile strikes targeting Indian military installations in the Northern and Western sectors. India successfully neutralized these threats using its integrated counter-drone grids and air defense systems.
* **Pivot Roles:**
  * **Narendra Modi (PM, India):** Authorized the precision strikes, defining the operation as a "measured and non-escalatory" response to cross-border terrorism under a nuclear overhang.
  * **Ajit Doval (NSA, India):** Oversaw the strategic coordination and security planning during the 4-day standoff.
* **Outcome & Perspective:** The active engagement lasted for about four days, ending with a mutually agreed ceasefire on May 10, 2025, mediated by the DGMOs of both nations. The conflict showcased India's shift to proactive deterrence, utilizing advanced indigenous air defense and drone warfare systems to neutralize threats.
