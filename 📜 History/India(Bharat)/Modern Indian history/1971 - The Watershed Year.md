# 1971: The Watershed Year of India (૧૯૭૧: ભારતનું સંક્રાંતિ વર્ષ)

For competitive exams (UPSC, GPSC, SSC CGL, PSI), **1971** is considered the most significant and defining year in post-independence India. It was a year of monumental changes across **Geopolitics, Military History, Constitution/Polity, and Domestic Politics**.

---

## 1. Geopolitics & Military History (The 1971 War)

The 1971 Indo-Pak War was a watershed event that redrew the map of South Asia and established India as the undisputed pre-eminent power in the region.

### A. The Crisis in East Pakistan
* **Background:** East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) was politically and culturally oppressed by West Pakistan. In the 1970 elections, the East Pakistani party, Awami League (led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman), won a majority, but West Pakistan refused to hand over power.
* **Operation Searchlight:** Pakistan launched a brutal military crackdown in East Pakistan, committing mass genocide(નરસંહાર). 
* **Refugee Crisis:** Around **10 million Bengali refugees** fled into India, causing a massive humanitarian and economic crisis for India.

### B. The War & Indian Strategy (December 3 – 16, 1971)
* **Sam Manekshaw's Leadership:** Chief of Army Staff (later Field Marshal) Sam Manekshaw advised PM Indira Gandhi to delay the war until winter to prevent Chinese intervention through Himalayan passes and ensure dry ground in the East.
* **Indo-Soviet Treaty (August 1971):** India signed the **20-Year Treaty of Peace, Friendship, and Cooperation** with the USSR to counter the US-Pakistan-China axis.
* **Key Operations:**
  * **Operation Trident (Dec 4) & Operation Python (Dec 8):** The Indian Navy attacked Karachi Port, destroying Pakistani warships and fuel reserves. *December 4 is celebrated as Navy Day in India.*
  * **Battle of Longewala (Rajasthan):** A legendary defensive battle where a tiny company of 120 Indian soldiers (led by Major Kuldip Singh Chandpuri) held off a massive Pakistani tank regiment.
* **The Surrender (December 16):**
  * Indian Forces alongside the **Mukti Bahini** (Bengali freedom fighters) defeated Pakistani forces.
  * **Instrument of Surrender:** General A.A.K. Niazi of Pakistan signed the surrender document in Dhaka, surrendering **93,000 Pakistani soldiers** to Lt. Gen Jagjit Singh Aurora. This is the largest military surrender in the world since World War II.
  * *December 16 is celebrated as Vijay Diwas in India.*

## 2. Constitutional Amendments & Indian Polity 

In 1971, the Parliament passed three major Constitutional Amendment Acts (CAAs) to assert **parliamentary supremacy** over the judiciary. These are high-yield topics for polity exams:

### A. 24th Amendment Act, 1971 (Article 13 & 368)
* **Background:** Passed to override the Supreme Court's ruling in the *Golaknath Case (1967)*, which held that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights.
* **Provisions:**
  1. Declared that Parliament has the power to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights, under Article 368.
  2. Clarified that Article 13 (laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights) does not apply to Constitutional Amendment Bills.
  3. **Made it compulsory for the President of India to give assent to a Constitutional Amendment Bill.**

### B. 25th Amendment Act, 1971 (Article 31C)
* **Background:** Passed to protect social welfare land-reforms from judicial review.
* **Provisions:**
  1. Curtailed the right to property.
  2. Inserted **Article 31C**, declaring that if a law is passed to implement the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs) under **Article 39(b) or 39(c)**, it cannot be declared void even if it violates Articles 14, 19, or 31 (Fundamental Rights).

### C. 26th Amendment Act, 1971 (Abolition of Privy Purses)
* **Background:** When princely states acceded to India in 1947, they were promised hereditary pensions and privileges called **Privy Purses**. PM Indira Gandhi sought to abolish this to promote equality and socialism.
* **Provisions:**
  1. **Abolished the Privy Purses** and royal privileges of the former rulers of princely states.
  2. Repealed Articles 291 and 362.

## 3. Domestic Politics & Security

* **"Garibi Hatao" Election (March 1971):**
  * Indira Gandhi won a landslide victory (two-thirds majority) in the 5th General Elections using the slogan **"Garibi Hatao" (Abolish Poverty)**. This centralized absolute power within her faction of the Congress.
* **MISA (Maintenance of Internal Security Act), 1971:**
  * A highly controversial law passed by Parliament giving the government sweeping powers of **preventive detention** without trial. It became the primary tool used to arrest opposition leaders during the Emergency (1975-1977).
