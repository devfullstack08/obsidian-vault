**Original name:** Nizam Khan
**Father:** Bahlul Lodi
**Reign:** 1489–1517 CE

Important achievements: Expansion into Bihar, development of Agra, land measurement reforms

Literary pen name: **Gulrukhi**

Sikandar Lodi continued his father's policy of territorial consolidation but attempted to establish stronger central authority over the Afghan nobility.

### Noble independence and administrative weakness

Problem: Under Bahlul, important Afghan chiefs enjoyed considerable independence. This made it difficult for the Sultan to enforce uniform administrative regulations.

Action: Sikandar increased royal supervision, required greater accountability from nobles and officials, and strengthened administrative discipline.

He sought to restrict unauthorized political activities and improve the reporting of government revenue.

Result: Central authority became stronger, although Afghan nobles continued to retain substantial influence.

UPSC significance: Sikandar attempted to transform a loose political arrangement based on noble cooperation into a more centralized monarchy.

### Administration of Bihar and eastern territories

Problem: Eastern territories remained vulnerable to the influence of former **Sharqi** rulers and independent regional chiefs. 

Action: Sikandar consolidated Jaunpur, advanced into Bihar and compelled several regional rulers to acknowledge his authority.

Result: Delhi's influence expanded significantly in the eastern Gangetic plains.

His reign represented one of the strongest phases of Lodi territorial authority.

### Rajput resistance and frontier expansion

Problem: Regional rulers in Rajasthan and central India controlled important forts and strategic routes. Their resistance restricted expansion beyond the territories already held by Delhi.

Action: Sikandar undertook military campaigns towards Dholpur, Narwar, Gwalior and neighbouring regions.

He sought to improve Delhi's access to important commercial and military routes connecting northern and central India.

Result: He secured gains in some areas, but the powerful fortress of Gwalior was not permanently conquered.

### Foundation of Agra (1504 CE)

Present-day Agra, which developed into an important **political centre** under Sikandar Lodi. The visible Mughal fort is from a later period.

One of Sikandar Lodi's most important decisions was the development of Agra in 1504 CE as a major administrative centre.

Agra was strategically situated on the **Yamuna River** and provided access to the Doab, eastern provinces and military routes towards Rajasthan and central India.

Its development allowed the Sultan to exercise closer supervision over territories distant from Delhi.

UPSC significance: The development of Agra was both an urban achievement and a strategic attempt to strengthen regional administration.

### Agricultural and revenue reforms

Sikandar Lodi sought to **standardize land measurement** and improve the assessment of agricultural revenue.

He is associated with the **Gaz-i-Sikandari**, a standardized measuring unit traditionally described as 32 anguls (finger-breadths).

The measure was intended to reduce uncertainty in assessing cultivated land.

He also encouraged agricultural production and took measures associated with the regulation of trade and prices.

Important distinction: Do not confuse the Gaz-i-Sikandari with Akbar's later land-measurement system, or with Alauddin Khalji's market-control regulations.

### Religious and cultural policies

Sikandar Lodi patronized Persian literature, scholars and education. He was also associated with poetry written under the pen name Gulrukhi.

His reign is associated with the translation of some Sanskrit medical material into Persian.

However, historical accounts also describe religiously restrictive policies, including temple destruction and discriminatory measures towards some Hindu communities.

Therefore, his reign combined administrative and intellectual patronage with episodes of religious intolerance.

Historical assessment: Sikandar strengthened the Lodi state through territorial expansion, improved administration and the development of Agra, but his policies also increased royal control over Afghan nobles, creating tensions that became more serious under his successor.
