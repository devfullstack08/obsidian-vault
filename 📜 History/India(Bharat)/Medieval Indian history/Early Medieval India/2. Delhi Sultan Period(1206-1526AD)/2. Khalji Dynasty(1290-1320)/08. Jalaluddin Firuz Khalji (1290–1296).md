The exam name is **Jalaluddin Firuz Khalji**. He is the first Khalji sultan, 1290–1296. His father is **Yughrush Khan**, also written Yoghresh. The mother's name is not recorded.

## Say this

In 1290 the Slave line had no strong heir, and Jalaluddin took Delhi. He was an old man, and a mild one. In July 1296 his nephew and son-in-law, Alauddin, killed him at Kara and took the throne. The reign that questions ask about is Alauddin's, not these six years.

## Why 1290 happened

Balban died in 1287. His chosen line had already broken: Prince Muhammad was dead, and Kaiqubad could not hold the crown. In 1290 Jalaluddin, the commander who could act, removed what was left of that house and became sultan.

The Khaljis are Khalaj Turks. Their people had long lived toward the Afghan hills. Balban had kept high office for Turks of his own Ilbari circle. Jalaluddin's throne is a change of family at the top of Delhi. The later Lodi kings are a different story. Calling Jalaluddin an Afghan king, in the Lodi sense, mixes two dynasties.

He did not repeat Balban's blood and iron. Chroniclers describe a king who pardoned, and who was too gentle for the nobles around him. In 1292 a Mongol army came into the Punjab. Jalaluddin made terms. A group of them converted and settled near Delhi. Later writers call those men the New Muslims. They come back in Alauddin's Gujarat campaign.

## The house

Yughrush had two sons who matter.

**Shihabuddin Mas'ud** died first. Jalaluddin brought the sons up. Four are named in the family accounts:

1. **Ali Gurshasp**, later **Alauddin Khalji**.
2. **Almas Beg**, later **Ulugh Khan**. He is the brother who brought Jalaluddin to Kara.
3. **Qutlugh Tigin**. The name is also written Tign. He is part of the tree. He never ruled.
4. **Muhammad**. The same. A name on the tree, not a reign.

**Jalaluddin** himself had three known sons and at least two daughters.

1. A daughter, given the title **Malika-i-Jahan**. Alauddin married her. That marriage made him nephew and son-in-law at once.
2. Another daughter. Her personal name is not recorded. She married Almas Beg, Ulugh Khan.
3. An elder son, titled **Khan-i-Khanan**. He died before 1296. He never became sultan.
4. **Arkali Khan**, the able son. When his father was killed he was at Multan, and he did not reach Delhi in time.
5. **Qadr Khan**. He took the title **Ruknuddin Ibrahim** and sat on the throne for a few months in 1296.

**Malika-i-Jahan is a title, used by two women.** Jalaluddin's widow used it. Her daughter, Alauddin's wife, used it too. The widow is the one who put Ibrahim on the throne, because she would not wait for Arkali Khan. The daughter is not that woman.

## Why 1296 happened

Alauddin was governor at Kara and Manikpur, on the Ganga. Early in 1296, before he was sultan, he raided Devagiri and came back with a fortune. He asked his uncle to come to Kara and receive the gold. Almas Beg went to fetch him.

In July 1296 Jalaluddin was killed at Kara. The usual account puts the blow at the moment the old sultan embraced his nephew. Alauddin then marched on Delhi with that same gold. Ibrahim's few months ended. Arkali Khan stayed out of reach at Multan, and that split in the family is why a mild king was replaced in one season.

The exam line of Khalji sultans is Jalaluddin, Alauddin, then Mubarak Shah. Ibrahim in 1296, and the child Omar in 1316, are real and too short to be the answer on their own.

## One answer

Jalaluddin Firuz Khalji took Delhi in 1290 because Balban's line had no strong heir. He was Yughrush Khan's son, an old and mild king, and he ruled for six years. His nephew Ali Gurshasp, already married to his daughter, raided Devagiri, brought him to Kara, and killed him in 1296. The widow put a younger son up as Ruknuddin Ibrahim for a few months. The able son, Arkali Khan, was at Multan. Alauddin entered Delhi with the Devagiri gold.
