The exam name is **Alauddin Khalji**. Before the throne his name was **Ali Gurshasp**. He is sultan from 1296 to 1316. He is Shihabuddin Mas'ud's son, and Jalaluddin's nephew and son-in-law.

## Say this

Alauddin killed Jalaluddin at Kara in 1296 and ruled from Delhi until 1316. He kept a large standing army, paid in cash, and he held prices down in Delhi so that army could be fed. Malik Kafur carried his raids to Warangal, Dwarasamudra, and Madurai. The south paid treasure and tribute. The government stayed in Delhi.

## The wives, and the sons

![[alauddin-lineage.png]]

The four wives are the ones the reign turns on.

1. **Malika-i-Jahan**, Jalaluddin's daughter. A political marriage. No child is recorded from it. She is not the widow who put Ibrahim on the throne. That widow used the same title. Mahru later held it too.
2. **Mahru**, sister of **Malik Sanjar**, later **Alp Khan**. She is the mother of the two elder sons, **Khizr Khan** and **Shadi Khan**. Alp Khan later governed Gujarat. Khizr was the heir. Neither son became sultan.
3. **Kamala Devi**, chief queen of **Karna** (Karnadeva) of Gujarat, of the Vaghela house. She was brought to Delhi after the Gujarat campaign of 1299. Her daughter **Deval Devi** was taken later and married to Khizr Khan. Amir Khusrau turned that marriage into a poem. The poem is not why Gujarat was attacked.
4. **Jhatyapali**, daughter of **Ramachandra** of Devagiri. She is the mother of **Shihabuddin Omar**. The 1320 account also calls her the mother of **Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah**.

**Shadi Khan** is the other elder son. He was imprisoned at Siri and blinded with Khizr Khan. He never ruled.

Other sons are named when the child Omar is put up in 1316. Isami lists **Farid Khan**, **Uthman Khan** (also written Osman), **Abu Bakr Khan**, **Mustafa Khan**, and **Muhammad Khan**. Their mothers are not recorded. In 1320 Farid Khan and Abu Bakr Khan are killed, and Uthman Khan is blinded. A later account of that killing also names two children, **Bahauddin Khan** and **Ali Khan**, who are blinded. None of these sons rules.

Charts that hang Farid Khan and Uthman Khan on Jalaluddin's daughter go past the record. That marriage is described as childless. Mahru later used the queen's title, which is how the mothers get mixed.

Khizr Khan was set aside in the last year of the reign, imprisoned and blinded. Mubarak, once he was sultan, had him killed. Omar was a child on the throne for a few weeks in 1316. Mubarak Shah then ruled from 1316 to 1320.

### 4. Gujarat(1299)

- **First Gujarat expedition:** Alauddin Khalji **did not personally lead the campaign**. He sent his two generals **Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan** against Gujarat, which was then ruled by the **Vaghela ruler Karna II (Rai Karan/Karna Vaghela)**. The Delhi forces entered Gujarat and attacked **Anahilwara/Anhilwara (present-day Patan)**, the Vaghela capital. Karna was defeated or abandoned the capital and fled; his daughter **Deval Devi** escaped with him, while his queen **Kamala Devi** was captured and taken to Delhi.
- The invading army then moved through wealthy parts of Gujarat. **Somnath** was attacked and its famous temple, which had been rebuilt after earlier destruction, was **plundered/sacked**. Other towns, religious establishments, palaces and commercial centres were also looted.
- **Khambhat/Cambay**, one of Gujarat's major trading ports, was also plundered. Its wealth came from extensive overseas commerce, and contemporary/near-contemporary accounts note that even wealthy **Muslim merchants were not spared** during the extraction of wealth.
- **Malik Kafur enters the story here.** At **Khambhat**, Nusrat Khan acquired/captured a slave named **Kafur** and later presented him to Alauddin Khalji. Kafur subsequently became one of Alauddin's most important commanders and eventually **Malik Naib**. He became known as **Hazar Dinari**, traditionally connected with a price of **1,000 dinars**
- **Kamala Devi** was taken to Delhi after the campaign. Later sources describe her as becoming associated with Alauddin's royal household. Her daughter **Deval Devi was NOT captured in this first 1299 campaign**; she had escaped with her father Karna. This distinction is important for MCQs.
- On the return journey toward Delhi, Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan carried **large quantities of treasure, prisoners, elephants and other booty**. A dispute arose over **khums**, the state's traditional **one-fifth share of war booty**. The commanders tried to determine how much private loot soldiers had concealed. Barani's account describes harsh methods being used to extract it.
- This led to the famous **“New Muslims' Mutiny”** near **Jalore**. The “New Muslims” here were largely **Mongols who had settled in the Sultanate and converted to Islam**. Several thousand reportedly rebelled against Ulugh Khan and Nusrat Khan.
- During the mutiny, **Malik A'izzuddin**, Nusrat Khan's brother and an official attached to Ulugh Khan, was killed. The rebels also attacked Ulugh Khan's camp and mistakenly killed **a nephew of Alauddin Khalji**, believing him to be Ulugh Khan. Ulugh Khan escaped and joined Nusrat Khan; loyal troops eventually suppressed the revolt.
- Some surviving rebel leaders later sought refuge with other rulers. In particular, **Muhammad Shah and some associates went to Hammiradeva of Ranthambore**. Their presence became part of the political background to Alauddin's later conflict with **Ranthambore in 1301**.
- **Important exam correction:** the **1299 expedition did not necessarily mean that Gujarat was permanently and completely annexed immediately**. The army returned to Delhi without establishing durable administration everywhere, and Karna was able to recover some authority. Some historical reconstructions therefore distinguish the **1299 raid/conquest** from the **final annexation around 1304–05**.
- Around **1304**, the hold on Gujarat is made firmer. **Karna** is pushed out for good, and **Alp Khan**, Mahru's brother, becomes governor. **Deval Devi** is not taken in 1299, and she is not taken in this year. She is taken later, from Baglana, in the paragraph under the second Devagiri campaign.
- Why Gujarat mattered: it was a **wealthy commercial region with important Arabian Sea ports such as Khambhat**. Control of Gujarat therefore brought enormous revenue, booty and access to major maritime trading networks, while also extending Delhi Sultanate power into western India.

### 5. Battle of Kili (1299), near Delhi

![[kili-1299.png]]

**Qutlugh Khwaja**

→ Mongol prince and commander
→ led a large Chagatai army toward Delhi

**Zafar Khan**

→ general of Alauddin Khalji
→ commanded the right wing of the Delhi army
→ attacked without waiting for Alauddin's order
→ pursued the Mongols who gave way
→ became separated from the main army
→ was surrounded and killed

Zafar Khan is dead. The Mongols do not take Delhi. They withdraw. Alauddin stays with the main line. The right wing does not.

### 6. Ranthambore(1301)
**Hammiradeva (Hammir Dev Chauhan)** was the **Chauhan ruler of Ranthambore**. He resisted Alauddin Khalji’s forces.

**Nusrat Khan** was one of **Alauddin Khalji’s senior generals**. During the siege of Ranthambore, he was **killed**, reportedly by a stone/projectile from the fortifications.

The sequence is:

**Alauddin Khalji**  
→ sends/commands forces against **Ranthambore**  
→ ruler there = **Hammiradeva Chauhan**  
→ **Nusrat Khan** dies during the siege  
→ Alauddin continues the campaign  
→ **Ranthambore Fort falls in 1301**

### 7. Chittor campaign of Alauddin Khalji(1303)

- **Chittor** = present-day **Chittorgarh, Rajasthan**
- Ruler = **Ratnasimha / Ratan Singh**, of the Guhila line
- **Alauddin Khalji besieged and captured Chittor in 1303**
- After the conquest, Chittor was associated with the name **Khizrabad**, after Alauddin's son **Khizr Khan**
- Chittor's later tradition remembers a **jauhar** and a last fight by the defenders. The line to write is the siege, the year **1303**, and the new name **Khizrabad**. **Padmavat**, and Padmini as the cause, is a sixteenth-century poem.

### 8. Outside Delhi, the same year, 1303

#### Who was Targhi?

**Targhi**, more accurately written **Taraghai**, was a **Mongol commander of the Chagatai Khanate**.

He had also participated in the earlier **1299 Mongol invasion led by Qutlugh Khwaja**. 

So keep these two separate:
**1299 → Qutlugh Khwaja → Battle of Kili → Zafar Khan killed**
**1303 → Taraghai/Targhi → siege of Delhi → Delhi not captured**

#### What happened in 1303?

Alauddin was campaigning at **Chittor** when the Mongol threat developed. He captured Chittor and hurried back toward Delhi.

His situation was difficult because much of his military strength was away or exhausted from other campaigns. The Mongols also blocked routes leading into Delhi.
Instead of risking a major open battle, Alauddin established a fortified position at **Siri**, protected with troops, elephants, a ditch, and defensive barriers.

The Mongols attacked and plundered areas around Delhi, but they **could not penetrate Alauddin's Siri position**. After roughly two months, Taraghai withdrew. The fortified camp becomes the city of Siri, and the palace of a thousand pillars, only after he has gone.

### 9. Malwa(1305)
- **Malwa** = a major region of present-day **Madhya Pradesh**, in central India.
- At that time Malwa was ruled by the **Paramara dynasty**, under **Mahalakadeva**.
- **Ain-ul-Mulk Multani** was an important general and administrator serving **Alauddin Khalji**.
- Alauddin sent forces against Malwa; the Paramara commander **Goga/Koka** was killed, and Mahalakadeva withdrew to the strong fort of **Mandu**. 
- **Mandu Fort was captured in November 1305**, and Mahalakadeva was killed. Malwa then came under the Delhi Sultanate, with Ain-ul-Mulk associated with its administration.

### 10. The Punjab frontier, 1305 and 1306

Keep this separate from Kili and from Taraghai.

- **1299** is Qutlugh Khwaja at Kili. Zafar Khan is killed. Delhi is not taken.
- **1303** is Taraghai outside Delhi, while Alauddin is at Chittor. The city holds. Then Siri.
- **About 1305**, a Mongol force under **Ali Beg** and **Tartaq** is defeated. **Ghazi Malik** is already on this frontier.
- **1306**, another Mongol force is broken in the Punjab. Ghazi Malik’s name is made here. He is the later Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq.

Delhi is not besieged in 1305 or 1306. The Mongol raids on the capital stop after this frontier fighting. Barani’s picture of a huge standing army belongs to this danger, not to a war inside the city.

### 11. Alauddin Khalji’s second Devagiri campaign(1307)

- **Devagiri** = present-day **Daulatabad, Maharashtra**.
- **Ramachandra** was the Yadava ruler of Devagiri.
- Alauddin had already raided Devagiri once in **1296**, before becoming Sultan.
- Ramachandra later stopped paying the tribute promised to Delhi, so Alauddin sent **Malik Kafur** again about **1307**.
- After submission, Ramachandra was not removed permanently. Alauddin **restored him as a subordinate ruler**.
- He received the title **Rai Rayan / Raja-i-Rajan**, meaning roughly **“king of kings.”**
- Ramachandra then became useful to Alauddin because **Devagiri became a base for later campaigns further south**, including Warangal and Dwarasamudra.
#### Who was Jhatyapali?

**Jhatyapali** was **Ramachandra’s daughter**.

According to the 14th-century historian **Isami**, Ramachandra gave Jhatyapali in marriage to **Alauddin Khalji**. Different texts give her name in forms such as **Jhatyapali, Jhitai, Jethapali, Chhitai or Kshetrapali**.

#### Connection with Deval Devi and Baglana

Around **1307–08**, while **Malik Kafur** was sent toward Devagiri, Alauddin’s Gujarat governor **Alp Khan** moved against **Baglana**, where **Karna** had taken refuge. **Deval Devi** was captured there and sent to Delhi. She was later married to **Khizr Khan**. That capture is not the 1299 campaign. In 1299 her mother, Kamala Devi, was taken, and Deval Devi escaped with her father.

Two Rajput forts fall in these same years and are easy to drop off the numbered map. **Siwana**, about **1308**. **Jalore**, about **1311**, under **Kanharadeva**. They are forts in Rajasthan. They are not a second Deccan.

### 12. Warangal, about 1309–1310

**Prataparudra** of the **Kakatiyas** rules at Warangal. **Malik Kafur** leads the expedition. Devagiri, now under Ramachandra as a tributary, is the road south.

Prataparudra pays a heavy tribute. The kingdom is left in his hands. This is not an annexation, and it is not the moment a diamond called the Koh-i-Noor enters the answer.

### 13. Dwarasamudra, then Madurai, 1311

**Ballala III** of the **Hoysalas** submits at **Dwarasamudra**. Kafur goes on into the Pandya country. The Pandyas are already divided between **Sundara Pandya** and **Vira Pandya**. Kafur takes treasure from the temple cities, including the road to **Madurai**, and he leaves.

He does not leave a governor who holds Madurai for Delhi. The later Madurai sultanate belongs to the Tughlaq years, not to 1311.

### 14. Devagiri again, about 1313

**Ramachandra** dies about 1311. His son **Shankaradeva** stops the tribute. **Malik Kafur** defeats him. Delhi’s hold on Devagiri is tighter, and the court still sits in Delhi.

The Yadava line does not end in Alauddin’s reign. Under **Mubarak Shah**, about 1318, **Khusrau Khan** defeats the last resistance there.

Amir Khusrau is the poet of these years, in *Khazain-ul-Futuh* and in the poem on Deval Devi. He is not the general. The general of the south is Malik Kafur, the slave taken at Khambhat in 1299, later **Malik Naib** and called **Hazar Dinari**.

## Why the market and the army

**Ziauddin Barani**, writing later in the Tughlaq age, is the source for the rules. Place them after the Mongol shocks, from about **1303**, when holding Delhi had become the point of the reign. The aim is one sentence: a large standing army, paid in cash from the treasury, which Delhi could feed at a fixed price.

**The army**

- **Dagh**: horses are branded, so a man cannot draw pay for a horse he does not bring.
- **Chehra**, also called **huliya**: a descriptive roll of each soldier.
- Pay is cash from the treasury. The central army is not left to live off an iqta.
- Barani gives a very large cavalry figure, often quoted as 475,000. That is his claim. It is not a counted muster roll.

**The Doab, which fed that army**

- The land is measured. The unit in the answer is the **biswa**.
- The demand is about **half the produce**.
- **Khuts, muqaddams and chaudharis** lose their old exemptions and pay like the peasant.
- Two extra taxes come into the answer: **ghari**, a house tax, and **charai**, a grazing tax.
- Much of this land is pulled into **khalisa**, land whose revenue comes straight to the crown.
- **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** is the office that chases arrears. The name is worth a mark.
- The iqta is tightened and watched. It is not abolished. These rules are the Doab, the country that supplied Delhi, not a price list for every province.

**The Delhi market**

Three markets are the usual answer: grain; cloth, sold through the **sarai-i-adl**; and horses, cattle and slaves.

- **Diwan-i-Riyasat** watches the market.
- **Shahna-i-mandi** is the officer in the market itself.
- **Barids**, the spies, report the prices and the hoarding.
- Grain, cloth, horses and slaves have fixed prices. The point is the army’s ration, not a law for every town in India.

**Four controls on the nobles**, as Barani groups them

1. Grants and pensions are taken back, and the land returns toward the crown.
2. Spies watch the nobles.
3. Wine and the drinking parties of the great men are forbidden.
4. The nobles are not to gather, or to marry among themselves, without leave.

These are police of the court. They are not a new religion, and they are not a parliament.

On his buildings and his coins he used the title **Sikandar-i-Sani**, the Second Alexander. That is his own claim.

## What he built in Delhi

Five names come up. Four of them can still be visited. The palace cannot.

**Siri**, his fortified city, begun after the Mongols camped outside Delhi in 1303. This is the fort that later notes call the Alai fort. It was the second city of Delhi, north-east of the Qutb. What stands now is the rubble wall.

![[siri-fort.jpg]]

The wall of Siri. The city is a ruin, not a finished fort. Photo: Yashvi Sahraya, CC BY-SA 4.0.

**Hazar Sutun**, the palace of a thousand pillars. He built it at Siri, on the ground where he had camped in 1303. Marble floors are described. The palace does not survive, so there is no photograph of it. The wall above is what remains of that city. Later builders, including Sher Shah, took the stone away.

**Hauz-i-Alai**, the tank, now called **Hauz Khas**. He dug it to hold water for Siri. Firoz Shah Tughlaq later cleared the silt and built the madrasa on the bank. The water is Alauddin's. The college beside the water is Firoz Shah's.

![[hauz-khas.jpg]]

The tank. The trees, the path, and the lamp are modern. Photo: BohunkaNika, CC BY-SA 4.0.

**Alai Darwaza**, 1311. It is the southern gateway of the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, inside the Qutb complex. Exams call it the entrance by the Qutb Minar. It is the mosque gate, not a door cut into the tower. The true arch here belongs to Alauddin. Aibak's screens at the mosque are corbelled. The inscription carries the Second Alexander title.

![[alai-darwaza.jpg]]

Alai Darwaza. Photo: Shadabgdg, CC BY-SA 4.0.

He also began the **Alai Minar**, meant to stand twice the height of the Qutb Minar. Only the first storey was built. It was left unfinished when he died.

**His tomb** is the central room of the madrasa he built in 1315, at the back of the Qutb complex, south-west of the mosque. This is the first tomb set inside a madrasa in India. The room has no inscription, and the dome over that room is gone. The grave is attributed to him. Barani says the body was brought from the palace at Siri.

![[alauddin-madrasa.jpg]]

The madrasa. His tomb is the central room of this building, not a separate domed monument out in the open. Photo: Varun Shiv Kapur, CC BY 2.0.

## What 1316 did

Alauddin's health failed. In the last year **Alp Khan**, Mahru's brother and the governor of Gujarat, was killed, and **Khizr Khan** was put aside. The chronicles give Malik Kafur the hand in that palace plot. Alauddin died at the start of 1316, of illness. Kafur then set the child **Shihabuddin Omar** on the throne and ruled in his name. The palace guards killed Kafur within weeks.

**Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah** (1316–1320) took the throne, blinded Omar, and had Khizr Khan killed. He loosened his father's market rules. He is the last Khalji sultan. In 1320 his favourite, **Khusrau Khan**, a slave from Gujarat and not a Khalji, killed him and sat in Delhi for a few months. Ghazi Malik marched from Dipalpur, killed Khusrau, and became **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**. That is the start of the next dynasty.

## One answer

Alauddin Khalji, born Ali Gurshasp, was Jalaluddin's nephew and son-in-law. As governor of Kara he raided Devagiri, then killed Jalaluddin there in 1296 and took Delhi. From 1299 to 1311 his armies took Gujarat, Ranthambore, Chittor, and Malwa, and Malik Kafur brought tribute back from the Deccan and the far south. At home he measured the Doab, took about half the crop, branded the army's horses, and fixed prices in Delhi so a cash army could be kept against the Mongols. He died in 1316. His heir Khizr Khan never ruled. A child, Omar, lasted weeks, and Mubarak Shah ruled until Khusrau Khan killed him in 1320.
