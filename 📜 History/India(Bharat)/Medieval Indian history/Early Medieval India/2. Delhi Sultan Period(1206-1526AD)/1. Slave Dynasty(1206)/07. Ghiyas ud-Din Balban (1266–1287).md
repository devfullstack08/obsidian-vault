The exam name is **Balban**, also written Ghiyas ud-Din Balban. Before he took the throne he was **Ulugh Khan**. He is not Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq, and he is not a Khalji.

Previous: [[06. Razia Sultan (1236–1240)]]. Next: [[08. Jalaluddin Firuz Khalji (1290–1296)]].

![[balban-dates.png]]

## Say this

> **Balban, sultan from 1266 to 1287, restored the authority of the Delhi crown after twenty years in which the Turkish nobles made and unmade kings. He destroyed the power of the Group of Forty, held the Mongols on the Indus frontier, and put down the rebel Tughril in Bengal. He did not conquer the Deccan. The Slave line ends three years after his death, in 1290.**

## The years before he is sultan

Razia’s death did not produce a strong king. The Group of Forty kept disposing of sultans.

| Years         | Sultan                                              | What to remember                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1240–1242** | **Muizuddin Bahram**, Razia’s brother               | Killed by his own officers. In **1241** the Mongols sack Lahore. That disaster belongs to these weak years, not to Razia and not to a war Balban won. |
| **1242–1246** | **Alauddin Masud**                                  | A stopgap. The nobles remove him.                                                                                                                     |
| **1246–1266** | **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, the younger son of Iltutmish | He is not the son who died in Bengal in 1229. Balban, his father-in-law, is the real power, with the title **Ulugh Khan**.                            |
| **1266–1287** | **Balban** himself                                  | The nobles raise him when Nasiruddin dies. He then breaks the group that had been raising sultans.                                                    |

He had been Iltutmish’s slave, an Ilbari Turk, and one of the Forty. The man who came from that corps is the man who ends its power. That is the pivot of the reign.

## Who he was

| Point      | What to write                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Person     | **Ghiyas ud-Din Balban**. Turkish. Ilbari. A slave of Iltutmish.                                                                                                  |
| 1246–1266  | Naib and father-in-law of Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud. The court already obeys Ulugh Khan.                                                                           |
| 1266       | Becomes sultan.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Policy     | Textbooks call it **blood and iron**. Spies, harsh punishment, no pardon for a rebel.                                                                             |

| Chronicler | **Ziauddin Barani**, *Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi*. Barani writes in the Tughlaq age, so this picture of an awful king is his, not a diary kept in 1266.                 |
|            |                                                                                                                                                                   |

## Why the crown had to be feared

After Razia, a sultan who was merely first among the Forty did not rule. Balban’s answer was to lift the crown above the nobles.

He separated the dangerous men, took their iqtas back, and punished revolt in public. The Group of Forty ceases to be the body that chooses the king. He also kept high office for Turks of his own kind. Non-Turks and Indian converts were not to stand where Yaqut had stood under Razia. That exclusion is part of why the next dynasty, the Khaljis, feels like a change of people and not only a change of name.

The ritual matters for the same reason. **Sijda**(prostration before the monarch) and **paibos**(kissing the feet of monarch) make every noble a subject. They are not a new religion. They are a Persian court borrowed to stop Turkish equals from treating the sultan as one of themselves.

Do not write that he ruled by massacre alone. The spies and the ceremonies are the system. Fear without a frontier policy would still have lost Delhi to the Mongols.

## The two frontiers

![[balban-map.png]]

Read the rivers before the cards. Delhi is on the Yamuna. The Mongols are beyond the Indus. Bengal is the far end of the Ganga.

**The Indus.** He does not march into Central Asia, and he does not go south to build a Deccan empire. The Mongol pressure of this reign is a frontier war, not Chinggis Khan. Chinggis has been dead since 1227. Balban’s own son **Muhammad**, called **Khan-i-Shahid**, is killed fighting the Mongols near Multan in **1285**. Some books say 1286. He never recovers from that death. An able kinsman, **Sher Khan**, had held the same frontier. Barani says Balban removed him because he had grown too strong. The guard was weaker afterwards. Write the removal. Do not make the poison the centre of the answer.

**Bengal.** About **1280**, **Tughril** rebels at Lakhnauti and takes a royal title. Balban, already old, marches east himself. Tughril is hunted down and killed. The punishment of the town is remembered as savage. Bengal is brought back under Delhi. It will slip again under later sultans. The point in this reign is that he would not leave a rebel king on the Ganga.

## What you can still see

![[balban-tomb.jpg]]

His tomb in **Mehrauli Archaeological Park**, Delhi. The dome has fallen. The pointed openings are cited as an early **true arch** in the sultanate buildings. A true arch is built of wedge-shaped stones. The corbelled arches of Aibak’s mosque were made by laying stones inward until they met. What you see now is a ruin, not a finished dome, and not the Qutb Minar. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, File:Tomb of Balban, Mehrauli Archaeological Park, New Delhi, India.jpg.

## Why 1290 follows so quickly

He wanted his line to continue through Prince Muhammad. Muhammad was already dead. The nobles put **Kaiqubad**, a grandson, on the throne in **1287**. Kaiqubad was given to pleasure. **Bughra Khan**, another son, stayed in Bengal and did not come to hold Delhi. In **1290** **Jalaluddin Khalji** ends the Slave dynasty.

Balban had made the crown feared. He had not left a son strong enough to wear it. [[08. Jalaluddin Firuz Khalji (1290–1296)|Jalaluddin Khalji]] begins there.

## Traps

| If you are about to write this | Write this instead |
| --- | --- |
| He founded the Delhi Sultanate | Aibak, 1206. Balban is late in the Slave line, 1266–1287. |
| He is Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | A different man, and a later dynasty, from 1320. |
| He fought Chinggis Khan | Chinggis died in 1227. Balban’s Mongol war is the frontier of the 1270s and 1280s. |
| The sack of Lahore in 1241 is his defeat | It falls in Bahram’s reign, twenty-five years before Balban is sultan. |
| Nasiruddin Mahmud of 1246 died in Bengal in 1229 | Two different sons of Iltutmish. Balban serves the later one. |
| He conquered the Deccan | He refused that road. The Indus and Bengal are his wars. |
| Sijda means he changed the religion of Delhi | Court ritual. The sultan standing in for a Persian king, not a new faith. |
| The Forty elected him and then kept power | He came from them, and then he broke them. |
| His tomb is the Qutb Minar | A ruined tomb at Mehrauli. The minar was begun by Aibak. |

## One answer

Balban was Iltutmish’s Turkish slave and one of the Group of Forty. From 1246 he ruled in all but name for Nasiruddin Mahmud, and from 1266 he was sultan. He lifted the crown above the nobles with spies, public punishment, and the Persian ritual of sijda and paibos, and he destroyed the Forty as a king-making corps. He held the Mongols on the Indus and would not spend the reign on the Deccan. About 1280 he killed the rebel Tughril in Bengal. In 1285 his son Muhammad was killed near Multan, and Balban died in 1287. Kaiqubad could not hold what he had built. Jalaluddin Khalji ended the Slave dynasty in 1290.
