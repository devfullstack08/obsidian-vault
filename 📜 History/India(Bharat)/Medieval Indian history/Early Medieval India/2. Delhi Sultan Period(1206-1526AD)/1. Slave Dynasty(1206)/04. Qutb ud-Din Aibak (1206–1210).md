The exam name is **Qutb ud-Din Aibak**. He is Ghori’s general, not another invader from Ghazni, and not a Khalji.

Previous: [[03. Muhammad of Ghori]]. Next: [[05. Iltutmish (1211–1236)]]. The short bridge is [[06. Ghazni to Ghor to Delhi]].

![[aibak-dates.png]]

## Say this

> **In 1206 Qutb ud-Din Aibak, a Turkish slave-general of Muhammad Ghori, became the first ruler of the Mamluk, or Slave, dynasty. That is the conventional beginning of the Delhi Sultanate. He ruled from Lahore for four years. He did not finish the state. Iltutmish did.**

If the question says “founder”, write **Aibak, 1206**. If it says “who consolidated Delhi” or “who received the caliph’s recognition”, write **Iltutmish**.

## Who he was

| Point              | What to write                                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Person             | **Qutb ud-Din Aibak**. Turkish. Born in Central Asia and sold as a child.                             |
| Master             | **Muhammad Ghori** (Muizz al-Din). Aibak was his slave, then freed, then his general in India.        |
| People             | Turkish military slave. Not an Arab, and not an Afghan. The court language around him is **Persian**. |
| Faith              | **Sunni**                                                                                             |
| Before 1206        | After the second battle of Tarain, **1192**, Ghori left him in charge of the north Indian conquests.  |
| 1206–1210          | Rules the Indian conquests on his own. His court is at **Lahore**.                                    |
| 1210               | Dies at Lahore. The chronicle account is a fall while playing **chaugan**, polo.                      |
| Name you will meet | **Lakh Baksh**, giver of lakhs, for his generosity.                                                   |

**Mamluk** means a military slave. The dynasty is called Slave, or Mamluk, because its first rulers came from that class. It does not mean the people of Delhi were slaves. Aibak had already been freed. His own slave **Iltutmish** later marries his daughter and succeeds him. The label fits the founding. It does not fit every later sultan.

![[aibak-1206-fictional.jpg]]

Fictional scene. The face is not a portrait of Aibak. The letter is only the idea that Ghori is dead. The tower is drawn as one unfinished storey, which is the right idea for Aibak’s lifetime: he began the **Qutb Minar**, and he did not finish it. The carved pillars echo the mosque he began. This is not a plan of Delhi in 1206, and it is not a Mughal city.

## The lineage

Wikipedia does not have a photograph of Aibak’s face, or of Iltutmish’s. No one painted either man from life. The faces on the tree are fictional, so the two people can be told apart. The real photographs are the tombs.

![[aibak-face-fictional.jpg]]

Fictional face of Aibak. Not a likeness.

![[aibak-tomb-lahore.jpg]]

Real. His tomb at Lahore, in Anarkali. The building has been restored. It is the place, not the man. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0, File:Qutbuddin Aibak Tomb ahore 02.jpg, Tahir8088.

![[iltutmish-face-fictional.jpg]]

Fictional face of Iltutmish. Not a likeness. He is a different man from the figure above.

The tree on popular charts is the right kind of picture, and several of its boxes are wrong. Use this one.

He was a Turk of the **Aibak** tribe. The name means moon-lord. He was born in Turkistan. His father’s name is not recorded, and he was sold as a child at **Nishapur**. He was not a prince of the **Qara Khanid** house. That was a different Central Asian kingdom.

**Minhaj** records **three daughters**, and he does not give their names.

| Daughter                       | What to write                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The first, and then the second | Married, one after the other, to **Nasir ud-Din Qabacha**, who held Multan and Uch. The first died, and the second took her place. |
| The third                      | Married **Iltutmish**. Her name is not part of the exam answer.                                                                    |

**Iltutmish** is the slave and the son-in-law. He is not a biological son, and he is not a stepson. Aibak called him his son and gave him the iqta of **Badaun**. That is favour. It is not a blood line.

**Aram Shah** is the hard box. Textbooks call him Aibak’s son, and that is the word to write if the question asks who succeeded in 1210. **Juvayni** says Aibak had no son. A later writer, **Isami**, calls Aram a real son. He ruled about a year, from Lahore, and Iltutmish removed him in **1211**. He is not the last ruler of the Slave dynasty. That dynasty runs to **1290**.

Do not add a wife named Qutlugh Khanum, a wife named Aram Khatun, or sons named Qutb al-Din and Nasir al-Din. Those boxes are not in the early account. One account does make **Taj ud-Din Yildiz** Aibak’s father-in-law. Yildiz was still the man who held Ghazni against him.

## Why 1206 splits

Ghori was killed near the Jhelum in **1206**. He left no son who could hold both sides of the mountains. The men who already had the forts kept them.

![[aibak-1206-split.png]]

Read the four cards before the dates.

| Man | Where in 1206 | What happens to him |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Qutb ud-Din Aibak** | Delhi, Ajmer, and the doab. His court is **Lahore**. | Keeps India. Dies in **1210**. |
| **Taj ud-Din Yildiz** | **Ghazni** | Claims to be Ghori’s successor. Aibak takes Ghazni for a short time, about **1208**, and cannot keep it. |
| **Nasir ud-Din Qabacha** | **Uch, Multan, Sindh** | Stays independent. Iltutmish, not Aibak, removes him, in **1228**. |
| Bengal, after **Bakhtiyar Khalji** | **Lakhnauti** | Bakhtiyar is killed about **1206** by one of his own officers. Bengal does not stay in Aibak’s hand. |

The pivot is possession, not a new invasion. Aibak was already the man with the Indian army and the forts of Delhi and Ajmer. Yildiz was on the far side of the mountains. Qabacha was on the Indus. No one in India had a reason to go back to Ghor.

He does not defeat those rivals. Four years is not a consolidated empire.

## What he had already done

| Year | What | The limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1192** | Left in the north after Ghori’s victory at the second Tarain. | Ghori is still the sultan. Aibak is his officer. |
| **1193** | Begins the **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque at Delhi, in the Rajput city, **Qila Rai Pithora**. This is not Shahjahanabad and not the Red Fort. | The mosque inscription says material from twenty-seven temples was reused. That is the inscription’s claim. |
| **1197** | Defeats **Bhima II** of the Chaulukyas and sacks Anahilapataka. | **Gujarat is not annexed.** The Gujarat note is [[Chapter 8 — Bhima II (c. 1178–1240 CE)]]. |
| **1202–03** | Campaign against the Chandela king **Paramardi** at Kalinjar. | Bundelkhand is struck. It does not become the centre. |

## Lahore, not yet Delhi

This is the fact that keeps his reign in proportion.

He holds Delhi. He does not make it the settled capital of a kingdom. He rules from **Lahore**, and he dies there. **Iltutmish** is the ruler who makes Delhi the capital and keeps it.

Careful historians also note that Aibak is not known to have struck coins in his own name, and that he did not receive a letter of investiture from the caliph. The caliph’s recognition comes to **Iltutmish in 1229**.

So the exam has two true sentences, and they answer different questions.

> **Aibak is the founder, in 1206.**
>
> **Iltutmish is the consolidator. The capital, the rivals, and the caliph’s recognition are his.**

## What you can still see

![[quwwat-ul-islam.jpg]]

**Quwwat-ul-Islam**, Delhi, the Might of Islam. Aibak began this mosque in **1193**. The carved pillars are older temple columns, set into his building. The tall screen arch was enlarged by later sultans, above all Iltutmish. The early arches are **corbelled**, built by laying stones inward until they meet. A true arch, with wedge-shaped stones, comes later. The **Alai Darwaza** of Alauddin Khalji, in **1311**, is the example to name for that. Do not give the whole screen to the year 1193. Photograph: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0, File:Quwwat ul-Islam mosque Screen and arcade (6197434888).jpg.

![[qutb-minar.jpg]]

**Qutb Minar**, in the same complex. Aibak began it. The first storey is his. Iltutmish added storeys. In the fourteenth century **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** repaired the tower after lightning and built the marble stage near the top. The photograph is the finished monument, not the stump Aibak left. Wikimedia Commons, File:Qutb Minar 2011.jpg.

Two explanations of the name exist. Textbooks attach it to **Qutb ud-Din Aibak**. Some historians attach it to the Chishti saint **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**, who lived in Delhi under Iltutmish. The saint did not build the tower. For the exam, write: begun by Aibak, continued by Iltutmish.

![[iron-pillar-qutb.jpg]]

The **Iron Pillar** stands in the same courtyard. It is a **Gupta** pillar, with an inscription of a king Chandra, identified with **Chandragupta II**. It is centuries older than Aibak. He did not cast it. A sliver of the minar is at the right edge, which is why the two are easy to mix. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, File:Iron Pillar at Qutb Complex.jpg, LangfordK.

![[adhai-din-ka-jhonpra.jpg]]

**Adhai Din ka Jhonpra**, Ajmer. Aibak began this mosque after the conquest. The great screen is largely **Iltutmish’s** work. The name means “the hut of two and a half days”. That is a later legend about how fast it was built. It is not a building schedule. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, File:20191216 Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra, Ajmer 1203 8870.jpg, Jakubhal.

## Death, and the next pivot

In **1210**, at Lahore, Aibak dies. Minhaj’s chronicle says he fell while playing chaugan(horseback ball game = polo). The state was still a general’s following, not a machine that could run without him.

**Aram Shah**, whom textbooks call his son, is put up at Lahore. The relationship is not certain, and he has no hold on the officers at Delhi. They invite **Iltutmish**, Aibak’s slave and son-in-law. In **1211** Iltutmish defeats Aram Shah and takes the throne.

That invitation is a fact of Delhi politics. It is not the later poem about Jayachandra inviting Ghori. Do not mix the two stories.

Writers of his own years include **Hasan Nizami**, who began the *Taj al-Maasir*, and **Fakhr-i Mudabbir**.

## One answer

Qutb ud-Din Aibak was Muhammad Ghori’s Turkish slave-general. After **1192** he held the north Indian conquests, began the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, and in **1197** sacked Gujarat without annexing it. When Ghori was killed in **1206**, the empire split: Yildiz at Ghazni, Qabacha on the Indus, Bengal outside his grip, and Aibak in India, ruling from Lahore. He is the conventional founder of the Mamluk dynasty and of the Delhi Sultanate. He died in **1210**. He did not remove his rivals, fix the capital at Delhi, or receive the caliph’s recognition. Those are Iltutmish’s work, from **1211**.
