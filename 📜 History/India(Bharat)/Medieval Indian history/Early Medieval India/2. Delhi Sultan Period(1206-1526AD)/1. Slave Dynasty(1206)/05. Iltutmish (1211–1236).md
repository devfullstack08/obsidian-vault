The exam name is **Iltutmish**, also written Shams ud-Din Iltutmish. Older books spell him **Altamash**. Same man. He is not Aibak, and he is not the later sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud.

Previous: [[04. Qutb ud-Din Aibak (1206–1210)]]. Next: [[06. Razia Sultan (1236–1240)]].

## Say this

> **Iltutmish, 1211–1236, is the consolidator of the Delhi Sultanate. He fixed the capital at Delhi, removed Aibak’s rivals, kept the Mongols beyond the Indus, and in 1229 received the caliph’s recognition. Aibak began the dynasty. Iltutmish made it a state.**

## The pivot: one kingdom, with Delhi at the centre

In 1210 the sultanate was still four men’s forts. Iltutmish’s reign is the joining of the Indian piece, and the decision not to chase the rest back to Ghazni.

**Bengal** is the place that will not stay joined. **Ghiyasuddin Iwaz Khalji** submitted, then broke away. In **1227** Iltutmish’s eldest son, **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, defeated Iwaz and reasserted Delhi’s authority in Bengal and Bihar. He died there in **1229**. Bengal is subdued more than once. It is not a quiet province.

**Gujarat** is still Chaulukya under **Bhima II**. Iltutmish does not annex it.

## Why he refused Jalaluddin

This is the decision that saves the new capital.

Chinggis Khan had destroyed the Khwarazm empire. Jalaluddin was the survivor, not a friend who could be enlisted for free. If Delhi gave him an army, Chinggis had a reason to cross the Punjab. Iltutmish delayed, sent no alliance, and let Jalaluddin move on.

The Mongols do raid toward the frontier in these years. They do **not** besiege(ઘેરો ઘાલવો) Delhi. They do **not** sack Lahore in his lifetime. The sack of Lahore is **1241**, five years after his death, when his successors are weak. Do not move 1241 back into this reign.

Baghdad itself falls to the Mongols only in **1258**. In 1229 the caliph is still there, which is why his letter still means something.

## What made it a state, not a general’s following

Aibak’s power died with him in a polo fall. Iltutmish left institutions the next ruler could use.

**1. The iqta.**

He assigns the **revenue of a district** to an officer, later called a **muqti** or **iqtadar**. The officer keeps troops out of that revenue and sends the surplus on. The land is not his property. The sultan can transfer him.

This is the Delhi form of a system already used in the Persian and Ghorid world. For the Indian exam, **Iltutmish** is the ruler who makes **iqta** the regular way of paying the sultanate’s commanders. Do not answer an iqta question with Alauddin’s price controls, and do not answer it with Akbar’s mansabdari.

**2. The coins.**

Before him, north India used a muddle of old Rajput and Ghorid pieces. He issues a standard **silver tanka** and a smaller **copper jital**. The tanka is about **11 grams**. He strikes them in his own name. Aibak is not known to have done that.

![[iltutmish-tanka.jpg]]

A **silver tanka** of Iltutmish, Sind type, about 11 grams. The writing is Arabic. This is one issue of the reform, not the only mint. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

![[iltutmish-coin.jpg]]

Smaller coins of his reign, copper alloy. This is the everyday **jital** class, not the silver tanka, and not a gold mohur. The gold mohur belongs to a much later century. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0, File:Coin of Iltutmish.jpg.

**3. The Group of Forty.**

The exam name is **Turkan-i-Chahalgani**, the corps of forty. It means his band of elite **Turkish slave-officers**, the men who held the iqtas and the forts. It was not a parliament, and it was not a written list of exactly forty seats.

Their power is the problem he leaves behind. They accept a slave-sultan like himself. They will not accept his daughter. **Balban**, much later, breaks what is left of them.

**4. The caliph’s letter, 1229.**

Inside India he was already sultan because he held Delhi. The **mansur(the letter of recognization)** matters outside that fact. It says the Abbasid caliph recognises him, so a rival Turkish officer cannot call him a mere rebel slave. It does not mean the caliph ruled Delhi, and it does not mean the caliph sent an army.

## What you can still see

![[iltutmish-tomb.jpg]]

His **tomb**, in the Qutb complex at Mehrauli(South Delhi), beside the mosque and the minar of the Aibak note. Red sandstone, bands of carving and calligraphy. The chamber is **open to the sky** today. This is the earliest royal tomb of the Delhi Sultanate you can stand in. It is not a portrait and not a Mughal dome. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, File:Inside the Tomb of Iltutmish, Qutb Minar Complex.jpg.

![[hauz-i-shamsi.jpg]]

**Hauz-i-Shamsi**, the great tank at Mehrauli, dug for the city in his reign, about **1230**. The houses behind it are modern. The small pavilion has been rebuilt. A later story says the Prophet’s horse left a hoofprint here. Remember the tank, not the hoofprint. Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, File:Hauz-i-shamshi-Mehrauli-Delhi-dl-89.jpg.

He also continued the **Qutb Minar**. The saint **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** lived in this Delhi and died in **1235**. Iltutmish honoured him. The saint did not build the minar, and the dargah you visit now is later work.

## Why Razia, and why the reign ends in an argument

His eldest son, **Nasiruddin Mahmud**, died in Bengal in **1229**. The grown son who was left, **Ruknuddin Firuz**, was not the man he trusted. Iltutmish named his daughter **Razia**.

That choice is the pivot of the succession. A daughter who had already taken part in the work of the capital was, in his judgement, safer than a weak son. The Turkish nobles did not share the judgement. When he died in **1236**, **Shah Turkan**, Ruknuddin’s mother, and those nobles put **Ruknuddin** on the throne first.

There are **two** men called Nasiruddin Mahmud. The son who died in Bengal in 1229 is one. The sultan of **1246–1266**, in whose reign Balban is the real power, is a younger son. Do not merge them.

Razia’s own reign is [[06. Razia Sultan (1236–1240)]]. Do not tell her story as if it had already happened in 1236.

| Iltutmish founded the sultanate in 1206 | That date is **Aibak**. Iltutmish begins in **1211**. |
| He ruled from Lahore | He makes **Delhi** the capital. Lahore is the frontier. |
| He defeated Chinggis Khan at Delhi | He refused Jalaluddin in **1221**. Chinggis never besieged Delhi. |
| The Mongols sacked Lahore in his reign | **1241**, after his death. |

| The Group of Forty was an elected council | Elite Turkish slave-officers. Balban later breaks their power. |
| Iqta means he gave them the land as property | Revenue assignment. Transferable. Not ownership. |
| The caliph governed Delhi | The caliph sent recognition from Baghdad. He sent no government. |
| He annexed Gujarat and Bengal for good | Gujarat stays Chaulukya. Bengal submits and slips. |
| Nasiruddin Mahmud of 1246 is the son who died in 1229 | Two different sons. |
| Altamash is a different sultan | An older spelling of **Iltutmish**. |

## One answer

Iltutmish was Aibak’s Turkish slave and son-in-law. In **1211** Delhi’s officers chose him over Aram Shah, and he made **Delhi** the capital. He defeated Yildiz at Tarain in **1216** and Qabacha in **1228**, so the split of 1206 was closed on the Indian side. In **1221** he refused shelter to Jalaluddin Mangbarni and kept Chinggis Khan’s war beyond the Indus. In **1229** the caliph al-Mustansir recognised him, the first such recognition for Delhi. He organised **iqta**, and he issued the **silver tanka** and the **copper jital**. He took Gwalior in **1232**. He died in **1236** having named his daughter Razia, because the son he had left was not strong enough. The nobles resisted that choice. The state he left was real. The succession was not settled.
