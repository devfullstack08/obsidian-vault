# Muhammad bin Qasim, 712 CE

He was an **Arab commander** of the **Umayyad Caliphate**. He was **not a caliph**.

The wider Umayyad setting is in [[04. Umayyads and the Road to Sindh]]. This page is the Indian campaign.

## Who he was

**Muhammad bin Qasim** (also Muhammad ibn al-Qasim al-Thaqafi), about **695–715**. The exact day of birth and death is not securely known, so do not memorise a calendar date.

- Tribe: **Banu Thaqif**, around Taif in western Arabia.
- He was a young relative of **al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf**, the Umayyad governor in Iraq. Hajjaj sent him.
- The caliph in Damascus was **al-Walid I**.

## Why Sindh, and when

The campaign is **711–712**. Remember **712** for the defeat of Raja Dahir.

Later chronicles, especially the *Chachnama*, say ships were attacked near **Debal** and captives were taken. That is the immediate story. Behind it is the Umayyad push along the Makran frontier. Arab sea trade with western India was already older than 712. Traders arriving, and an army taking a kingdom, are different facts.

Sindh was then ruled by the **Brahman dynasty**. **Raja Dahir** was its king. His capital was **Aror** (Alor), on the Indus near modern Rohri.

## The route, in the order it happened

**Iraq → Makran coast → Debal → interior of Sindh → Aror and the defeat of Dahir → Multan**

Do not put Debal after Sindh. Debal is the first place he took in Sindh.

| Place  | What to say                                                                                                             |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Debal  | Port on the lower Indus coast. Often placed near modern Karachi; the exact site is debated (Banbhore is one candidate). |
| Aror   | Dahir’s capital. He was defeated and killed in **712**.                                                                 |
| Multan | Taken after Aror. It lies in present-day Punjab. This is not the conquest of all Punjab.                                |

The later chronicle tradition says Dahir was killed and his head was sent to al-Hajjaj in Iraq. Treat that as the chronicle story, not as a report written on the day.

## What he established, and what he did not

He set up Arab rule in **Sindh and the lower Indus**, up to **Multan**. He became the province’s governor. This is the first **durable** Muslim political conquest in the subcontinent.

He did **not** conquer India. He was **not** the first Muslim to reach India. He did **not** found the Delhi Sultanate. Almost three centuries separate **712** from Mahmud of Ghazni in **998**.

In the conquered territory, non-Muslims were required to pay **jizya** (not “jijiya”). That tax applied in this province. It was not a tax over the whole subcontinent. The same later tradition says local people were left to their own worship once they paid.

## 715

Al-Walid died in **715**, and the new caliph was Sulayman. Hajjaj had already died in **714**. Qasim was **recalled**. Stories of his death do not agree: some say he died in custody, and the place of burial is also disputed. Learn “recalled in 715; death accounts differ.” Do not learn one city as the certain place of death.

His recall did not end Arab rule in Sindh. Later governors stayed. They could not turn this frontier into a conquest of northern India.

## Impact of Qasim

His own time as governor was short, about **712 to 715**. The impact is the foothold he left.

### Political

The **Brahman dynasty of Sindh ended**. Raja Dahir was defeated and killed in **712**. Arab rule was planted in **Sindh and the lower Indus, up to Multan**. This is the first **durable** Muslim political rule in the subcontinent.

After Qasim was recalled, Arab governors remained. In **750** the province passed, with the rest of the central empire, from the Umayyads to the **Abbasids**. Later Arab centres were **Mansura** in lower Sindh and **Multan** in the north.

He did not conquer India. Later Arab governors tried to push into **Gujarat, Malwa and Rajasthan**, and they were stopped.

| Check                      | Who                                                 | What to say                                                          |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Toward Malwa and Rajasthan | **Nagabhata I**, Gurjara-Pratihara                  | Credited with stopping an Arab advance. Do not invent a battle name. |
| **Navsari, 738**           | A Chalukya governor, **Avanijanashraya Pulakeshin** | An Arab force was defeated. This is after Qasim, not his own battle. |

So the lasting political result stayed in **Sindh and Multan**.

### Religion and society

Inside that province, non-Muslims paid **jizya** and were left to their own worship once they paid. That policy belongs to Sindh and Multan. It was not a tax or a law for the whole subcontinent.

Islam became the religion of the **rulers** there. The people of Sindh did not all become Muslim in 712. Conversion was slow, over later centuries.

The *Chachnama*, written centuries later, says some local groups, including Buddhists at odds with Dahir’s dynasty, accommodated the new rule. Treat that as the later chronicle, not as a count of converts.

### Trade and learning

**Debal** and the mouth of the Indus came under a Muslim government. Sea trade between western India and the Gulf was already old. After 712 that trade had an Arab administration at the Indian end.

The Arab presence in Sindh became one road by which Indian knowledge reached the caliphate. Under the Abbasids, Indian astronomical works were translated into Arabic at **Baghdad** from the **770s** (the Sindhind tradition). That work belongs to Baghdad, decades after Qasim. It is a result of the contact his conquest opened. It is not a book he carried home in 712.

### What did not follow from 712

| Claim | The fact |
| --- | --- |
| He conquered India | He held Sindh to Multan. |
| He founded the Delhi Sultanate | That begins in **1206**, with Qutb ud-Din Aibak. |
| He began Turkish rule in India | Turkish campaigns begin with **Mahmud of Ghazni**, from **998**. |
| 712 runs straight into 998 | Almost **three centuries** lie between them. |
| His recall in 715 ended Arab Sindh | Governors stayed. Mahmud later found Muslim rulers already at **Multan**, and in **1006** he attacked them. |

## Say this, and nothing else, if asked

Qasim was a **commander**. Walid was the **caliph**. Hajjaj **sent** him. Dahir **fell in 712**. The land taken was **Sindh to Multan**, not India. The impact was Arab rule in that strip, **jizya** there, and a frontier later governors could not push across the rest of India.
