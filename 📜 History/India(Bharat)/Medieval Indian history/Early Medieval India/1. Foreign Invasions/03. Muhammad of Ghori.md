The exam name is **Muhammad Ghori**. His name was **Muizz al-Din Muhammad bin Sam**. He is also called Shihab al-Din. He is not Mahmud of Ghazni, and he is not Mahmud’s son Muhammad.

Previous campaign: [[02. Muhammad of Ghazni( 998–1030AD)]]. Next: [[04. Qutb ud-Din Aibak (1206–1210)]]. The short bridge is [[06. Ghazni to Ghor to Delhi]]. The country he entered is on [[Bharat in the early medieval age]].

## Who he was

**Ghor** is a mountain region in present-day Afghanistan. **Ghazni** is a city. The Ghurids and the Ghaznavids were different houses, and they were enemies.

| Point   | What to write                                                                                                                 |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Person  | **Muizz al-Din Muhammad**, known as Muhammad Ghori                                                                            |
| Brother | **Ghiyath al-Din**. The elder brother held Ghor and the western lands. India was Muizz al-Din’s sphere.                       |
| People  | Eastern Iranian dynasty. The court language was **Persian**. The army’s slave-commanders were largely **Turkish**. Not Arabs. |
| Faith   | **Sunni**                                                                                                                     |
| 1173    | He took **Ghazni**. That became his own seat.                                                                                 |
| 1203    | Ghiyath al-Din died. Muizz al-Din was then the senior Ghurid sultan. He still did not rule from Delhi.                        |
| 1206    | He was killed near the Jhelum, on the road back from India.                                                                   |

Ghor was a poor mountain country. His brother kept the west. A kingdom in India was Muizz al-Din’s way to build a power of his own. That is why he differs from Mahmud. Mahmud carried treasure home. Ghori meant to stay.

![[ghori-campaigns-map.png]]

Read the map from west to east. **Ghor** is the homeland. **Ghazni** is the city he took in 1173. **Multan** and **Lahore** are the Indian base. **Tarain** is the battle that decides north India. **Chandawar** opens the Ganga plain. The gold box in the east is **Bakhtiyar Khalji**, not Ghori in person. Delhi is marked because **Aibak** held it. Ghori did not live there.

## Mahmud and Ghori

This comparison is a standard question. Learn it as a table.

| Point | Mahmud of Ghazni | Muhammad Ghori |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House | Ghaznavid. Turkish slave origin. | Ghurid. From Ghor. |
| Dates | 998–1030. Raids about 1000–1027. | Indian campaigns **1175–1206**. |
| Aim | Plunder, and a frontier in the northwest. | Conquer and keep north India. |
| Base he kept | Ghazni, and the Punjab frontier. | Lahore after **1186**, then forts under his officers. |
| Battle to name | Somnath, **1025**. | Tarain, **1191** and **1192**. |
| Delhi | Not taken. | Taken after 1192 and left to Aibak. |
| Result | No Delhi Sultanate. | The foundations of one. He is not its first sultan. |
| Death | 1030. | **1206**, assassinated. |

## Tarain

Tarain, or Taraori, lies north of Delhi, near modern Karnal in Haryana. There are two battles, one year apart. Do not merge them.

| | First, 1191 | Second, 1192 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winner | **Prithviraj III** | **Muhammad Ghori** |
| What happened | The Chahamana charge, with elephants, broke Ghori’s line. He was wounded. | The usual account says his divisions attacked and fell back, tiring the Rajput line, and a cavalry reserve then struck. |
| Result | He retreated to the Punjab and prepared a return. | Prithviraj was defeated. Persian histories say he was captured and killed. Delhi and Ajmer passed under Ghurid control. |

Prithviraj was the Chahamana king of Shakambhari and Ajmer. The Chauhans had taken Delhi from the Tomaras in the twelfth century. He is not “the king of Delhi” as if Delhi were a separate old empire, and he is not the Chauhan king of the year 1000.

The *Prithviraj Raso* is a later heroic poem in Braj, linked to **Chand Bardai**. It tells two stories that exams sometimes copy. One is that Prithviraj carried off **Samyukta** (Sanyogita), Jayachandra’s daughter. The other is that a blinded Prithviraj killed Ghori with an arrow. Ghori died in **1206**, fourteen years after Tarain. The rivalry between the two kings is the fact. The abduction and the arrow are the poem. Do not write the poem as the battle report.

Jayachandra of Kannauj did not fight beside Prithviraj. The two houses were rivals. That rivalry is the fact, and it is the pivot.

The popular sentence is: after the first Tarain, Jayachandra helped Ghori defeat Prithviraj, because he disliked him. Do not write that sentence in the exam. It comes from later poems and retellings, above all the tradition around the *Prithviraj Raso*. The early chronicles do not put Jayachandra’s army on Ghori’s side at the second Tarain.

What you can write is narrower, and it is enough:

> **Jayachandra did not come to Prithviraj’s aid. The two Rajput kings did not make one army. That disunity left Prithviraj to face the second Tarain alone.**

A real alliance is also hard to fit with the next campaign. In **1194**, at **Chandawar**, Ghori defeated Jayachandra himself. The man of Kannauj was the next enemy, not a partner who stayed loyal. Write the rivalry and the absence of help. Do not write that Jayachandra won Tarain for Ghori.

## Why 1192 held, when Mahmud’s raids did not

Use these causes together. One of them is not the whole answer.

1. **The aim was different.** He needed a kingdom, so he left men in the forts. Mahmud needed treasure, so he went home to Ghazni.
2. **The Gujarat door had already shut.** In **1178** Naikidevi’s Chaulukyas defeated him. He did not make Gujarat the road into north India. He turned to the Punjab, took Lahore in **1186**, and only then came down on Delhi from the north. The base is the pivot. After Lahore, the next campaign did not have to begin from Ghor.
3. **He used slave-commanders.** **Qutb ud-Din Aibak** held the north. **Bakhtiyar Khalji** took the east. They collected revenue and did not wait for the sultan to return each winter.
4. **The second battle was fought differently.** Mounted archers and a reserve were used against a Rajput line that had stood in the open.
5. **North India had no single king.** The Chahamanas and the Gahadavalas did not combine. There had been no north Indian empire of Harsha’s kind since 647.
6. **He could lose and return.** Gujarat in 1178 and the first Tarain in 1191 were defeats. The conquest is the return in 1192, not an unbroken list of victories.

Do not write that Indians were cowards, or that religion alone decided the battles. A Chaulukya army defeated him in Gujarat. Prithviraj defeated him at the first Tarain.

## The officers

| Officer | Where | What the exam wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Qutb ud-Din Aibak** | Delhi, Ajmer, and the north | Ghori’s slave-general. From **1206** he is the first ruler of the **Mamluk**, or Slave, dynasty. |
| **Bakhtiyar Khalji** | Bihar, then Bengal | A Khalji officer, not one of the three Turkish slaves. Defeated the Sena king **Lakshmana Sena**. His base in Bengal was **Lakhnauti**. |
| **Nasir ud-Din Qabacha** | Uch, Sindh, Multan | Held the Indus country. Later a rival of Delhi. |
| **Taj ud-Din Yildiz** | Ghazni | Held Ghori’s own seat after the murder. |

Guidebooks spell **Yildiz** as Yalduz and **Qabacha** as Kubacha. Same two men. After the murder, Yildiz took **Ghazni**, Qabacha held **Multan and Uch**, and Aibak held the Indian conquests, with **Lahore** as his own seat.

**Bakhtiyar Khalji** needs a separate sentence from those three. He was not a Turkish military slave. He came into Ghori’s service as a Khalji adventurer. He did not begin as a governor under a Rajput king. Do not write that line.

His conquest runs Bihar, then Bengal. Guidebooks say he took **Odantapuri** in **1193** and **Lakhnauti** in **1204**. The order is right. The safe dating is **about 1203–05**. Odantapuri, Vikramshila, and Nalanda were wrecked in that conquest. He did not empty every monastery in India on one afternoon.

He then marched through **Kamarupa**, in Assam, toward Tibet. The march failed. He did not occupy Tibet or Assam. Back at **Devkot**, about **1206**, one of his own officers, **Ali Mardan**, killed him. Bengal after that belongs to Iltutmish’s reign, not to a second war of Ghori’s. **Ghiyasuddin Iwaz** took the throne there, and Iltutmish’s son Nasiruddin Mahmud defeated him in **1227**. That note is [[05. Iltutmish (1211–1236)]].

In **1206** these men did not become one Indian state. Aibak ruled from the Indian side. Yildiz held Ghazni. Qabacha held the Indus. **Iltutmish** later defeated the rivals and made **Delhi** the capital of a sultanate. Ghori laid the base. Aibak began the dynasty. Iltutmish consolidated it.

## Iqta

Ghori gave conquered districts to his commanders. They held the forts and took the revenue. That is the start of ruling through slave-officers.

The **iqta** system, as the Indian exam uses the word, belongs to the Delhi Sultanate and above all to **Iltutmish**. Do not answer a question on iqta with “Ghori’s seventeen raids,” and do not answer it with Ghori alone.

## Impact

| What changed | The limit |
| --- | --- |
| Turkish political power in north India became permanent. | Gujarat, Malwa, and the Deccan were not conquered. |
| Delhi, Ajmer, and the Ganga-Yamuna doab passed to his officers. | He did not sit in Delhi, and he is not the first Delhi sultan. |
| Bihar and Bengal were taken by Bakhtiyar Khalji. The great Buddhist monasteries of Bihar, including the textbook names **Odantapuri, Vikramshila, and Nalanda**, were destroyed in that conquest. | Do not write that every monk in India died on one afternoon. Write that the monastic order of Bihar ended with this campaign. |
| The Slave dynasty becomes possible. | **1206** is Aibak’s date. The sultanate’s real shape is Iltutmish’s reign. |
| North India was not converted in a single reign. | Islam had been in Sindh since 712, and on the Punjab frontier since Mahmud. Ghori’s new fact is rule, not the first arrival of the religion. |

## Traps

| If you are about to write this | Write this instead |
| --- | --- |
| Ghori and Mahmud are the same man | Mahmud dies in 1030. Ghori’s wars begin in 1175. |
| Ghor is another name for Ghazni | A region, and a city. |
| Naikidevi, Bhima II, and Mularaja II all defeated him together at Anhilwara | 1178 is the child Mularaja II and Naikidevi, at Kayadara, on the road to Anhilwara. Bhima II is Aibak’s campaign in 1197. Neither year annexes Gujarat. |
| Prithviraj won both battles of Tarain | He won the first. He lost the second. |
| The Raso explains how Ghori died | Ghori died in 1206, near the Jhelum. The usual name for the killers is the Khokhars. |
| Jayachandra invited Ghori | Unproven. Write that the two Rajput houses were rivals. |
| Ghori was the first Sultan of Delhi | Aibak, from 1206. Ghori made that possible. |
| Bakhtiyar Khalji was a rival dynasty from the start | He was Ghori’s officer, and he was not one of the three Turkish slaves. Bengal was his conquest. He was killed at Devkot in 1206. He did not conquer Tibet. |
| He first served as a governor under Rajput rulers | Not in the early accounts. He served Ghori, then took Bihar and Bengal. |

## One answer

Muhammad Ghori, Muizz al-Din, was the Ghurid prince who conquered north India between **1175 and 1206**. Unlike Mahmud of Ghazni, he meant to keep the land. He took Multan in **1175**, was defeated in Gujarat in **1178**, and ended the last Ghaznavid at Lahore in **1186**. Prithviraj III defeated him at the first battle of Tarain in **1191**. Ghori won the second in **1192**, then defeated Jayachandra at Chandawar in **1194**. He left Aibak in the north and Bakhtiyar Khalji in Bihar and Bengal. He was killed in **1206**. Aibak, not Ghori, begins the Delhi Sultanate.
