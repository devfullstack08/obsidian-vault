**Full name:** Baji Rao Ballal Bhat
**Also known as:** Baji Rao I, Thorale Bajirao
**Born:** 18 August 1700
**Father:** Balaji Vishwanath
**Chhatrapati:** Shahu I
**Peshwa:** 1720–1740
Death: 28 April 1740, Raverkhedi

Central question: How did a young Peshwa transform the Marathas from a major Deccan power into a political and military force capable of challenging the Mughal Empire across northern and central India?

In Chapter 8, we learned how Balaji Vishwanath secured recognition of Maratha claims to Swarajya, chauth and sardeshmukhi through the imperial grants of 1719.

But there was a difference between receiving revenue rights on paper and enforcing them across enormous territories.

That was the challenge Baji Rao inherited.

## 1. India in 1720 — why was expansion possible?

![Book Excerptise: The Mughal Empire: The new Cambridge history of India, I.5 by John F. Imagine India in 1720.

The Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah (1719–1748) occupied the throne at Delhi.

The Mughal Empire still claimed sovereignty over extensive territories, but its provincial governors and regional elites were becoming increasingly independent.

### Major political powers

| Power              | Leadership around 1720     | Political situation                                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mughal Empire      | Muhammad Shah              | Imperial authority weakening in several provinces      |
| Maratha kingdom    | Shahu I                    | Growing influence in the Deccan                        |
| Hyderabad / Deccan | Nizam-ul-Mulk              | Building an increasingly autonomous political position |
| Awadh              | Saadat Khan from 1722      | Emerging regional state                                |
| Bengal             | Murshid Quli Khan          | Substantial provincial autonomy                        |
| Rajput kingdoms    | Jaipur, Jodhpur and others | Important regional political powers                    |
| Bundelkhand        | Maharaja Chhatrasal        | Regional kingdom under pressure from Mughal forces     |

### Why was the Mughal Empire weakening?

After Aurangzeb's death in 1707:

- Imperial succession disputes weakened continuity.
    
- Powerful nobles competed for offices.
    
- Provincial governors acquired increasing autonomy.
    
- Maintaining armies across distant territories was expensive.
    
- Regional powers challenged imperial revenue collection.

The Empire was not yet destroyed, but Delhi's ability to enforce its authority was weakening.
### What did Baji Rao understand?

He recognized an opportunity to extend Maratha military and fiscal influence beyond Maharashtra.

Instead of concentrating only on defending the Deccan, he increasingly pursued campaigns in **Malwa**, **Bundelkhand**, **Gujarat** and **northern India**.

Historian Stewart Gordon identifies northern expansion as the defining theme of Baji Rao's Peshwaship.
## 2. Baji Rao becomes Peshwa in 1720

Balaji Vishwanath died in April 1720.

Shahu needed a successor capable of managing powerful Maratha chiefs and dealing with the Nizam and Mughal government.
### Why did Shahu appoint such a young man?

Baji Rao was only about 19–20 years old.

Some senior ministers preferred a more experienced candidate.

But Shahu appointed Baji Rao, who already possessed military experience and had accompanied important political expeditions, including the journey to Delhi in 1719.
### What strengths did Baji Rao possess?

- Knowledge of Mughal-Maratha political relations.
    
- Experience of mounted campaigning.
    
- Ability to organize rapid operations.
    
- Confidence in dealing with powerful commanders.
    
- A strategy of expanding Maratha influence beyond the Deccan.

### Was Baji Rao already king?

No.

Shahu remained the Chhatrapati at Satara.

Baji Rao was the Peshwa, an office becoming increasingly important.

Chhatrapati Shahu I — Satara

Royal legitimacy and supreme authority

Peshwa Baji Rao I

Principal military and political leader

Maratha commanders and regional chiefs

Cavalry, territorial campaigns and revenue collection

UPSC distinction: Baji Rao's increasing influence did not immediately eliminate Shahu's authority. The shift from Chhatrapati-centred government towards Peshwa dominance was gradual.

## 3. Baji Rao's expansion strategy — why move north?

The old Mughal-Maratha struggle had largely centred on the Deccan.

Baji Rao believed the weakening Mughal Empire presented wider opportunities.

A famous saying attributed to him compares the Mughal Empire to a decaying tree and advocates striking at its trunk rather than its branches.

The precise wording is preserved in later historical narratives, so it is better remembered as a summary of his strategy than as a verified verbatim speech.
### What did his strategy mean?

Earlier priority: Defend Maratha territory against Mughal invasion.

New priority: Exploit Mughal weakness and extend Maratha influence into other provinces.
### Why was Malwa especially important?

![Omkareshwar National Park,Khandwa](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/BgKS4i4EUosY1Y99jT2VsOx9vtEq-4DSWrivtOofRmtB2AKxmgMluoenyIgCAe_8cOyKe9kQB-2YTINQvhWVCf83lE3_3gwMb8qtfGI0oXKA5x_-67m0aGxtYFoMOr_-ItY91tMkiJDCUhBZ_hg-hzo321dpNGkkyFDq4xdrSu8?purpose=inline)

Malwa was a major region between the Deccan and northern India.

It was strategically valuable because:

1. It connected central India with important northern routes.
    
2. Its agricultural and commercial revenues could support armies.
    
3. It provided access towards Bundelkhand, Rajasthan and Delhi.
    
4. Controlling its routes made Maratha northern operations easier.

The Narmada and adjoining regions formed important geographical connections between the Deccan and central India.

### The strategy's financial logic

A distant military campaign needed money.

Rather than relying entirely on revenue from Maharashtra, Maratha commanders could seek tribute and fiscal rights from newly contested regions.

This created opportunities for expansion—but also increased the autonomy of commanders who collected those revenues.

## 4. Nizam-ul-Mulk — Baji Rao's principal rival in the Deccan

![Liberation of Hyderabad Samsthan | Asaf Jahi Dynasty](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/333VhejYw_4fk6kMgo3t6hVN4yL-BdUawNUjhmGnPJ5pl5cKcgBn518KpAXISVu8xzicJ_g9n5QrymXSNajmn0_VsDjHoQKrdwOoOJ9VPIFB285EsHL68gt_UY8i3MgyT2JjKMZRp8BodlYwZF_DkgLc36FCiVrPdjctnNYOOzg?purpose=inline)

**Name:** Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I
**Position:** Former Mughal grandee and Deccan viceroy
**Political centre:** Hyderabad
**Dynasty:** Founder of the Asaf Jahi ruling house
**Principal objective**: Consolidate his authority over the Deccan
### Why did the Nizam become powerful?

The Mughal Empire was weakening.

Nizam-ul-Mulk was an experienced commander and administrator who sought greater control over Deccan affairs.

After defeating Mubariz Khan at the **Battle of Shakar Kheda** in 1724, he consolidated the foundation of the autonomous Hyderabad state.
### Why did the Nizam and Marathas clash?

The Deccan contained overlapping political and financial claims.

The Nizam wanted:

- Control over provincial revenues.
    
- Recognition of his position as the principal regional authority.
    
- Freedom from excessive Maratha demands.
    
- Influence over rival Maratha factions.

Baji Rao and Shahu wanted:

- Enforcement of the 1719 chauth and sardeshmukhi grants.
    
- Recognition of Shahu's political position.
    
- Protection of their territories.
    
- Freedom to expand Maratha influence.
### The Kolhapur problem

Remember Chapter 7.

Shahu faced a rival royal line at Kolhapur under Sambhaji II.

The Nizam supported Sambhaji II's claim as a way of challenging Shahu.

This made the conflict more than a dispute about money.

It was also a struggle over which Maratha ruler the Nizam would recognize.

## 5. Battle of Palkhed (1728) — Baji Rao defeats the Nizam

![Peshwa Bajirao Ballal, Part 1: The Great Maratha Expansion - Words and Swords](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/Tok9IdCHk-3zSdLeypK8FbzIpm7r3cHXkDPxeWo_9HuYYk3A_06xFMEelCQ9D5ODcJzGO5vQy9BOvIWvSFdJGtGP_pkTzTwQI4mFPLrGScP9lpi4wx7y4z33RIGNXFuwok48ggEqX_mMqbi3t2FvXDdmBQd_C1bCpDojcHtBD3o?purpose=inline)

The campaign map is illustrative; military movements should be understood as approximate rather than precise modern road routes.

This is one of the most important battles for UPSC.
### Why did the war begin?

The Nizam contested Shahu's revenue claims and sought to use the Satara–Kolhapur rivalry to weaken the Peshwa.

In 1727, relations escalated into open warfare.

The Nizam entered the Pune region, while Baji Rao conducted rapid cavalry operations elsewhere.
### What did the Nizam expect?

He wanted Baji Rao to fight a major conventional battle in which Mughal-style artillery and a larger, heavily equipped army could be effective.

### What did Baji Rao do instead?

He avoided that kind of battle.

1. Rapid movement: Baji Rao's cavalry moved across the Deccan, attacking vulnerable areas.
    
2. Pressure on important centres: His operations threatened the Nizam's territories and communications.
    
3. Forced pursuit: The Nizam had to respond, undertaking difficult marches.
    
4. Supply disruption: The Nizam's forces became increasingly vulnerable to shortages of food, water and fodder.
    
5. Encirclement: Near Palkhed, Baji Rao gained a decisive positional advantage.
    
6. Negotiated settlement: The Nizam accepted terms rather than continuing the campaign.
### Date

The decisive confrontation near Palkhed occurred on 25 February 1728.

The Nizam was compelled to negotiate.
### Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon — 6 March 1728

|Terms|Significance|
|---|---|
|Recognized Shahu's position|Weakened Sambhaji II's rival claim|
|Stopped supporting Kolhapur against Shahu|Reduced interference in Maratha succession|
|Accepted important Maratha revenue claims|Strengthened enforcement of chauth and sardeshmukhi|
|Settled immediate conflict|Allowed Baji Rao to pursue further expansion|

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer details how Baji Rao's mobile operations exhausted the Nizam's army and led to the agreement.
### Why did Baji Rao win?

Military mobility + attacks on communications + supply pressure + avoidance of unfavourable battle.

He did not need to annihilate the Nizam's entire army.

He needed to create a situation in which the Nizam could no longer operate effectively.

### Historical significance

Palkhed demonstrated the effectiveness of coordinated mobile warfare against an army burdened by heavy equipment and extended supply lines.

It also established Baji Rao as a major political and military figure in the Deccan.

Prelims trap: Palkhed was fought against Nizam-ul-Mulk, not Ahmad Shah Abdali or the British.

## 6. Bundelkhand campaign (1728–1729) — Chhatrasal asks for help

![Maharaja Chhatrasal story: जब विंध्यवासिनी मंदिर तोड़ने आए मुस्लिम सैनिकों पर टूट पड़े छत्रसाल... - maharaja chhatrasal story](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8zo0V68AvbYRv4-h48QiuWAm360CKka6gPAAI1540VBHnZAWnBYmBcQlrnZakshOOHTa9pj8p2chYmpDPZ53_q9w-pVy-90NmCdHfhj75i-aAbLtlFAyTK_21Zrc-KVgG-jeztg3rVPAw8_qVSvDgw6K-hLhyXatXLJeSw1ezwA?purpose=inline)

### Who was Chhatrasal?

Maharaja Chhatrasal (1649–1731) was an important Bundela ruler who had established an independent political position in Bundelkhand.

Bundelkhand lies in parts of present-day Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh.

Chhatrasal had a long history of resisting Mughal authority.
### What happened in 1728?

The Mughal commander Muhammad Khan Bangash, associated with the Farrukhabad region, attacked Chhatrasal's territories.

Chhatrasal faced serious military pressure and sought assistance from Baji Rao.

### Why would Baji Rao help him?

1. Political opportunity: Chhatrasal was an established regional ruler opposed to certain Mughal interests.

2. Strategic entry into Bundelkhand: Helping him could give the Marathas a foothold north of the Deccan.

3. Financial rewards: An alliance could provide revenue rights and territorial benefits.

4. Reputation: Supporting Chhatrasal would demonstrate that Maratha armies could operate effectively far from Maharashtra.

### What happened?

In 1729, Baji Rao moved north with Maratha forces.

His intervention helped defeat or force the withdrawal of Bangash's forces, relieving Chhatrasal.

### What did Baji Rao gain?

Chhatrasal later made important territorial and financial arrangements in favour of Baji Rao.

These became the basis for Maratha interests in Bundelkhand.

The often-repeated claim that Chhatrasal immediately transferred exactly one-third of his entire kingdom should be treated as a conventional summary: the actual territorial settlement and its implementation were more complicated.
### What about Mastani?

Mastani was associated with Chhatrasal's household and later became Baji Rao's partner.

A popular tradition describes her as Chhatrasal's daughter, given in marriage to Baji Rao following the Bundelkhand campaign.

However, surviving sources provide differing accounts of her ancestry, her status and the details of their relationship.

## 7. Expansion into Malwa — the gateway to northern India

The Palkhed victory strengthened Baji Rao's position in the Deccan.

He could now devote greater attention to northern expansion.
### Why Malwa?

Malwa offered routes towards Rajasthan, Bundelkhand and Delhi.

Maratha forces had raided Malwa before Baji Rao's appointment, but during his Peshwaship campaigns became more sustained.
### Important commanders

Malhar Rao Holkar

Important Maratha commander in Malwa; associated with the rise of the Holkar house.

Ranoji Shinde (Scindia)

Important commander whose family later became dominant in parts of central and northern India.

### What did these commanders do?

They organized expeditions, maintained military forces, negotiated with local elites and sought revenue.

As Maratha power expanded, they acquired increasing influence over particular territories.
### A significant development in 1728

Maratha forces under Chimaji Appa, Baji Rao's younger brother, defeated Mughal forces at Amjhera (1728), where the Mughal governor Giridhar Bahadur was killed.

This weakened Mughal authority in Malwa.

### Long-term significance

The expansion created the foundations of the future Holkar and Scindia regional states.

But these houses were not yet fully independent princely states with fixed borders.

UPSC concept: Military expansion preceded stable administrative consolidation.

## 8. Gujarat — why was it important to Maratha politics?

Gujarat was an economically valuable Mughal province with important agricultural revenues, trading towns and maritime connections.

Maratha commanders had sought chauth and other fiscal claims there for years.

### Who were the major Maratha interests?

- The Dabhade family, which held the office of Senapati.
    
- The Gaekwad family, associated with the expansion of Maratha power in Gujarat.
    
- Baji Rao and the Peshwa's political establishment.
### Why did conflict emerge?

The problem was not simply Marathas versus Mughals.

Different Maratha leaders competed over the right to collect revenue from Gujarat.

The central question: Who would control the military and financial benefits of expansion—regional chiefs or the Peshwa?

That conflict produced the Battle of Dabhoi in 1731.

## 9. Battle of Dabhoi (1 April 1731) — Baji Rao versus Trimbak Rao Dabhade

![Carved Hira Bhagol, the Eastern gate named after it's architect; Hiradhar, located in Dabhoi, Gujarat, India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/gzXdUDZG2JS7Y41hLsKb6Tefx3FPKn_kuUhgUWujkmoEZR-VsYaOOZAQv2cjgAdruPWI-l-ecesRBqY_-5Qzh1rEJI_gbR-GOY-yP6WYoa_w0eIFRdBPpSmVFNsJAuHNDcfvh_h_oppzyTlnRRefV_XIWmleTmcV47GZXLS-E3svKQG7yfEs3PBdzas0b4CW?purpose=inline)

This battle is particularly important for understanding internal Maratha politics.

### Who was Trimbak Rao Dabhade?

Trimbak Rao Dabhade was the Maratha Senapati and an influential member of the Dabhade family.

His family possessed established interests in Gujarat.

He resented the Peshwa's growing involvement in these affairs.
### Why did he oppose Baji Rao?

The dispute concerned:

- Control over Gujarat revenue rights.
    
- Authority over military commanders.
    
- The Peshwa's growing influence.
    
- Political relations with the Nizam and rival chiefs.

The Nizam encouraged opposition to Baji Rao.

### What happened?

Baji Rao confronted Dabhade's forces near Dabhoi in Gujarat on 1 April 1731.

Trimbak Rao Dabhade was killed in the battle.

Baji Rao emerged victorious.
### Result

Shahu's government subsequently arranged a settlement with the Dabhade family.

The family retained significant status and Gujarat revenue interests, but the Peshwa's political supremacy had increased.

The Gaekwads gradually became especially influential in Gujarat.

### Why is Dabhoi so important?

The Battle of Palkhed demonstrated Baji Rao's ability to defeat an external regional rival.

The Battle of Dabhoi demonstrated his ability to overcome a powerful rival within the Maratha political establishment.

| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Enemy: Nizam-ul-Mulk                              | Rival: Trimbak Rao Dabhade                          |
| Main dispute: Deccan authority and Shahu's rights | Main dispute: Gujarat revenues and Peshwa supremacy |
| Result: Nizam negotiated                          | Result: Dabhade killed                              |

## 10. Satara and Kolhapur — Treaty of Warna (1731)

Remember the rivalry studied in Chapters 7 and 8.

Shahu ruled from Satara, while Sambhaji II maintained the rival royal establishment at Kolhapur.

The rivalry continued during Baji Rao's early Peshwaship.
### Why was reconciliation necessary?

Repeated conflict between royal factions consumed Maratha resources and created opportunities for outside intervention.

The Nizam had already used the dispute to challenge Shahu.
### What happened?

Following military and political developments around 1729–1730, Shahu and Sambhaji II reached a settlement known as the **Treaty of Warna (1731)**.

It recognized separate spheres of authority for the two royal houses.
### Why is the treaty important?

It reduced the immediate danger of civil conflict, although disputes and rivalry did not disappear permanently.

UPSC trap:

- 1728: Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon — Baji Rao and Nizam.
    
- 1731: Treaty of Warna — Satara and Kolhapur royal branches.

They were different agreements.

## 11. Pune becomes increasingly important — Shaniwar Wada (1732)

![The Concrete Paparazzi: Shaniwar Wada, Pune](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/EsnuokooNk0Uq5eP6Oj53PqqwPrY9sBMOEijSOmjKY4RH7CfPcqG8YHFSYlzJdu3xHLmdAGm4lUIE5hpLI6XGfJZZQ6_6YvzuHEvFPIhaRB7R-7cjo1pV3S4BYiCbG3hz8OV8Vvqa2KOgWbJpWim54tM4Voy4a2qImGbzaJpLGY?purpose=inline)

### Why did Pune become important?

Shahu's royal seat remained at Satara.

But the Peshwa required a political and administrative centre for organizing campaigns, maintaining records, negotiating with chiefs and coordinating military resources.

Pune possessed several advantages:

- It was connected with the earlier Bhonsle territorial base.
    
- It occupied an important position in western Maharashtra.
    
- It was accessible to regional military routes.
    
- The Peshwa's family and administrative networks were becoming established there.
### Shaniwar Wada

The construction of Shaniwar Wada began in 1730, and the residence was inaugurated in 1732.

It became an important political and residential centre of the Peshwas.

### Did Baji Rao move the Chhatrapati's capital from Satara to Pune?

No.

Shahu continued ruling from Satara.

Pune became the increasingly influential seat of the Peshwa, while Satara remained the royal capital.

This distinction is essential for understanding the later Maratha political structure.

## 12. Baji Rao's advance towards Delhi (1737)

![View of the Lahore Gate showing the moat, Shahjahanabad (now Lal Qila or Red Fort), Delhi, India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/c5v__3eI995L8mmmXYgxLtt-HsAJ0Y4pnsZNoixmjKOUxoNMMIHx7qsf5ua7wFog3vf8OoK6__CJJYtP2uMU3tn8eS-vPnfHVAMcM5BmVShpM73wRlFVUbFLAcFmK-Fna4IaHYFPYNECVD2RCFcHNu7lHuseSHtdBf053igh3ic?purpose=inline)

By the 1730s, Maratha power in central India had grown.

The imperial government at Delhi was alarmed by Maratha revenue demands and military operations.
### Why did Baji Rao advance towards Delhi?

He wanted to demonstrate that the Mughal emperor could no longer ignore Maratha claims in Malwa and northern India.

The advance was also intended to strengthen the Marathas' negotiating position.

### What happened in March 1737?

Baji Rao led a fast-moving cavalry force northwards.

His troops advanced towards Delhi, avoiding the slow conventional methods of marching with a heavy army.

On 28 March 1737, his forces defeated a Mughal detachment near the imperial capital.

The advance demonstrated the vulnerability of Delhi's surrounding defences.

### Did Baji Rao capture Delhi?

No.

He did not seize the Red Fort or depose the Mughal emperor.

The operation was a rapid military demonstration, followed by withdrawal.

### Why was this historically important?

Delhi had long symbolized imperial power.

The ability of a Maratha army to reach its outskirts showed that Mughal military control had weakened considerably.

But it is important to distinguish reaching and threatening Delhi from conquering and governing Delhi.
### Important examination comparison

| Event                                                        | Meaning                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Baji Rao reaches Delhi, 1737                                 | Maratha cavalry demonstrates imperial vulnerability     |

Do not merge these separate historical episodes.

## 13. Battle of Bhopal (1737) and Treaty of Doraha Sarai (1738)

![100 Best Bhopal Captions & Quotes for Instagarm Posts and Reels](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/ceNHZY1VIJJ3nAlQjc2HnKT7_jDzOAjsRaKLvBAsZmP6kJWrtorN6SOqq2wpJ8sHHklARfyxqe8WrbKcRFaGXFdrI9Od1PDHZpG0L0DgRn-xnXDMXeIrruvNmgqoM1mo94qNTyTPh4WYH7yVV8ChYOQF86hyN9uQAnELedEE9hg?purpose=inline)

The Mughal emperor now sought to contain Maratha expansion.

The experienced Nizam-ul-Mulk became central to this effort.

### Why did the Nizam confront Baji Rao again?

Baji Rao's campaigns threatened Mughal authority over Malwa.

The Nizam hoped to prevent Maratha domination in central India while strengthening his own political position.

### The campaign

In late 1737, Mughal-aligned forces under the Nizam confronted Baji Rao in the region around Bhopal.

Rather than attack the Nizam's strongest defensive positions directly, Baji Rao employed mobile forces to restrict supplies and communications.
### What was Baji Rao's strategy?

The underlying principle resembled Palkhed:

Do not fight the enemy where he is strongest. Make it difficult for him to obtain supplies, move freely or sustain his army.

Baji Rao's troops operated around the Nizam's positions and applied pressure through movement and disruption.
### Result

The Nizam was forced to negotiate.

A settlement was concluded at Doraha Sarai on 7 January 1738.

### Important terms

The settlement involved:

- Recognition of substantial Maratha claims in Malwa.
    
- Arrangements concerning territory in central India.
    
- A financial payment or indemnity commonly recorded as ₹50 lakh.
    
- The Nizam's undertaking to obtain appropriate Mughal imperial approval.
- 
Important qualification: This did not mean that every part of Malwa and Bundelkhand instantly came under an entirely unified Maratha civil administration. Formal grants, actual military control and regional revenue arrangements developed over time.
### Palkhed versus Bhopal

| Feature           | Palkhed, 1728                                     | Bhopal, 1737–1738                              |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Main opponent     | Nizam-ul-Mulk                                     | Nizam-ul-Mulk                                  |
| Main issue        | Deccan revenue rights and Shahu's authority       | Maratha expansion in Malwa                     |
| Baji Rao's method | Mobility and disruption of supplies               | Encirclement and supply pressure               |
| Treaty            | Mungi-Shevgaon, 1728                              | Doraha Sarai, 1738                             |
| Main consequence  | Recognition of Shahu's position and fiscal claims | Greater recognition of Maratha claims in Malwa |

UPSC trap: Baji Rao's Bhopal campaign belongs to 1737–1738, not the 1728 Battle of Palkhed.

## 14. Maratha victory over the Portuguese at Bassein/Vasai (1739)

![File:Main entrance to the Citadel at Vasai Fort.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/qiTYA7yeJHUANgHr_Bha6Hk4IlBpBSXubhSHDUDaYPc3EWt-UGsHaQPUFQiny3XknOPOU03F3T-U1Wgky6lLz3nTYKCKaIr8x35VWPONGkl8EzCGID6FKzwnCFQlTrq52_Rm9JghSCMQ3gSV52Sb5wXjN2LDa4jBhWG8Xiw9GW8?purpose=inline)

This event is especially important for understanding Maratha relations with European powers.

### What was Bassein?

Bassein, known today as Vasai, was an important Portuguese fortified settlement north of present-day Mumbai.

The Portuguese controlled parts of the northern Konkan, including valuable coastal territory.

### Why did the Marathas attack?

Relations between the Marathas and Portuguese had become tense over territory, coastal security, religious and political disputes, and commercial interests.

The Marathas wanted to weaken Portuguese control over the northern Konkan.

### Who led the campaign?

Chimaji Appa, the younger brother of Baji Rao I.

![Chimaji Appa: The Unsung Hero of Maratha History - History and politics](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/ClNiXdE3WURXN6Uqa3Q8IrdymrojMkZ62Q8jUgeTwKbEJyWaKc8WrNaJrehH4rrxiTEFBrj3Dki8ePkJyyzPzgdaJPTxSdkjILfeU6U0C0MVsCSME6mQEWIxPIXFGuQHR1QUXSzx_HPcgQzc9Lu4tEwuehq55TPwmvTtbWXfLWo?purpose=inline)

This distinction matters.

Baji Rao provided wider political and military leadership to the Maratha state, but the principal commander of the successful Vasai campaign was Chimaji Appa.
### What happened?

Maratha forces captured important Portuguese positions in the northern Konkan.

After a difficult siege, Vasai Fort surrendered in May 1739.
### Result

Portugal lost a major fortified centre and much of its northern coastal territory to the Marathas.
### Why was this significant?

- Strengthened Maratha influence over the western coast.
    
- Reduced Portuguese territorial power in the northern Konkan.
    
- Demonstrated that European fortified settlements could be defeated by regional Indian armies.
    
- Increased the political reputation of Chimaji Appa and the Peshwa establishment.

### Important distinction

The Portuguese did not lose Goa in 1739.

Goa remained under Portuguese rule for much longer.

UPSC Prelims: Vasai/Bassein — 1739 — Portuguese defeated — Chimaji Appa.

## 15. Nadir Shah's invasion of India (1739) — what was Baji Rao doing?

![Today in South Asian history: Nader Shah sacks Delhi (1739)](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/Rq6RtgFm-Ek4K0pLbUZIp5RAgTH8teKgWmptON2vo3cI4Ef22mOkpM6Bh3Ewpo7ucoGdmlBUPpJHORo__x5hHa3uRHx_X6Sga5iBXUVIo7VGpoNKMZ_7GEsgYtxOwxC-blqf8UhMLzspz-xJen8LWXXJu1UN84lKQXYtAcfYpTQ?purpose=inline)

While the Marathas were expanding, another major event shook northern India.

Nadir Shah, ruler of Iran, invaded the Mughal Empire.

### Why did he invade?

The invasion arose from Persian-Mughal frontier disputes, concerns about Afghan groups and the enormous financial attractions of Mughal India.

### Major events

|Date|Event|
|---|---|

|24 February 1739|Battle of Karnal; Mughal forces defeated|

### Why was this important for the Marathas?

It exposed the severe weakness of the Mughal Empire.

The emperor's inability to defend Delhi damaged imperial prestige.

However, Baji Rao did not personally defeat Nadir Shah or drive him out of India.

### Did Nadir Shah establish permanent Persian rule in Delhi?

No.

He plundered Delhi and withdrew, although his invasion had significant territorial and political consequences in the northwest.

### Broader historical significance

Nadir Shah's invasion accelerated the weakening of Mughal imperial authority.

Regional powers, including the Marathas, subsequently operated in an increasingly fragmented political environment.

## 16. The final campaign and death of Baji Rao I (1740)

### What happened in the final years?

Relations between the Peshwa and the Hyderabad establishment remained tense despite earlier settlements.

Baji Rao conducted operations against Nasir Jung, the son of Nizam-ul-Mulk, in 1739–1740.

Maratha pressure resulted in a settlement that included territorial concessions in the Narmada region.
### Where did Baji Rao die?

Baji Rao died on 28 April 1740 at Raverkhedi, on the banks of the Narmada River, in present-day Madhya Pradesh.

He was only 39 years old.
### What was the immediate consequence?

His son Balaji Baji Rao, also called Nanasaheb Peshwa, succeeded him in 1740.

The Maratha state had now developed influence across a much larger part of India than it had controlled in 1720.

That expansion brought new opportunities as well as serious administrative and political challenges.

### Did Baji Rao create a fully centralized Indian empire?

No.

His armies extended Maratha power across large territories, but effective administrative control remained uneven.

Some areas were under direct occupation, while others paid tribute or recognized particular revenue claims.

Powerful regional commanders accumulated their own military and fiscal resources.

## 21. UPSC Mains model answer

Question: “Baji Rao I transformed the Marathas from a regional Deccan power into a major force in Indian politics.” Examine. (250 words)

Baji Rao I, Peshwa under Chhatrapati Shahu from 1720 to 1740, played a major role in the territorial expansion and political transformation of Maratha power.

Military strategy: Baji Rao emphasized mobile cavalry warfare, strategic manoeuvre and disruption of enemy supplies. His victory over Nizam-ul-Mulk at Palkhed in 1728 demonstrated the effectiveness of these methods and strengthened Shahu's position in the Deccan.

Northern expansion: Baji Rao recognized opportunities created by the weakening Mughal Empire. Maratha forces expanded their influence into Malwa, Bundelkhand and Gujarat. His intervention in support of Chhatrasal in 1729 established important Maratha interests in Bundelkhand.

Challenge to Mughal authority: Baji Rao's rapid advance towards Delhi in 1737 revealed the vulnerability of the imperial capital. His subsequent campaign against the Nizam at Bhopal led to the Treaty of Doraha Sarai in 1738, strengthening Maratha claims in Malwa.

Growth of regional houses: Commanders such as Malhar Rao Holkar and Ranoji Shinde acquired increasing territorial and military influence, laying foundations for the later confederacy.

Consolidation of Peshwa authority: His victory over Trimbak Rao Dabhade at Dabhoi in 1731 strengthened the political position of the Peshwa among Maratha chiefs.

However, military expansion outpaced centralized administrative integration, and regional commanders retained substantial autonomy.

Thus, Baji Rao's reign established the foundations of Maratha predominance across much of India while also encouraging the decentralized political structure that later characterized the confederacy.
