**Period:** 1775–1782
**Main cause:** Disputed succession to the Peshwaship
**British Governor-General of Bengal:** Warren Hastings
**Principal Maratha leaders:** Nana Fadnavis, Mahadji Scindia and allied commanders
**Major Maratha victory:** Wadgaon, January 1779
**Ending treaty:** Salbai, 17 May 1782

Central question: How did a dispute within the Peshwa family become a war with the British East India Company, why did the British suffer a major defeat at Wadgaon, and why did the conflict end through negotiation rather than complete conquest?

This chapter is important for understanding the British policy of exploiting political divisions among Indian powers—and also the ability of the eighteenth-century Marathas to resist the Company's expansion.

## 1. Where did Chapter 12 end?

In Chapter 12, we studied the Maratha succession crisis after Madhavrao I's death.

Remember the sequence:

- 1772: Madhavrao I died; his younger brother Narayanrao became Peshwa.
    
- 1773: Narayanrao was murdered.
    
- 1773: His uncle Raghunathrao claimed the Peshwaship.
    
- 1774: Narayanrao's posthumous son, Madhavrao II, was recognized as Peshwa by the Barbhai Council.
    
- 1775: Raghunathrao approached the British at Bombay.

On 6 March 1775, Raghunathrao and the Bombay government concluded the Treaty of Surat.

He promised territorial and financial concessions in exchange for British troops.

But the Pune government did not recognize Raghunathrao's authority to make such an agreement.

This created two opposing political positions.

Raghunathrao's side

Claimed the Peshwaship

Supported by Bombay's British authorities

Pune Regency

Recognized the infant Madhavrao II

Led by Nana Fadnavis and allied chiefs

The war began because British troops intervened in this dispute in support of Raghunathrao.

## 2. Political situation in India in 1775

The British Company had already established substantial power in eastern India.

After Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) and the grant of the Diwani (1765), it controlled major financial resources in Bengal, Bihar and Odisha.

But western and central India remained politically contested.

| Pune Maratha government      | Regency of Madhavrao II             | Protect legitimate succession and Maratha territories |
| Raghunathrao                 | Rival Peshwa claimant               | Regain political control                              |
| British Bombay Presidency    | Company's western government        | Expand around Bombay and support Raghunathrao         |
| British Bengal government    | Warren Hastings and Supreme Council | Coordinate Company strategy and limit costly conflict |

### Why were Salsette and Bassein important to Bombay?

Salsette, near Bombay, offered strategically valuable land and routes near the Company's principal western settlement.

Bassein (Vasai) was a fortified coastal centre.

The British wanted stronger security, territorial depth and commercial influence around Bombay.

Raghunathrao's political desperation offered them an opportunity to gain those advantages.

## 3. Treaty of Surat (1775) — the immediate cause of war

### Who signed it?

Raghunathrao and the Bombay government of the East India Company.

### Main provisions

| Provision           | Meaning                                                            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| British troops      | Approximately 2,500 soldiers were promised to support Raghunathrao |
| Salsette            | Company claims to the island were to be recognized                 |
| Bassein             | Promised as an important territorial concession                    |
| Gujarat             | Revenue and territorial concessions were included                  |
| Financial payments  | Raghunathrao undertook to pay towards the Company's troops         |
| Political objective | Restoration of Raghunathrao as Peshwa                              |
### Why was the treaty controversial?

Raghunathrao claimed that he was the legitimate Peshwa.

But the Pune Regency recognized Madhavrao II.

Therefore, the regency regarded his proposed territorial concessions as unauthorized.
### Why did the treaty lead to war?

The Bombay authorities began supporting Raghunathrao militarily.

This transformed the Maratha succession dispute into a confrontation with the Company.

UPSC fact: The Treaty of Surat helped begin the First Anglo-Maratha War; it did not end it.
## 4. First military operations (1775)

British forces under Colonel Keating moved in support of Raghunathrao.

The Pune Regency organized troops to stop them, including forces associated with **Haripant Phadke**.

### What happened?

The forces fought in Gujarat, including an engagement around Adas in May 1775.

Neither side achieved a rapid political settlement through these early operations.

The fighting exposed the likelihood of a prolonged and expensive conflict.
### Why was this a problem for the British?

The Company's Bombay establishment did not possess unlimited money, men or supplies.

A major war against the Maratha military network could impose considerable costs.

There was also an internal constitutional problem within the Company.

## 5. Bombay versus Calcutta — why did the British disagree?

This is an important connection between British constitutional history and the Anglo-Maratha Wars.

The Regulating Act of 1773 had established greater supervisory authority at Bengal over the Company's other presidencies in important political matters.

Warren Hastings, Governor-General of Bengal, and his council were therefore concerned about Bombay's independent diplomacy.
### The disagreement

Bombay's argument: Supporting Raghunathrao could secure valuable territory and increase the Company's political influence.

Bengal's concern: The Treaty of Surat risked dragging the Company into an expensive war without sufficient central authorization or a reliable prospect of victory.

The Supreme Council therefore sent Colonel John Upton to negotiate with the Pune Regency.

### Why did this matter?

The British Company was not a unified state bureaucracy acting with perfect coordination.

Officials at Bombay and Calcutta disagreed about the best approach to the Marathas.

This led to the second important treaty of the war.

## 6. Treaty of Purandar (1 March 1776)

### Who negotiated the treaty?

Colonel John Upton, representing the Bengal government, negotiated with ministers of the Pune Regency.

### What were the principal terms?

| Provision                              | Meaning                                                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treaty of Surat                        | To be superseded(સ્થાનાંતરિત)                           |
| Madhavrao II                           | The infant's Peshwaship was recognized                  |
| Raghunathrao                           | British support for his claim was to cease              |
| Pension                                | Raghunathrao was to receive a financial allowance       |
| Salsette                               | British possession was accepted                         |
| Other territorial and financial issues | Arrangements were negotiated to settle earlier disputes |

### Why was this important?

The Bengal government effectively accepted the Pune Regency as the legitimate authority.

It also attempted to remove the immediate cause of military conflict.

### Did the war end permanently in 1776?

No.

The agreement proved difficult to enforce.

Raghunathrao was unwilling to abandon his ambitions, and British officials continued to disagree about policy.

The territorial concessions and the handling of Raghunathrao remained controversial.

## 7. Why did fighting resume after Purandar?

The Treaty of Purandar had not resolved the distrust between the two sides.

### Three problems remained

1. Raghunathrao's ambitions

Raghunathrao still hoped to become Peshwa. His continued presence created an opportunity for renewed British intervention.

2. Conflict within the Company

The Bombay government remained dissatisfied with a settlement that limited its ambitions.

3. French connections

In 1777, the Pune government established contacts with the French, whose rivalry with Britain was intensifying.

The arrival of French representatives alarmed British officials, particularly because Britain and France were entering a wider international conflict connected with the American War of Independence.

### Why were French relations important?

The French and British companies had previously fought for influence in India.

The British feared that French military expertise and support might strengthen the Marathas.

However, this does not mean the Marathas had already become a French-controlled state.

Nana Fadnavis sought diplomatic options that could strengthen the Pune government's bargaining position.

### What did the British decide?

The Bombay authorities renewed their attempt to place Raghunathrao in power.

A British expedition marched towards Pune in late 1778.

Its progress led to one of the most important defeats suffered by the Company during the eighteenth century.

## 8. Battle of Talegaon–Wadgaon (January 1779)

![Maharashtra Board Class 12 History Textbook - IndCareer Docs](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/rQAqSw-PBTCm3rYdp5k1MxvAfk7aei7Hhv6_d6n9COwtiwb4JSWkYxyy7up_oeYDCZ54808r05om4MJ7ngDsJWrOk52WYI0L53YdqvkuSUKiryJN76KcDf4U9kTBXDnYH_LvLZyiK16j3d4qzdgDxkyq3Oe3ybr_4_FK-nKvPhM?purpose=inline)
### Where was Wadgaon?

Wadgaon Maval lies northwest of Pune, near Talegaon, on the approaches through the Western Ghats from Bombay.

The area was strategically important because an army travelling from the Konkan coast to Pune had to move through mountain passes and valleys.

### Why did the British march towards Pune?

The Bombay government hoped to defeat the Barbhai Regency and install Raghunathrao as Peshwa.

Its expedition included British and Indian Company soldiers, artillery and a large transport establishment.

### Why was this a difficult campaign?

The army had to cross the Western Ghats while maintaining food, ammunition, animals and transport supplies.

A large force burdened with artillery and baggage could be vulnerable if its communication routes were cut.

### What did the Marathas do?

Maratha forces, with Mahadji Scindia and other commanders playing leading roles, avoided giving the British an easy decisive battle.

Instead, they exploited the terrain and the expedition's supply difficulties.

1. British advance: Company forces moved from Bombay towards Pune across the Western Ghats.
    
2. Maratha harassment: Cavalry attacked vulnerable units and communications.
    
3. Supply difficulties: The British struggled to maintain their transport and provisions.
    
4. Retreat from Talegaon: The British attempted to withdraw towards the coast.
    
5. Encirclement near Wadgaon: Maratha forces trapped the retreating expedition.
    
6. British capitulation: The expedition agreed to humiliating terms in January 1779.

### Why was this such a significant Maratha victory?

The British did not merely lose a few soldiers in a skirmish.

An entire expedition was placed in a position where it could not continue effectively and had to negotiate its withdrawal.

### Compare Wadgaon with Palkhed

|Palkhed (1728)|Wadgaon (1779)|
|---|---|
|Baji Rao I versus the Nizam|Maratha forces versus British Bombay expedition|
|Mobile operations disrupted the enemy|Maratha cavalry harassed British communications|
|Enemy supply problems became decisive|British food and transport problems became decisive|
|Nizam negotiated|British expedition capitulated|

The similarity is that both campaigns demonstrated the importance of terrain, mobility and logistics.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer provides a detailed account of the British advance and its defeat.
## 9. Convention of Wadgaon (January 1779)

Following their defeat, British officers were compelled to accept the Convention of Wadgaon, commonly dated 16 January 1779.

### What did the Marathas demand?

The terms included:

- British withdrawal from the offensive against the Pune government.
    
- Surrender of territorial gains made through the recent intervention.
    
- Abandonment of Raghunathrao's claim.
    
- Guarantees to secure the settlement.

### Did Wadgaon end the First Anglo-Maratha War?

No.

This is the single most important examination distinction.

The local British expedition had accepted defeat.

But the Bengal government under Warren Hastings rejected the convention.

Hastings refused to treat the agreement as binding on the Company's wider government and continued military operations.

### Why did Hastings reject it?

The Company feared that accepting such a defeat would damage its political prestige and weaken its strategic position.

Hastings sought to recover the initiative by opening campaigns in other regions.

Remember:

Wadgaon = major Maratha battlefield success.

Salbai = final political settlement ending the war.

## 10. Why did the British shift the war to Gujarat and Malwa?

![Gwalior Fort, Gwalior Qila, Madhya Pradesh, India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/GmkhrYezghUFVTtR_BbcRKx3VmeAXWMl4ouzNSg1Chj_eBL8JZBpcExa5sdNb2YIgJmlyMd3lAcZ9XJRCRTqC7VoRHEnXo4TTEyqeNnF1o_HyLBaBf2XVG2JLn_OXkYHppY_qPMWWbRfqNGtwffuS1CK1_B5Hn3vWnqrgvG6CwVEyevR-Hf8SbJjZ0zQEjjr?purpose=inline)

After Wadgaon, the British realized that a direct advance from Bombay to Pune could be extremely difficult.

Warren Hastings therefore pursued campaigns on several fronts.

### The strategic idea

Instead of attacking Pune along the same route again, Company forces could strike Maratha interests elsewhere.

This might compel important chiefs to withdraw troops from western Maharashtra and force the Pune government to negotiate.

### Why Gujarat?

Gujarat contained important commercial centres, ports and territories associated with Maratha chiefs such as the Gaekwads.

It was accessible from the Company's western bases.

### Why Malwa and Gwalior?

Malwa was associated with Mahadji Scindia's expanding authority.

If British forces threatened his northern territories, he might have to divert troops away from the struggle around Pune.

The British were now confronting the wider Maratha political system, not just the Peshwa's forces near the capital.

## 11. General Goddard's campaigns (1779–1781)

### Who was Goddard?

Thomas Goddard was a Company military commander entrusted with major operations in western India.

He conducted campaigns in Gujarat and later against Maratha territory nearer Pune.

### Major events

| Year          | Event                                                     | Significance                                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| 1779          | Company forces expanded operations into Gujarat           | Attempt to recover initiative after Wadgaon          |
| February 1780 | Ahmedabad captured                                        | Major British success in Gujarat                     |
| December 1780 | Bassein captured<br>(vasai Palghar district, Maharashtra) | Important British coastal victory                    |
| 1781          | Goddard attempted another advance towards Pune            | Renewed pressure on the Peshwa government            |
| 1781          | Goddard withdrew after Maratha attacks on communications  | Failure to force the Pune government into submission |

### Why was the capture of Ahmedabad important?

Ahmedabad was a major urban and commercial centre of Gujarat.

Its loss damaged Maratha interests in the region.

It also showed that the British could achieve substantial victories outside the mountainous approaches to Pune.

### Why did Bassein matter?

Bassein had been one of the territories sought by the Company since the Treaty of Surat.

Its capture strengthened Britain's position on the western coast.

### Why did the advance towards Pune fail in 1781?

Maratha forces again threatened British supplies and communications through the Western Ghats.

Goddard withdrew without securing the political submission of the Pune government.

### Historical lesson

Neither side could assume that success in one region would produce victory across the whole Maratha political system.

The Marathas could defeat a British expedition near Pune, while the British could seize important fortresses elsewhere.

## 12. Capture of Gwalior Fort (3 August 1780)

![Gwalior fort Madhya Pradesh India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/f00oRX0chibY7VOr-uULPkf8agL8XqDr2Iz3WH1OdLHAxznEEI0zsYCWsPy1OHV2PYRBbVEzkeMSGvm-5RsSx98Q-YjwfwdRvZutUM0ZAfarcfl51jW_40onsin3GMyUSLaLmXq37qNdSTWNXoq1e1dKyCSQ9KhmHDaBNRwWzJGHX70610e6nosy_AnYtzYm?purpose=inline)

### Why was Gwalior important?

Gwalior was one of the strongest fortresses in central India.

Its location gave it strategic value over routes connecting northern and central India.

It was associated with the territorial interests of Mahadji Scindia.

### Who captured it?

British forces under Major William Popham captured the fort on 3 August 1780.

The operation involved a surprise assault on its defences.

### Why was this a shock?

Gwalior's natural and constructed defences made it appear difficult to capture.

The loss damaged Scindia's military prestige and demonstrated that even formidable hill forts could be vulnerable to surprise operations.

### Did the British retain Gwalior permanently?

No.

The fort changed hands again in subsequent years.

Maratha forces under Scindia's leadership recovered it in 1783.

### UPSC significance

The capture of Gwalior illustrates the broader geographical scale of the war.

Fighting took place not merely around Pune and Bombay, but also in Gujarat and central India.

## 13. Mahadji Scindia versus British forces in Malwa (1780–1781)

Mahadji Scindia (Shinde)

An influential Maratha military and political leader.

Controlled important interests in Malwa and northern India.

Played a leading role in the resistance to the British and later in peace negotiations.

### Why did Scindia become so important?

Mahadji possessed extensive military and political connections in northern and central India.

The Pune government needed his cooperation, particularly after the British opened campaigns against his territories.

### British campaign under Camac

Company forces under Colonel William Camac entered Malwa to challenge Scindia.

Maratha forces responded through cavalry attacks, supply disruption and manoeuvring.

### What happened in March 1781?

Camac's forces achieved a significant success through a surprise night attack on Scindia's camp in March 1781, capturing artillery and other material.

But the British did not destroy Scindia's military power.

Fighting continued, and both sides faced difficulties sustaining prolonged operations.

### Why did Scindia become a peace negotiator?

He possessed enough military power to resist the British, but he also had reasons to seek a settlement.

Continuing war endangered his northern territories and diverted resources from his wider ambitions.

The Company, meanwhile, needed an influential Maratha leader capable of negotiating with Pune.

Scindia therefore became increasingly central to the diplomatic resolution of the conflict.

## 14. Nana Fadnavis and diplomacy during the war

![महाराष्ट्र के चाणक्य कहे जाने वाले फडणवीस जिनकी राजनीति और कूटनीति ने मराठा साम्राज्य को बचाया | Maratha's Chanakya Nana Fadnavis, Strategist Who Saved the Maratha Empire](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/NvOho_Y_ZtFKGkfDjC7Yut1fASEif66SfAb_5FiV6yD0hAs3Z_45X_Z1hph34VmgJ-SYI6kjLVtFIb4ChfgSd69Yb3BePU9Ja1dklj82YPlTAcdmuGVEd9R40zPR5bF-n4LCEhRz1X8oRrDqgfZcifwtC2FYME10fPc3GkQsrsY?purpose=inline)

### What was Nana Fadnavis doing during the war?

Nana Fadnavis was a principal political organizer of the regency government at Pune.

He coordinated diplomacy, revenue and relationships among Maratha chiefs.

His challenge was different from that of an army commander.

He needed to maintain a political coalition while facing threats in several regions.

### What was his strategy?

He sought to:

1. Preserve Madhavrao II's position as legitimate Peshwa.
    
2. Prevent British domination of Pune.
    
3. Coordinate important Maratha chiefs.
    
4. Explore cooperation with powers also threatened by the Company.
    
5. Avoid granting the British a permanent military presence in the Peshwa's government.
### Why was Mysore relevant?

Haidar Ali had been fighting the British in southern India.

He represented another powerful Indian ruler opposed to Company expansion.

Nana considered wider diplomatic arrangements involving Mysore and Hyderabad.

However, the political interests of these states were not identical, and their cooperation was never entirely simple.

## 15. The Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784) — why it influenced the Maratha war

![Hyder Ali (1722-1782) was an Indian ruler and military strategist who played a significant role in the history of South India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/Yrj9EjziB5dddBNzyLEHOmxSxprWYA_8OOOpxkI23sh56bniD8GTL0rJ9fDCzkWcRUkhFu0h2RKcfuhkPPviPs3bSL6qDXyPhFT23NuhkfI0EWYF-1jNVnqP0aBBNuj2anyEZP9_4-uRGCecGo6JvamRmRP1G5-DqFTxi9s4sbGC8QBFCIXI_eMKjG2mQGrf?purpose=inline)

While Britain was fighting the Marathas, it was also involved in another major war.

The Second Anglo-Mysore War began in 1780.

Haidar Ali's forces inflicted a serious defeat on Company troops at Pollilur in September 1780.

Mysore threatened British interests in the Carnatic and around Madras.

### Why was this important to Warren Hastings?

The Company could not concentrate all its military resources against the Marathas while also facing a major crisis in southern India.

Britain was additionally fighting France in a broader international conflict.
### What did the Company need?

It needed to reduce the number of enemies it faced simultaneously.

A peace with the Marathas would allow it to devote greater attention to Mysore.

### What did the Marathas need?

The Maratha government wanted:

- Recognition of Madhavrao II.
    
- An end to British support for Raghunathrao.
    
- Return of important territories.
    
- Security from further British intervention.
    

Thus, both parties had reasons to negotiate.

UPSC analytical point: The Treaty of Salbai cannot be understood solely through the battles of the First Anglo-Maratha War. British difficulties against Mysore and France also shaped the decision to seek peace.

## 16. Why did the war end in a negotiated settlement?

By 1781–1782, neither side had achieved all its objectives.

### What had the Marathas achieved?

- Defeated the British expedition at Wadgaon.
    
- Preserved the authority of Madhavrao II's regency.
    
- Prevented Raghunathrao from being installed through British force.
    
- Continued resisting British military campaigns across several regions.

### What had the British achieved?

- Retained important interests around Bombay.
    
- Captured Bassein and Ahmedabad during the war.
    
- Captured Gwalior temporarily.
    
- Demonstrated the ability to campaign far into Maratha territory.

### What prevented a decisive conclusion?

Both sides were powerful enough to impose serious costs on the other, but neither could easily eliminate the other's military and political position.

The war was expensive, geographically extensive and strategically complicated.

A negotiated peace became preferable to indefinite warfare.

Mahadji Scindia played a central role in arranging the settlement.

## 17. Treaty of Salbai — 17 May 1782

![Life story of Mahadji Scindia (Shinde)](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/jLTU9qPs9Z62J34fpR5Ds1lmHkyWzNGotcMVtoOTW2ZdyjpTeVJXrnwjJJ2VmMX6vHcYF_HvF5KHwAdJOzgD5J6KXOAI0sblIIAoJEm7Gc-YdrJpbLn6kVxB0F4a03DFAT0iVUOCw6tFLd_nwTXL_EZBTfeYSbVIThRE8JtQTrk?purpose=inline)

This is the most important treaty of the First Anglo-Maratha War.

### Who negotiated it?

The settlement was negotiated principally through Mahadji Scindia, acting as an intermediary between the Pune government and the British.

It was concluded on 17 May 1782 and formally ratified(મંજૂર કરેલું) in February 1783.

### Main provisions

| Provision               | Meaning                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Madhavrao II recognized | British accepted the infant Peshwa's legitimate position                                        |
| Raghunathrao abandoned  | British agreed to cease supporting his claim                                                    |
| Raghunathrao's pension  | Provision was made for his maintenance                                                          |
| Salsette                | Remained under British control                                                                  |
| Bassein                 | Restored to the Marathas                                                                        |
| Other conquests         | Important British acquisitions made after Purandar were to be restored, subject to treaty terms |
| Gujarat                 | Arrangements protected specified Gaekwad interests                                              |
| European relations      | Restrictions applied to Maratha support for rival European powers                               |
| Mysore                  | Provisions sought restoration of territories taken from the British and their allies            |
| Mahadji Scindia         | Acted as guarantor of the settlement                                                            |

The treaty is recorded in the Maharashtra State Gazetteer and in contemporary treaty collections.q
### Did Salbai give the British complete control of the Maratha Empire?

No.

The Peshwa's government remained independent, and the British abandoned their attempt to install Raghunathrao.

### Did the British lose everything?

No.

They retained Salsette and important commercial advantages.

### Who won the war?

The Marathas won the decisive Wadgaon campaign, and the final treaty was a diplomatic success for the Pune Regency because the British accepted Madhavrao II and withdrew support from Raghunathrao.

However, the war as a whole ended in a negotiated compromise, not the unconditional defeat of one side.

## 18. Why is the Treaty of Salbai a turning point?

### For the Marathas

It helped secure the political position of the Peshwa's regency and reduced immediate British interference.

It also increased Mahadji Scindia's diplomatic importance.

### For the British

It stabilized relations with a major Indian power and allowed the Company to concentrate more resources on Mysore.

### For Indian politics

It produced a period of relative peace between the Company and the principal Maratha government, lasting approximately twenty years until the crisis of 1802–1803.

But the treaty did not permanently resolve all tensions.

Historical significance: Salbai demonstrated that the Company still had to negotiate with powerful Indian states rather than simply impose its authority through military superiority.

## 22. UPSC Mains — model answer

Question: The First Anglo-Maratha War demonstrated both the military strength of the Marathas and the growing interventionist policy of the British East India Company. Discuss. (250 words)

The First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782) originated in a succession dispute following the murder of Peshwa Narayanrao. Raghunathrao sought British assistance against the Barbhai Council, which supported the infant Madhavrao II.

British intervention: Through the Treaty of Surat in 1775, the Bombay government promised military assistance to Raghunathrao in exchange for territorial and financial concessions. This demonstrated the Company's willingness to exploit internal disputes to expand its influence.

Diplomatic complications: The Bengal government questioned Bombay's policy and negotiated the Treaty of Purandar in 1776. However, disagreements over Raghunathrao and territorial claims caused renewed hostilities.

Maratha military success: In January 1779, Maratha forces exploited the Western Ghats and disrupted British supplies, forcing the Company's expedition to capitulate at Wadgaon. This demonstrated the continued effectiveness of Maratha mobile warfare.

British counteroffensive: Warren Hastings rejected the convention and opened campaigns in Gujarat and Malwa. The British captured Ahmedabad, Bassein and Gwalior, but failed to secure the submission of the Pune Regency.

Final settlement: The Treaty of Salbai in 1782 recognized Madhavrao II, ended British support for Raghunathrao and restored important territories, while allowing the Company to retain Salsette.

Thus, the conflict ended through compromise rather than decisive conquest. It demonstrated that the Marathas remained a major military power, while the Company increasingly pursued territorial and political ambitions through warfare and diplomacy.

## 23. Rapid revision — Chapter 13 in one minute

1. 1775 — Surat: Raghunathrao obtained British military support.
    
2. 1776 — Purandar: British Bengal government negotiated with Nana Fadnavis's regency.
    
3. 1777–1778 — Renewed tension: French contacts and the succession dispute complicated peace.
    
4. 1779 — Wadgaon: Marathas forced a British expedition to capitulate.
    
5. 1779 — Hastings rejected Wadgaon: The war continued.
    
6. 1780 — Ahmedabad and Gwalior: British achieved important regional victories.
    
7. 1780 — Bassein: British captured the strategic coastal fort.
    
8. 1781 — Continued fighting: British failed to force Pune's submission.
    
9. 1782 — Salbai: British accepted Madhavrao II and abandoned Raghunathrao.
    
10. After Salbai: A period of relative peace followed, while the Company continued competing with Mysore.
