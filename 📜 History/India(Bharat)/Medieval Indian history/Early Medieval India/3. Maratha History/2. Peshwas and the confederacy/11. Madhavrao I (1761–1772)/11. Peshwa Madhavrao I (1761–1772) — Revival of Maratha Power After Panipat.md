**Full name:** Madhavrao Ballal Bhat
**Known as:** Madhavrao I / Thorale Madhavrao
**Born: 1745**
**Father:** Balaji Baji Rao (Nanasaheb)
**Mother:** Gopikabai
**Peshwa:** 1761–1772
**Death:** 18 November 1772, Theur, near Pune
**Successor:** Narayanrao

Central question: The Marathas suffered a catastrophic defeat against Ahmad Shah Abdali at Panipat in 1761. How did the young Madhavrao restore the military strength, finances and political influence of the Maratha state within just eleven years?

This chapter explains an important distinction: the Third Battle of Panipat weakened Maratha power, but did not destroy it.

We will study Madhavrao's reign chronologically, including his conflict with his uncle Raghunathrao, the Nizam's invasion, the Mysore campaigns and the revival of Maratha influence in Delhi.

## 1. India in 1761 — what happened after Panipat?

![Makar Sankranti: Marathas’ Loss at Panipat Resonates Even Today](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/g0I-KGpFlNLW4fbdKW01EAN8m96dwg7BFZGXWVy4Fnax81fN70i6Oj0uS2k_k1441sJ5OcWiL-x2yCQoTMsycNa-9pFUTQ2oA6USZvyakjC6gzcmI8K3Qzjt1ge9o7SAOOnvUI2rHB3-HFf3NXqQSMX_4lkv1NyxnmFMqdrbvKI?purpose=inline)

In Chapter 10, we studied the Third Battle of Panipat, fought on 14 January 1761.

The Maratha army commanded by Sadashivrao Bhau suffered a devastating defeat against Ahmad Shah Abdali's coalition.

Important Maratha leaders, including Vishwasrao and Sadashivrao Bhau, were killed.

Nanasaheb Peshwa died on 23 June 1761.
### What was the situation now?

|Problem|Situation after Panipat|Why it was dangerous|
|---|---|---|
|Military losses|Large numbers of experienced soldiers and commanders had been killed|Difficult to rebuild armies quickly|
|Financial crisis|Campaign expenditure and losses strained the treasury|Soldiers and officials needed payment|

### Did Ahmad Shah Abdali conquer Maharashtra?

No.

Abdali defeated the Maratha army at Panipat, but he did not occupy Pune, Satara or the entire Maratha kingdom.

The Marathas still possessed their core administrative institutions, forts, cavalry networks and regional chiefs.

This is why recovery remained possible.
## 2. Madhavrao becomes Peshwa (1761)

![File:Shaniwar Wada Main Gate 01.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/q5Iedyt0Ba7jCiFnUFtJ3KKEOhJTO5-zzJNW8owLXCJLDAsb6hY2zN6ucJwhxVMSWf_CT83nijlKE0on_64E7y2tOc0NnMP3qbHerqQGaqL6RiydqSqHKQXmSfg2W3Tvcc4PbgWzBsT6rMbSh-EWkaEsUK8aI1B4X4tquA-ML2s?purpose=inline)

After Nanasaheb's death, his second son Madhavrao I became Peshwa in July 1761.

He was only about 16 years old.
### Why was his age important?

Madhavrao had inherited a powerful office, but he lacked the experience and established personal following of his father and grandfather.

His uncle Raghunathrao, an experienced military commander, expected an important role in government.
### Who was Raghunathrao?

![Index - Folio Bootstrap Template](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8EkPVgPWCNIA5ubu-xTqol8BmzHTCm5wzkKaj8dUxKMyAzafeaAsFG9FGkvekaRga0SkVSF4nKX1I92S0Eq1p5ID55VwMly6vKN1CUKnVB0aEtR4ehFEo4xoM-t37k0G0buPouR4E-T9jbD_r84qWCReLaceP2sr7dVOl7pppF0?purpose=inline)

Raghunathrao (Raghoba)
Younger brother of Nanasaheb.
Uncle of Madhavrao.

Had commanded major Maratha campaigns in northern India, including the Punjab expedition of 1758.

Initially exercised substantial influence in the government.
### Why did conflict develop?

Both men had competing political positions.

Madhavrao's perspective: He was the legally appointed Peshwa and wanted to exercise effective control over government.

Raghunathrao's perspective: He had considerable military experience and believed he deserved a dominant role in administration and military decisions.

Different ministers and military chiefs supported different factions.
### Role of Gopikabai

Madhavrao's mother, Gopikabai, was an influential member of the Peshwa household.

She became involved in the family's political struggles and sought to protect her sons' interests.

The dispute was therefore both a family conflict and a contest over control of the Maratha government.
## 3. First major challenge — conflict with the Nizam (1761–1762)

Remember Chapter 10.

In February 1760, the Marathas had defeated Hyderabad's forces at the Battle of Udgir.

The Nizam's government had made important territorial concessions.

But after Panipat, the situation changed.
### Why did Nizam Ali Khan attack?

![Lot 204 - A STANDING PORTRAIT OF NIZAM ALI KHAN](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/Oc-GtCa2edF-Wm947LJwNYa9ybJru2BsL81zfSdcHCSy-FF9oxq9TC5PDCdx4rmj9AqOZ9YktAJnf2_AKvcb1JceAUMQluXJ-tBURf4TQvi3r_0An-fRw-2E8Z5z6UvlJM8skyYTDH86L9oQqQnQUN8yn8QcBFumslMOpJ-GPZQ?purpose=inline)

The Hyderabad establishment wanted to recover its lost territories and reduce Maratha influence in the Deccan.

The defeat at Panipat made the Marathas appear vulnerable.

Nizam Ali Khan therefore increased military pressure on the Peshwa's government.
### What did the Marathas do?

Madhavrao and Raghunathrao initially cooperated in resisting the Nizam.

Their forces used mobile operations and diplomatic negotiations to confront Hyderabad.

However, disagreements arose over military decisions and the terms of peace.

### Why did this matter?

Although the immediate conflict was contained, it exposed serious differences between Madhavrao and his uncle.

Raghunathrao's willingness to negotiate arrangements independently of Madhavrao deepened the political distrust.

UPSC significance: External threats and internal rivalry reinforced one another. The Nizam could exploit divisions among Maratha leaders, while those divisions made a coordinated response more difficult.
## 4. Madhavrao versus Raghunathrao — Battle of Alegaon (1762)

![15 Acre Agricultural/Farm Land for Sale in Shirur, Pune (REI1242563)](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/HLgChWSn5sUpMZHJ4mK846CY1DFad-3MkD2W9coes75aMd70Al4S1EkIgiiCY7syAJdfj1tUYiqx9GWyZXc5qADSK1GnfYzSzkRY_OoblJp1_UOLqUx0tVe_f-c1p_UltjK0KVKgOzWsBSkBiNhaYScxd_lFCvNAJVbqryuQkEA?purpose=inline)

This is an important event that many brief history notes skip.
### Why did the uncle and nephew fight?

Raghunathrao wanted greater control over the government.

Madhavrao resisted becoming a ceremonial Peshwa while his uncle exercised actual authority.

The rivalry intensified during 1762.

### What did Raghunathrao do?

Raghunathrao formed an understanding with Nizam Ali Khan, a rival of the Maratha state.

He then opposed Madhavrao militarily.
### What happened at Alegaon?

In November 1762, forces associated with Raghunathrao and the Nizam confronted Madhavrao.

Madhavrao was compelled(compelled) to submit to his uncle.

The commonly cited date is 12 November 1762.
### Was Madhavrao removed permanently?

No.

He remained Peshwa, but Raghunathrao temporarily gained substantial influence over government
### Why was this episode dangerous?

An external rival was now participating in an internal Maratha conflict.

The Nizam could use the division to recover territory and weaken Pune.

Historical lesson: Even after Panipat, the most immediate danger to Maratha recovery was not just the loss of soldiers. It was the possibility that political rivalry would prevent coordinated leadership.
## 5. Nizam Ali Khan invades Maratha territory (1763)

The temporary ascendancy of Raghunathrao did not solve relations with the Nizam.

Nizam Ali Khan continued pursuing his own territorial ambitions.
### What did the Nizam want?

- Recovery of lands lost after Udgir.
    
- Reduced Maratha influence in the Deccan.
    
- Increased control over important revenue-producing territory.
    
- A stronger position relative to the Peshwa's government.
### The invasion

The Nizam's forces moved into Maratha territory and devastated important settlements.

They threatened the Pune region in 1763.

This created a new danger.

The Maratha leadership now recognized that internal rivalry had allowed Hyderabad to become a serious threat again.
### Madhavrao and Raghunathrao cooperate

Despite their previous conflict, the uncle and nephew joined forces against the Nizam.

Maratha commanders used mobile operations to pressure Hyderabad's troops and their supply arrangements.

The confrontation culminated in the Battle of Rakshasbhuvan.

### Where was Rakshasbhuvan?

Rakshasbhuvan lies in the Godavari region of present-day Maharashtra, in the wider Paithan–Georai area.

### Who fought?

|Maratha side|Hyderabad side|
|---|---|
|Peshwa Madhavrao I|Nizam Ali Khan|
|Raghunathrao|Vitthal Sundar and other Nizam commanders|
|Maratha cavalry and allied chiefs|Hyderabad's infantry, cavalry and artillery|
### What happened?

On 10 August 1763, the Marathas confronted the Nizam's forces near the Godavari.

The fighting involved significant cavalry operations and attacks on the Hyderabad army.

Vitthal Sundar, a senior commander and influential minister associated with Nizam Ali, was killed.

The Hyderabad army suffered a major defeat.

### Why did Madhavrao win?

Three factors were especially important:

1. Temporary unity: Madhavrao and Raghunathrao cooperated against the Nizam.
    
2. Mobile operations: Maratha forces used their cavalry and manoeuvring capabilities effectively.
    
3. Disruption of enemy command: The death of Vitthal Sundar contributed to the collapse of Hyderabad's position.,
### Result

The Nizam was forced into a settlement involving substantial territorial concessions.

The victory restored much of the military prestige that had been damaged after Panipat.

### Why is Rakshasbhuvan important?

1761 — Panipat

Catastrophic defeat and loss of prestige

1763 — Rakshasbhuvan

Major victory and restoration of confidence

Rakshasbhuvan did not reverse every consequence of Panipat, but it marked a decisive stage in rebuilding Maratha power in the Deccan.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer describes Rakshasbhuvan as a major turning point in the relationship between Madhavrao and the Nizam.
## 7. Madhavrao's next challenge — Haidar Ali of Mysore

![आखिर कौन थे मैसूर के शासक टीपू सुल्तान के पिता? जानें रोचक तथ्य  | know about hyder ali father of tipu sultan | Herzindagi](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/WB0XHYm6uGNW_JP52ucvvJFXnJchM0O-f6pxNzGUhWQm4HNOMPJr8oqS8qj8QRTve87ZxCb-HoqAKspL3rOgEQK-urT0YltL8pX8AEwC5sSVcXDOGtdm9JHimds4ruw2EADk1ToCA2CaZJttz8wKO448e5i57KLAnBcz2kty-Qk?purpose=inline)
### Who was Haidar Ali?

Haidar Ali rose through the military establishment of the Kingdom of Mysore and became its effective ruler during the early 1760s.

Although the Wodeyar dynasty retained nominal royal status, Haidar exercised dominant political and military power.
### Why was Mysore expanding?

Mysore possessed a significant military establishment and sought control over territories in Karnataka and adjoining regions.

Haidar Ali took advantage of the weakening of neighbouring powers and the political instability created by the Carnatic conflicts.
### Why did this threaten the Marathas?

The Marathas claimed revenue rights and political influence over parts of northern and central Karnataka.

Some local rulers there were tributaries or allies of the Peshwa.

Haidar's expansion threatened these arrangements
### What did Madhavrao want?

He sought to:

- Protect Maratha revenue interests.
    
- Recover territories seized by Mysore.
    
- Defend Maratha-aligned chiefs.
    
- Prevent Mysore from becoming dominant in northern Karnataka.
    
- Compel Haidar to recognize Maratha claims.
    

Thus, the conflict was principally about territory, revenue and regional political supremacy.

## 8. Madhavrao's campaigns against Haidar Ali (1764–1772)

Madhavrao undertook several campaigns against Mysore, although commanders and detailed objectives varied across the campaigns.

### First phase — 1764–1765

Madhavrao's forces moved into Karnataka to challenge Haidar's territorial expansion.

The Marathas recovered important positions and put pressure on Mysore's northern frontier.

Dharwad, an important fortified centre, fell to Maratha forces in 1764.

Haidar was compelled to negotiate, but the settlement did not permanently resolve the conflict.

### Second phase — 1766–1767

Maratha forces again invaded Mysore-controlled territory.

Madhavrao achieved important successes, including the capture of Sira in 1767.

Haidar negotiated another settlement and made payments and territorial concessions.
### Why was Haidar willing to negotiate?

Because he faced multiple threats.

He had to protect his kingdom against the Marathas while also dealing with the Nizam, the British East India Company and other regional powers.

Rather than fight all enemies simultaneously, Haidar sometimes made temporary concessions.
### Third phase — 1770–1772

Hostilities resumed when earlier agreements failed to produce lasting stability.

Maratha forces campaigned again in Mysore territory.

Madhavrao became increasingly ill and eventually returned to Pune, leaving important operations to Trimbakrao Pethe and other commanders.

Maratha forces captured positions including Gurramkonda, and another settlement followed in 1772.

### What did the Marathas achieve?

They recovered or asserted control over several strategic territories, obtained payments and temporarily restrained Mysore's expansion.

However, these successes did not permanently eliminate Haidar Ali's military power.

Following Madhavrao's death, Mysore recovered several positions.

The Maharashtra Gazetteer records both the Maratha successes and the subsequent reversals.

### One very important historical connection

Remember the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769).

The British, Nizam and Haidar Ali were involved in shifting political alignments during this period.

The Marathas also pursued their own independent interests against Mysore.

Do not assume that the Marathas were permanently allied with the British or the Nizam. Alliances changed according to circumstances.

## 9. Why was Madhavrao's administration important?

Madhavrao's achievements were not limited to battlefield victories.

The government had been seriously weakened by the Panipat defeat and subsequent political disorder.

Recovery required a functioning treasury, reliable officials and military discipline.
### Financial administration

Madhavrao sought to restore revenue collection and improve financial supervision.

He required more reliable accounts and worked to reduce the misuse of public resources.

### Military organization

He emphasized discipline among military commanders and attempted to coordinate forces more effectively.

This was especially important because powerful chiefs maintained substantial troops and regional resources.
### Control over officials

Madhavrao sought to prevent influential ministers and military officers from operating entirely independently.

His government relied on experienced administrators, but he attempted to ensure that their authority remained connected to the Peshwa's office.

### Important personalities

| Official             | Role                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nana Fadnavis        | Important financial and administrative officer who later became a leading statesman |
| Sakharam Bapu        | Senior political and administrative figure                                          |
| Ram Shastri Prabhune | Prominent judicial authority associated with the Peshwa court                       |
| Trimbakrao Pethe     | Important military commander, especially in Karnataka                               |
| Mahadji Scindia      | Major Maratha leader in northern India                                              |
### Was Madhavrao's government a modern centralized bureaucracy?

No.

The Maratha state still depended on hereditary military families, regional revenue assignments and negotiations with local chiefs.

Madhavrao's contribution was to improve coordination and discipline within this existing political structure.

## 10. Madhavrao versus Janoji Bhonsle of Nagpur (1765–1769)

![Nagardhan Fort](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/SSKPIZx180WYvYHR2xMKHdZX94aCEY_1da9v_HoDxO6K3nr9qjbVTWuLQ_cG_yy2loZHd-RjlqYEhKqgG1fnYV82lOFssj4Q-LpOm1s3d4DpP1LL2QUvaxmlcW7fxkv1S_t7O7rl0i8sCt_mskMzMAXTgKZfJgPE3UV5MAR2f5o?purpose=inline)

This is another important development often omitted from short examination notes.

### Who was Janoji Bhonsle?

Janoji Bhonsle was the ruler of the Nagpur Bhonsle establishment, one of the most important regional centres of Maratha power.

The Nagpur family had substantial military and revenue interests in Berar, Nagpur and eastern India.
### Why did Madhavrao clash with him?

Janoji pursued an independent political policy.

During conflicts among the Peshwa, Raghunathrao and the Nizam, he made alliances that were not always favourable to Madhavrao.

His relationship with Hyderabad caused particular concern.
### What did Madhavrao do?

Madhavrao used military pressure and diplomacy to compel Janoji to accept terms more favourable to the Peshwa's government.

In 1769, a settlement reduced Janoji's ability to pursue a completely independent policy against the Peshwa.
### Why is this important?

It shows that Madhavrao had to rebuild authority not only against external opponents, but also over powerful members of the Maratha political network.

UPSC Mains connection: The revival of Maratha power required both foreign policy success and internal political consolidation.

## 11. Renewed conflict with Raghunathrao (1768)

![Dhodap Fort under clouds, Chandwad, Nashik](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/oJzcTcU3gfci7zvFXaEiQI4dYXcE0fLyGGM3mqJmCi7FlwB5CFaszTkxkSJzDy3lc8yKzxlP0azEDwCK5uEdbQnntTi6DorTXWGTfGToP7GGdE4AFMVkRQniRNKq7E8WjUD22ydfeVgPPTeiwa52YYZqbzgHZfqpYlLY-Rl-Ce0nnoknYmSUCL9cu_TJijLN?purpose=inline)

Despite the cooperation against the Nizam in 1763, the rivalry between Madhavrao and Raghunathrao was not permanently resolved.
### Why did conflict continue?

Raghunathrao wanted an important independent share of political and military authority.

Madhavrao increasingly insisted on controlling the Peshwa government.

Disagreements involved:

- Military appointments.
    
- Control of territories and revenues.
    
- Relationships with powerful chiefs.
    
- The uncle's ambitions for a more autonomous political position.

### What happened in 1768?

Raghunathrao gathered forces and challenged Madhavrao.

Madhavrao defeated his uncle's position around Dhodegaon, near Chandwad in the Nashik region, in June 1768.

Raghunathrao was brought under restraint and remained confined for a period.

### Why was this significant?

In 1762, Madhavrao had been forced to submit to his uncle.

By 1768, the situation had reversed.

1762 — Alegaon

Raghunathrao gained the upper hand; Madhavrao submitted.

1768 — Dhodegaon

Madhavrao defeated Raghunathrao's challenge.

The victory substantially strengthened Madhavrao's personal authority over the Peshwa's government.

### Did this completely end the problem?

No.

Raghunathrao remained a politically significant figure.

After Madhavrao's death, he became central to another succession crisis, which eventually contributed to the First Anglo-Maratha War.

We will examine those events in later chapters.

## 12. Recovery of Maratha influence in northern India (1769–1772)

![Red Fort in Delhi: The Complete Guide (2026)](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/2Ul-PPQRTpiZDu5vLBuq1MmElb1mTnfrQ72VevpBxf-HTAxVx1ktcosaR8IjmcNzWCY36knOVdcjyRYZ_2Ab6oAso2zlR6p-y1K1EeED_OPfLeBwZGpAu3wIOCWWFMwM1L1LhWWmmwak8amUA5jy9coefcgxGyUtPiIzdlY9-jM?purpose=inline)

Now we return to the consequences of Panipat.
### What happened to northern India after 1761?

Ahmad Shah Abdali defeated the Marathas but did not establish permanent direct government throughout northern India.

The Mughal emperor Shah Alam II continued to claim imperial sovereignty, although he lacked effective control over Delhi for much of the period.

Rohilla chiefs, Jat rulers, Awadh, the British East India Company and various Mughal nobles competed for influence.
### Who was Shah Alam II?

Shah Alam II became Mughal emperor in 1759.

After military and political setbacks, he spent several years away from Delhi.

Following the Battle of Buxar (1764), he entered arrangements with the East India Company.

By the Treaty of Allahabad (1765), the Company obtained the Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar and Odisha from the Mughal emperor.

### Why does the British Company matter here?

Because by Madhavrao's reign, the Company was no longer merely a coastal trading corporation.

It had become a major territorial and financial power in eastern India.

This changed the balance of power in northern Indian politics.

### What did the Marathas want?

Madhavrao's government sought to restore the political influence that had been lost after Panipat.

It also wanted revenue settlements and recognition of Maratha interests in Delhi and neighbouring regions.

### Who led the northern expeditions?

Prominent leaders included:

- Mahadji Scindia (Shinde).
    
- Tukoji Holkar.
    
- Visaji Krishna Biniwale.
    
- Other Maratha commanders.
    

These leaders conducted the campaigns; Madhavrao himself did not personally lead every northern operation.

## 13. Maratha recovery of Delhi (1771)

![Call for Applications: World Heritage Young Professionals Forum 2024 - UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/KVsTJPvMyJaQ-puvgJOS_GBHtHsuYKgqEj1aUDaOZaHIvtZztQJNFK3jm_VSO1mwm6gFd6oMIb3CyWJGV1Lm-CO5iZaIrBsQ5ad8QZatXTp12k6YUyjebZ7czkaQE7-OWublMNgdVDp7GY295YNSa2FI_kaxFBEWfOEDvl0j2j0?purpose=inline)

### What happened in 1771?

Maratha forces advanced into northern India and defeated opponents who had been exercising influence around Delhi.

In February 1771, the Marathas gained control of the city.

This marked a major restoration of their northern political position.

### Why was Delhi important?

Even though Mughal imperial authority had weakened, Delhi remained an important source of political legitimacy.

Influence over the imperial court could strengthen a regional power's right to negotiate revenue and territorial arrangements.

### Was this revenge for Panipat?

It was certainly part of a recovery of Maratha influence after the 1761 catastrophe(આપત્તિ).

But calling it simply revenge overlooks the broader objectives of political authority, regional diplomacy and financial claims.

UPSC significance: Panipat did not permanently end Maratha ambitions in northern India.

## 14. Shah Alam II returns to Delhi — January 1772

### Why had Shah Alam II remained away from Delhi?

The emperor had very limited military resources.

Regional chiefs and competing political establishments controlled much of northern India.

He could not simply return and govern Delhi without protection.

### Why did the Marathas support his return?

The Marathas could gain political legitimacy by acting as protectors of the Mughal emperor.

They also expected territorial and financial advantages.

For Shah Alam II, Maratha assistance offered an opportunity to return to the imperial capital.
### What happened?

After negotiations, Shah Alam II travelled from Allahabad towards Delhi under Maratha protection.

He returned to Delhi in January 1772.
### What changed?

The Mughal emperor regained residence in his imperial capital, but his political position remained dependent on powerful regional forces.

The Marathas exercised substantial influence over court politics.

However, relations between the emperor and Maratha chiefs were not always harmonious; disputes over money and offices persisted.
### Why was this a remarkable reversal?

1761 — Panipat

Maratha northern army destroyed; influence severely weakened.

1771 — Delhi

Maratha forces restored their influence in the imperial capital.

1772 — Shah Alam II returns

Mughal emperor returned to Delhi under Maratha protection.

Historical interpretation: Within approximately eleven years of Panipat, Maratha power had recovered enough to become a decisive force in the politics of the Mughal capital.

But Shah Alam II did not become a fully independent and powerful emperor again. His position remained precarious.
## 15. Madhavrao's illness and death (1772)

Madhavrao suffered from a prolonged illness, generally identified in historical accounts as tuberculosis(ક્ષય રોગ) or consumption.

His health deteriorated during the later years of his reign.

Despite his illness, he continued to oversee important matters of government.

### Death

Madhavrao died on 18 November 1772 at Theur, near Pune.

He was only about 27 years old.

His wife Ramabai performed sati following his death.
### Who succeeded him?

His younger brother Narayanrao became Peshwa.

But Madhavrao's death created another opportunity for Raghunathrao and other political factions to compete for authority.

The resulting crisis belongs to Chapter 12.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer confirms Madhavrao's death on 18 November 1772 and the succession crisis that followed.

### Did Madhavrao permanently solve the problems of Maratha politics?

No.

Important weaknesses remained:

- Regional chiefs retained substantial autonomy.
    
- Raghunathrao still had political ambitions.
    
- The Peshwa government depended heavily on individual leadership.
    
- Conflicts with Mysore and Hyderabad could resume.
    
- The British East India Company was becoming increasingly powerful.

His achievements were significant, but much of the stability depended on his personal authority.

UPSC Mains interpretation: Madhavrao restored Maratha power without fundamentally eliminating the decentralized political structure that made the confederacy difficult to coordinate.

## 19. UPSC Mains model answer

Question: “The reign of Madhavrao I marked a remarkable revival of Maratha power after the Third Battle of Panipat.” Examine. (250 words)

The Third Battle of Panipat in 1761 severely weakened Maratha military strength and political prestige. Madhavrao I, who became Peshwa at a young age, played a major role in restoring the state's position during his reign from 1761 to 1772.

Political consolidation: Madhavrao faced rivalry from his uncle Raghunathrao. Although initially compelled to submit at Alegaon in 1762, he subsequently strengthened his authority and defeated his uncle's challenge in 1768.

Military recovery: The Battle of Rakshasbhuvan in 1763 resulted in a major Maratha victory over the Nizam. It restored military confidence and strengthened Maratha influence in the Deccan.

Southern campaigns: Madhavrao conducted successful operations against Haidar Ali of Mysore, recovering strategic territories and securing financial concessions.

Administrative reforms: He attempted to improve revenue collection, financial supervision and discipline among officials and military commanders.

Northern revival: Maratha forces returned to northern India, recovered Delhi in 1771 and helped Mughal emperor Shah Alam II return to the capital in January 1772.

However, Madhavrao did not eliminate the political autonomy of powerful chiefs or resolve all internal rivalries. His early death in 1772 contributed to renewed instability.

Thus, Madhavrao's reign demonstrated the resilience of Maratha power after Panipat and the importance of effective leadership, administrative discipline and military coordination in restoring a weakened state.
