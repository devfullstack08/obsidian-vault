Central question: How did the death of one capable Peshwa, Madhavrao I, lead to a family murder, a struggle over an infant's succession and the intervention of the British East India Company in Maratha politics?

This chapter is especially important because it connects Maratha history with the rise of British political power in India.

## 1. Where did Chapter 11 end?

In Chapter 11, we studied the reign of Madhavrao I (1761–1772).

After the disastrous defeat at Panipat in 1761, Madhavrao rebuilt Maratha power through military campaigns, improved administration and political leadership.

His achievements included:

- Victory over the Nizam at Rakshasbhuvan (1763).
    
- Successful campaigns against Haidar Ali of Mysore.
    
- Defeat of his uncle Raghunathrao's rebellion in 1768.
    
- Restoration of Maratha influence at Delhi in 1771.
    
- Return of Mughal emperor Shah Alam II to Delhi in 1772 under Maratha protection.

But Madhavrao died at Theur on 18 November 1772, at approximately 27 years of age.

### Why was his death so dangerous?

The Marathas had regained considerable strength, but their political system still depended heavily on individual leaders.

Powerful chiefs controlled military forces and revenue resources.

Madhavrao had successfully constrained his uncle Raghunathrao, but he had not eliminated his political ambitions.

With Madhavrao dead, the question of political leadership reopened.

## 3. Narayanrao becomes Peshwa (December 1772)

![File:Old Citadel know as Shaniwar Wada,Pune.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/C6fvlNGstcYAI950hyCWf4l80ll6AJIfV7AaM7___SP7lStF_mFs6TmyUu0_60wViitB6FI6uFCJVqmYiUPWmQMFgxYzG5J6XW3Qafn-NdC9qr3G6eNz1xNY6ID96gaHsgpLVGB8ZW4qyG3CZQQkilYR4sC52SilZHpF4EOJkBU?purpose=inline)

### Who was Narayanrao?

Narayanrao was the youngest son of Nanasaheb Peshwa and younger brother of Madhavrao I.

When Madhavrao died, Narayanrao was still a teenager.

He became Peshwa in December 1772, receiving formal investiture from Chhatrapati Rajaram II at Satara.
### Why did his succession appear natural?

His two elder brothers were dead:

- Vishwasrao had died at Panipat in 1761.
    
- Madhavrao I had died in 1772.

Narayanrao was therefore the surviving son of Nanasaheb with the strongest immediate claim to the family office.

### But there was a problem

His uncle Raghunathrao had considerable military experience and had previously tried to dominate the government.

For a young Peshwa, the presence of such an ambitious uncle was politically dangerous.
### Narayanrao's dilemma

He could either:

1. Allow Raghunathrao substantial authority and risk becoming a figurehead.
    
2. Restrict his uncle's influence and risk renewed rebellion.

Narayanrao initially sought to manage the relationship, but tensions escalated rapidly.
## 4. Why did Narayanrao and Raghunathrao become enemies?

### Remember the earlier conflict

During Madhavrao I's reign, Raghunathrao had already challenged the Peshwa's authority.

In 1762, Madhavrao had been forced to submit to Raghunathrao after Alegaon.

In 1768, Madhavrao defeated his uncle's renewed challenge and restricted his freedom.

Therefore, the family was already divided before Narayanrao became Peshwa.
### What happened in 1773?

Raghunathrao tried to exercise influence over appointments and administrative decisions.

Narayanrao and his supporters became increasingly suspicious of him.

In April 1773, Raghunathrao was again placed under restraint within the Peshwa's palace complex.
### Why did this intensify the conflict?

From Narayanrao's perspective, his uncle threatened the stability of his government.

From Raghunathrao's perspective, he had been excluded from political power and confined by a much younger relative.

Raghunathrao's supporters began looking for ways to reverse the situation.
### The role of Anandibai

Raghunathrao's wife Anandibai was an influential member of his political household.

Later narratives frequently accuse her of participating in the conspiracy that led to Narayanrao's death.

However, the precise degree of her responsibility, particularly claims involving the alteration of a written order, is debated.

For a serious history answer, distinguish the well-established murder from disputed details of the conspiracy.

## 5. Narayanrao's murder — 30 August 1773

![Shaniwar Wada Palace, Pune: How To Reach, Best Time & Tips](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/1mf2FNyjs9IJnPHIjMZZR4nzmWt0IUwidOk-nhDSPbzWKB4bvNCUlpxEDAuYD33WGcyiPuFi0S86eaMjjekZ0jDa3dyAYKt6jdN2ajBWGfoMXcKlf2VUfXarm2e1brVk6j1L358tdugup9g0HriwUET3CO_9yfZRpDVGnzZJZgk?purpose=inline)

This is the central event of Chapter 12.

### What happened?

On 30 August 1773, a group of armed soldiers associated with the Peshwa's Gardi infantry entered Shaniwar Wada in Pune.

The episode occurred amid unrest and disputes involving troops and their payments.

The soldiers penetrated the palace and attacked Narayanrao.

He tried to seek protection from his uncle Raghunathrao, but he was killed.

Narayanrao was approximately eighteen years old.

The Maharashtra Gazetteer records the murder and notes contemporary suspicions concerning Raghunathrao's involvement.
### Who were the Gardis?

The term Gardi was used for certain infantry groups in Maratha service, including soldiers trained in more regular military methods.

Their forces included people from different regional and social backgrounds.

Important names associated with Narayanrao's assassination include Sumer Singh Gardi (also written Somar Singh) and Muhammad Yusuf.
### Why was the murder a political catastrophe?

The Peshwa had not died in battle against the British, the Nizam or Mysore.

He had been murdered inside his own political headquarters.

This raised three urgent questions:

- Who had ordered the killing?
    
- Who could legitimately succeed him?
    
- Could the ministers and commanders trust Raghunathrao?
    

The death damaged confidence in the Peshwa government and opened a struggle that soon drew in the East India Company.

## 6. The famous “Dhara–Mara” story — fact or legend?

You may encounter this story in books, documentaries and historical dramas.

According to a popular tradition, Raghunathrao wrote an instruction in Marathi to “Dhara” (धरावे), meaning seize or arrest him.

The story claims that the instruction was altered to “Mara” (मारावे), meaning kill him, and that Anandibai was responsible for the alteration.

### Can we accept the story as fully proven?

Not without qualification.

The story became an important part of popular accounts of Narayanrao's murder. But historians have questioned whether the precise wording and alleged alteration can be demonstrated securely from contemporary evidence.

Different narratives place responsibility on Raghunathrao, Anandibai or other conspirators.

What is more firmly established is that Narayanrao was murdered by armed men associated with the Gardi troops and that Raghunathrao's conduct and possible involvement were strongly suspected.
### UPSC answer-writing rule

Do not write that Anandibai definitely changed one letter and caused the murder as an unquestioned historical fact.

Write instead:

> Traditional accounts allege that an order to seize Narayanrao was altered into an instruction to kill him; however, the precise circumstances of the conspiracy remain disputed.

This lets you discuss the famous story without confusing folklore and historical proof.

### Who was Ram Shastri?

Ram Shastri Prabhune was a prominent judicial official associated with the Peshwa's administration.

He had earned a reputation for learning and integrity.
### What was his role after Narayanrao's murder?

Historical accounts describe an inquiry into the killing and associate Ram Shastri with a judgment holding Raghunathrao responsible.

The episode became an enduring symbol of judicial independence in Maharashtra.
### Why is this important?

Raghunathrao was a powerful member of the Peshwa family.

Condemning his actions carried political risks.

The episode raises an important question about precolonial institutions:

Could judicial authority challenge a ruler or senior political official?

Ram Shastri's remembered stance suggests that judicial legitimacy and norms of justice could be invoked against powerful individuals.

However, specific courtroom dialogues and dramatic declarations found in later retellings should not automatically be treated as verbatim historical records.
## 8. Raghunathrao becomes Peshwa (1773)

![Index - Folio Bootstrap Template](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8EkPVgPWCNIA5ubu-xTqol8BmzHTCm5wzkKaj8dUxKMyAzafeaAsFG9FGkvekaRga0SkVSF4nKX1I92S0Eq1p5ID55VwMly6vKN1CUKnVB0aEtR4ehFEo4xoM-t37k0G0buPouR4E-T9jbD_r84qWCReLaceP2sr7dVOl7pppF0?purpose=inline)

After Narayanrao's murder, Raghunathrao assumed control of the Peshwa government.

He received formal investiture as Peshwa in 1773.

### Why did his accession appear possible?

Narayanrao was dead and had no living child at that moment.

Raghunathrao was an experienced member of the Peshwa family and had previously exercised substantial political authority.

### But why did the ministers oppose him?

Their concerns included:

- His suspected involvement in Narayanrao's murder.
    
- His previous rebellions against Madhavrao I.
    
- His unpredictable political alliances.
    
- The danger that he would marginalize established ministers.
    
- The possibility that Narayanrao's widow was pregnant.

### Why did Gangabai's pregnancy change the situation?

If Gangabai gave birth to a son, that child would possess a direct hereditary claim through Narayanrao.

The ministers could then support an infant Peshwa rather than recognize Raghunathrao as the legitimate successor.

This possibility transformed a dispute over one man's accession into a struggle over dynastic succession.
## 9. Formation of the Barbhai Council (1773–1774)

![Nana Fadnavis - Bharatpedia](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/jdjjjx2BVj7jwSPsvylqpzCMaXZiehK-YwN6D6Htb1-C6swvVeusakjAJlEbLKGq2Fwsmj-mtzUByA_Pa4gj1XCU539Yyd9xjDPXuhyfJRt78Nr2k9VnLmuxSzmK430_OBiJvdVfK3oE6T7UYXlfy1fk5T400Lne_yyLh8CscQ4?purpose=inline)

**Name:** Nana Fadnavis (Balaji Janardan Bhanu)
**Born:** 1742
**Position:** Senior minister and diplomat
**Political role:** Leading organizer of the opposition to Raghunathrao
**Historical importance:** Became a central statesman in the Peshwa government during the regency of Madhavrao II.
### What does Barbhai mean?

Barbhai (बारभाई) literally means twelve brothers.

In this context, it refers to a coalition of leading Maratha ministers and chiefs who opposed Raghunathrao.

It is often called the Barbhai Council or Barbhai Confederacy.

The label should not be understood as proving that its membership always remained fixed at exactly twelve individuals.
### Why was the council formed?

The ministers wanted to:

1. Remove or neutralize Raghunathrao.
    
2. Preserve the legitimacy of Narayanrao's line.
    
3. Protect Gangabai and her unborn child.
    
4. Prevent a further breakdown of the Peshwa government.
    
5. Maintain cooperation among important Maratha chiefs.
### Important figures

| Leader                | Role in the political coalition                            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nana Fadnavis         | Diplomatic, financial and political organizer              |
| Sakharam Bapu         | Experienced senior minister                                |
| Haripant Phadke       | Important military commander                               |
| Trimbakrao Mama Pethe | Senior commander and political figure                      |
| Mahadji Scindia       | Powerful Maratha leader in northern India                  |
| Tukoji Holkar         | Important military leader associated with the Holkar house |

The participation and influence of individual chiefs developed over time; the coalition was not a modern political party with a fixed constitution.
### What did the council do first?

It moved to protect Gangabai, Narayanrao's pregnant widow.

She was placed at Purandar Fort, where her safety could be better secured.

The ministers also began organizing political and military opposition to Raghunathrao.

### Why Purandar?

![Treks in Sahyadri: Purandar Fort Trek](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/vRBknkfoZQsbEI8AlcAjj1KI5uWalo5flLio5qYDMKxt9vDzIc6WZHNVn-HzA725MBgLrXM_QnuFXJn84CSnlMgf4F9fYvPweml6N6JZKMURRaNT2mmjvcxkkifI3nIFtDixK12_4AIVNNud-u54Dj7U7uxQxHLBRjfSaZQU0RM?purpose=inline)

Purandar was a defensible hill fort with a long history in Maratha politics.

It offered a secure location for the pregnant widow of the murdered Peshwa.

If a male child were born there, the council could establish a legitimate succession without leaving the child vulnerable in Pune.

UPSC significance: The Barbhai Council sought to preserve dynastic legitimacy while ensuring that effective government remained in the hands of the regency ministers.

### What happened?

On 18 April 1774, Gangabai gave birth to a son at Purandar Fort.

He was named Madhavrao Narayan, also known as:

- Sawai Madhavrao
    
- Sawai Madhavrao Narayan
### Why was his birth politically decisive?

The infant was Narayanrao's posthumous son.

His existence provided the Barbhai Council with a dynastic claimant whose position was stronger than that of an uncle accused of involvement in the father's murder.
### Who became Peshwa?

The council recognized the infant as Peshwa.

He received the formal insignia of office in May 1774, when he was only about forty days old.

### Could a forty-day-old baby govern?

Obviously not.

The council exercised the powers of government in his name.

## 11. Why did Raghunathrao refuse to accept Madhavrao II?

Raghunathrao had briefly occupied the Peshwa's office.

Accepting the infant's succession meant surrendering his own claim.

He believed he could still gather military support and defeat the council.
### What resources did he possess?

Raghunathrao had:

- Previous experience as a senior military commander.
    
- Connections among Maratha nobles.
    
- Supporters in parts of Gujarat.
    
- Opportunities to negotiate with regional rulers.
    
- A willingness to seek outside assistance.

### What did the Barbhai Council possess?

The council controlled:

- The infant Peshwa's legitimacy.
    
- Important parts of Pune's administrative establishment.
    
- Access to financial resources.
    
- Support from powerful Maratha commanders.

The struggle could therefore continue even though Madhavrao II had been formally recognized.
### The central issue

Who controlled the Peshwa's authority—the infant's regents or his ambitious great-uncle?
## 12. Raghunathrao's military defeat and flight towards Gujarat (1774–1775)

The Barbhai Council organized forces against Raghunathrao.

Military commanders, including **Haripant Phadke**, pursued him.

### What happened?

Raghunathrao moved north and west, seeking military and political allies.

His position weakened as major commanders declined to commit fully to his cause.

In February 1775, forces supporting the Pune regency defeated his army near the Mahi River region of Gujarat.

He escaped and sought refuge through contacts connected with the British East India Company's western settlements.

### Why did he approach the British?

Because he could no longer be confident of defeating the Barbhai Council using his own forces.

The Company's Bombay establishment possessed professional troops and access to maritime resources.

Raghunathrao hoped that British intervention could restore him to the Peshwaship.

### Why was this dangerous?

A succession dispute within the Maratha government was becoming an opportunity for an external commercial and territorial power to intervene.

This was precisely the kind of political division the East India Company had exploited elsewhere in India.
## 13. Why was the British East India Company interested?

By 1775, the British Company had become much more powerful than it had been during Shivaji's early years.

### What did the Bombay government want?

The Bombay Presidency wanted to strengthen the Company's position on the western coast.

Of particular interest were:

- Salsette Island, adjacent to Bombay.
    
- Thane, an important fortified position.
    
- Bassein (Vasai), a strategically valuable coastal settlement.
    
- Commercial and revenue rights in the Gujarat coastal region.

### Why Salsette and Bassein?

Bombay was a valuable port, but its wider security and commercial expansion depended partly on control of surrounding islands, harbours and coastal routes.

Salsette and Bassein could improve the Company's military and economic position.

### Why was Raghunathrao useful to the Company?

He offered the possibility of obtaining valuable concessions in return for military assistance.

Instead of fighting the whole Maratha political establishment without any justification, the Bombay authorities could claim they were supporting a legitimate Peshwa candidate.

Historical lesson: British intervention was shaped by both Maratha internal politics and the Company's own territorial ambitions.

## 14. Treaty of Surat — 6 March 1775

This is the most important treaty in Chapter 12.
### Who signed it?

Raghunathrao and representatives of the British East India Company's Bombay government.

### What did Raghunathrao want?

He wanted Company military assistance to defeat the Barbhai Council and regain the Peshwa's office.

### What did the British want?

They wanted territorial concessions(છૂટછાટો), payments and greater influence on India's western coast.

### Main provisions

| Provision                 | Meaning                                                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| British military support  | Company agreed to provide approximately 2,500 troops, including European soldiers and artillery |
| Salsette and Thane        | Territorial concessions to the Company                                                          |
| Bassein (Vasai)           | Raghunathrao agreed to surrender this important coastal possession                              |
| Gujarat revenue interests | Additional concessions involving areas near Surat and Bharuch                                   |
| Financial obligations     | Raghunathrao agreed to contribute to the cost of British forces and provide financial security  |
| Political objective       | British assistance to restore Raghunathrao's claim to the Peshwaship                            |
### Why did Raghunathrao accept such expensive terms?

Because regaining political power was his highest priority.

He was willing to give the Company valuable territory and financial advantages in return for an army.

### Did he physically hand over every territory immediately?

No.

The treaty contained promises and claims that still had to be enforced.

The Company already had military involvement in parts of the coastal region. The promised cessions were politically and legally contested by the Pune regency.

### Why did the treaty cause war?

The Barbhai Council did not accept Raghunathrao as the legitimate Peshwa.

It therefore rejected his authority to dispose of Maratha territories.

When British troops began supporting his cause militarily, the conflict became a war between the Company and the Pune government.

This marked the beginning of the First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782).

## 15. Why did the Company's Bombay and Calcutta governments disagree?

This is a particularly important topic for UPSC because it connects Maratha history with British constitutional and administrative developments.
### The Regulating Act of 1773

The British Parliament passed the Regulating Act of 1773, which strengthened central supervision over Company affairs in India.

It established the office of Governor-General of Bengal, supported by a council.

Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal under the new system in 1774.

### The problem

The Bombay government negotiated the Treaty of Surat with Raghunathrao.

But the government at Calcutta had supervisory authority over important matters involving war and diplomacy, subject to the details and limits of the Regulating Act.
### Why did Warren Hastings object?

The treaty risked involving the Company in a major war with the Maratha political establishment.

It had been concluded by Bombay without the necessary prior central approval.

The Bengal government therefore questioned the agreement and sought a negotiated settlement with the Pune regency.

### Why is this significant?

The Maratha conflict exposed weaknesses and ambiguities in the Company's own system of government.

The British were not a perfectly unified political organization. Officials at different presidencies sometimes pursued conflicting strategies.

The disagreement eventually contributed to negotiations resulting in the Treaty of Purandar (1776).

The Marathas had survived Panipat and recovered under Madhavrao I.

However, his death reopened rivalries among members of the Peshwa family and powerful ministers.

The British Company exploited the political opportunity created by the struggle.

The First Anglo-Maratha War began as a conflict over succession and political authority, not because the British had already conquered the Maratha kingdom.

## 19. UPSC Mains — model answer

Question: How did the Maratha succession crisis following Madhavrao I's death provide an opportunity for British intervention? (250 words)

The death of Peshwa Madhavrao I in 1772 weakened the political stability restored after the Third Battle of Panipat. His young brother Narayanrao succeeded him but soon faced opposition from their ambitious uncle Raghunathrao.

Succession crisis: Narayanrao attempted to restrict Raghunathrao's political influence. In August 1773, Narayanrao was murdered inside Shaniwar Wada. Raghunathrao subsequently assumed the Peshwaship, but suspicions concerning his involvement undermined his legitimacy.

Rise of the regency: Leading ministers, including Nana Fadnavis and Sakharam Bapu, formed the Barbhai Council. Following the birth of Narayanrao's posthumous son Madhavrao II in April 1774, the council recognized the infant as Peshwa and governed on his behalf.

Political fragmentation: Raghunathrao refused to surrender his claim and sought assistance from various regional powers. After military setbacks, he approached the British East India Company's Bombay government.

British intervention: Through the Treaty of Surat in March 1775, Raghunathrao promised territorial and financial concessions, including Salsette and Bassein, in exchange for British military support.

The Pune regency rejected the treaty because it did not recognize Raghunathrao's authority. This dispute initiated the First Anglo-Maratha War.

Thus, the succession crisis exposed weaknesses within the Maratha political structure and provided the Company with an opportunity to pursue its territorial ambitions under the pretext of supporting a rival claimant.
