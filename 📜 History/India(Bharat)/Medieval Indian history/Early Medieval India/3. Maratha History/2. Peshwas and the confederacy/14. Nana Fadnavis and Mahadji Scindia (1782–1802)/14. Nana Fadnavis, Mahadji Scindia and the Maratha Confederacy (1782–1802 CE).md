Leading Maratha military and political figure in northern India

Central question: After defeating British intervention in the First Anglo-Maratha War, why did the Marathas remain powerful for another two decades, yet become so politically divided that **Peshwa Baji Rao II** accepted British protection through the T**reaty of Bassein in 1802**?

This chapter explains how the Marathas reached a position of great influence in Indian politics, but how competition between the Peshwa, ministers and regional chiefs gradually weakened their ability to act together.

## 1. Where did Chapter 13 end?

In Chapter 13, we studied the First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782).

The war began because Raghunathrao sought British assistance to regain the Peshwaship.

Important events included:

- 1775: Treaty of Surat.
    
- 1776: Treaty of Purandar.
    
- 1779: British defeat at Wadgaon.
    
- 1780–1781: Campaigns in Gujarat and Malwa.
    
- 1782: Treaty of Salbai.
    

The Treaty of Salbai recognized the position of the Peshwa Madhavrao II and ended British support for Raghunathrao.

It also helped create a period of relative peace between the East India Company and the Pune government.

### But who actually governed?

Madhavrao II had become Peshwa as an infant in 1774.

Therefore, much of the government had been directed by experienced ministers, especially **Nana Fadnavis**.

Meanwhile, powerful Maratha commanders governed or influenced extensive territories far from Pune.

The two most important political figures during the 1780s were:

Nana Fadnavis at Pune and Mahadji Scindia in northern India.

They were connected through overlapping royal authority, military obligations, revenue claims, family relationships and political alliances.

This structure is commonly described as the Maratha Confederacy.

It enabled widespread influence, but it also created rivalry among powerful regional houses.

## 3. Nana Fadnavis — the principal statesman at Pune

![“Take All of Them”: Eclecticism and the Arts of the Pune Court in India, 1760–1800: The Art Bulletin: Vol 100 , No 2  - Get Access](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/egRioi5eq9-pIGbA4_4C-dlhdvo9cpHHkEVg3fe_OhfHK3RlCgUe0B0RUxppvgOKHwCf5XOmBQUfASEViuKeYkyZsIGsrsRlmUlTQD0q4mY-EmwUM-nVlth6WZrHPq6EjDQ7ibT_0zV2VPCpqe3ISVxyYcDXuPYHhPp3Q-Lx6cM?purpose=inline)

### Who was Nana Fadnavis?

His personal name was **Balaji Janardan Bhanu**.

He came from a family connected with the Peshwa administration and became a prominent financial and political officer.

After Narayanrao's murder in 1773, he played a leading role in organizing the Barbhai Council, which defended the claim of the infant Madhavrao II.

### What was his principal responsibility?

Nana managed important aspects of:

- Finance and treasury administration.
    
- Political correspondence.
    
- Negotiations with Maratha chiefs.
    
- Foreign relations.
    
- Protection of the Peshwa's authority.
    
- Coordination of the Pune court.

### Why was he important?

Military commanders possessed armies, but the government also needed money, agreements, appointments and reliable political coordination.

Nana's strength lay primarily in diplomacy and administration.

### Was Nana Fadnavis the Peshwa?

No.

The Peshwa was Madhavrao II.

Nana was the powerful minister who exercised considerable authority during the young Peshwa's government.

The Royal Asiatic Society identifies Nana as a leading administrator and diplomat involved in the regency after 1773.

## 4. Mahadji Scindia — the powerful Maratha leader in the north

![Life story of Mahadji Scindia (Shinde)](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/jLTU9qPs9Z62J34fpR5Ds1lmHkyWzNGotcMVtoOTW2ZdyjpTeVJXrnwjJJ2VmMX6vHcYF_HvF5KHwAdJOzgD5J6KXOAI0sblIIAoJEm7Gc-YdrJpbLn6kVxB0F4a03DFAT0iVUOCw6tFLd_nwTXL_EZBTfeYSbVIThRE8JtQTrk?purpose=inline)

**Name:** Mahadji Shinde (Scindia)
**House:** Scindia/Shinde
**Main regions**: Malwa, Gwalior, Delhi and northern India
**Military focus:** Cavalry and increasingly professional infantry and artillery
**Death:** 12 February 1794, near Pune
### How had Mahadji become powerful?

Remember the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761.

Mahadji survived the disaster and subsequently helped rebuild Scindia power.

During the 1770s, Maratha forces recovered influence over Delhi and the Mughal court.

Mahadji gradually established himself as one of the most important political leaders in northern India.

His importance increased after the Treaty of Salbai because he had served as a major negotiator and guarantor of the peace.

### What were his objectives?

He sought to:

1. Strengthen his territorial authority in Malwa.
    
2. Control important military positions in northern India.
    
3. Influence the Mughal imperial court.
    
4. Obtain revenue to finance his army.
    
5. Maintain a powerful position within Maratha politics.
### Did he rule India as Mughal emperor?

No.

Shah Alam II remained the Mughal emperor.

Mahadji sought influence over the emperor and the imperial administration while retaining his position as a Maratha chief.

## 5. Nana Fadnavis versus Mahadji Scindia — cooperation and rivalry

This is one of the central themes of the chapter.

Both men contributed to Maratha power, but their interests were not identical.

| Feature                  | Nana Fadnavis                             | Mahadji Scindia                                           |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal centre         | Pune                                      | Malwa, Gwalior and Delhi                                  |
| Main source of influence | Administration, finance and diplomacy     | Army, territory and Mughal court politics                 |
| Formal role              | Minister under the Peshwa                 | Powerful Maratha chief                                    |
| Political priority       | Preserve authority of the Pune government | Strengthen his northern establishment                     |
| Major concern            | Excessive independence of military chiefs | Interference with his territorial and political ambitions |
| Strength                 | Coalition-building                        | Military organization and diplomacy                       |

### Why did they cooperate?

Both wanted to preserve Maratha power and prevent the British from dominating the political system.

They also benefited from mutual recognition.

Nana needed influential military commanders.

Mahadji needed legitimacy and cooperation from Pune.
### Why did they compete?

Mahadji's growing military and financial resources made him less dependent on the Peshwa's ministers.

Nana feared that a chief with a large standing army could dominate Pune.

Mahadji, meanwhile, resisted attempts to restrict his northern authority.
### Historical significance

The same decentralization that enabled Maratha expansion made internal political coordination increasingly difficult.

Neither Nana nor Mahadji was simply the enemy of the Maratha state. Both operated within it while competing over influence.

## 6. Mahadji Scindia's influence over the Mughal emperor (1784–1785)

![290 The Later Mughals (1707-1857) ideas | mughal, mughal paintings, mughal empire](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/T65az5R0j66PM13nFU-ZP21aqK-ZM8HOagvnMHKzGpplgluHFQUj0uH0mfll_AR2vnrMcINGEMJejkj1qO9Qf4XJZQF33b1RFjFsXy5Fap3MIjgGwR5TxP3-RaJrvuca_ivcjww1MJMcVYaK_W8gJ002EgRvlo31-F_s2qB-tSg?purpose=inline)

### Who was Shah Alam II?

Shah Alam II (1759–1806) was the Mughal emperor.

Remember Chapter 11: he returned to Delhi in 1772 with Maratha assistance.

However, his government lacked sufficient independent military power to control all of northern India.

He needed support from regional powers.
### What happened in the mid-1780s?

Mahadji Scindia strengthened his influence over the Mughal court.

In 1784–1785, imperial arrangements granted Mahadji an important position in the management of Mughal affairs.

The position was associated with the title **Vakil-i-Mutlaq**, or imperial representative/deputy, although historical accounts distinguish between the title granted to the Peshwa and the deputy authority exercised by Scindia.
### Why was this important?

Mahadji could use Mughal imperial legitimacy to support his military and revenue claims in northern India.

Meanwhile, the emperor gained the protection of a powerful army.
### Did this restore the Mughal Empire to its former strength?

No.

The emperor's titles and grants still possessed great symbolic authority.

But actual power increasingly rested with the military and financial resources of regional leaders such as Mahadji.

UPSC concept: A declining empire can remain politically important because its institutions continue providing titles, legitimacy and recognized authority.

## 7. Military modernization under Mahadji Scindia

![Battle of Wadgaon: A glorious moment in Maratha history](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/iFRQo--wimTiZsYJzPrthTd-ECd8BkR0eCh_VxV8Hwof_4-TtzLfcPA3_89lT7Rdhyki8RFlGoyONuR5x9dQEWu_ar1ZwNpm0DLCHJvMRbYqiGO8mfOSWO63Y64f5X52fcFjHIpEjLvwjpLD6KSUpiwIcvf0U2DnpFHHvLuOl5U?purpose=inline)

### Why did Mahadji reorganize his army?

Earlier Maratha military success had depended heavily on mobile cavalry.

But fighting in northern India increasingly involved:

- Fortified cities.
    
- Large artillery units.
    
- Regularly drilled infantry.
    
- Professional military formations.
    
- Regional rulers with modernizing armies.

Mahadji recognized that cavalry alone would not always be sufficient.

### The role of Benoît de Boigne

**Benoît de Boigne** was a European military officer from Savoy who entered Mahadji's service.

He helped organize regular infantry battalions, artillery and eventually larger brigades.
### What changed?

| Earlier military emphasis                    | Mahadji's developing system                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Cavalry mobility                             | Cavalry supported by drilled infantry          |
| Strong dependence on regional mounted chiefs | More structured regular formations             |
| Raiding and manoeuvring                      | Ability to conduct large set-piece battles     |
| Varied military equipment                    | Greater organization of artillery and infantry |

### Why was this significant?

Mahadji's forces became capable of fighting powerful northern Indian opponents in conventional battles.

However, these professional formations were expensive.

They required regular pay, weapons, ammunition and dependable revenue.

UPSC interpretation: Military modernization strengthened Scindia but also increased the financial demands of maintaining a large army.

## 8. The crisis at Delhi — Ghulam Qadir and Shah Alam II (1788)

This episode is essential for understanding Mahadji's dominance in northern India.

### Who was Ghulam Qadir?

Ghulam Qadir was a Rohilla Afghan chief who became involved in the struggle for influence over Delhi.

He sought control over the imperial court and its resources.

### What happened in 1788?

Ghulam Qadir seized control of Delhi's imperial palace and subjected Shah Alam II and members of the royal household to brutal treatment.

Shah Alam II was blinded.

The episode was a major humiliation for the Mughal monarchy.
### What did Mahadji do?

Mahadji's forces intervened and recovered Delhi from Ghulam Qadir's control in 1788.

Ghulam Qadir was captured and later executed in 1789.

### Why was this important?

Mahadji emerged as a principal protector of the emperor.

His authority in the politics of northern India became even more significant.

### A historical irony

The Mughal emperor still represented imperial sovereignty.

Yet he depended for protection on Maratha power, despite the long history of conflict between the Mughals and the Marathas.

The Haryana State Gazetteer describes the events of 1788–1789 and Scindia's establishment of authority at Delhi.

## 9. Mahadji's campaigns in Rajasthan — Patan and Merta (1790)

Mahadji's influence in northern India was not uncontested.

Rajput rulers resisted Maratha demands and territorial ambitions.

### Why did conflict occur?

Maratha commanders sought tribute and revenue from Rajput states.

Rajput rulers opposed excessive demands and attempted to defend their political independence.

### The campaigns

In 1790, Scindia's forces under de Boigne defeated opposing Rajput and allied forces in major engagements at:

- Patan, in present-day Rajasthan.
    
- Merta (Medta), in the Marwar region.

### Why were these victories important?

They demonstrated the growing effectiveness of Mahadji's regular infantry and artillery.

They also strengthened his ability to enforce revenue claims in Rajasthan.

### Did the victories mean Rajasthan became a single directly governed Scindia province?

No.

Maratha influence expanded through military pressure, tribute claims and political settlements, but the Rajput states retained their own rulers and institutions.

UPSC significance: Patan and Merta illustrate both the military modernization and fiscal ambitions of the Scindia establishment.

## 10. Maratha relations with Mysore and the British (1782–1792)

The Treaty of Salbai in 1782 reduced immediate British–Maratha conflict.

But important changes were taking place in southern India.

### Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan

Haidar Ali died in December 1782.

His son Tipu Sultan succeeded him as the effective ruler of Mysore.

The Second Anglo-Mysore War ended with the Treaty of Mangalore (1784).

### Why did the Marathas fight Tipu Sultan?

Mysore and the Marathas had competing territorial and revenue claims in Karnataka.

Tipu sought to strengthen Mysore's regional power.

The Marathas wanted to preserve their own rights and protect allied chiefs.

### Maratha–Mysore conflict, 1785–1787

Nana Fadnavis coordinated Maratha efforts against Tipu.

Maratha forces fought alongside Hyderabad's forces during parts of the conflict.

A settlement followed in 1787, commonly associated with Gajendragad.

It involved territorial and financial arrangements between Mysore and its opponents.

### What happened during the Third Anglo-Mysore War?

The Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792) brought the British Company, Hyderabad and the Marathas into a coalition against Tipu Sultan.

The Maratha role included military operations and cooperation with the other powers.

The war ended with the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792), under which Mysore surrendered territory and accepted a substantial indemnity.

### Why is this strategically important?

The Marathas gained from weakening a regional rival.

But Britain's power also increased considerably.

This produced a difficult long-term consequence: the British Company emerged stronger from a war in which Maratha leaders had been its allies.

UPSC Mains point: Indian regional states often pursued immediate territorial interests, even when their alliances indirectly strengthened a power that later threatened their own independence.

## 11. Death of Mahadji Scindia (1794)

Mahadji Scindia died near Pune on 12 February 1794.

### What did he leave behind?

- An influential territorial establishment in Malwa.
    
- Strong political connections with Delhi.
    
- A professional army with trained infantry and artillery.
    
- Significant authority over the Mughal emperor.
    
- A powerful regional house within the Maratha Confederacy.

### Who succeeded him?

Daulat Rao Scindia, a young relative and adopted successor, became head of the Scindia establishment.

He lacked Mahadji's long experience and inherited an expensive military and political system.

### Why was Mahadji's death important?

Mahadji had balanced several competing responsibilities: Delhi, northern territories, relations with Pune and regional warfare.

His death created opportunities for new rivalries within the Scindia establishment and between Scindia and other Maratha chiefs.

At first, his disappearance also reduced one of Nana Fadnavis's most formidable political rivals.

But it did not create lasting unity.

## 12. Battle of Kharda — 11 March 1795

![Kharda – Bhuikot Fort – Karjat Jamkhed](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/cydtmTwSe1sd3n_Ph2Ff1dzFJBQ1nMRK3MPsznUALhP5jipWBsy3-Ce1Jbytq8ieECffvYgZftWCFgjLLwCWNt7cuZ-V1pmvVzlI0w8mSrrTwcs1NZlS9YK0eVRB2GCyg3tMhJm8rMVtZL6RGOJgx91DgH4qZca99iXpquHHYo4?purpose=inline)

This is one of the most important battles of the late Maratha period.

### Who fought?

| Maratha coalition                               | Hyderabad                               |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Peshwa Madhavrao II's government                | Nizam Ali Khan (Asaf Jah II)            |
| Nana Fadnavis, political organizer              | Hyderabad ministers and commanders      |
| Parashuram Bhau Patwardhan and other commanders | Hyderabad's army                        |
| Scindia, Holkar and Nagpur Bhonsle contingents  | Regular infantry, cavalry and artillery |

### Why did the war begin?

The Marathas claimed substantial outstanding payments arising from earlier chauth and related fiscal arrangements.

The Nizam disputed the demands.

Diplomatic negotiations became increasingly hostile.

The disagreement was also a struggle over the relative political influence of Pune and Hyderabad in the Deccan.

### What happened?

In March 1795, the opposing armies confronted one another near Kharda, in present-day Maharashtra.

The principal fighting took place around 11 March 1795.

Maratha forces defeated the Nizam's army, which withdrew into the fortifications of Kharda.

Facing continued pressure, the Nizam negotiated terms.

### Why did the Marathas win?

A number of leading Maratha houses cooperated against a common opponent.

The combined forces were able to exert strong military pressure on Hyderabad.

### Result

The Nizam accepted major territorial and financial concessions.

The terms included territory yielding substantial annual revenue and large payments that were difficult to realize fully in practice.

### Why is Kharda historically important?

It is often described as one of the last major victories achieved by a broadly united Maratha coalition before the internal crises of the late 1790s.

The Marathi Vishwakosh records the fighting on 11 March 1795, while historical accounts sometimes describe the broader engagement and settlement over several days.

## 13. Death of Madhavrao II — 27 October 1795

![File:Old Citadel know as Shaniwar Wada,Pune.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/C6fvlNGstcYAI950hyCWf4l80ll6AJIfV7AaM7___SP7lStF_mFs6TmyUu0_60wViitB6FI6uFCJVqmYiUPWmQMFgxYzG5J6XW3Qafn-NdC9qr3G6eNz1xNY6ID96gaHsgpLVGB8ZW4qyG3CZQQkilYR4sC52SilZHpF4EOJkBU?purpose=inline)

Only months after the victory at Kharda, the Maratha government suffered a major political shock.

### Who was Madhavrao II?

He was Narayanrao's posthumous son.

He had become Peshwa as an infant in 1774.

Nana Fadnavis and other ministers had governed during his childhood.

As he grew older, tensions developed over the degree of independence he could exercise.

### Why was he unhappy?

Madhavrao II wanted greater personal authority.

Nana Fadnavis remained a powerful minister, and several rival factions sought influence over the Peshwa.

Relations became increasingly difficult.

### What happened in October 1795?

Madhavrao II fell from an upper part of Shaniwar Wada and suffered fatal injuries.

He died on 27 October 1795.

Some historical accounts describe the fall as intentional, while others emphasize uncertainty over his state of mind and the circumstances.

For UPSC, the securely established facts are the fall, his death in October 1795 and the resulting succession crisis.
### Why was his death so dangerous?

He left no surviving son.

The Peshwa's succession now became contested again.

This reopened competition between Nana Fadnavis, the Scindia establishment and members of the Peshwa family.

Remember the parallel:

- 1772: Death of Madhavrao I led to a succession crisis.
    
- 1795: Death of Madhavrao II led to another succession crisis.

## 14. Succession crisis of 1795–1796 — who would become Peshwa?

The leading surviving male claimant from the Bhat family was Baji Rao II, son of Raghunathrao.

### Why did Nana Fadnavis distrust him?

Remember Chapter 12.

Nana had opposed Baji Rao II's father, Raghunathrao, during the succession struggles of the 1770s.

Baji Rao II had reasons to resent Nana and the regency establishment.

Nana feared that appointing him might lead to retaliation and loss of political influence.

### Who was Chimnaji II?

Chimnaji II (Chimaji Appa) was Baji Rao II's younger brother.

Different political factions supported different succession arrangements.

### What happened?

During 1796, the struggle became extremely complicated.

At one stage, Chimnaji II was installed as Peshwa under the influence of a rival political group.

But that arrangement was short-lived.

Nana Fadnavis and Daulat Rao Scindia eventually reached another political accommodation.

### Final result

Baji Rao II became Peshwa in December 1796.

## 15. Baji Rao II and Nana Fadnavis — why did they clash?

Baji Rao II
Born: 1775
Father: Raghunathrao
Became Peshwa: 1796

Main political problem: Competition with influential ministers and regional Maratha chiefs.

Historical significance: His decisions eventually brought the Peshwa government into a British subsidiary alliance.

### Why was the relationship unstable?

Baji Rao II wanted to exercise personal authority as Peshwa.

Nana Fadnavis wanted to retain influence over government and protect his existing political network.

Daulat Rao Scindia also wished to control important appointments and financial decisions at Pune.

### Why was Scindia's army influential?

Daulat Rao inherited the professional forces developed under Mahadji.

These troops could be used to pressure the Peshwa's government and competing ministers.

### What happened to Nana?

In late 1797, Nana was arrested during political intrigues involving Daulat Rao Scindia and Baji Rao II.

He was detained during 1798 before eventually securing his release.

The episode exposed the extent to which the Pune government had become dependent on factions supported by powerful armies.

### Why was this harmful to Maratha power?

Government officials were increasingly occupied with protecting their own positions.

The politics of Pune were influenced by coercion, military pressure and the struggle to control the treasury.

This weakened the ability of the Peshwa government to coordinate relations among Maratha chiefs.

## 16. Death of Nana Fadnavis — 13 March 1800

Nana Fadnavis died at Pune on 13 March 1800.

### Why was his death important?

For more than two decades, Nana had served as an important political organizer and diplomatic leader.

He had helped manage:

- The regency of Madhavrao II.
    
- Relations with the British after Salbai.
    
- Competition among Maratha chiefs.
    
- Military and financial arrangements against Hyderabad and Mysore.
    
- Difficult negotiations at Pune.

### What happened after his death?

The political system lost a minister with extensive experience and connections.

Baji Rao II remained Peshwa, but he faced powerful regional chiefs, particularly Daulat Rao Scindia and the Holkar establishment.

The Royal Asiatic Society confirms Nana's death date and identifies his important administrative and diplomatic role.
### Was Nana personally responsible for every Maratha success?

No.

Military commanders, regional chiefs and the Peshwa establishment all contributed.

His achievement lay in political organization and diplomacy, although some of his choices also intensified factional rivalries.

UPSC Mains conclusion: Nana was important as a statesman, but the Maratha Confederacy's structural problems could not be solved by one individual.

Yashwantrao Holkar
Powerful Holkar leader who challenged Scindia and the Peshwa.

### Why did the rivalry develop?

The Scindia and Holkar families had acquired extensive military and revenue interests in Malwa.

They also competed for influence over appointments, territories and Maratha politics.

The rivalry intensified during disputes over succession within the Holkar family.

Yashwantrao Holkar emerged as an important military leader after these internal conflicts.

### Why was Baji Rao II involved?

The Peshwa government had become closely aligned with Daulat Rao Scindia.

Therefore, conflicts between Scindia and Holkar increasingly affected the politics of Pune.

### What made the situation worse in 1801?

Vithoji Holkar, Yashwantrao's brother, led armed operations against Peshwa-controlled territory.

He was captured by forces loyal to the Peshwa.

On Baji Rao II's orders, he was executed brutally in 1801.

### Why was the execution important?

Yashwantrao regarded his brother's death as a serious provocation.

The conflict intensified, and Holkar began preparing to confront the forces of Scindia and the Peshwa.

Historical significance: A dispute between regional Maratha houses was now threatening the central Peshwa government itself.

## 18. Battle of Poona / Hadapsar — 25 October 1802

![ब्रिटिशांपूर्वी मुघल नव्हे मराठेच भारताचे राज्यकर्ते](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/PEfdqa59cunOxz31fPUUl5Ruy6FgBIeYn8wSIk_t9AiNo_dgGrJRF-VvX71L9_lx2ziAl71uFI3dTe11coAh0X88umR7CfifH53dTgh0TE17OeWr9TU4X1qzQUkaOpw-J7ltgIpDGgDOynx84m-iw08irHhiUxtDIFKak4L0I0I?purpose=inline)

This battle is the decisive turning point of Chapter 14.

### Where did it occur?

In the region around Hadapsar, Ghorpadi and Wanowrie, near Pune.

### Who fought?

| Side 1                                 | Side 2                                    |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Yashwantrao Holkar                     | Baji Rao II                               |
| Holkar military forces                 | Peshwa's forces                           |
| Supporters of the Holkar establishment | Troops associated with Daulat Rao Scindia |

### Why did Holkar attack?

Yashwantrao sought to challenge the Scindia–Peshwa alliance, which had become hostile to his interests.

His brother Vithoji's execution had intensified the conflict.

### What happened?

On 25 October 1802, Holkar's forces defeated the combined Peshwa–Scindia army near Pune.

The victory was decisive.

Baji Rao II could no longer depend on the defending army to preserve his position.

### What did Baji Rao II do?

He fled Pune, moving towards fortified positions and eventually reaching the western coast.

He then sought assistance from the British East India Company.

### Why was this historically disastrous?

The principal Maratha political leader was now requesting foreign military support to restore him against another Maratha chief.

Compare this with 1775.

UPSC distinction: The Battle of Poona in 1802 was primarily an internal Maratha conflict. It was not the battle in which British troops defeated Holkar.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer identifies 25 October 1802 as the date of Holkar's victory over the Peshwa–Scindia coalition.
## 19. Why did Baji Rao II seek British protection?

### What options did he have?

After the defeat near Pune, Baji Rao II had lost effective control of his government.

He could attempt to:

1. Negotiate with Yashwantrao Holkar.
    
2. Rely on Daulat Rao Scindia to recover Pune.
    
3. Gather new Maratha supporters.
    
4. Ask the British for military assistance.

He chose the fourth option.

### Why were the British willing to help?

The Company's Governor-General was **Lord Richard Wellesley** (1798–1805).

Wellesley pursued an expansionist policy based heavily on military alliances and subsidiary arrangements with Indian rulers.

The Company had recently defeated Tipu Sultan in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799).

This had strengthened British military and political influence in southern India.

### What did Wellesley want?

He wanted to prevent a powerful independent Maratha coalition from threatening British interests.

An alliance with the Peshwa could allow the Company to influence the principal political centre of Maratha power.

The British opportunity: Restore Baji Rao II using Company troops, but make him dependent on British protection.

## 20. Treaty of Bassein (Vasai) — 31 December 1802

![File:Main entrance to the Citadel at Vasai Fort.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/qiTYA7yeJHUANgHr_Bha6Hk4IlBpBSXubhSHDUDaYPc3EWt-UGsHaQPUFQiny3XknOPOU03F3T-U1Wgky6lLz3nTYKCKaIr8x35VWPONGkl8EzCGID6FKzwnCFQlTrq52_Rm9JghSCMQ3gSV52Sb5wXjN2LDa4jBhWG8Xiw9GW8?purpose=inline)

This is one of the most important treaties in modern Indian history.

### Who signed it?

Baji Rao II, the Peshwa, and representatives of the British East India Company.

The agreement was concluded at Bassein, or Vasai, on 31 December 1802.

### Main provisions

| Provision                       | Meaning                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| British military protection     | Company agreed to assist in restoring and defending the Peshwa                                  |
| Subsidiary force                | Approximately 6,000 Company troops were to be maintained for his protection                     |
| Financial cost                  | The Peshwa would assign territory yielding approximately ₹26 lakh annually to support the force |
| Foreign relations               | The Peshwa accepted major restrictions on independent diplomacy                                 |
| Other European officers         | Employment of Europeans from rival powers was restricted                                        |
| Disputes with Nizam and Gaekwad | Certain matters would be submitted to Company mediation or arbitration                          |
| Political restoration           | The Company agreed to restore Baji Rao II to power                                              |

The original treaty text is preserved in Aitchison's collection of treaties and sanads.
### What is a subsidiary alliance?

A subsidiary alliance was an arrangement in which an Indian ruler accepted the military protection of the Company while making major concessions.

These often included:

- Maintaining Company troops at the ruler's expense.
    
- Restricting independent diplomatic relations.
    
- Accepting a British political representative.
    
- Becoming increasingly dependent on British military power.
### Why was the treaty controversial?

Baji Rao II was formally a principal political leader of the Maratha Confederacy.

But he had entered into an agreement with an external power that restricted his independent authority.

Other Maratha chiefs had not collectively approved the arrangement.

### Why did Scindia and Bhonsle oppose it?

They saw the treaty as a threat to the political autonomy of the Maratha states.

British forces could now intervene directly in the affairs of the Peshwa and potentially influence the wider Confederacy.

### Did the treaty immediately abolish the Peshwaship?

No.

Baji Rao II remained Peshwa.

The British promised to restore him to office.

But his freedom to conduct diplomacy and organize defence was substantially restricted.

UPSC Mains interpretation: The Treaty of Bassein did not instantly annex the Maratha state. It marked a major loss of political autonomy and created conditions for further British intervention.

## 21. Why was the Treaty of Bassein a turning point?

Internal rivalry

Scindia versus Holkar; Baji Rao II depended heavily on Scindia.

25 October 1802 — Battle of Poona

Holkar defeated the Peshwa–Scindia forces.

Baji Rao II fled

He sought British military protection.

31 December 1802 — Treaty of Bassein

Company obtained a subsidiary alliance with the Peshwa.

Political consequences

Other Maratha chiefs opposed British intervention, leading towards renewed war.

### Three principal consequences

1. British influence increased: The Company obtained a major role in the military and diplomatic affairs of the Peshwa's government.

2. Maratha divisions deepened: Scindia, Bhonsle and Holkar did not accept a unified policy towards the Company.

3. Independence was weakened: The Maratha political system still existed, but its principal office had become dependent on British military protection.

Important: The Second Anglo-Maratha War began in 1803, not on the exact day the Treaty of Bassein was signed in 1802.

## 24. UPSC Mains — model answer

Question: “Internal divisions within the Maratha Confederacy paved the way for British intervention through the Treaty of Bassein.” Examine. (250 words)

The period between 1782 and 1802 witnessed both significant Maratha military influence and increasing political fragmentation. The Treaty of Bassein represented the culmination of conflicts among the Peshwa, ministers and regional Maratha chiefs.

Decentralized political structure: Powerful houses such as Scindia, Holkar, Gaekwad and Bhonsle controlled substantial military and revenue resources. Their competing interests made unified political action difficult.

Rival political leadership: Nana Fadnavis exercised considerable authority at Pune, while Mahadji Scindia became dominant in northern India. Although both strengthened Maratha power, rivalry over political influence complicated coordination.

Succession instability: The death of Madhavrao II in 1795 produced a struggle over the Peshwaship. Baji Rao II eventually became Peshwa in 1796, but tensions with Nana and Daulat Rao Scindia contributed to instability.

Military factionalism: The rivalry between the Scindia and Holkar establishments intensified. The execution of Vithoji Holkar in 1801 further provoked Yashwantrao Holkar.

British opportunity: In October 1802, Holkar defeated the combined forces of Baji Rao II and Scindia near Pune. The Peshwa fled and sought British military assistance.

The Treaty of Bassein (31 December 1802) obliged Baji Rao II to accept Company troops, financial obligations and restrictions on independent foreign relations.

Thus, Maratha internal divisions enabled the Company to intervene in the principal political institution of the Confederacy. The treaty marked a major reduction in Maratha autonomy and prepared the ground for the Second Anglo-Maratha War.
