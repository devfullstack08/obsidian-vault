## The major political change of 1636

This event is essential to understanding why Shivaji's political career developed around Pune.

After years of conflict, the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan defeated the remaining Nizam Shahi resistance in 1636, bringing the Ahmadnagar Sultanate to an end as an independent power.

The Mughals then reached agreements with Bijapur and Golconda.

Shahaji, who had supported Nizam Shahi resistance, entered Bijapur's service and subsequently became heavily involved in campaigns and administration in Karnataka.

### What happened to Pune?

Shahaji retained substantial interests in the Pune region under Bijapur's authority.

But much of his career required him to remain away from Maharashtra.

His wife Jijabai and young Shivaji consequently became closely associated with the family's Pune establishment.

This arrangement mattered because it gave Shivaji early experience of regional affairs while his father was occupied elsewhere.

UPSC connection: Shivaji's rise was shaped by the Mughal conquest of Ahmadnagar and the subsequent restructuring of power in the Deccan, rather than occurring independently of those developments.

### What was the Pune jagir?

A jagir was a grant of revenue rights assigned by a ruler, usually in connection with service.

Shahaji's Pune jagir included villages, agricultural lands, local chiefs and strategically important routes.

It was not an independent kingdom. It remained within the wider political authority of Bijapur.

### What problems existed there?

The region had suffered from warfare, disruption of agriculture and disputes among hereditary local chiefs.

**Dadoji Konddev**, a trusted administrator associated with Shahaji, played an important role in managing the jagir, restoring cultivation and collecting revenue.

Jijabai also occupied an important position in the family's affairs and Shivaji's upbringing.

Their exact respective roles, particularly claims about who was Shivaji's principal teacher, have been debated. It is safest to distinguish established administrative associations from later popular traditions.

### What did Shivaji learn from this environment?

Rather than imagining only classroom teaching, think of the practical political knowledge available to a young member of a military-aristocratic household.

Shivaji would have encountered questions such as:

- How is agricultural revenue collected?
- Why do village chiefs resist orders?
- How are disputes over hereditary rights settled?
- Which forts control the approaches to Pune?
- Which families can provide soldiers?
- How can a ruler secure local cooperation without constant warfare?

These were precisely the problems he later faced when consolidating authority.

### A political lesson from Pune

A ruler could control a fort but still struggle to control the villages around it.

For stable territorial power, both were necessary:

Military control + local support + revenue collection = sustainable political authority.

## Why were the Mavals important?

**The Maval region**, broadly associated with the valleys west of Pune, contained hills, forests, agricultural settlements and routes through the Western Ghats.

Its inhabitants included farmers, local military retainers and communities familiar with the hill terrain.

Shivaji's expanding following drew on military and social networks in this region.

### What advantages did this provide?

| Advantage                    | Practical importance                             |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Knowledge of mountain passes | Helped forces move along difficult routes        |
| Hill forts                   | Provided defensible positions and storage        |
| Local networks               | Assisted recruitment, information and provisions |
| Small mobile forces          | Could avoid unfavourable open-field battles      |
| Control over valleys         | Helped collect revenue and protect settlements   |

### Important correction

Do not imagine that all Maval inhabitants automatically supported Shivaji.

Several local families had their own political interests. Some cooperated; others resisted or remained loyal to Bijapur.

Historical significance: Shivaji's early success depended on winning, negotiating with and sometimes coercing local elites—not simply on geography.

## The beginning of territorial expansion (c. 1646–1648)

By the mid-1640s, Shivaji was beginning to act with increasing independence in the western Deccan.

His first major objective was to control strategic forts near his family's territorial base.

### Why start with forts rather than large cities?

A large city required money, administrators and troops to defend.

A strong hill fort could be held by a comparatively smaller garrison and used to store supplies.

Control of several forts could also protect the surrounding countryside and allow further expansion.

3. Purandar

Strategically situated southeast of Pune.

Control over this area strengthened Shivaji's regional position. Events around its acquisition unfolded amid local political rivalries.

### Why did this alarm Bijapur?

The Adil Shahi government expected the holders of jagirs and local military chiefs to recognize its superior authority.

A jagirdar's son independently acquiring forts and strengthening his armed following threatened that political relationship.

Even if Shivaji had not formally proclaimed sovereignty, his actions increasingly challenged Bijapur's control.

Cause → effect:
> Shivaji secures strategic forts
> Controls nearby routes and settlements
> Gains greater revenue and military resources
> Increasing conflict with Bijapur

## Shahaji's imprisonment (1648–1649)

Bijapur's relations with Shahaji and his family became strained during the late 1640s.

In 1648, Shahaji was detained by Bijapur authorities while serving in the south. He was subsequently released in 1649.

### Why was Shahaji imprisoned?

The episode occurred amid suspicions about Shahaji's activities and the growing independence of his sons.

However, historians should be cautious about explaining the imprisonment entirely as punishment for Shivaji's fort acquisitions. Shahaji also had his own complicated military and political relations with Bijapur.

### What was the effect on Shivaji?

It exposed a fundamental political danger:

Shivaji's attempts to expand local power could bring retaliation not only against his forces but also against his family.

He therefore had to balance expansion with diplomacy and the protection of his existing possessions.

## Consolidation of local authority (c. 1649–1655)

After the disturbances of the late 1640s, Shivaji's early state-building continued.

The central challenge was no longer merely acquiring forts. He had to establish authority over neighbouring landholding families and settle conflicting claims.

### Why were local chiefs an obstacle?

A deshmukh might possess hereditary rights over multiple villages and maintain an armed following.

Such a chief might accept Shivaji's authority if cooperation protected his rights.

But another chief could believe loyalty to Bijapur offered greater security.

Thus, Shivaji's expansion involved political bargaining as well as coercion.

### One example: The Jedhe family

Kanhoji Jedhe, an influential deshmukh associated with the Maval region, is remembered for supporting the Bhonsle cause.

His position shows why alliances with powerful local hereditary families mattered.

Their cooperation could provide manpower, local information and legitimacy among neighbouring elites.

### Administrative significance

The emerging Maratha leadership had to build a relationship between:

- Fort commanders.
    
- Village-level officials.
    
- Revenue collectors.
    
- Military chiefs.
    
- The central leadership around Shivaji.
    

This would become essential as the territorial base expanded.

## Conquest of Javli (1656–1657) — a turning point

![Visit Mahabaleshwar Hill Station To See Attractive Spots, Sightseeing, Famous Places, Tourist Points, Venna Lake, Mapro Garden & Market | Ravine Hotel](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/3Wk3ONIMeJL3bIMyLRPDi6XSaDT8Dyd6U7AmymeT-QJoDiY4tVoJBtiY2cDd0fSjAfG5XTksfXM457zGxBYFtPZXZtASN5gwB5m_OthV6cIXkALc77YBSEKLoELJEZY0i8zRlToQTlslFhACjuS_7q3UxcALzeDmoEI-b0v3RwE?purpose=inline)
### Where was Javli?

Javli (Jawali) was a rugged, forested region near Mahabaleshwar in the Western Ghats.

It was strategically situated between the Deccan plateau and the Konkan.

The area was dominated by the More (Moré) family, influential local chiefs associated with Bijapur.

### Why did Shivaji want Javli?

There were three main reasons.

1. Strategic routes: Javli commanded approaches through difficult hill country.

2. Territorial continuity: Control of Javli could connect and strengthen Shivaji's positions in the western Deccan.

3. Political threat: A powerful neighbouring chief who did not recognize Shivaji's authority could obstruct expansion.

### What happened?

Relations between Shivaji and the More family deteriorated, leading to the violent takeover of Javli, generally dated 1656, though some chronologies place the decisive annexation in 1657.

The More leadership was displaced and Shivaji secured the region.

UPSC qualification: Do not portray Javli as a peaceful transfer of territory. The episode involved coercion and killings, and accounts differ over the precise sequence and circumstances.

### Why was Javli so important?

Its conquest gave Shivaji a stronger base in the Western Ghats and improved his access towards the Konkan.

It also enabled him to build a major defensive stronghold that would become famous in 1659.

That fort was Pratapgad.

##  Construction of Pratapgad Fort (c. 1656)

![Maratha Forts That Won UNESCO Recognition](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/I3zM5pE1fyn04BkFnam8eU84ARlTFqRTUhS8gSjPnaiOQQZuxibWuEL55kMsel60ZCznkEjokMR_8whkimhwPbHejIhbVTPd_VTAk3suElf-F-aH9MKC9vGMfxRoZ53gWuwNiIWpYu1tnfHuXxBDSwwYojTl-fVJmQLrPV6HtNk?purpose=inline)

Pratapgad was constructed under Shivaji's authority in the strategically important Javli region.

It stood on elevated terrain surrounded by steep slopes and difficult approaches.

### Why build this fort?

A fortified position at Pratapgad could help:

- Secure control over the recently acquired Javli territory.
    
- Protect approaches from the Konkan.
    
- Observe military movement in the surrounding valleys.
    
- Support forces operating in difficult hill country.
    

Its location later played a crucial role when Bijapur sent an army to suppress Shivaji.

### Why is this historically significant?

Javli represents an important transition:

Before Javli: Shivaji was consolidating an expanding group of forts around Pune.

After Javli: He possessed a stronger and more connected territorial base extending into strategically important Western Ghat country.

## The political crisis of 1657–1658

By the late 1650s, the wider political environment changed again.

### What was happening in the Mughal Empire?

Shah Jahan became seriously ill in 1657, triggering a succession struggle among his sons.

The principal contenders were:

- Dara Shikoh
    
- Shah Shuja
    
- Aurangzeb
    
- Murad Baksh
    

Aurangzeb eventually emerged victorious and became emperor in 1658.

### Why did this matter to Shivaji?

Aurangzeb had previously served as Mughal viceroy in the Deccan.

In 1657, Shivaji's forces had already raided Mughal-held territory, including the Ahmadnagar-Junnar frontier region, creating tensions with the Mughals.

The Mughal succession struggle temporarily diverted imperial attention and resources.

Shivaji therefore operated in a complex environment in which both Bijapur and Mughal authority faced political and military pressures.

Important: Shivaji did not simply act as Aurangzeb's permanent ally against Bijapur. His relations with the Mughals shifted according to circumstances and were already strained before Aurangzeb became emperor.

## Why did Bijapur send Afzal Khan in 1659?

By 1659, Shivaji's territorial expansion could no longer be treated as a minor local disturbance.

He had acquired forts, established a military following and challenged Bijapur's regional authority.

### What did Bijapur want?

The Adil Shahi government sought to:

1. Reassert its sovereignty over the territories Shivaji controlled.
    
2. Suppress his increasingly independent military activities.
    
3. Recover or neutralize strategic forts.
    
4. Prevent other local chiefs from following his example.
    

To achieve this, Bijapur entrusted a major campaign to **Afzal Khan**, an experienced military commander.

### Who was Afzal Khan?

Afzal Khan was a senior general of the Bijapur Sultanate.

He had substantial experience in Deccan political and military affairs and commanded a larger expeditionary force than Shivaji could easily confront in open terrain.

### Why did Shivaji avoid an open battle?

Shivaji's principal advantages lay in his regional military networks, his knowledge of the hills and his defensive strongholds.

Fighting a large Bijapur force on open ground could have sacrificed those advantages.

The Javli-Pratapgad region, by contrast, offered restricted approaches and conditions more favourable to carefully positioned local forces.

## Battle of Pratapgad — 10 November 1659

![After 350 Years, Shivaji Maharaj’s Wagh Nakh Returns to Bharat | Shiv Jayanti | Prachyam](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/v2HuDErFqFKnMQ1Vz76OhHBcZ1flYpbygZUfkZXOpu__jxXOp_cJ3N_7Hpa6nZE0Hp49wo-M7M7pRcOQKb5S-m57HG6sa0efebYnMJTb3S8LtzOkYWFidBqB4YRsYvSwPCzxLr068q3IF9EtINZ9LgsjAQosUl7UXH25LFdeVDI?purpose=inline)

### Step 1. The Bijapur army approached

Afzal Khan entered the region with a substantial force, intending to suppress Shivaji.

Shivaji positioned himself around Pratapgad rather than committing his army to a conventional open-field engagement.

### Step 2. Negotiations were arranged

The two sides exchanged messages and agreed to a personal meeting near the fort.

The meeting was intended, at least formally, to discuss terms and resolve the confrontation.

Both leaders had strong reasons to distrust the other.

### Step 3. The meeting turned violent

On 10 November 1659, Shivaji and Afzal Khan met under arrangements that limited the attendants immediately accompanying them.

The encounter turned into a physical struggle.

Afzal Khan was killed.

### Who attacked first?

This is a major issue of historical interpretation.

Traditional Maratha narratives frequently describe Afzal Khan attempting to kill Shivaji during an embrace, after which Shivaji defended himself using concealed weapons, including the **wagh nakh** (tiger-claw weapon) and a dagger.

Other accounts present different interpretations of the planning and responsibility for the first attack.

For UPSC, the firmly established outcome is more important than treating one dramatic version as unquestionably proven: Afzal Khan died during the meeting, and Shivaji's forces subsequently defeated the Bijapur expedition.

### Step 4. Shivaji's forces attacked

Following Afzal Khan's death, Maratha forces positioned around Pratapgad attacked the Bijapur troops.

The loss of the commander, the difficult terrain and the Marathas' preparations contributed to the defeat of the expedition.

### Step 5. The result

The victory strengthened Shivaji's reputation and military position.

It also enabled further expansion into areas associated with southern Maharashtra and the Konkan.

The success increased the importance of Shivaji as a regional power, and made renewed conflict with Bijapur and the Mughals increasingly likely.

## Why was the victory at Pratapgad a turning point?

| Before Pratapgad                                     | After Pratapgad                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shivaji was an expanding regional leader             | He had defeated a major Bijapur expedition                            |
| His authority was concentrated in a limited region   | Greater opportunity for territorial expansion                         |
| Bijapur could treat him as a rebellious subordinate  | He had demonstrated substantial military independence                 |
| His reputation depended on local acquisitions        | His victory attracted wider political attention                       |
| His military resources were comparatively restricted | Captured supplies and increased influence supported further campaigns |

### Three major reasons for success

1. Terrain: The Western Ghat environment reduced the advantages a large expeditionary force could ordinarily enjoy.

2. Preparation: Shivaji's forces were positioned to exploit the outcome of the negotiations.

3. Leadership disruption: Afzal Khan's death seriously affected the command and morale of Bijapur's expedition.

UPSC Mains conclusion: Pratapgad demonstrated how strategic terrain, military preparation and an effective local command structure could offset an opponent's superiority in numbers and resources.
