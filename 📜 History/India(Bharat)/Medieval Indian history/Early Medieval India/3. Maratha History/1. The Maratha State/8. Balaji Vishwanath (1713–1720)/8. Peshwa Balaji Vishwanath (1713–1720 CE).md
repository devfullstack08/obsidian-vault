**Full name:** Balaji Vishwanath Bhat
**Peshwa:** 1713–1720
**Chhatrapati:** Shahu I
**Family:** Bhat family of Shrivardhan, Konkan
**Successor:** His son, Baji Rao I
**Death:** April 1720, Saswad

Central question: How did Balaji Vishwanath strengthen Shahu's divided kingdom, obtain powerful revenue rights from the Mughals, and lay the foundation for the rise of the Peshwas?

In Chapter 7, we learned how Shahu returned from Mughal captivity in 1707, defeated Tarabai's supporters at Khed, and established his court at Satara.

But winning the throne was not the same as controlling the entire Maratha state.

Shahu faced powerful nobles, a rival royal court, Mughal political pressure and independent-minded military commanders.

Balaji Vishwanath became important because he could solve problems through diplomacy, financial organization and political alliances, not merely through warfare.

## 1. The political situation in 1713

Imagine Maharashtra in 1713.

The Mughal Empire was weakening after Aurangzeb's death. Yet it still maintained armies, provincial governors and imperial claims over much of the Deccan.

The Marathas had survived the long Mughal war, but they were politically divided.

|Power|Leadership|Main challenge or objective|
|---|---|---|
|Satara Marathas|Shahu I|Unite chiefs and consolidate royal authority|
|Kolhapur Marathas|Tarabai's faction, then Sambhaji II from 1714|Maintain a rival dynastic claim|
|Mughal Empire|Farrukhsiyar (1713–1719)|Preserve imperial authority amid court conflicts|
|Konkan coast|Kanhoji Angre|Maintain naval and territorial autonomy|
|Deccan Mughal administration|Imperial governors, including Nizam-ul-Mulk and later Husain Ali Khan|Protect Mughal revenue and political interests|
### Shahu's biggest problem

He was recognized as Chhatrapati by important chiefs, but he lacked complete control over the military and fiscal resources nominally associated with his crown.

A commander could acknowledge Shahu one year and support a rival the next.

The central problem was therefore:

How could Shahu convert disputed royal legitimacy into dependable political authority?

## 2. Who was Balaji Vishwanath before becoming Peshwa?

Balaji Vishwanath came from a Chitpavan Brahmin family associated with Shrivardhan on the Konkan coast.

He began his career in revenue and administrative service and gained experience working with Maratha regional leaders.

He later developed connections with Shahu's influential commanders, including Dhanaji Jadhav.

### Why did his background matter?

Unlike a chief whose power depended mainly on inherited military forces, Balaji developed influence through:

- Understanding revenue and accounts.
    
- Negotiating with local notables.
    
- Managing political relationships.
    
- Organizing support among commanders.
    
- Knowledge of the Konkan and its maritime interests.

He became an important supporter of Shahu during the succession struggle.

### Was he the first Peshwa in Maratha history?

No.

Shivaji had already established the office of Peshwa as part of the **Ashta Pradhan system**.

**Moropant Trimbak Pingle** was among the important Peshwas under Shivaji.

Balaji Vishwanath's significance was that he became the first Peshwa of the influential Bhat family line, whose successors eventually dominated Maratha politics.
### His appointment

On 17 November 1713, Shahu appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa, according to the chronology accepted by historians such as Jadunath Sarkar.

The precise sequence of the Angre crisis and the appointment differs across older narratives, but the appointment year 1713 is firmly established.
## 3. First major challenge — Kanhoji Angre and the Konkan coast

![File:Vijaydurg Fort 08.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8MsnA6ht1WolYvVHaED0FNYe-Qom8YWBaYqGTkD7DzsHqpTDmUu6sqB1vvmCQDHN1yaunCjoYXDuaPTTzUdcR6LUoUHLf2fME74VrFRGeMAiS6mgDlsxwqvbyXHS8IHW0L0xry9zqiG0WCJKbTVBL0m6C8ePoYtFjI12TRRNVfQ?purpose=inline)

### Who was Kanhoji Angre?

Kanhoji Angre (c. 1669–1729) was a powerful Maratha maritime commander.

He held important coastal fortifications and maintained a fleet capable of challenging regional maritime rivals, including European trading companies.
### Why was he difficult for Shahu to control?

Kanhoji's authority depended on his ships, forts, coastal revenues and local military networks.

Shahu's government at Satara did not possess the immediate capacity to defeat him easily.

Moreover, Kanhoji had supported the rival royal establishment associated with Tarabai.
### What made the situation worse?

Kanhoji extended his influence inland, seizing important positions such as **Lohagad** and **Rajmachi**.

He also defeated and detained **Bahiroji Pinge**, who had held the Peshwa office.

This demonstrated how serious the challenge had become.
### What were Shahu's options?

He could either launch a costly military campaign or attempt to win Kanhoji's allegiance.

Balaji Vishwanath favoured negotiation.
## 4. Agreement with Kanhoji Angre (1714)

Balaji understood that Kanhoji was unlikely to abandon his authority and maritime resources merely because Shahu demanded obedience.

A successful agreement therefore had to recognize Kanhoji's position while securing his political support.
### What did Balaji offer?

The arrangements recognized Kanhoji's status as Sarkhel, or naval commander, and confirmed important forts and interests under his control.

In return, Kanhoji accepted Shahu's authority and withdrew support from the rival Kolhapur claimant.

The settlement is generally dated 1714.
### What changed?

| Before the agreement                          | After the agreement                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Kanhoji supported Shahu's rival               | Kanhoji recognized Shahu                  |
| Shahu faced a powerful coastal opponent       | Shahu gained a valuable maritime ally     |
| Naval and inland authorities were in conflict | Political cooperation became possible     |
| Military confrontation threatened resources   | Negotiated accommodation preserved forces |
### Why is it important?

Balaji turned a powerful opponent into an ally without having to destroy his military establishment.

This was a major diplomatic achievement.

It also demonstrated a pattern that became increasingly important in Maratha politics: regional chiefs might recognize the Chhatrapati while retaining substantial control over local military and revenue resources.

Historian Richard M. Eaton identifies Kanhoji's shift as a major reversal for Tarabai's faction.
## 5. The Kolhapur palace coup (1714)

In Chapter 7, we learned that Tarabai upheld her son Shivaji II's claim against Shahu.

However, Tarabai faced problems inside her own court.
### Who opposed Tarabai?

Rajasbai, another wife of Rajaram, had a son named Sambhaji II.

Her supporters wanted Sambhaji II to replace Tarabai's son Shivaji II as ruler of the Kolhapur branch.

### What happened in 1714?

A palace coup displaced Tarabai and her son.

Rajasbai's son Sambhaji II became the ruler of Kolhapur.

Tarabai and Shivaji II were confined.

### Did this end the Satara–Kolhapur rivalry?

No.

The claimant at Kolhapur changed, but the competing royal establishment continued.

### Why was this helpful to Shahu?

Tarabai had been a formidable political leader.

Her removal weakened her personal network and changed the balance of power among the rival Maratha factions.

However, Shahu still faced Sambhaji II and other powerful chiefs.

UPSC distinction: Tarabai was displaced in 1714 but did not disappear permanently from Maratha politics. She returned to prominence many years later.

## 6. Balaji's second challenge — powerful Maratha chiefs

Winning Kanhoji's allegiance did not resolve all of Shahu's difficulties.

Some chiefs possessed considerable autonomy and maintained their own armed supporters.

Prominent figures included **Chandrasen Jadhav**, **Damaji Thorat** and several other regional commanders.

### Why were chiefs difficult to control?

During the long war against Aurangzeb, individual commanders had acquired responsibility for collecting resources and maintaining troops.

This strengthened their local positions.

But after the war, Shahu wanted greater political coordination.

Some chiefs resisted interference in their territories or revenue arrangements.
### What did Balaji do?

He combined negotiation, military pressure and appointments to weaken opposition.

For example, Damaji Thorat presented a serious challenge around the Pune region. Balaji himself was at one stage detained during dealings with Thorat before the conflict was eventually resolved in Shahu's favour.
### Why is this important?

It shows that Balaji was not simply a brilliant minister who faced no resistance.

Even the Peshwa could be vulnerable to powerful local chiefs.

His achievement lay in gradually building a more dependable network of political relationships.

UPSC interpretation: The Peshwa's authority expanded because the Chhatrapati needed effective coordination among semi-autonomous military and revenue holders.

## 7. What was happening in the Mughal Empire?

Now we must shift our attention from Satara to Delhi.

This is essential because Balaji's most important achievement was connected with Mughal court politics.

### Mughal emperors after Aurangzeb

| Emperor                        | Reign     | Historical context                                 |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Bahadur Shah I                 | 1707–1712 | Struggled to consolidate authority after Aurangzeb |
| Jahandar Shah                  | 1712–1713 | Short reign amid succession disputes               |
| Farrukhsiyar                   | 1713–1719 | Conflict with the powerful Sayyid Brothers         |
| Rafi-ud-Darajat                | 1719      | Briefly ruled after Farrukhsiyar                   |
| Rafi-ud-Daulah / Shah Jahan II | 1719      | Another short-lived emperor                        |
| Muhammad Shah                  | 1719–1748 | Became emperor amid continued factional politics   |
### Why had the empire become unstable?

Aurangzeb's long Deccan campaigns had imposed severe military and financial burdens.

After his death, struggles among princes and powerful nobles weakened imperial coordination.

Provincial governors and regional rulers increasingly pursued their own interests.

Important: The Mughal Empire did not collapse immediately in 1707. It still possessed substantial institutions, armies and political legitimacy. But imperial authority had become more difficult to enforce.
## 8. The Sayyid Brothers — why were they called kingmakers?

![File:Miniature. ‘The Mughal Emperor Bahadur Shah on his throne’ India, Mughal; c. 1707-1708.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/yNdcV-r0jccdmIcHTLJ6leYt813p1Rgr2D-k5GFa-zrU7Rw5DH8Ku57FrkIIPGc12Ehd1WenLKcnSrfZK9LvD5LuZ0diwCLHXgCRQtMR4ZiViGvC9J4pM2dvb5Uzc4UKig_N5GRHlJGJJltt7Hvdlq6NziLzPAEuiTUAoRh7HWE?purpose=inline)

Two powerful nobles became central to Mughal politics:

| Person                 | Position                                              |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Sayyid Abdullah Khan   | Leading noble and wazir (chief minister)              |
| Sayyid Husain Ali Khan | Powerful military commander; appointed Deccan viceroy |
The brothers played an important role in installing Farrukhsiyar as Mughal emperor in 1713.

But their relationship with the emperor deteriorated.
### Why did Farrukhsiyar turn against them?

The Sayyid Brothers possessed extensive military and administrative influence.

Farrukhsiyar wanted greater independent control over his government.

The brothers, meanwhile, feared losing their offices and influence.

This created intense political rivalry.
### Why did the Deccan matter to them?

Husain Ali Khan became Mughal viceroy in the Deccan in 1715.

He faced continuing conflict with Maratha forces.

At the same time, he needed to protect himself against political intrigues at Delhi.

Therefore, he had a strong incentive to reach an agreement with Shahu and Balaji Vishwanath.

The key connection: Mughal political divisions created an opportunity for Maratha diplomacy.

## 9. The 1718 agreement between Shahu and Sayyid Husain Ali Khan

This is one of the most important events in eighteenth-century Indian history.
### What did Husain Ali want?

He wanted:

1. To reduce Maratha attacks in Mughal-controlled Deccan territories.
    
2. To obtain military support against enemies at the imperial court.
    
3. To stabilize the region while his political conflict with Farrukhsiyar continued.
### What did Shahu want?

Shahu wanted:

1. Mughal recognition of his position as the legitimate Maratha ruler.
    
2. Recognition of territories associated with Shivaji's Swarajya.
    
3. Legal rights to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi from the Deccan provinces.
    
4. Release of his mother Yesubai and other royal relatives still in Mughal custody.

Balaji Vishwanath helped negotiate these demands.
### Major terms of the agreement

| Provision                     | Meaning                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recognition of Swarajya       | Shahu's claims to Shivaji's territories and certain later acquisitions were acknowledged |
| Chauth                        | Right to claim 25% of the relevant revenue in six Mughal Deccan provinces                |
| Sardeshmukhi                  | Additional 10% revenue claim                                                             |
| Maratha military assistance   | Shahu agreed to provide approximately 15,000 cavalry for imperial service                |
| Payment to the Mughal emperor | Agreement included an annual payment commonly given as ₹10 lakh                          |
| Security responsibilities     | Marathas were expected to help maintain order and restrain attacks                       |
| Royal family                  | Release of members of Shahu's family formed part of the political settlement             |

The agreement was negotiated in 1718, but full imperial authorization remained contested under Farrukhsiyar. Formal grants followed in 1719.
### What were the six Deccan subahs?

| Province             | Broad region                                 |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Aurangabad           | Central Deccan                               |
| Khandesh             | Northern Maharashtra                         |
| Berar                | Vidarbha region                              |
| Bidar                | Northern Karnataka and adjacent Deccan areas |
| Bijapur              | Western and southern Deccan                  |
| Hyderabad / Golconda | Eastern Deccan                               |
These are historical administrative divisions, not modern state boundaries.

### Why did these rights matter?

Before the agreement, Maratha commanders had often demanded revenue through military pressure or local arrangements.

Imperial recognition could provide a stronger legal and political basis for collecting these revenues.

But the right to collect money was not the same as direct territorial sovereignty.

That distinction is crucial for UPSC.

## 10. Understand Swarajya, Chauth and Sardeshmukhi perfectly

|Term|Meaning|Type of power|
|---|---|---|
|Swarajya|Territories recognized as belonging to Shivaji's kingdom and its successors|Direct political authority|
|Chauth|One-fourth revenue claim in specified territories|Fiscal right|
|Sardeshmukhi|Additional one-tenth revenue claim|Fiscal and political right|

### Simple example
Suppose a Mughal province has an eligible assessed land-revenue base of ₹1,00,000.
Chauth — 25%
# ₹25,000
Sardeshmukhi — 10%
# ₹10,000

The combined nominal claim is ₹35,000 when both percentages are calculated against the same base.

Actual historical assessment and collection could differ according to the wording of grants and local arrangements.

### Did the Mughals give all six provinces to the Marathas?

No.

This is an important examination trap.

The agreement recognized Maratha fiscal claims across these provinces. It did not immediately transfer every Mughal fort, village and administrative office there into direct Maratha sovereignty.

UPSC takeaway: Swarajya is about territorial authority; chauth and sardeshmukhi are primarily revenue claims.

## 11. Why did Farrukhsiyar oppose the agreement?

Farrukhsiyar saw the proposed settlement as politically dangerous.
### From the emperor's perspective

The agreement would give the Marathas major recognized claims over imperial revenues.

It could also strengthen Husain Ali Khan by giving him Maratha military support.

Farrukhsiyar was already struggling to reduce the influence of the Sayyid Brothers.

Therefore, he was reluctant to grant full approval.
### The contradiction

Husain Ali needed Maratha assistance to strengthen his position.

But the emperor feared that the same assistance would make Husain Ali even harder to control.

This was the political crisis that brought Balaji Vishwanath into the affairs of Delhi.
## 12. Balaji Vishwanath's expedition to Delhi (1718–1719)

![Red Fort Delhi white marble architecture with intricate carvings of the Diwan-i-khas.](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/tKNsejW4FuUejY119_17BGzMbqkXm4V8clH1CICvCxQ9CF35aNufb0gD6ZsyEZUZTXG7TpZCHmSsnvsCzlXEHCk9uvks0XH6Td33552n69MEQtPIW4Amw633chDaXdHeevdp95nTRO7pskCQ_JZYYl7WWXzdEp2mrK618BNjzy2qLwY7LsHtX2eV0Zggs_NU?purpose=inline)

### Why did Balaji travel to Delhi?

The proposed Deccan agreement required effective imperial recognition.

But the emperor's conflict with the Sayyid Brothers threatened the settlement.

Balaji accompanied Sayyid Husain Ali Khan with Maratha forces towards Delhi in late 1718.
### Why was this extraordinary?

Only a few decades earlier, Aurangzeb had spent enormous resources trying to destroy Maratha resistance.

Now Maratha forces were participating in struggles at the centre of Mughal politics.

This represented a remarkable shift in the balance of power.
### What happened in Delhi?

Farrukhsiyar's conflict with the Sayyid Brothers escalated.

In February 1719, the brothers deposed him.

He was subsequently killed.

They installed **Rafi-ud-Darajat** as emperor.

### Was Balaji the person who personally killed Farrukhsiyar?

No.

The overthrow was led by the Sayyid Brothers. Balaji and the Maratha contingent were politically and militarily aligned with them.

It is incorrect to credit Balaji alone with removing or killing the emperor.

### Result

The political change at Delhi made it possible to issue the disputed imperial grants to Shahu.

The Delhi expedition also strengthened Balaji's reputation as a statesman capable of influencing events far beyond Maharashtra.

## 13. The imperial grants (Sanads) of 1719 — Balaji's greatest achievement

Sanad means a formal document granting or confirming a right, privilege or position.

After Farrukhsiyar's removal, Shahu's claims received formal imperial authorization through grants issued under the new Mughal regime.
### Three main rights

1. Swarajya

Recognition of Shahu's claims over Shivaji's former kingdom and certain additional territories.

2. Chauth

Recognition of a one-fourth revenue claim in the six Mughal Deccan provinces.

3. Sardeshmukhi

Recognition of an additional one-tenth revenue claim in those territories.

### 1718 versus 1719 — understand the difference

|Year|What happened?|
|---|---|
|1718|Shahu's government and Husain Ali Khan negotiated the political settlement|
|1719|Imperial sanads formally confirmed the principal territorial and revenue claims|

This distinction is particularly valuable in Prelims because study materials sometimes describe the arrangement entirely as the 1718 Treaty or the 1719 Sanads.

Both refer to stages of the same political settlement.

### Why was the achievement important?

Political legitimacy: It strengthened Shahu's claim as the recognized Maratha ruler.

Financial resources: Chauth and sardeshmukhi offered access to revenue beyond the territory directly governed from Satara.

Imperial influence: The Marathas had demonstrated that they could participate in determining political outcomes at Delhi.

Future expansion: The new fiscal claims encouraged commanders to organize campaigns and revenue collection across wider territories.

### Did Mughal recognition mean that Shahu surrendered independence?

The settlement involved an acknowledgment of Mughal suzerainty in formal terms.

However, Shahu's government exercised extensive autonomy, and Mughal officials could not consistently dictate Maratha affairs.

The arrangement was a political compromise between a weakening empire and a rising regional power.

UPSC Mains insight: Legal recognition by the Mughal emperor could strengthen a regional ruler's position even while effective imperial authority was declining.

## 14. Release of Yesubai and other royal relatives (1719)

Remember Chapter 6.

In 1689, the Mughal army captured Raigad and took Sambhaji's widow Yesubai and her son Shahu into custody.

Shahu had been released in 1707.

But Yesubai remained under Mughal control.

### What changed in 1719?

The settlement at Delhi enabled the release of Yesubai and other members of the Maratha royal household.

They returned after years in captivity.
### Why was this politically significant?

It strengthened Shahu's royal position and fulfilled a major personal and dynastic objective.

It also provided a symbolic reversal of Mughal power.

A useful way to remember the sequence:

- 1707: Shahu released.

## 15. How did Balaji lay the foundation of the Maratha Confederacy?

This is a major conceptual question for UPSC.

### The revenue problem

Shahu had secured the right to demand revenue from a vast region.

But his central administration did not possess enough officers and troops to collect it everywhere directly.

It therefore depended on important Maratha chiefs.
### The practical arrangement

Different commanders acquired responsibilities for military operations and revenue collection in particular regions.

They often retained substantial resources to finance their armed followings and meet political obligations.
### What happened over time?

Mughal recognition of wider Maratha revenue claims

Revenue collection delegated among chiefs and officials

Some chiefs accumulate troops, revenue and regional influence

Increasingly decentralized Maratha political system
### Was the Maratha Confederacy formally founded in 1719?

No.

The grants and Balaji's political arrangements helped create conditions for its emergence.

The later confederacy developed gradually as the Peshwas and powerful regional houses expanded their territories and acquired increasingly independent authority.

The familiar **Scindia**, **Holkar**, **Gaekwad** and **Nagpur Bhonsle** political centres became especially influential in the following decades.

Historical interpretation: The very system that facilitated Maratha expansion also strengthened regional chiefs who could later challenge central coordination.

## 16. Why did the office of Peshwa become increasingly powerful?

Under Shivaji, the Peshwa had been an important minister subordinate to the Chhatrapati.

Under Shahu, Balaji's responsibilities expanded.
### Compare the two situations

|Under Shivaji|Under Shahu and Balaji|
|---|---|
|Strong personal leadership by the Chhatrapati|Shahu needed assistance coordinating influential chiefs|
|Peshwa was one of eight major ministers|Peshwa became an increasingly central political organizer|
|Kingdom concentrated in western and southern India|Fiscal and military claims expanded over a much wider area|
|Royal authority depended heavily on direct command|Political coordination depended increasingly on negotiation and delegation|
|Ministerial offices were not permanently hereditary|The Bhat family began a succession of powerful Peshwas|
### Did Balaji legally make the Peshwaship hereditary?

It is common to read that Balaji made the post hereditary.

A more precise statement is:

Balaji established the Bhat family's dominance of the Peshwa office, and the succession of his son Baji Rao I in 1720 strengthened its hereditary character.

The office did not instantly become an independent monarchy through a single constitutional act.

This distinction matters for analytical answers.

## 17. What were Balaji's limitations?

A balanced assessment is important.

Balaji was highly successful diplomatically, but he did not create a perfectly unified Maratha state.
### Continuing problems

- Kolhapur rivalry: Sambhaji II continued to challenge Shahu's position.
    
- Autonomous chiefs: Powerful commanders retained independent interests.
    
- Uncertain collections: Having a recognized right to revenue did not guarantee it could be collected peacefully.
    
- Mughal opposition: Not every imperial governor accepted the new arrangements.
    
- Dependence on military chiefs: The government continued to need negotiated cooperation.

### Why were the 1719 grants not the end of the struggle?

A document issued in Delhi could recognize a right, but enforcing that right across the Deccan required troops, local alliances, administrators and political pressure.

Balaji secured a valuable legal foundation.

His successors would still have to transform these claims into effective power.

## 18. Death of Balaji Vishwanath (1720)

Balaji Vishwanath died at Saswad, near Pune, in April 1720.

Many standard reference works give 12 April 1720; some chronologies give 2 April. For UPSC, his tenure 1713–1720 and the succession of Baji Rao I in 1720 are more secure and important than choosing between these disputed death dates.
### What did he leave behind?

He had:

1. Strengthened Shahu's political position.
    
2. Won Kanhoji Angre's cooperation.
    
3. Managed important conflicts with regional chiefs.
    
4. Built an alliance with the Sayyid Brothers.
    
5. Secured the imperial sanads of 1719.
    
6. Helped obtain the release of Shahu's family.
    
7. Established a powerful position for the Bhat family in Maratha administration.

### Who succeeded him?

His son, Baji Rao I, became Peshwa in 1720.

Baji Rao would use military expansion on a much larger scale.

That belongs to Chapter 9.

## 21. UPSC Mains model answer

Question: Balaji Vishwanath laid the political and financial foundations of the rise of the Peshwas and the expansion of Maratha power. Examine. (250 words)

Balaji Vishwanath, who served as Peshwa under Chhatrapati Shahu from 1713 to 1720, played a decisive role in consolidating Maratha power during a period of dynastic rivalry and Mughal political decline.

Political consolidation: Following Shahu's victory over Tarabai's faction, several powerful Maratha chiefs retained substantial independence. Balaji negotiated with important commanders and helped strengthen Shahu's authority. His agreement with Kanhoji Angre in 1714 secured the cooperation of a major naval leader.

Diplomatic success: Balaji exploited the conflict between Mughal emperor Farrukhsiyar and the Sayyid Brothers. The agreement with Husain Ali Khan in 1718 enabled Maratha participation in the Delhi political crisis.

Revenue expansion: The imperial sanads of 1719 recognized Shahu's territorial claims and granted important rights to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi in six Mughal Deccan provinces. These claims significantly strengthened the financial prospects of the Maratha state.

Dynastic legitimacy: The settlement also facilitated the release of Yesubai and other members of Shahu's family from Mughal custody.

Institutional transformation: By coordinating powerful regional chiefs and expanding the responsibilities of the Peshwa, Balaji laid the foundations for the subsequent dominance of the Bhat family.

However, regional rivalries and fiscal decentralization remained unresolved.

Thus, Balaji's achievements transformed the Peshwaship into a central institution of Maratha politics and prepared the way for the military expansion pursued by his son Baji Rao I.
