Regent, 1700–1707 (continued afterward)

Central question: After Sambhaji was executed and the Mughals captured the Maratha capital, how did the Marathas continue fighting Aurangzeb for another eighteen years?

This chapter explains one of the most important turning points in Maratha history: the transformation of a kingdom centred on one ruler and capital into a dispersed military resistance that the Mughal Empire struggled to defeat.

## 1. Where did the previous chapter end?

In Chapter 5, we studied the reign of Sambhaji Maharaj (1680–1689).

By 1689, Aurangzeb had achieved major military successes:

- 1686: Conquered Bijapur.
    
- 1687: Conquered Golconda.
    
- February 1689: Captured Sambhaji at Sangameshwar.
    
- March 1689: Executed Sambhaji and Kavi Kalash.
    

The Maratha kingdom now faced a crisis.

Its reigning king was dead, its capital was threatened, and the Mughal army possessed overwhelming resources.

Yet Aurangzeb had not eliminated the Maratha military networks or secured lasting control of the countryside.

That difference explains the next eighteen years of history.

## 2. Political situation in 1689 — who would lead the Marathas?

### The royal family

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj

Founder of the sovereign Maratha kingdom

Sambhaji: Died in 1689

Shahu: Young son of Sambhaji and Yesubai

Rajaram: Younger son of Shivaji

Shivaji II : Son of Rajaram and Tarabai

### Why did Shahu not become ruler immediately?

Shahu was a young child in 1689.

The Marathas were engaged in an existential military struggle, so they needed a leader capable of directing political and military affairs.

Rajaram, Shivaji's younger son and Sambhaji's half-brother, was therefore installed as the next Chhatrapati.

### Was this another succession war?

Not immediately on the scale of the 1680 dispute.

The more urgent problem was survival against Aurangzeb.

Shahu's future claim would become decisive after 1707. For now, he remained a child within the royal family.

## 3. Rajaram Maharaj (1689–1700) — the new Chhatrapati

**Born:** 1670
**Father:** Shivaji Maharaj
**Mother:** Soyarabai
**Half-brother:** Sambhaji
**Wife:** Tarabai (among his wives)
**Reign:** 1689–1700
**Death:** 2 March 1700, Sinhagad
### His first challenge

Rajaram inherited a kingdom that had recently lost its reigning ruler.

Aurangzeb believed this was an opportunity to destroy the Maratha monarchy.

Rajaram needed to preserve the dynasty, avoid capture and maintain coordination among commanders in different territories.

He was established as Chhatrapati in 1689.
### Why was his survival important?

If Rajaram had been captured immediately, the Mughals might have gained control of both the Maratha capital and much of the visible royal leadership.

The Maratha commanders would have found it harder to maintain a common source of political legitimacy.

Therefore, saving Rajaram became an urgent military priority.

## 4. Siege and fall of Raigad (1689)

![Book tickets to Raigad Fort](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/J9Q9JJ31nZPifnFltd1CTF_KDc4dMyVa0qk7wfGZo_ntiiMfCV2dNv0T3RmW0Cz9L6pVsvGDrn6GztmjuibxpIZxVKkQG40EGt2KvVNMR6f7sZ6jidut3hkN9mFrEJ8qbYrPxvDLp0EcBl2QHf2z4mKNDnLG7Zk-v7BSF8NrDy8?purpose=inline)

### Why did the Mughals target Raigad?

Raigad was the capital established by Shivaji and the centre of Maratha royal authority.

It contained important political and administrative establishments.

Capturing Raigad would allow Aurangzeb to claim that he had destroyed the centre of the Maratha kingdom.

### What happened?

In 1689, Mughal forces under **Zulfiqar Khan**, also known earlier as Itiqad Khan, laid siege to Raigad.

Rajaram escaped from the fort before it fell.

On 3 November 1689, the Mughals captured Raigad.

### Who was captured?

The Mughals took several members of the royal household into custody, including:

- **Yesubai**, widow of Sambhaji.
    
- **Shahu**, the young son of Sambhaji.

Shahu remained in Mughal custody for many years.

### Why was this such a serious setback?

The Marathas had lost:

1. Their reigning king Sambhaji.
    
2. Their established capital Raigad.
    
3. Important royal family members.
    
4. Access to administrative and financial resources associated with the capital.

To Aurangzeb, this must have appeared to be a decisive achievement.

But Rajaram had escaped, and Maratha forces remained active across western and southern India.

## 5. Rajaram's escape to Gingee (1689) — the most important strategic decision

![Gingee Fort: The Troy of the East and a Hidden Gem of Tamil Nadu](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/ffFBLvr0U8kN1FcrC0FTqRslkQh7X70jvKoU5dcULVJlMaI0ZG_UKyawX0SY_C7N4YH31z5chqhzNRjHTX_TFmlUGNN-fKjZBCHVNjYYEnP4oIa_qP59_EyxJShmQOVFdovxIYqafddWc6RFm5X0Jn_Eta-y89XomQSpbtjRRYY?purpose=inline)

### Where is Gingee?

Gingee (Jinji/Senji) is in present-day Tamil Nadu, far southeast of the main Maratha territories in Maharashtra.

It contains massive fortified hills, strong defensive positions and a complex of fortifications.

### Why did Rajaram choose Gingee?

Remember Chapter 4.

During his southern expedition in 1677, Shivaji had acquired Gingee and important territories in southern India.

That earlier expansion now became extremely valuable.

The Marathas possessed a distant fortified position where the royal leadership might survive even if Maharashtra came under heavy Mughal attack.
### The escape sequence

1. Raigad — April 1689
    
    Rajaram escaped before the Mughal conquest of the capital.
    
2. Western Maharashtra
    
    He moved through fortified positions, including Pratapgad and Vishalgad, while avoiding Mughal pursuit.
    
3. Southern journey
    
    With assistance from local allies and supporters, his party travelled across the Deccan towards Tamil country.
    
4. Gingee — late 1689
    
    Rajaram established his royal court at Gingee, creating an alternative centre for Maratha government and resistance.
    

Some traditional accounts describe Rajaram travelling in disguise and receiving help from Keladi's ruler Chennamma. The broad journey to Gingee is well established, although its dramatic details vary between sources.

### Why was the strategy effective?

If Aurangzeb concentrated all his forces on Maharashtra, Rajaram's court could operate in southern India.

If the Mughals pursued Rajaram towards Gingee, they would have to stretch their resources over an enormous area.

Meanwhile, Maratha commanders could continue military operations in Maharashtra.

This created a two-region war:
Western Deccan

Maratha cavalry, local administrators and fort commanders maintained resistance in Maharashtra.

Gingee / Southern India

Rajaram's court preserved dynastic leadership far from the original capital.

UPSC significance: Gingee shows why Shivaji's southern expedition was strategically important even after his death.

## 6. How was the Maratha kingdom governed while Rajaram was at Gingee?

Rajaram was far from Maharashtra, but most of the Maratha fighting forces and revenue networks remained in the western Deccan.

Therefore, administration could not depend on the king personally supervising every village and fort.

### Important administrators

| Personality               | Position / importance             | Main role                                                         |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ramchandra Pant Amatya    | Senior minister and administrator | Coordinated administration, finance and resistance in Maharashtra |
| Shankaraji Narayan Sachiv | Important minister                | Managed forts and political administration                        |
| Santaji Ghorpade          | Leading military commander        | Rapid cavalry attacks against Mughal forces                       |
| Dhanaji Jadhav            | Leading military commander        | Campaigns against Mughal forces and territorial recovery          |
| Rajaram                   | Chhatrapati at Gingee             | Provided legitimacy and overall political direction               |
### What was Ramchandra Pant Amatya's contribution?

Ramchandra Pant was an experienced administrator connected with Shivaji's political system.

During Rajaram's absence, he helped coordinate the war effort, revenue collection and the cooperation of regional officers.

He operated with substantial delegated authority.
### Why was this arrangement necessary?

Imagine that a fort commander near Satara needed money, food and reinforcements.

If every decision required a messenger to travel to Gingee and return, military operations could become dangerously slow.

The government therefore needed ministers and commanders who could act without constantly consulting the king.

This made the resistance more flexible.
### A major political transformation

Under Shivaji, central royal supervision had been especially important.

Under Rajaram, the Maratha state increasingly operated through multiple military and administrative centres.

This decentralization helped the kingdom survive, although it also increased the influence and independence of powerful commanders.

## 7. Santaji Ghorpade and Dhanaji Jadhav — commanders who challenged the Mughals

![Aurangzeb fled in fear until his death, terrified by the Maratha war strategies: How Marathas avenged brutal torture of 'Chhaava' Chhatrapati Sambhaji Maharaj](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/BR0tHIlg66ciwJCDGt9__4v3XbF0zIQe5a0NvsbGa91Kr6Iz-P3jklE5RzRxmcgS5lcp3iZlh8Y1vmTNlXLRG5X_bH9ZiG9cX9rfYIuqrnrMHt8TM6D-ovjG1OZTLb4UOySL8T7CGs19dFpkLm1NSEoSRRCtWv8Bnhawd6rFaOI?purpose=inline)

### The military problem

Aurangzeb possessed a large imperial army, artillery and substantial financial resources.

However, maintaining large armies across the Deccan was expensive.

They needed:

- Food and fodder.
    
- Horses and transport animals.
    
- Ammunition.
    
- Secure roads.
    
- Reliable communication.
    
- Money to pay troops.
### What did Santaji and Dhanaji do?

They launched rapid operations against Mughal forces and their supporting networks.

Their methods included:

1. Attacking isolated military detachments.
    
2. Disrupting supply routes and military communications.
    
3. Capturing equipment, animals and valuables.
    
4. Avoiding unfavourable confrontations.
    
5. Forcing Mughal commanders to divert troops to defensive duties.
### Why was this effective?

A large imperial army might capture a fort but still struggle to move safely through the surrounding countryside.

A mobile Maratha force could attack supply convoys, disappear into another region and return when conditions became favourable.

This imposed financial and logistical costs on the Mughal campaign.

### Important events

| Year      | Event                                                                              | Importance                                            |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 1689–1690 | Maratha cavalry raids against imperial positions                                   | Continued resistance after Raigad fell                |
| 1690      | Mughal commander Sarza Khan (Rustam Khan) captured in a Maratha victory            | Demonstrated that Mughal officers remained vulnerable |
| 1692–1693 | Successful operations in Karnataka and against Mughal detachments                  | Reduced pressure on Gingee                            |
| 1695–1696 | Santaji defeated important Mughal commanders, including Qasim Khan and Himmat Khan | Damaged imperial military prestige                    |
| 1697      | Santaji killed after internal rivalries                                            | Loss of a major commander                             |

### Did Santaji and Dhanaji always cooperate?

No.

Their rivalry intensified during the 1690s and eventually developed into serious conflict.

Santaji lost political and military support and was killed in 1697.

This was a significant internal weakness.

UPSC interpretation: Maratha decentralized warfare was militarily effective, but it also created disputes among commanders over rank, authority, resources and rewards.

## 8. Mughal siege of Gingee (1690–1698)

### Aurangzeb's objective

The Mughal emperor wanted to capture Rajaram.

As long as Rajaram remained free, the Marathas retained a recognized royal leader.

Therefore, Aurangzeb ordered an expedition against Gingee.

The principal Mughal commander associated with the siege was Zulfiqar Khan.

### Why did the siege last so long?

Gingee's defences were exceptionally difficult to overcome.

The fortress complex included separated fortified hills, extensive walls and strong defensive positions.

A successful siege required the Mughals to maintain supplies and coordinate troops over a large area.

Meanwhile, Maratha cavalry forces interfered with Mughal movements and communications.

### What happened?

- 1690: Mughal siege operations began.
    
- 1690–1697: Fighting, supply problems and changing military pressures prolonged the campaign.
    
- 1697: Rajaram escaped before the final capture of the fortress.
    
- January 1698: Gingee fell to the Mughals.

### Did Gingee's fall mean a Mughal victory over Rajaram?

No.

The Mughal army captured the fortress, but its principal political target had escaped.

Rajaram returned towards Maharashtra and re-established his court in the western Deccan.

### Why was Gingee strategically successful for the Marathas?

The fortress had kept Rajaram safe for years.

The Mughals committed substantial resources to besieging it while Maratha forces continued fighting elsewhere.

Exam distinction: The Mughals captured Gingee in 1698, but Rajaram was not captured there.

## 9. Rajaram returns to Maharashtra (1697–1698)

After escaping from Gingee, Rajaram travelled back towards the western Deccan.

He later established his principal court at **Satara**.
### Why Satara?

Satara was situated in western Maharashtra, near important Maratha military networks and forts.

The shift helped Rajaram coordinate regional commanders more directly.

### What had changed since 1689?

In 1689, the Marathas had been defending themselves after the death of Sambhaji and the loss of Raigad.

By the late 1690s, they had:

- Preserved the Chhatrapati's leadership.
    
- Continued active cavalry operations.
    
- Recovered or contested important strongholds.
    
- Demonstrated that Mughal forces could be defeated in regional engagements.
    
- Expanded some revenue demands beyond the original core territories.

### Did Rajaram personally command every military operation?

No.

Much of the fighting depended on his generals and administrators.

His primary achievement was maintaining a recognizable political centre while allowing commanders considerable operational flexibility.

### Why is this important for the later Maratha Confederacy?

The influence of individual military commanders and administrators increased during the Mughal-Maratha struggle.

This helped produce the more decentralized structures that developed further in the eighteenth century.

However, the Maratha Confederacy did not formally begin during Rajaram's reign.

That later transformation must be studied separately.

## 11. Maharani Tarabai (1700–1707) — the Maratha resistance continues

![Rani Tarabai: The Warrior Regent – The Royal Women](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/gG92bs1avgVE4rq84Iz7QOLC62XZIIOiQFcM-sBeFAjo9f2DZXRLm7zkZ16M70GqY_slO7nYn4YTf96bL_yMjztOPUpHDcYdJXaNbU_AjEIwh7Hi8hf6UAPceoATRhP4KQ4mBSVoCdZhsHZfFTyih905f-AzvXzTlEz8Wj0h8yA?purpose=inline)

Tarabai (1675–1761) was Rajaram's wife and the daughter of **Hambirrao Mohite**, an important Maratha military commander.

She had been closely connected with the ruling and military establishments.

After Rajaram's death, she assumed leadership of the Maratha government on behalf of her young son.

### Who became Chhatrapati?

Tarabai's son Shivaji II (also called Shivaji III in some numbering traditions) was recognized as ruler.

Tarabai governed as **regent** because her son was a child.

Royal legitimacy
Tarabai — Regent

Effective political and military leadership
### Why was Tarabai's leadership remarkable?

Tarabai took charge when the Maratha kingdom had already fought decades of war.

The Mughals possessed great military resources, and the Marathas faced financial pressure and political divisions.

As regent, Tarabai had to secure the loyalty of commanders, organize revenue and maintain pressure on Mughal forces.

### What did she do?

1. Preserved dynastic legitimacy: Governed in the name of her son.

2. Directed military resistance: Supported continued campaigns against imperial forces.

3. Coordinated commanders: Worked with powerful generals and regional administrators.

4. Maintained revenue collection: Military operations depended on money and supplies from controlled or raided territories.

5. Expanded offensive activity: Maratha forces increasingly carried raids into Mughal-controlled provinces outside the core of Maharashtra.

### Was Tarabai only a ceremonial ruler?

No.

She exercised substantial political leadership and actively directed the resistance.

The Mughal chronicler Khafi Khan, despite writing from a Mughal perspective, acknowledged her capabilities as a leader.

Historian Richard M. Eaton also highlights her importance in the later Mughal-Maratha struggle.

## 12. Aurangzeb's fort campaigns (1700–1705) — why did Mughal victories fail to end the war?

By 1700, Aurangzeb was an elderly emperor but continued personally overseeing military campaigns.

His strategy increasingly emphasized the capture of Maratha forts.

### Why focus on forts?

Forts were important administrative and military centres.

Capturing them could potentially give the Mughals control over routes, resources and strategic positions.

### Important Mughal captures

|Year|Fort|Significance|
|---|---|---|
|1700|Satara|Maratha political centre captured|
|1701|Panhala|Important southern Maharashtra stronghold|
|1702|Vishalgad|Major fortified position|
|1703|Sinhagad|Important fort near Pune|

### Why did these victories not produce lasting control?

The Mughals encountered three interconnected problems.

1. Holding territory was harder than capturing it.

A fort could be seized, but defending it required a permanent garrison, supplies and reliable communications.

2. Maratha forces avoided complete destruction.

Instead of concentrating the entire army around one fortress, Maratha commanders continued operating elsewhere.

3. Captured positions remained vulnerable.

When Mughal troops moved to besiege another fortress, the Marathas could attack neighbouring positions or threaten the recently conquered area.

### An easy example

Suppose Aurangzeb captured Fort A after a siege lasting several months.

He then moved his main army to attack Fort B.

To keep Fort A, he needed troops and supplies there.

Meanwhile, Maratha forces could attack the roads connecting Fort A to the Mughal camp.

The emperor might possess both forts on paper, but maintaining them required enormous expense.

UPSC Mains concept: Military conquest without secure administrative and logistical control did not necessarily translate into durable sovereignty.

## 13. Tarabai's offensive strategy — from defending forts to attacking Mughal territories

![Who is Malik Ambar? The African Slave turned Indian Mercenary Kingmaker | TheCollector](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/L7Ji4qGF50u3vueSam3hnNWgZR9qVxWihF3UlRgAD7h8Zf-6vOjssQ0RySQsToilo3OwMc7bb0MO1h5jzgoEuFZIqSrCD8DcL301cJZ-s2pr7AWYK1AyqUDgSB4_PkfNuiJ66UhSte_sqFGoFT7R7umuzZEB2fbj5NBw-MxmjRM?purpose=inline)

Under Tarabai, Maratha warfare increasingly took the form of widespread cavalry operations.

### Earlier priority

Protect Shivaji's kingdom and prevent Mughal conquest.

### Growing priority under Tarabai

Extend the conflict into Mughal-controlled territories and make imperial government more difficult and expensive.

Maratha forces attacked and collected revenue in regions including:

- Khandesh.
    
- Berar.
    
- Malwa.
    
- Gujarat.
    
- Parts of the wider central Indian region.
    

The intensity of operations varied over time, and these raids did not necessarily amount to permanent annexation.

### What did the Marathas gain?

1. Revenue: Resources to maintain their troops.

2. Mobility: They could choose vulnerable targets.

3. Military pressure: Mughal forces had to defend distant routes and settlements.

4. Political influence: Local chiefs might negotiate or cooperate with Maratha commanders.

### Why was this effective against Aurangzeb?

Aurangzeb needed to defend an empire extending across an enormous area.

A Maratha force only needed to find vulnerable points in that system.

The Mughal army could win individual sieges while still failing to stop raids across its territories.

### Did the Marathas control all northern India by 1707?

No.

Their raids demonstrated a growing military reach, but the extensive territorial and revenue system of the eighteenth-century Maratha power developed later.

Do not confuse raiding capacity in 1705 with the more organized expansion under the Peshwas.

## 14. Why did Aurangzeb struggle to defeat the Marathas?

This is the most important analytical question in the chapter.

|Reason|Explanation|
|---|---|
|Difficult terrain|Mountain passes and hill forts complicated military operations|
|Mobile warfare|Maratha cavalry attacked vulnerable locations and supply lines|
|Decentralized command|Resistance continued even when a principal leader was absent or killed|
|Extended geography|Campaigns stretched from Maharashtra to southern India and beyond|
|Fort networks|Mughal armies had to invest significant time and resources in sieges|
|Revenue pressure|War disrupted local resources and required heavy imperial spending|
|Maratha adaptation|The Marathas could shift from defending forts to offensive raids|
|Leadership continuity|Rajaram and Tarabai maintained political coordination|
|Imperial overstretch|Aurangzeb struggled to govern and defend a huge territorial empire|

### The central contradiction

The Mughal Empire was stronger in men, resources and artillery.

Yet that superiority did not guarantee the destruction of a mobile and geographically dispersed opponent.

The Marathas could lose individual forts or battles without losing the whole war.

### Was Aurangzeb completely unsuccessful?

No.

He conquered Bijapur and Golconda and captured many Maratha forts.

These were significant territorial and military achievements.

But he failed to bring the Maratha resistance to a decisive end.

Historical assessment: Aurangzeb's Deccan campaigns simultaneously extended Mughal territorial control and exposed the limitations of imperial military and financial power.

## 15. Death of Aurangzeb — 3 March 1707

![Why Aurangzeb lies buried under the open sky](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/6wUUukc4fKjfFd4A6d6Ey3R0LGhi4ycmYCGmpHj9SAtqDPcCDWmplOifEKG0O11BCHuX9hLJZUFbGAAgV9PZNEphELTMJHssIIerjQcHH8qnED659u6iwS_U32V1wFllU6LQubGG7kQH1pgl-L5nqhAx7OA-nMk73mZM3fBT7b0?purpose=inline)

Aurangzeb died at Ahmednagar on 3 March 1707, at approximately 88 years of age.

He had been personally involved in prolonged campaigns in the Deccan since 1681.

### What did he achieve?

- Conquered Bijapur in 1686.
    
- Conquered Golconda in 1687.
    
- Captured important Maratha forts.
    
- Greatly expanded the formal territorial extent of Mughal rule in southern India.
### What did he fail to achieve?

He did not permanently eliminate Maratha resistance.

By the time he died, Maratha forces remained active and the Mughal administration faced mounting military and financial burdens.

### Why was his death a turning point?

Aurangzeb's sons now contested the Mughal succession.

The new emperor would have to consider how to reduce the cost of the prolonged Deccan war.

This created opportunities for the Marathas.

But it also introduced a new challenge: the future position of Shahu, Sambhaji's son, who remained in Mughal custody.

That belongs to Chapter 7.
