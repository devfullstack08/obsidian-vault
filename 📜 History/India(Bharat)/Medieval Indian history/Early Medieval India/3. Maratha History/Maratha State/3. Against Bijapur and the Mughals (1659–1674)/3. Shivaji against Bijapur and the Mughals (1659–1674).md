Central question: How did Shivaji survive the attacks of Bijapur and the Mughal Empire, recover from a major territorial defeat, and establish the political authority necessary for his coronation in 1674?

In Chapter 2, we studied how Shivaji acquired Torna, Rajgad and other forts, conquered Javli, and defeated Afzal Khan at Pratapgad on 10 November 1659.

That victory brought Shivaji prestige and military resources. But it also created a serious problem: Bijapur now considered Shivaji a major threat, while Mughal emperor Aurangzeb was determined to expand imperial authority in the Deccan.

The next fifteen years would test Shivaji's military leadership, diplomacy and political survival.

Afzal Khan's death did not immediately end the conflict with Bijapur.

Shivaji took advantage of the victory to expand his influence in southern Maharashtra.

His forces captured additional positions, including Panhala Fort, near present-day Kolhapur.

### Why was Panhala important?

Panhala controlled important routes across southern Maharashtra.

Possession of the fort could help Shivaji extend his influence towards Kolhapur and the territories connecting Maharashtra with northern Karnataka.

But by expanding farther south, he also became more exposed to attacks from Bijapur.

### What did Bijapur do?

The Sultanate organized another major expedition against Shivaji.

This time the commander was Siddi Jauhar, who led the campaign against Panhala in 1660.

## 3. Siege of Panhala (1660) — Shivaji trapped

### The problem

In 1660, Bijapur forces under Siddi Jauhar besieged Panhala while Shivaji was inside.

A siege is different from a normal battle.

Instead of immediately attacking the strongest walls, the besieging army attempts to isolate a fortified position and prevent the defenders from receiving supplies or reinforcements.

### How did the siege affect Shivaji?

- His movement was restricted.
    
- Supplies became a concern.
    
- The enemy maintained pressure around the fort.
    
- Continuing the siege indefinitely risked capture or surrender.

### Shivaji's decision

Shivaji planned an escape from Panhala towards Vishalgad Fort, another defensible position.

The escape led to one of the most celebrated episodes of early Maratha history: **Pavan Khind**.

## 4. Pavan Khind (1660) — Baji Prabhu Deshpande

![Pavankhind battle - tale of Baji Prabhu Deshpande's sacrifice](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/hTNJXbmUGy09RMVLRFRNoL4l_4K4q13egWAbEFRiBEkpZVt8cggx_xmzNwoRcFSpuATuvXdgQ3Fa6QApM9AfwXHpSrHVntfYO9M1AzuJCq8zLXFe23_avpXUfTcPSr0Z3F5mfmxFexkgmxBJAvF5L-VpsGmUB1LRg2WW-jWzRmQ?purpose=inline)

### Why did fighting occur at a mountain pass?

When Shivaji escaped towards Vishalgad, Bijapur forces pursued him.

The pursuing army had to move through the difficult terrain of the Western Ghats.

A relatively narrow pass gave a smaller rearguard force an opportunity to delay a larger pursuing force.

**Baji Prabhu Deshpande**, one of Shivaji's commanders, is traditionally associated with this action.

### What happened?

According to the well-known Maratha tradition:

1. Shivaji escaped from Panhala with a selected force.
2. Bijapur troops pursued him.
3. Baji Prabhu and other soldiers held a narrow pass to delay the pursuers.
4. Shivaji reached Vishalgad.
5. Baji Prabhu died during the fighting.

The pass became famous as Pavan Khind.

Accounts differ over the precise date, troop numbers and several dramatic details, so these should not be presented as equally certain historical facts.

### Why was the episode important?

Even though Shivaji lost effective control of Panhala for a time, he escaped with his leadership intact.

That was strategically important.

A kingdom could lose territory and later recover it. But the capture of its principal leader could have threatened its survival.

UPSC significance: The Panhala escape illustrates the importance of defensive strongholds, local terrain, rearguard action and the preservation of command leadership.

At the same time that Bijapur was attacking Shivaji, the Mughals intensified military operations against him.

Aurangzeb appointed Shaista Khan, an experienced Mughal noble and his maternal uncle, to lead operations in the Deccan.

### Why did Aurangzeb send him?

The Mughal emperor wanted to secure the empire's territories and restrain Shivaji's expansion.

Shaista Khan commanded substantial imperial resources and advanced into the Pune region.

### What happened?

Mughal forces occupied Pune, a city with deep personal and political importance to Shivaji.

They also captured the strategically important **Chakan Fort** in 1660, following a determined defence associated with **Firangoji Narsala**.

Shaista Khan established his presence in Pune, occupying the Lal Mahal complex traditionally associated with Shivaji's early life.
### Why was this dangerous for Shivaji?

Pune was more than a town.

It was linked to his family's revenue resources, local supporters and administrative networks.

The Mughal occupation threatened the core region from which he had developed his power.

Situation by 1660: Shivaji had survived Panhala, but Bijapur and Mughal military pressure had cost him important territory and limited his freedom of action.
## 6. Shivaji's surprise attack on Shaista Khan (April 1663)

![Sutradhara’s Tales: When Pune city became Shaiste Khan’s military camp | Hindustan Times](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/FQeYebvQRkOCjHDcE5m2L1iPAo3RkHqMyoZVxH5XYl_wjj9pCAID5aAjiWZbajH1PM0bAC7MxeMMpliY1T4RVO6MV4B64htdqFA3o2RulfzPkboTf9-KaFE71pAnP6PKuwcORwok8Hek8sVN0ponw1x2wPFmRA_TZft1adRJ-M4?purpose=inline)

For several years, Shaista Khan's forces remained in and around Pune.

A direct attack against a major Mughal army in open terrain would have been dangerous.

Instead, Shivaji planned a surprise operation.
### What happened?

In April 1663, Shivaji led a selected group of men in a nighttime raid on Shaista Khan's quarters in Pune.

The attackers penetrated the residence and caused considerable confusion.

Shaista Khan escaped but was wounded, traditionally said to have lost fingers.

A member of his household, commonly identified as his son, was killed.

Shivaji and his men withdrew.

### Why was this a significant success?

It did not destroy the Mughal army.

Its principal impact was psychological and political.

Shaista Khan was an important imperial commander stationed in Shivaji's former base of Pune.

A successful raid on his residence severely damaged his military prestige.

Aurangzeb subsequently transferred him to Bengal.

### Historical significance

Shivaji demonstrated that an occupying army could still be vulnerable to intelligence-led attacks, even when it possessed superior numbers.

Exam distinction: This was a surprise raid on the Mughal command establishment, not a large conventional battle in which the entire Mughal army was defeated.

## 7. First raid on Surat (January 1664)

![Unknown, 1670 - View of the Harbor of Surat in Gujarat - fine art prin – Artprinta](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/58o6gnmebHn_abupYZbItdyOa3KFVth4oFnBcXnOhYh3xqNiTQW1MdT1Y1fLAXu6Si03JqXWxpZq5XVd4XhqVRT5iBF3K2WtqimBjk24EcjatRn4lavhNvoJ9We9M2YVL7vc22LA0M6_6hsTQaO-mfestWfgNoAP-g5edc2YMdA?purpose=inline)
### Why was Surat important?

During the seventeenth century, Surat, in present-day Gujarat, was one of the Mughal Empire's most important commercial ports.

It connected inland Indian markets with Indian Ocean trading networks, including commerce with western Asia.

Surat contained merchants, bankers, warehouses, religious institutions and trading establishments operated by European companies.
### Why did Shivaji attack Surat?

Three principal considerations explain the decision.

1. Economic resources: Surat was a wealthy commercial city. Raiding it offered access to portable wealth that could support Shivaji's military establishment.

2. Pressure on the Mughal Empire: Surat was an important imperial port. Attacking it demonstrated that Mughal commercial centres were vulnerable.

3. Strategic compensation: Shivaji had suffered losses during prolonged warfare and needed resources to maintain forces and forts.
### What happened?

In January 1664, Shivaji's forces entered Surat and carried out a raid.

Wealth was seized from merchants and other targets.

Some foreign trading establishments defended their premises.

### Was the raid simply a clean military victory?

No. It should also be understood as an episode of violent urban plunder.

Contemporary accounts describe suffering and losses among civilians and merchants. The degree of damage varied across the city.

A historically balanced UPSC answer should explain both its strategic importance and its human and commercial consequences.

### Result

The raid provided resources and demonstrated Shivaji's ability to strike a wealthy Mughal centre far from his main mountain strongholds.

However, it also intensified Aurangzeb's determination to suppress Shivaji.

UPSC trap: Shivaji's first Surat raid was in 1664. His second major Surat raid occurred in 1670.

### Why did Aurangzeb change his strategy?

Shaista Khan's campaign had failed to eliminate Shivaji's resistance.

The attack on Surat had further exposed the weakness of Mughal regional security.

Purandar was a powerful hill fort and one of Shivaji's important defensive positions.
### Why did the Mughals target Purandar?

The fort helped secure territory close to Pune.

Its fall would demonstrate that the Mughal army could systematically reduce Shivaji's fortified positions rather than merely occupy towns.
### Murarbaji Deshpande

Murarbaji Deshpande commanded the defence of Purandar.

He and the defenders resisted the Mughal siege.

Murarbaji died during the fighting and is remembered for his role in the defence.

Jai Singh's campaign threatened Shivaji's important forts and territories.

The surrendered forts were associated with annual revenue estimated at four lakh hons, while the retained forts were associated with approximately one lakh hons. A hon was a gold monetary unit used in the Deccan.

These are conventional treaty figures, not a claim that Shivaji had only 35 forts at every earlier or later moment.
### Why did Shivaji agree to such harsh terms?

Think strategically.

Shivaji had already survived attacks by Bijapur and the Mughals, but Jai Singh's campaign created the risk of losing the principal forts on which his power depended.

If he continued fighting without an opportunity to recover, the entire territorial base could be endangered.

By accepting an unfavourable settlement, he preserved his remaining political and military resources.
### Did Shivaji permanently surrender his independence?

The treaty imposed substantial obligations and recognized Mughal superiority within its terms.

But the settlement did not end Shivaji's political career.

He retained important territory and continued to seek opportunities to restore his position.

UPSC Mains interpretation: Purandar was a major tactical and diplomatic setback, but Shivaji's political power survived. The distinction between temporary submission and permanent elimination of a rival is crucial.

## 11. Shivaji's participation in the Mughal campaign against Bijapur (1665–1666)

After the Treaty of Purandar, Jai Singh launched a campaign against Bijapur.

Under the new political arrangement, Shivaji participated in Mughal military operations.

### Why would Shivaji fight alongside the Mughals?

Because the political alignment had temporarily changed.

Previously, the Mughals had been his main enemy.

Under the treaty, Shivaji sought to preserve his territory and political standing through cooperation with Jai Singh.

The Mughal leadership also hoped to use Shivaji's regional knowledge and military resources against Bijapur.

### What happened?

The Mughal campaign against Bijapur did not achieve the intended decisive result.

Shivaji also faced difficulties in operations involving Panhala.

The failure of the wider campaign and mutual distrust complicated the relationship between Shivaji and the Mughal authorities.

Historical lesson: In the seventeenth-century Deccan, political alliances were flexible. A ruler could negotiate with one enemy in order to confront another.

## 12. Shivaji's visit to Agra (1666)

![File:Shivaji Maharaj in the court of Aurangzeb, by Dhurandhar.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8dhoRaIhfsN0GrdVJhtk_ABv_iZ8-d1ZWFQJL7TlwnBym1i_QALEyA7U31zx0wI9WErKNbKcc_aD_dqaBGhMNknUJv337-XisKrqD_r5fYBw6ZEXhMFhxZm07I8TxlHODJNn20bOoiTdrcOrBNnKUcosLKVNNd6pL6cwuRa8QTo?purpose=inline)

### Why did Shivaji go to Agra?

After the Purandar settlement, Jai Singh encouraged Shivaji to visit Aurangzeb's imperial court.

The visit was intended to define Shivaji's future relationship with the Mughal Empire.

Shivaji expected treatment appropriate to his political position and the assurances given through Mughal intermediaries.

### What happened at the court?

Shivaji arrived in Agra in May 1666 with his son Sambhaji.

On 12 May 1666, he attended Aurangzeb's court during celebrations connected with the emperor's birthday.

Shivaji was dissatisfied with the rank and ceremonial position assigned to him.

He believed that he was being treated below the status he deserved.

His displeasure became apparent during the audience.

### Why was this so serious?

The Mughal court operated through a carefully ordered system of rank and precedence.

Where a noble stood was not an unimportant matter of etiquette.

It publicly represented that person's position within the imperial hierarchy.

For Shivaji, accepting an inferior position could imply a level of political subordination he considered unacceptable.

### What happened afterward?

Shivaji was placed under guarded residence or house detention in Agra.

His freedom of movement became restricted.

This created a direct threat to the survival of his political establishment in the Deccan.

## 13. Escape from Agra — August 1666

![Lessons from Battlefield to Boardroom: The Great Escape – Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's Strategic Masterstroke from Trap to Triumph](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/aZEtqfD-9OuRmd3ZwHeScbKMYuI0HfVXIku0vvqhyUFMiHwnE4QGQubh4OiZYYrZVZyWRV3Bl941QppejBFDDdDSrg_bCyW6TxMWrfQupSc84gD1wQoBibGoNmJRthJDOBZm9pKyHCb0iTJHiQw_Lj-Zt_Ma2TPuW-kObavCDbU?purpose=inline)

The concealed-basket escape is part of a well-known historical tradition. Details vary among the surviving accounts.

### Why did Shivaji need to escape?

He was far from his main territories and surrounded by Mughal authority.

He could not effectively direct his government while under confinement, and prolonged detention created uncertainty among his supporters.

### How did he escape?

The popular story describes Shivaji arranging the distribution of sweets or charitable gifts in large covered baskets.

According to versions of this tradition, he and Sambhaji escaped by concealing themselves in baskets carried out of the guarded residence.

Other accounts provide different details.

What is historically secure? Shivaji escaped Mughal surveillance in August 1666 and eventually returned to the Deccan.

The exact means, route and chronology of his return remain debated.

### Why was the escape important?

- Shivaji recovered his freedom.
    
- The emerging Maratha political leadership survived.
    
- Mughal prestige suffered an embarrassment.
    
- Shivaji could rebuild his territorial and military position.
    

Exam distinction: Do not confuse his escape from Panhala in 1660 with his escape from Agra in 1666.

## 14. Relative peace and reconstruction (1667–1669)

After the Agra episode, Mughal-Maratha relations entered a period of relative accommodation.

### Why did Shivaji not immediately begin a full-scale war?

He needed to rebuild military resources and restore political stability.

A renewed major confrontation immediately after Agra could have placed his remaining territory at risk.

Meanwhile, Mughal commanders also faced other political and military responsibilities.

### What was Shivaji doing?

He continued consolidating his administration, fort positions and regional alliances.

Diplomatic contacts with the Mughal authorities produced a temporary settlement, although the precise arrangements changed over time.

This period gave Shivaji an opportunity to reorganize before the next major phase of conflict.

### Why did peace break down?

The relationship remained fragile.

Questions concerning imperial service, revenue, territorial rights and mutual trust were not fully resolved.

By 1670, open conflict had resumed.

## 16. Battle of Sinhagad — February 1670

![Khadakwasla Dam, Sinhagad Fort Tour by Car in Pune | Pelago](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/MtcVS9duwXxqGA2wanM3-84FkpfMTqRPaTg_ATYV-siZcfv035fT_koRh1nQeGa2YbNCdOwRYXK0NC8wkIjUwBd0m1AjSHFRl81sBslRQcLqJAXsRvOGldmRpI2JIZCETEealuNC1QxfDzdAGl8UJWmItNmSISJYs0azoZ_8920?purpose=inline)

### Why was Sinhagad important?

The fort was then commonly known as **Kondhana**.

Located near Pune, it was strategically important for controlling the surrounding hill country and approaches to the city.

Under the Treaty of Purandar, Shivaji had surrendered Kondhana to the Mughals.

Recovering it therefore had both military and political significance.

### What happened?

In February 1670, Maratha forces under **Tanaji Malusare** attacked the fort.

The Mughal-side commander was **Udaybhan Rathod**, associated with Jai Singh's former military establishment.

Tanaji died during the fighting, but Maratha forces captured the fort.

His brother **Suryaji Malusare** is also remembered for his role in the operation.

### The famous story

Tradition relates that Tanaji's men climbed the steep fort using ropes and, in some versions, a trained monitor lizard named Yashwanti.

The detailed lizard episode is part of popular tradition and should not be treated as securely verified military evidence.

The celebrated saying “Gad ala pan sinh gela” means:

> The fort was captured, but the lion was lost.

It expresses the victory's cost in Tanaji's death.

UPSC significance: Sinhagad symbolized the recovery of forts lost under the Treaty of Purandar.

## 20. Why did Shivaji seek coronation in 1674?

By 1674, Shivaji controlled substantial territory, forts and revenue resources.

He had resisted both Bijapur and the Mughal Empire.

But there was a problem.
### Military power was not the same as formal sovereignty

Shivaji had emerged from a family whose original military and territorial rights were connected to the Deccan Sultanates.

He wanted to establish his status not merely as a powerful commander, jagirdar or rebellious subordinate, but as an independent sovereign king.

Coronation could strengthen:

1. Political legitimacy: Public recognition of independent royal authority.
    
2. Administrative authority: A stronger institutional basis for issuing orders, appointments and revenue demands.
    
3. Dynastic succession: Recognition of an established royal house.
    
4. Diplomatic standing: A more clearly defined position in relations with other rulers.
    
5. Religious and ceremonial legitimacy: Formal kingship recognized through established coronation rituals.

### The coronation

On 6 June 1674, Shivaji underwent his formal coronation at **Raigad**, taking the title **Chhatrapati**.

The scholar **Gaga Bhatt**, associated with Varanasi, played an important ceremonial role.

The coronation marked the formal establishment of Shivaji's sovereign kingship.
