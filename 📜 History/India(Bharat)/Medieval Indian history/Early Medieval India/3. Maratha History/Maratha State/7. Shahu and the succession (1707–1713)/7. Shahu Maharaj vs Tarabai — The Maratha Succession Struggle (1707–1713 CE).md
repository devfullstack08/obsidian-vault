Widow of Rajaram | Regent for Shivaji II

Central question: The Marathas had successfully resisted Aurangzeb under Tarabai. Then why did they begin fighting among themselves after his death in 1707? How did Shahu emerge victorious, and how did this conflict prepare the way for the rise of the Peshwas?

This chapter is particularly important for understanding the transition from Shivaji's centrally led monarchy to the powerful Peshwa administration of the eighteenth century.

## 1. Where did our previous chapter end?

In Chapter 6, we studied the prolonged Mughal-Maratha struggle.

Remember the sequence:

- 1689: Sambhaji was executed by Aurangzeb.
    
- 1689: Rajaram became Chhatrapati and escaped to Gingee.
    
- 1689: Mughals captured Raigad; Yesubai and Shahu were taken into custody.
    
- 1700: Rajaram died.
    
- 1700–1707: Tarabai governed as regent for her son, Shivaji II.
    
- 3 March 1707: Aurangzeb died at Ahmednagar.
    

By 1707, Tarabai had become a major leader of Maratha resistance.

Yet her authority was about to face a challenge from a member of the same royal family.

That person was Shahu, the son of Sambhaji.

## 2. Understanding the Maratha royal family

This family relationship is essential. Without it, the entire succession dispute becomes confusing.

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (1630–1680)

Founder of the sovereign Maratha kingdom

Sambhaji: Elder son of Shivaji
Wife: Yesubai
Shahu I: Grandson of Shivaji

Claimant released in 1707

Rajaram: Younger son of Shivaji
Wife: Tarabai
Shivaji II: Grandson of Shivaji

Claimant supported by Tarabai

### Why did both sides claim the throne?

Shahu's claim: He was Sambhaji's son. Sambhaji had succeeded Shivaji as Chhatrapati in 1680. Therefore, Shahu claimed a legitimate right to the Maratha throne through the senior line.

Tarabai's claim: Her husband Rajaram had succeeded Sambhaji in 1689. She argued that her son Shivaji II represented the ruling line that had continued to fight the Mughals while Shahu was a captive.

The dispute involved not only inheritance but also military leadership, political legitimacy and control over the state.

## 3. Why was Shahu in Mughal captivity?

Return briefly to 1689.

After Aurangzeb ordered Sambhaji's execution, Mughal forces continued attacking the Maratha kingdom.

They captured Raigad in November 1689.

Among those taken into Mughal custody were:

- Yesubai, Sambhaji's wife.
    
- Shahu, their young son.
    

Shahu was approximately seven years old.

### What happened during his captivity?

Shahu remained with the Mughal establishment for many years and grew up under imperial supervision.

He became familiar with Mughal court practices and political culture.

He was not simply forgotten in a prison. His royal position made him useful to the Mughal government as a potential instrument in dealing with the Marathas.

Important distinction: Shahu was held in Mughal custody until 1707, while his mother Yesubai remained in Mughal custody for considerably longer.

## 4. Aurangzeb dies in 1707 — a new political situation

![Aurangabad: Aurangzeb's Tomb Closed By ASI For Five Days, Security Tightened](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/0hZvwvFPQeFZQB5heiUkvjuFbE6NkWz8Puh0Ar-8T4tcUwlvGrstcVa9XzFeuW_GQxyIvGSGa9Onc8EVV3F7ZxsOhLMDXzOtkzCorWOvBdbiYOkZIAVAieiUNeckNy4T01ZGJwEQmfwsGl_mpniV26cNt4XnbeNz93kHSE4kCEU?purpose=inline)

Aurangzeb died at Ahmednagar on 3 March 1707.

After his death, the Mughal princes began competing for the imperial throne.

### Who were the important claimants?

Two principal contenders were:

- Prince Mu'azzam, who became Emperor Bahadur Shah I.
    
- Prince Azam Shah, another son of Aurangzeb.

The empire faced a succession struggle.

### Why was this important for the Marathas?

For decades, the Marathas had faced the attention of an emperor personally campaigning in the Deccan.

Now Mughal leaders had to worry about succession, army loyalty and control of Delhi.

This reduced the unity of imperial direction in the Deccan.

However, the Mughal authorities still sought ways to influence Maratha politics.

One such decision was the release of Shahu.

## 5. Why did the Mughals release Shahu in 1707?

This is the most important cause-and-effect question in the chapter.

### Who released him?

Shahu was released in 1707, under the authority of **Prince Azam Shah**, with **Zulfiqar Khan** associated with the decision.

The release is commonly interpreted as a deliberate political strategy, although the exact terms and motives are described differently across historical accounts.

### What did the Mughals hope to achieve?

Consider the situation.

For seven years, Tarabai had directed Maratha resistance against the Mughal Empire.

She had influential commanders and an established political network.

If the Mughals released another legitimate royal claimant, those commanders might divide their support.

The political calculation was simple:

Maratha leadership under Tarabai

Mughals release Shahu in 1707

Two rival claims to the Chhatrapati's throne

Maratha commanders choose different sides

Opportunity to weaken united Maratha resistance

This policy is often summarized as an attempt to encourage internal Maratha divisions.

It did create divisions, although its later consequences were not wholly favourable to the Mughals. Shahu eventually consolidated a powerful Maratha state.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer records that Azam Shah's decision was associated with the expectation that rival Maratha claims would weaken unity.
### Was Shahu a Mughal agent?

That would be too simplistic.

His release was politically useful to the Mughals, but Shahu had his own dynastic claims and interests.

He subsequently built an independent political base among Maratha nobles.

A foreign power can attempt to influence a succession dispute without fully controlling the claimant's later actions.

## 6. Shahu returns to Maharashtra — 1707

After his release, Shahu travelled towards the Deccan to assert his claim.
### What was his immediate problem?

Tarabai already governed in the name of Shivaji II.

She controlled important royal and administrative networks established during the war against Aurangzeb.

Shahu therefore needed supporters.
### Which chiefs supported Shahu?

Important Maratha nobles gradually aligned themselves with Shahu, including:

- Parsoji Bhonsle.
    
- Nemaji Shinde.
    
- Haibatrao Nimbalkar.
    
- Other regional chiefs and commanders.

Their allegiance strengthened his military and political position.

### Why did some chiefs support him?

Different chiefs had different reasons:

1. They considered Shahu the legitimate senior-line heir.
    
2. They expected political appointments or revenue rights.
    
3. They disagreed with Tarabai or her advisers.
    
4. They believed Shahu would offer more favourable arrangements.

Historical lesson: Succession disputes involve arguments about legitimacy but also the practical interests of military and landed elites.

## 7. Why did Tarabai refuse to recognize Shahu?

![Queen Tarabai Birth Aniversary: छत्रपति शिवाजी की छोटी बहू, जिसने जिंदा रखा स्वराज का आदर्श, बरसों तक लड़ी औरंगजेब से लड़ाई - queen tarabai fought with mughals to keep swaraj ideals and maratha kingdom - GNT](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/HHPyX71g42DcoctfqDmfw4IvQY6_NEJt5ALr88JrPKdIqqczBO4Xf-_rLnCygqm_47WLFmEJs9dqpguEbyp-U5AJ0pqMijuGR1hgoB_c5SfuspCqc1kjO4aZhIkNPWBLhex2Hg2xhvTAVVSsgqMLONJwnX4s5wR0paNiC48MZaw?purpose=inline)

Tarabai had governed as regent since 1700.

Under her leadership, the Maratha kingdom survived the final years of Aurangzeb's massive Deccan campaign.

Now Shahu, absent from Maratha politics for nearly eighteen years, claimed leadership.

### Tarabai's reasoning

From her perspective:

- Her husband Rajaram had preserved the Chhatrapati's office after Sambhaji's death.
    
- Her son Shivaji II had been recognized as ruler after Rajaram died.
    
- She and her supporters had sustained the war effort.
    
- Shahu had been released by the Mughals, creating uncertainty about his political commitments.

Some accounts describe her questioning Shahu's identity, calling him an **impostor**.

But the broader dispute was over legitimacy and political authority, not merely whether she recognized his face.
### What was at stake?

If Tarabai accepted Shahu's claim, she and her son risked losing the principal royal position.

If Shahu withdrew, he would surrender his claim as Sambhaji's heir.

Neither side was willing to concede.

The dispute moved towards war.

## 8. Battle of Khed — 12 October 1707

![Bridge on Bhima river at Rajguru Nagar, District Pune, Maharashtra, India](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/yhZl-x03vso2qJrGISgVpGyqljEUM2asR6UkXBXuh6PI8X0230di9nDv6Wm_kAoyZhYXH-fEpVLfp8cG0TiplTD4eNKSasPRi07JYjJbYjE5M6zLivm5PZSW08Ejj0lCp5b46rHboPOOkf3BofwZCE6fmN0Yh4KHKtSctY9qLCEnkMUCm-hnlKc3hWVgpVYZ?purpose=inline)

The Battle of Khed occurred in the Pune region near the Bhima River, not at Khed in Ratnagiri district.

This is the decisive battle of Chapter 7.
### Where was Khed?

Khed, now commonly associated with **Rajgurunagar**, lies north of Pune.

### The opposing forces

| Shahu's side                           | Tarabai's side                               |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Shahu                                  | Troops sent in support of Shivaji II's claim |
| Supporting Maratha nobles              | Dhanaji Jadhav                               |
| Growing network of military supporters | Parashuram Trimbak Pratinidhi                |
### Why is Dhanaji Jadhav so important?

Remember Chapter 6.

Dhanaji Jadhav was one of the commanders who had fought against Aurangzeb during Rajaram's and Tarabai's leadership.

He possessed considerable military influence.

Tarabai needed his support to defeat Shahu.

But Shahu's supporters opened negotiations with Dhanaji.
### The role of Balaji Vishwanath

Balaji Vishwanath, who would become Peshwa in 1713, was already active in Maratha military and administrative affairs.

He had worked in networks connected with Dhanaji Jadhav and became an important supporter of Shahu.

His political abilities helped Shahu win important loyalties, although accounts differ about the exact details of his role in the negotiations at Khed.
### What happened at Khed?

On 12 October 1707, Shahu's forces confronted the army sent against him.

Dhanaji Jadhav did not fully support the attack on Shahu and shifted his allegiance.

Parashuram Trimbak Pratinidhi was consequently left in a much weaker position and withdrew.

Shahu emerged victorious.
### Why was the victory significant?

This was not simply a victory achieved through greater battlefield strength.

The change in allegiance of a leading commander was decisive.

It demonstrated that Shahu could attract important members of the military establishment that had previously supported Tarabai.

The Maharashtra State Gazetteer records both the battle date and the central role of Dhanaji's change of allegiance.
### Three immediate results

1. Shahu's claim became much stronger.
    
2. Tarabai lost an influential military commander.
    
3. Shahu gained the opportunity to march towards Satara.
    

UPSC Prelims: Battle of Khed — 1707 — Shahu versus Tarabai's faction — Dhanaji Jadhav changed allegiance.

## 9. Shahu captures Satara (1707–1708)

![Beautiful Satara](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/kqx1EysJKoBI4pMVEJGk3rSepm8PDjf3oQ-m7NecHlNPwUNKlFmYWSjkztHz_iBBXVVFMoOyaUXa00mAlAyfATyv9tt6DMYVtiNLX1UuC3DTMrKBpSKvCcRmRVV8cK4CvFEcMzZwFVGmtPizRcBIQ3smKx-k7g6jTv3CQhLai_Y?purpose=inline)

### Why was Satara important?

Satara had been an important royal centre during Rajaram's final years.

Possession of Satara helped strengthen a claimant's status as Chhatrapati.

A victory at Khed alone was not sufficient. Shahu needed a recognized political seat.
### What happened after Khed?

Shahu advanced southwards.

Tarabai withdrew to stronger positions, while her supporters attempted to defend Satara.

The fort eventually passed into Shahu's hands.
### Shahu's coronation

Shahu was formally seated on the throne at Satara on 12 January 1708, according to the Satara Gazetteer's chronology.

Some older accounts give alternative dates for aspects of the accession process. For examination preparation, the standard sequence is:

- 1707: Battle of Khed.
    
- 1708: Shahu's coronation at Satara.
### Why was this a major turning point?

Shahu now possessed:

- A recognized royal seat.
    
- Support from important commanders.
    
- Stronger claims to Shivaji's dynastic inheritance.
    
- An emerging administrative organization.

But Tarabai had not surrendered.

Therefore, the victory did not reunite every Maratha territory under Shahu.

### Was the kingdom formally divided into two equal states in 1708?

No.

The split emerged through political and military rivalry. The relative power and territories controlled by each side changed over time.

The later settlement between the Satara and Kolhapur houses came much later, in the T**reaty of Warna (1731)**.

Also remember that the ruler of Kolhapur changed in 1714, after a palace coup.

We will distinguish that event from the developments of 1707–1713 below.

## 11. Why did powerful Maratha chiefs become so important?

During Shivaji's reign, the Chhatrapati exercised strong personal leadership.

During the long war against Aurangzeb, however, ministers and military commanders had received substantial responsibilities.

By 1707, many chiefs controlled armed followings, revenue arrangements and regional influence.

A royal claimant therefore needed to win their allegiance.
### Why could a chief change the balance of power?

Imagine a commander who controlled 3,000 cavalrymen and collected revenue from several districts.

If he joined Shahu, Shahu gained troops and resources.

If he joined Tarabai, her military position improved.

Therefore, political legitimacy alone did not determine the result.

The distribution of military power, land-revenue rights and offices was equally important.

### Important figures

| Chief or officer              | Importance                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Dhanaji Jadhav                | His decision helped Shahu win at Khed                |
| Chandrasen Jadhav             | Son of Dhanaji; later opposed Shahu                  |
| Parashuram Trimbak Pratinidhi | Important official supporting Tarabai                |
| Kanhoji Angre                 | Powerful naval commander on the western coast        |
| Balaji Vishwanath             | Emerging diplomat and administrator supporting Shahu |

The growing importance of these men explains why the Peshwa would eventually acquire greater powers.

## 12. Dhanaji Jadhav's death (1708) — Shahu faces another challenge

After Khed, Shahu retained Dhanaji Jadhav as a leading military commander.

But Dhanaji died in 1708.
### Why was this a problem?

Dhanaji had played an important role in Shahu's victory and could command influential military networks.

His death removed a stabilizing figure.

His son **Chandrasen Jadhav** acquired a major military position but later entered into conflict with Shahu's government.
### What happened next?

Disputes developed over military authority, revenue arrangements and political influence.

Chandrasen eventually moved into opposition to Shahu.

This meant that Shahu could not take the loyalty of the Maratha military elite for granted.
### Why did Balaji Vishwanath become important?

Balaji had administrative experience and connections among powerful Maratha families.

He could help Shahu negotiate, organize resources and deal with disaffected chiefs.

Over the following years, his position at court grew stronger.

UPSC significance: Balaji Vishwanath's rise was connected to the difficulties of consolidating Shahu's political authority after 1707.

## 13. Balaji Vishwanath before becoming Peshwa

### Who was Balaji Vishwanath?

Balaji Vishwanath Bhat came from a **Chitpavan Brahmin** family associated with the **Konkan region**, particularly **Shrivardhan**.

He worked in administration and revenue-related positions before gaining prominence in Shahu's government.

He was not the founder of the Maratha kingdom.

Rather, he rose within an already established but politically divided Maratha state.
### Why did Shahu trust him?

Balaji proved useful in areas where military force alone could not solve problems:

- Negotiating with important commanders.
    
- Managing political rivalries.
    
- Strengthening revenue arrangements.
    
- Organizing support for Shahu.
    
- Working with powerful regional interests.

### Was Balaji already Peshwa at the Battle of Khed?

No.

He was an increasingly important official and political supporter, but he was appointed Peshwa only in November 1713.

This is a common Prelims trap.

### Who was Peshwa before him?

Bahiroji (Bahiro Pant) Pingle held the Peshwa office under Shahu.

His difficulties with the powerful naval commander Kanhoji Angre contributed to the political crisis that preceded Balaji's appointment.

## 14. Kanhoji Angre — why did a naval commander influence the succession dispute?

![वास्तुशिल्प का चमत्कार विजयदुर्ग - vijaydurg maharashtra](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/9L8KUTphjcMFifWWj0cEe8DKSDQRZEwxHFPxeK3Y6QaqotWY7QkTbDMhFCFr75F-HRYWn3944VwdKznr7vXIahomxyHrgMSu2JhmA_EV91KKBiPkXZOzaLGV9362CWqXCJNZHBJVzS5LqaOc--Vf5FrIvoNNfLkyaOu6edVrBzU?purpose=inline)

You already studied the foundations of the Maratha navy under Shivaji in Chapter 4.

By the early eighteenth century, Kanhoji Angre had become an exceptionally influential maritime commander.
### What was his power based on?

Kanhoji controlled or influenced important coastal fortifications, ships and maritime routes.

He possessed military resources largely independent of the inland political centres.
### Why did both Shahu and Tarabai need him?

Because the Konkan coast was valuable for trade, revenue and strategic security.

A powerful naval commander could not easily be ignored by either claimant.

Kanhoji's alignment was consequently a significant political issue.
### The crisis

Kanhoji supported the rival political establishment associated with Tarabai.

He also seized important forts and took Shahu's Peshwa, Bahiroji Pingle, captive.

This was a serious challenge to Shahu's authority.
### Why not simply attack Kanhoji?

Kanhoji possessed strong coastal fortifications and naval capabilities.

Defeating him would be costly and uncertain.

Shahu needed someone capable of dealing with him through political negotiation as well as military pressure.

Balaji Vishwanath appeared particularly suitable because of his Konkan background, connections and diplomatic abilities.

This crisis helped Balaji Vishwanath rise to the office of Peshwa.
## 15. Balaji Vishwanath appointed Peshwa — 17 November 1713

This is the final major event in our chapter.
### What did Shahu need?

He needed an officer capable of:

1. Securing the loyalty of influential Maratha chiefs.
    
2. Managing political disputes.
    
3. Strengthening the administrative centre at Satara.
    
4. Negotiating with Kanhoji Angre.
    
5. Organizing military and financial resources.

### Shahu's decision

On 17 November 1713, Shahu appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa.

This was a major appointment.
### Why is it so important for Indian history?

Under Shivaji, the Peshwa was one of the eight principal ministers subordinate to the Chhatrapati.

Under Shahu, and particularly through Balaji Vishwanath and his successors, the office became increasingly influential.

The authority of the Peshwas eventually expanded to the point that they became the principal political leaders of the Maratha state.

However, Balaji's appointment in 1713 did not immediately make the Chhatrapati powerless.

The shift of authority was gradual.
### What about Kanhoji Angre?

Balaji subsequently negotiated with Kanhoji.

Their agreement was concluded in 1714, when Kanhoji accepted Shahu's authority under terms that recognized his important coastal position.

This belongs in our next chapter because it occurred after Balaji became Peshwa.

The detailed episode is discussed by Richard M. Eaton in A Social History of the Deccan.
## 16. Important clarification — what happened at Kolhapur in 1714?

Our chapter ends in 1713, but you must understand one later event to avoid confusion.

Tarabai's authority was challenged within her own political establishment.

In 1714, a palace coup associated with Rajasbai, another wife of Rajaram, displaced Tarabai and her son Shivaji II.

Rajasbai's son, Sambhaji II, became ruler of the Kolhapur branch.

Kolhapur succession

1700–1714: Tarabai governed for Shivaji II.
1714 palace coup: Tarabai and Shivaji II were displaced.
Sambhaji II: Son of Rajaram and Rajasbai, became the Kolhapur ruler.

UPSC trap: Sambhaji II of Kolhapur was not the same person as Sambhaji Maharaj, the son of Shivaji who died in 1689.

We will examine the 1714 political changes in Chapter 8 rather than going further here.

## 17. Why was the period 1707–1713 a turning point?

|Development|Immediate result|Long-term significance|
|---|---|---|
|Aurangzeb's death (1707)|Mughal succession crisis|Reduced unified imperial pressure in the Deccan|
|Shahu's release (1707)|Rival claimant emerged|Maratha political division|
|Battle of Khed (1707)|Shahu defeated Tarabai's forces|Important chiefs shifted allegiance|
|Shahu's coronation (1708)|Satara court established|Senior-line royal legitimacy strengthened|
|Satara–Kolhapur rivalry|Two competing royal establishments|Persistent political divisions|
|Dhanaji's death and noble rivalries|Shahu needed capable political organizers|Increased importance of ministers|
|Kanhoji Angre's challenge|Peshwa's authority challenged|Created an opening for Balaji Vishwanath|
|Balaji Vishwanath's appointment (1713)|Stronger Peshwa leadership|Beginning of a major long-term political transformation|

### The most important conclusion

The Mughals failed to destroy the Marathas by military force, but the release of Shahu in 1707 contributed to a serious dynastic division.

Shahu overcame the initial challenge largely by gaining the allegiance of influential commanders.

That process also increased the political importance of the men who could mobilize military and financial resources on his behalf.

Among them, Balaji Vishwanath emerged as especially important.
