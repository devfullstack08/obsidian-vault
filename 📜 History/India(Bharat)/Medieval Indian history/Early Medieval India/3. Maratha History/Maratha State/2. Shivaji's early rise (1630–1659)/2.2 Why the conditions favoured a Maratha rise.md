
## Why did the conditions favour the rise of Maratha power?

| Factor                        | Historical development                                            | How it created an opportunity                              |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Political fragmentation       | The Bahmani state split into rival Sultanates                     | Created competition and room for local leaders             |
| Mughal expansion              | Repeated campaigns weakened Ahmadnagar                            | Disrupted existing political authority                     |
| Maratha military experience   | Maratha chiefs served different Deccan rulers                     | Developed cavalry, leadership and administrative expertise |
| Hereditary local institutions | Deshmukhs, patils and other officeholders retained influence      | Provided networks of revenue and manpower                  |
| Geographical advantages       | Western Ghats, Maval valleys and hill forts                       | Favoured local mobility and defensive strategies           |
| Bhonsle family position       | Maloji and Shahaji accumulated military and political influence   | Provided an established family network                     |
| Military developments         | Malik Ambar effectively used mobile warfare against Mughal forces | Demonstrated ways to challenge stronger armies             |
| Economic resources            | Agricultural revenues, local trade and control over routes        | Could sustain troops, forts and political authority        |

### The key relationship

Political factor: Rival kingdoms and Mughal pressure
Geographical factor: Forts, mountains and passes
Social factor: Influential Maratha chiefs and local networks
Military factor: Experienced regional commanders and mobile forces

### Favourable conditions for the later rise of Maratha power

A crucial distinction: These factors made the rise of an independent Maratha state possible, not inevitable. Leadership, military decisions, political organization and changing alliances determined what happened later.
