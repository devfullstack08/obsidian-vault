
## The Bhonsle family — from regional service to major military influence

To understand Shivaji's background, we now come to his family.

### 1. Maloji Bhonsle — Shivaji's grandfather

Maloji Bhonsle was the father of Shahaji and grandfather of Shivaji.

He rose through military service in the Ahmadnagar Sultanate.

His increasing importance brought his family military status, patronage and valuable revenue rights.

He is associated with the **Bhonsle family's** position in territories around **Pune** and the surrounding region.

Why important?
Maloji helped create the family's political and economic foundation.

Shivaji therefore did not begin life as the son of a landless farmer. He belonged to a family that had already acquired standing within the Deccan military aristocracy.

Shahaji Bhonsle (c. 1594–1664) was an ambitious military commander who participated in the politics of **Ahmadnagar**, **Bijapur** and the **Mughal-Deccan struggle**.

His career involved changing political allegiances as the balance of power shifted.

This was not unusual in the Deccan, where commanders often sought to preserve their military following, hereditary claims and assigned territories despite political instability.

Jijabai (Jijau) belonged to the powerful **Jadhav family.**

Her father, **Lakhuji Jadhav**, was an important Maratha noble associated with the Ahmadnagar political system.

Her marriage to Shahaji linked two influential Maratha families.

Jijabai's later role in Shivaji's upbringing was historically important, though many detailed childhood anecdotes preserved in later traditions cannot be independently verified.

Remember: The Bhonsle and Jadhav families already possessed regional political importance before Shivaji was born.

## Why did Shahaji change political allegiances?

This question is essential for understanding the political culture of the Deccan.

Shahaji's activities took place during the struggle between Ahmadnagar and the expanding Mughal Empire.

Consider the position of a military commander serving a weakening kingdom.

If that ruler lost territory, the commander's soldiers might go unpaid. His jagir could be confiscated by a new authority.

He therefore had to decide whether to:

- Continue supporting his existing ruler.
- Negotiate with an invading power.
- Enter another Sultanate's service.
- Establish greater independent control over local territories.

Shahaji's career reflected these choices.

He served the **Nizam Shahi** establishment and, during subsequent years, moved between competing political alignments.

### A simple example

Suppose Ahmadnagar controlled a region in 1615, but the Mughals occupied part of it in 1625.

Shahaji might hold a military command and revenue assignment there.

The Mughal conquest could threaten both his income and his political position.

By negotiating with another power or supporting a Nizam Shahi claimant, he could attempt to preserve his influence.

This helps explain why Deccan politics cannot be reduced to a permanent Hindu-versus-Muslim division. Alliances crossed religious boundaries, and the political survival of families, states and military followings was often decisive.

## Events between 1626 and 1630 — the crisis deepens

By 1626, Malik Ambar had died.

His successors struggled to maintain the same degree of political and military control over the Ahmadnagar state.

Meanwhile, the Mughal Empire continued to seek domination over the Deccan.

### What was happening in northern India?

- Jahangir ruled the Mughal Empire until 1627.
    
- Shah Jahan became emperor in 1628.
    
- Mughal interest in the Deccan remained strong.
    

### What was happening in the Deccan?

**Ahmadnagar:** Its political structure was increasingly fragile. Court politics, military rivalries and Mughal intervention weakened it.

**Bijapur:** Remained a major independent power under the **Adil Shahi dynasty**, competing and negotiating with both Ahmadnagar and the Mughals.

**Golconda:** Continued as an important independent eastern Deccan state.

Maratha chiefs: Continued to participate in these political struggles. Some sought to protect local rights, while others used warfare to increase their influence.

**Shahaji:** Emerged as an important military figure within these conflicts. His major attempt to **preserve a Nizam Shahi state** belongs especially to the events after 1630, so we will examine it in the next chapter's background rather than jumping ahead here.
