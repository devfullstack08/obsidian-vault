**Full name:** Sambhaji Shivaji Bhonsle
**Born:** 14 May 1657, Purandar Fort
**Father:** Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj
**Mother:** Saibai Nimbalkar
**Reign:** 1680–1689
**Formal coronation:** 1681
**Death:** 11 March 1689

Central question: After Shivaji's death in 1680, how did Sambhaji secure the throne, defend the Maratha state against Aurangzeb, and why did his execution in 1689 fail to end Maratha resistance?

This is the story of a kingdom fighting for survival against an empire with far greater resources.

In Chapter 4, we studied how Shivaji established sovereign kingship at Raigad in 1674, created administrative institutions, developed forts and naval forces, and extended Maratha influence into southern India.

On 3 April 1680, Shivaji died at Raigad.

His death created an immediate political crisis.

---
## 1. Why was the situation dangerous in 1680?

Shivaji had created a strong regional kingdom, but it still faced powerful opponents.

| Political power            | Ruler or leadership in 1680               | Relationship with the Marathas           |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Maratha kingdom            | Succession disputed after Shivaji's death | Internal struggle for leadership         |
| Mughal Empire              | Aurangzeb (1658–1707)                     | Major territorial and military rival     |
| Bijapur Sultanate          | Sikandar Adil Shah                        | Rival, with shifting political relations |
| Golconda Sultanate         | Abul Hasan Tana Shah                      | Important potential diplomatic partner   |
| Siddis of Janjira          | Siddi military leadership                 | Contested Konkan coast and sea routes    |
| Portuguese Estado da Índia | Portuguese administration at Goa          | Commercial and territorial rival         |

### Three immediate problems

1. Succession: Who would become the next Chhatrapati?

2. Unity: Would Shivaji's ministers, commanders and family members support one ruler?

3. External enemies: Would Aurangzeb and Bijapur exploit the uncertainty?

The greatest immediate danger was that a divided leadership could weaken the army and allow an enemy to capture the kingdom's strategic forts.

## 2. Succession crisis — Sambhaji versus Rajaram (1680)

### Who were the claimants?

**Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj**
Sambhaji Eldest son
Mother: Saibai
Born: 1657

**Rajaram**
Younger son
Mother: Soyarabai
Born: 1670

### Why was there a dispute if Sambhaji was the eldest?

Sambhaji's relationship with Shivaji had been strained in the later 1670s.

In 1678, he had temporarily joined the Mughal camp associated with Diler Khan, before returning to the Marathas.

By the time Shivaji died, Sambhaji was staying at Panhala, rather than controlling the royal capital of Raigad.

Some influential members of the court supported the younger Rajaram.

Rajaram's mother Soyarabai and her political allies sought to place him on the throne.

### What happened?

1. Shivaji died on 3 April 1680.
    
2. Rajaram was installed as ruler under the influence of the court faction supporting him.
    
3. Sambhaji rejected this arrangement.
    
4. Sambhaji gathered supporters at Panhala.
    
5. Hambirrao Mohite, the important Maratha commander, supported Sambhaji.
    
6. Sambhaji gained control of Raigad and displaced the rival faction.

He emerged as the effective ruler during 1680 and underwent a formal coronation in 1681.

The exact dating and ceremonial details are reported differently in some sources, but the essential outcome is clear: Sambhaji secured the Maratha throne.

### Why did Hambirrao Mohite's support matter?

Hambirrao was a powerful military commander and also related to Soyarabai.

His support demonstrated that military allegiance did not necessarily follow family relationships.

For a newly established kingdom, the backing of the army was essential.

### Historical significance

The succession conflict exposed a structural weakness of hereditary kingship: even a powerful ruler could leave behind competing claims and political factions.

Sambhaji secured the throne, but the dispute consumed political energy at a time when the Mughal threat was increasing.

Although Sambhaji inherited Shivaji's institutions, the pressures of continuous warfare placed enormous demands on his government.

### Who was Kavi Kalash?

Kavi Kalash was a close associate of Sambhaji, known for his literary interests and political influence.

His prominence sometimes caused resentment among other ministers and court factions.

He remained with Sambhaji during his final crisis in 1689.

### Did Sambhaji abolish the Ashta Pradhan?

No evidence supports a simple claim that he abolished Shivaji's entire ministerial structure.

However, personnel changed, rivalries developed and trusted advisers such as Kavi Kalash acquired substantial influence.

UPSC point: Sambhaji inherited a functioning but still evolving kingdom. Maintaining military resources and political loyalty became more difficult under prolonged Mughal pressure.

## 4. Why did Aurangzeb personally come to the Deccan in 1681?

![Last of the Great Mughal Emperors - WSJ](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/52OgT18spn4Gj9cNehJfjowfVV34QuPt75JsFlYMYwz8QCOfJymUTjntgvaOHs2RUNV0hVgErqVL3lo87zClsqLkdIFHhU5I9_1LmX98BDiChRNA4_fyFNKR1N70sEK9dIjuYV5JxYL4vknNr2Om-j1wopLs5ZnwDLNC-lyC4wc?purpose=inline)

This is the most important political background of Sambhaji's reign.

Aurangzeb had become Mughal emperor in 1658.

By 1681, he had ruled for more than twenty years, but Mughal control over the Deccan remained incomplete.

### Aurangzeb faced several challenges

1. Independent Maratha power: Shivaji had established a sovereign kingdom, and Sambhaji continued to resist Mughal expansion.

2. Independent Deccan Sultanates: Bijapur and Golconda still controlled important territories.

3. Rebellion of Prince Akbar: Aurangzeb's son, Muhammad Akbar, had rebelled against him.

4. Imperial security: Raids and military activity in the Deccan threatened Mughal communications, revenues and political authority.

### The rebellion of Prince Akbar

Prince Akbar rebelled against his father during the conflict involving the Rajputs.

After failing to secure his position in northern India, he travelled towards the Deccan.

He sought support from Sambhaji.

### Why would Sambhaji shelter Aurangzeb's son?

Because Prince Akbar represented a possible political weakness within the Mughal dynasty.

If Aurangzeb had to deal with both an internal claimant and the Maratha kingdom, his military position might become more difficult.

For Sambhaji, supporting or sheltering Akbar was a diplomatic opportunity.

However, the relationship did not produce a successful replacement of Aurangzeb.

### Aurangzeb's major decision

Aurangzeb moved personally towards the Deccan in 1681, bringing the imperial political and military centre closer to the campaigns.

His Deccan operations became a prolonged struggle lasting until his death in 1707.

Important distinction: Aurangzeb did not come south only because of Sambhaji. The Marathas, the independent Sultanates and Prince Akbar's rebellion all influenced his decision.

## 5. The Maratha attack on Burhanpur (1681)
(Chhava - First war scene )

![Burhanpur | Timings, History & Visitor Guide](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/SWOsYlc6v-rgQvSl-iRnPrU4TDI15XlgQljjGtIOjVaA7d2QChlJcKeuk-6wRHMitmwYMrnRMgh040K7q0SNC-mXIfYVctM7NXB8w9s8S6NnioU70aa8UPwQzcLFTvXNpFE9gYvoNo_F9HLdqlq1W5mQbG5xxXf2fc7PpK5ImlM?purpose=inline)

Burhanpur, in present-day Madhya Pradesh, was a major Mughal city and an important military and commercial centre associated with routes into the Deccan.
### Why was it important?

Burhanpur helped connect northern Mughal territories with the Deccan.

It supported military movements, supply arrangements and trade.
### What happened in 1681?

Maratha forces under **Hambirrao Mohite** carried out a significant raid on Burhanpur in early 1681.

The attack inflicted losses and exposed vulnerabilities in Mughal regional security.

### Why was this serious for Aurangzeb?

The Mughal Empire possessed much greater resources than the Marathas, but a large empire could not defend every commercial centre equally at all times.

Mobile Maratha forces could strike at valuable targets and withdraw before a major imperial army arrived.
### Result

The attack increased pressure on Mughal authority and contributed to the escalating conflict.

UPSC significance: The Marathas continued using mobile operations against Mughal revenue and supply networks after Shivaji's death.

## 6. Siege of Ramshej Fort (from 1682) — why did a small fort trouble the Mughals?

![Ramshej Fort](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/Z_lROJUyQm_T1o__3Jr_s7cwxTzKtlBHzxhKfDXcm0drcOZJtX6G0zwYiUEBAzj9kJrjOsFWqEGdkhUW9JeDbUanrkbuZ0H0QFTNoKOR60JzTDvZeH0xM-y_VCZdxiFjeLEyWdsqLcG5PJ69VR7QQQwpgfm3Hg22Qh1cN1fYW8YssAo82wKOJ3C_ZiAyhALM?purpose=inline)

### Where was Ramshej?

Ramshej Fort is near Nashik in Maharashtra.

It occupied an elevated defensive position in a strategically important part of the northern Deccan.

### What did the Mughals want?

Aurangzeb's forces wanted to reduce Maratha fortifications systematically.

Capturing forts could help them control territory, secure communication routes and deny defensive bases to the Marathas.

### What happened?

A Mughal siege of Ramshej began in 1682.

The defenders offered prolonged resistance.

Despite artillery, manpower and siege efforts, the Mughals initially failed to take the fort quickly.

Control of Ramshej was achieved only much later, generally dated 1687, after years of resistance and changes in command.

### Why could a small fort resist a large army?

- Its elevated position restricted approaches.
    
- A defending garrison could fight from prepared positions.
    
- Attackers had to maintain supplies and siege equipment.
    
- The surrounding region remained contested.
    
- A siege could absorb troops without producing rapid strategic gains.

### What is the historical significance?

A powerful empire could capture territory but still struggle to maintain continuous control over a network of fortified positions.

This was one of the fundamental problems Aurangzeb faced in the Deccan.

UPSC analytical point: Ramshej illustrates the difference between superiority in military resources and effective territorial control.

## 7. Conflict with the Siddis of Janjira (1681–1682)

![Forts in Maharashtra – Rich Maratha Heritage | Club Mahindra](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/UvTea-7WpoMqWKYQGrNMQrmG3awPtlUP6bSNaUgHuvm0NA6XKGRnmoWwYaviNcWJ9FHwO24bOX9cUdb-myUbPo_05B0PDDhlo0kx4g6IGdv-jT4qaKpWqKpyKqOs6S4Ino2JGA6FvpS-g4dreaAPk7HHWBAAsxgBa9bk60opURU?purpose=inline)

### Who were the Siddis?

The Siddis of Janjira were a powerful maritime military establishment based around the fortified island of Janjira on the Konkan coast.

They were frequently connected with the politics of the Deccan Sultanates and Mughal maritime interests.

### Why was Janjira important?
Janjira controlled a strong coastal position and could threaten Maratha shipping and settlements.

The sea fort was difficult to capture because:

- It was surrounded by water.
    
- Its walls were strongly constructed.
    
- Supplies and reinforcements could reach it by sea.
    
- Coastal warfare required different capabilities from inland campaigns.
### What did Sambhaji do?

Sambhaji's forces launched operations against Siddi positions, including efforts directed towards Janjira.

However, they failed to conquer the island fortress permanently.

### Why did this matter?

Sambhaji could not concentrate only on fighting the Mughals.

He also had to defend his western coastline against maritime rivals.

This placed additional pressure on manpower, naval resources and revenue.

UPSC significance: The struggle shows that control of the Konkan required naval and coastal capabilities, not merely possession of inland forts.

### Why did the Portuguese become involved?

The Portuguese had long maintained important settlements along India's western coast, particularly Goa.

Their authorities pursued their own commercial, religious and political interests.

Relations with the Marathas were shaped by disputes over coastal territory, trade, shipping and the behaviour of allied or hostile military groups.

Portuguese actions and their interactions with Mughal interests contributed to tensions with Sambhaji.
### Sambhaji's campaigns

In 1683, Sambhaji launched major operations against Portuguese-controlled territories in the Goa region.

Maratha forces attacked areas including **Bardez** and **Salcete**.

Portuguese settlements experienced serious military pressure.

### Did Sambhaji conquer Goa?

No.

He threatened Portuguese territory and achieved important military successes, but he did not permanently conquer Goa.

Mughal military developments and broader strategic concerns helped draw Maratha forces away from sustained operations.
### Why was the Portuguese conflict significant?

It demonstrated Sambhaji's ability to challenge a European colonial power.

But it also showed the dangers of fighting several opponents simultaneously.

## 9. Aurangzeb changes his strategy — Bijapur and Golconda (1685–1687)

This development transformed the political situation of the Deccan.

### Why did Aurangzeb attack the Sultanates?

Bijapur and Golconda had remained independent despite Mughal pressure.

Their continued existence complicated imperial expansion and allowed competing political alliances in the Deccan.

Aurangzeb decided to eliminate these independent kingdoms.

## 10. Why did Sambhaji's position weaken after 1687?

It would be inaccurate to say that Sambhaji simply stopped resisting.

However, conditions became increasingly difficult.

### Challenge 1. Concentrated Mughal pressure

With Bijapur and Golconda conquered, Mughal commanders could devote greater attention to Maratha territories and fortifications.

### Challenge 2. Loss of experienced military leaders

Sambhaji's important commander Hambirrao Mohite died in 1687.

The loss weakened the continuity of senior military leadership.

### Challenge 3. Desertions and political divisions

Some regional chiefs and military officers changed allegiance.

Such changes could provide the Mughals with intelligence, local access and additional manpower.

### Challenge 4. Resource pressure

Continuous fighting required supplies, horses, fort garrisons and money.

The Marathas could raid and disrupt Mughal positions, but they also had to protect their own settlements and revenue base.

### Challenge 5. Scattered military commitments

Conflict with the Mughals, Siddis and Portuguese had occupied different regions.

UPSC conclusion: Sambhaji's difficulties resulted from an interaction of external military pressure, political divisions and resource constraints—not simply from one personal decision.

## 11. Capture of Sambhaji at Sangameshwar (February 1689)

### Where is Sangameshwar?

Sangameshwar is in the Konkan region of present-day Ratnagiri district, Maharashtra.

Its location in the Western Ghats–Konkan region was important for movement between coastal settlements and inland forts.
### What was Sambhaji doing there?

In early 1689, Sambhaji was staying in the Sangameshwar area with a relatively limited entourage, including his trusted adviser Kavi Kalash.

The Mughal authorities obtained information concerning his presence.
### Who captured Sambhaji?

The Mughal commander **Muqarrab Khan**, also known as **Shaikh Nizam**, organized a surprise operation.

His forces moved rapidly towards Sangameshwar.

In early February 1689, Sambhaji and Kavi Kalash were captured.

Many historical chronologies give 1 February 1689, although some sources differ over the precise capture date.
### Was Sambhaji betrayed?

Some later accounts identify **Ganoji Shirke**, a relative with political grievances, as having supplied information to the Mughals.

However, the exact role of individual informants is less securely established than the capture itself.
### Why was the capture possible?

It illustrates the danger of surprise operations against an exposed command group.

A ruler might command numerous forts and military units, yet remain vulnerable when travelling with limited protection.

Intelligence and speed enabled the Mughal commander to achieve what years of large military campaigns had failed to accomplish.

## 12. Execution of Sambhaji — 11 March 1689

After his capture, Sambhaji and Kavi Kalash were brought before the Mughal authorities.

They were subjected to harsh treatment, and Aurangzeb ordered their execution.

The commonly accepted date is 11 March 1689.

The execution is associated with the **Tulapur–Vadhu region**, near the **Bhima River** in present-day Maharashtra.

### Why did Aurangzeb execute Sambhaji?

The execution occurred within the context of a prolonged war against a ruler who had resisted Mughal supremacy and harboured the emperor's rebellious son.

Several historical narratives describe demands involving the surrender of forts, treasury or information, as well as religious conversion.

However, the precise conversations, conditions and final exchanges are reported differently in the sources.

It is important to distinguish:

- Established: Sambhaji was captured by Mughal forces, tortured and executed on Aurangzeb's orders.
    
- Debated details: The exact terms offered to him, his responses, the role of informants and particular descriptions of his last days.
### Why did this matter so much?

Aurangzeb eliminated the reigning Chhatrapati, hoping to weaken Maratha political resistance.

But the Maratha leadership did not abandon the struggle.

The kingdom possessed a network of forts, military commanders and local administrative relationships that continued beyond the life of one ruler.

Historical lesson: Capturing a monarch did not automatically destroy a decentralized resistance movement.

## 13. Was Sambhaji a successful ruler?

A balanced UPSC assessment must recognize both achievements and limitations.

| Area               | Achievements                                                  | Limitations                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Succession         | Secured authority after the 1680 crisis                       | Court factions and political rivalries persisted             |
| Military           | Continued resistance against Mughal expansion                 | Could not permanently prevent Mughal territorial advances    |
| Diplomacy          | Sheltered Prince Akbar, exploiting Mughal dynastic divisions  | Akbar's presence produced no decisive political reversal     |
| Western coast      | Challenged Siddis and Portuguese                              | Janjira and Goa were not permanently conquered               |
| Fort defence       | Maratha strongholds resisted prolonged sieges                 | Several forts and territories came under increasing pressure |
| Political survival | Maintained independent Maratha kingship for almost nine years | Capture in 1689 created a severe leadership crisis           |

### The most important historical judgment

Sambhaji inherited an established kingdom but faced a more concentrated imperial threat than Shivaji had faced during much of his reign.

His military policies did not secure a decisive victory over Aurangzeb.

Nevertheless, the Maratha state continued resisting one of the most powerful empires of the period.

His death was a major setback, but it did not bring the Mughal-Maratha War to an end.
