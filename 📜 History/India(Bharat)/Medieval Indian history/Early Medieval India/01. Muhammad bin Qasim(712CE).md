
Muḥammad ibn al-Qāsim al-Thaqafī (31 December 695–18 July 715) was an **Arab military commander** in service of the **Umayyad Caliphate** who led the Muslim conquest of Sindh (and Punjab, part of ancient Sindh), inaugurating the Umayyad campaigns in India.

***Arab invasion of Sindh by Muhammad bin Qasim (711–712)***

His military exploits led to the establishment of the Islamic province of Sindh, and the takeover of the region from the **Sindhi Brahman dynasty** and its ruler, **Raja Dahir**, who was subsequently decapitated with his head sent to al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf in Basra. With the capture of the then-capital of Aror by Arab forces, Muhammad ibn al-Qasim became the first Muslim to have successfully captured Indian land, which marked the beginning of Muslim rule in South Asia. 

Muḥammad ibn al-Qāsim al-Thaqafī belonged to the **Banu Thaqif**, an Arab tribe that is concentrated around the city of Taif in western Arabia. After the Muslim conquest of Persia, he was assigned as the **governor of Fars**, likely succeeding his uncle **Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi**. From 708 to 711, Muḥammad ibn al-Qāsim al-Thaqafī led the Sindh conquest. He established Islamic rule throughout the region, serving as governor of Sindh from 712 until his death in 715. After his last conquest of Multan (Punjab), he returned to Arabia where on the way he died in Mosul, in modern Iraq, though some sources[who?] record that his body was buried in Makran, a semi-desert coastal region in Balochistan.


#### Short story 

**Muhammad bin Qasim (c. 695–715 CE)** was a young Arab general of the **Umayyad Caliphate**. In **711 CE**, he entered the Indian subcontinent through the **Sindh region** with an Arab army.

He first captured **Debal**, an important port near present-day Karachi. Then he defeated **Raja Dahir**, the ruler of Sindh, in **712 CE**. After Dahir's defeat, Muhammad bin Qasim captured several important places in Sindh and moved north toward **Multan**, which he also conquered.

So remember the route roughly as:

**Arabia → Makran → Sindh → Debal → defeat of Raja Dahir → Sindh → Multan**

His campaign lasted only a few years. After political changes in the Umayyad government, he was **recalled from India around 715 CE**. He died soon afterward, probably while in custody, although different historical accounts give different stories about his death.
