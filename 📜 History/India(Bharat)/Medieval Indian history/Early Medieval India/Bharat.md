# Bharat in the early medieval age

Three maps. Same coastline. Read them in order. The colors are who holds the ground. Cream land is smaller rulers, and they are real. The shades are classroom guides, not surveyed borders.

There is no photograph of these kings. The pictures on the 1000 map are the buildings their houses are remembered by.

Mahmud's raids, numbered 1 to 10, are on [[02. Muhammad of Ghazni( 998–1030AD)]]. This page is the country he rode into.

## The north, as a memory sketch

This is only the north. The maps also show the south.

```
KASHMIR
```

## 1. About 850 — three powers, one prize

![[bharat-c850.png]]

Harsha dies in **647** and leaves no heir who keeps his empire. By about **850** three houses fight for **Kannauj**.

| House | Where | Peak | End |
| ----- | ----- | ---- | --- |
| Pratiharas | West and the Ganga plain | Mihira Bhoja, about 836–885 | The line is a shadow by 1000, and gone by about 1036 |
| Palas | Bengal and Bihar | Devapala, about 810–850 | Replaced in Bengal by the Senas in the 12th century |
| Rashtrakutas | The Deccan | From about 753, capital Manyakheta | Tailapa II ends them in **973** |

## 2. About 1000 — your sketch, plus the south

![[bharat-c1000.png]]

The three empires have broken. The north is the sketch above. The south is the part a north-only sketch misses: **Western Chalukyas** in the Deccan, from 973, and the **Cholas** under **Rajaraja I (985–1014)**. In 1000 the Cholas are the strongest state on the map.

Ghaznavids are at the gate. Mahmud raids. He keeps the northwest. He does not take these kingdoms.

| House in 1000             | Falls by                                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hindu Shahi               | 1026, under Mahmud                                                                         |
| Pratiharas of Kannauj     | about 1036. Rajyapala already flees in 1018                                                |
| Chahamanas                | 1192, Second Tarain. Prithviraj III is not the king of 1000                                |
| Tomaras of Delhi          | 12th century, when the Chauhans take Delhi                                                 |
| Chaulukyas of Gujarat     | Vaghelas about 1244. Delhi annexes Gujarat in 1304. Bhima I keeps his throne after Somnath |
| Paramaras of Malwa        | 1305. Bhoja, about 1010–1055, is the great king, just after this map                       |
| Chandelas                 | Fade under the Sultanate. Kalinjar falls in the Khalji period. Mahmud does not keep it     |
| Palas                     | Senas in the 12th century                                                                  |
| Western Chalukyas         | about 1189. Yadavas, Hoysalas and Kakatiyas divide that country                            |
| Cholas                    | 1279, the Pandyas                                                                          |
| Kashmir, Lohara from 1003 | Sangramaraja stops Mahmud in 1015. Hindu rule ends with the Kashmir Sultanate, 1339        |

## 3. About 1206 — the old north has fallen

![[bharat-c1206.png]]

**1192** Tarain, the Chahamanas fall. **1194** Chandawar, Jayachandra of the Gahadavalas falls. **1206** Aibak at Delhi. Gujarat and Malwa are still standing. The Deccan has new names: Yadavas, Kakatiyas, Hoysalas, and the Pandyas are rising.

That Delhi is the start of the next maps: [[Bharat from the later medieval age to 1947]].
