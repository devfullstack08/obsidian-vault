## 18. UPSC Mains — Model answer

Question: Examine the factors responsible for the early rise of Shivaji from a regional jagirdar's family to an independent political power by 1659. (250 words)

The early rise of Shivaji between 1630 and 1659 resulted from the interaction of political instability, geographical advantages, local institutional networks and military leadership.

Political conditions: The Mughal conquest of Ahmadnagar and the consequent reorganization of the Deccan weakened established authority. Bijapur's involvement in multiple military conflicts provided opportunities for regional commanders to expand their influence.

Family background: Shivaji belonged to the Bhonsle family, which already possessed military connections and revenue rights. The Pune jagir supplied an initial territorial foundation for political consolidation.

Geographical advantages: The Maval valleys and the forts of the Western Ghats offered defensible positions and strategically important routes. Shivaji exploited knowledge of these locations to strengthen control over surrounding settlements.

Local support: Cooperation with influential deshmukhs and military retainers helped develop an increasingly effective political and military following.

Military strategy: The acquisition of forts such as Torna and Rajgad consolidated his base, while the conquest of Javli extended his influence into vital Western Ghat territory.

The defeat of Afzal Khan's Bijapur expedition at Pratapgad in 1659 marked a decisive achievement. It enhanced Shivaji's prestige and demonstrated his ability to resist superior military forces.

Thus, his early success represented the transformation of inherited local resources into an expanding political authority through strategic leadership and institution-building.

## 19. Rapid revision — remember the story in sequence

1. 1630 — Birth: Shivaji born at Shivneri to Shahaji and Jijabai.
    
2. 1636 — Political change: Mughals ended Ahmadnagar's independent rule; Shahaji entered Bijapur service.
    
3. Pune jagir: Local administration and military networks provided Shivaji's early political base.
    
4. c. 1646 — Torna: Shivaji began acquiring strategically important forts.
    
5. 1648–1649 — Shahaji's detention: Highlighted the dangers of political independence from Bijapur.
    
6. 1656–1657 — Javli: Conquest strengthened Shivaji's control of the Western Ghats.
    
7. Pratapgad: A new fortified position supported control over Javli.
    
8. 1657–1658 — Mughal politics: Shivaji challenged Mughal frontier positions while Aurangzeb fought for the throne.
    
9. 1659 — Afzal Khan: Bijapur attempted to suppress Shivaji through a major military expedition.
    
10. 10 November 1659 — Pratapgad victory: Shivaji emerged as a much more formidable regional power.
