
**Name:** Ghora  
**Full name:** Ghora Angirasa  
**Tradition:** Vedic / Upanishadic tradition  
**Mentioned in:** Chandogya Upanishad  
**Known for:** Teaching spiritual wisdom to Krishna, the son of Devaki  

Ghora Angirasa was an ancient Vedic sage. He is mainly known from the Chandogya Upanishad, where he gives spiritual teaching to Krishna, the son of Devaki.

---

## 2. Meaning of the Name

### Ghora

The word **Ghora** can mean intense, terrible, deep, powerful, or serious.

In the name **Ghora Angirasa**, it likely shows that he was a sage of deep spiritual intensity.

### Angirasa

**Angirasa** means he belonged to the lineage of **Angiras**.

Angiras was one of the great ancient Vedic sages. So Ghora Angirasa means:

> Ghora of the Angirasa lineage.

---

## 3. Where Is Ghora Mentioned?

Ghora Angirasa is mentioned in the **Chandogya Upanishad**, especially in section **3.17**.

The Chandogya Upanishad is one of the major Upanishads and belongs to the **Samaveda** tradition.

In this section, Ghora Angirasa teaches Krishna, the son of Devaki, a spiritual doctrine related to life, sacrifice, discipline, and death.

---

## 4. Connection with Krishna

One of the most important points about Ghora Angirasa is his connection with **Krishna Devakiputra**, meaning **Krishna, son of Devaki**.

The Chandogya Upanishad says that Ghora Angirasa gave this teaching to Krishna, the son of Devaki.

This is important because Devaki’s son Krishna is traditionally connected with Lord Krishna by many Hindu traditions.

However, in academic discussion, some scholars say that the text only mentions “Krishna, son of Devaki” and does not describe him in the full Puranic form as Lord Krishna of the Mahabharata.

So we should remember both views:

| View | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Traditional Hindu view | Krishna Devakiputra is Lord Krishna |
| Academic cautious view | The text mentions Krishna, son of Devaki, but does not give a full biography |

---

## 5. What Did Ghora Teach Krishna?

Ghora Angirasa taught that **human life itself can be understood as a yajna**, meaning a sacred sacrifice.

Normally, yajna means a ritual sacrifice performed with fire, offerings, and mantras.

But Ghora gave a deeper spiritual meaning:

> Life itself is a yajna.

This means every action, discipline, suffering, self-control, charity, truthfulness, and final death can be understood as part of a sacred spiritual journey.

---

## 6. Main Teaching: Life as Sacrifice

In Vedic tradition, sacrifice was very important. People performed yajnas for spiritual and social purposes.

Ghora Angirasa took this idea deeper.

He taught that a person’s whole life can become a sacrifice if it is lived with:

- Tapasya
- Self-control
- Truth
- Charity
- Simplicity
- Non-violence
- Inner purity
- Knowledge

So his teaching moves from outer ritual to inner spiritual discipline.

---

## 7. Important Virtues in His Teaching

Ghora Angirasa’s teaching is connected with spiritual virtues such as:

| Virtue | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tapasya | Discipline, austerity, self-control |
| Dana | Charity, giving |
| Arjava | Straightforwardness, honesty |
| Ahimsa | Non-violence |
| Satya-vachana | Truthful speech |
| Inner purity | Clean intention and spiritual life |

These values are very important in Upanishadic spirituality.

---

## 8. Teaching About Death

Ghora Angirasa also gives a teaching connected with the final moment of life.

At the time of death, a seeker should remember the highest truth.

The idea is that a person should not fear death if he has understood the immortal truth.

In simple words:

> The body may end, but the higher Self is not destroyed.

This teaching is very close to the Upanishadic idea that the Atman is immortal.

---

## 9. “He Never Thirsted Again” Meaning

Some translations explain that after Krishna received this teaching from Ghora Angirasa, he became free from spiritual thirst.

This does not mean normal physical thirst.

It means:

> Krishna no longer had spiritual doubt or inner incompleteness.

He became satisfied with the knowledge of truth.

---

## 10. Why Is Ghora Angirasa Important?

Ghora Angirasa is important for three main reasons:

1. He is mentioned in an ancient Upanishad.
2. He taught Krishna, son of Devaki.
3. He gave a deep spiritual teaching that life itself is a yajna.

His importance is not because of many stories, but because of one powerful philosophical teaching.

---

## 11. Difference Between Ghora and Other Famous Sages

| Sage | Famous for |
|---|---|
| Vyasa | Dividing Vedas and composing Mahabharata |
| Vashishtha | Royal guru, wisdom, Ramayana tradition |
| Vishwamitra | Gayatri Mantra and tapasya |
| Durvasa | Anger and curses |
| Narada | Devotion, travel, divine messages |
| Ghora Angirasa | Teaching Krishna in Chandogya Upanishad |

So Ghora is mainly an Upanishadic teacher, not a Puranic story-based sage.

---

## 12. Is Ghora Angirasa Krishna’s Guru?

We can say:

> Ghora Angirasa was one of the spiritual teachers connected with Krishna in the Upanishadic tradition.

But we should not say he was Krishna’s only guru.

In popular tradition, Krishna also studied under **Guru Sandipani**.

So better sentence:

> Ghora Angirasa is mentioned as a sage who gave spiritual teaching to Krishna, son of Devaki.

---

## 13. Difference Between Ghora Angirasa and Sandipani

| Point | Ghora Angirasa | Sandipani |
|---|---|---|
| Text tradition | Upanishadic |
| Connected text | Chandogya Upanishad |
| Role | Spiritual teacher |
| Teaching | Life as sacrifice, immortality, discipline |
| Sandipani’s role | Krishna’s guru in popular/Puranic tradition |
| Sandipani taught | General education, scriptures, weapons, arts |

## 14. Simple Story Form

In ancient times, there lived a sage named **Ghora Angirasa**. He belonged to the Angirasa lineage of Vedic sages.

He had deep knowledge of spiritual truth. He taught that human life should not be seen only as ordinary living. Life itself can become a sacred yajna.

According to the Chandogya Upanishad, Ghora Angirasa gave this knowledge to **Krishna, the son of Devaki**.

He taught Krishna that discipline, charity, truthfulness, non-violence, and purity are like offerings in the sacrifice of life.

He also gave teaching about the final moment of life, where a person should remember the immortal truth and not fear death.

After receiving this knowledge, Krishna became spiritually satisfied and free from inner thirst.

---

## 15. Main Philosophy

Ghora Angirasa’s philosophy can be understood in this way:

### Outer Yajna

Fire sacrifice with offerings, rituals, and mantras.

### Inner Yajna

Life lived with truth, self-control, charity, purity, and knowledge.

His teaching shows that spirituality is not only ritual. It is also how we live daily life.

Sage Ghora, also known as Ghora Angirasa, was an ancient Vedic sage from the Angirasa lineage. He is mentioned in the Chandogya Upanishad. ---

## 19. Long Answer

Ghora Angirasa was an important Upanishadic sage. He belonged to the Angirasa lineage and is mentioned in the Chandogya Upanishad. His most important role is that he gave spiritual teaching to Krishna, son of Devaki.

His teaching explains that life itself is a yajna. A human being should live with discipline, charity, honesty, truthfulness, non-violence, and inner purity. These qualities become spiritual offerings in the sacrifice of life.

Ghora Angirasa also gave teaching about the final moment of life. A person who understands the immortal truth should not fear death. This reflects the Upanishadic idea that the Self is beyond death.

Because of this, Ghora Angirasa is remembered as a sage who connected Vedic yajna with inner spiritual life.
