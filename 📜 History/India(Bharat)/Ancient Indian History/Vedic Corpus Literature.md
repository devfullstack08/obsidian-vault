vid - to know.
veda mean knowledge or wisdom.
## According to Hindu tradition
In Hindu belief, the Vedas were not composed in any particular year. They are considered apauruṣeya ("not of human origin"). According to tradition:

The Vedas are eternal and have always existed.
Ancient sages (rishis) did not write them; they "heard" or realized them during deep meditation.
At the beginning of each cosmic cycle, the knowledge of the Vedas is revealed again.
Later, the sage Vyasa is believed to have compiled and organized the Vedas into four collections.
So, in the traditional Hindu view, there is no year in which the Vedas came into existence.

## According to modern historical scholarship
Historians and linguists study the Vedas as ancient literature and estimate that:
The Rigveda was composed approximately 1500–1200 BCE (some scholars suggest parts could be somewhat earlier or later).
The other Vedas (Samaveda, Yajurveda, and Atharvaveda) were composed over the following centuries, roughly until 1000–600 BCE.

## Are the Vedas the oldest texts available to humankind?
They are among the oldest surviving religious texts and among the oldest preserved works in an **Indo-European** language. However, they are not the oldest known written texts. Earlier written works include:
Instructions of Shuruppak (around 2600 BCE)
Epic of Gilgamesh (earliest versions around 2100 BCE)
Ancient Egyptian texts such as the Pyramid Texts (around 2400 BCE)

What makes the Vedas distinctive is that they were preserved through an exceptionally precise oral tradition for many centuries before being written down.
So, the answer depends on the perspective:

Hindu tradition: The Vedas are eternal and have no date of origin.
Historical scholarship: The oldest portions of the Rigveda are generally dated to about 1500–1200 BCE.
