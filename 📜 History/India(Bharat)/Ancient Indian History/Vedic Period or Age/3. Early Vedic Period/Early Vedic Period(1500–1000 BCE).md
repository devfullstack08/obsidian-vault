The Early Vedic Age, also called the Rigvedic Age, is primarily known through the Rigveda, the oldest of the four Vedas.

### Geography

The early Vedic communities were concentrated in the region called Sapta Sindhu, or the land of seven rivers, broadly associated with Punjab, the Indus river system and surrounding north-western areas(**north-western part of the Indian subcontinent**).
 
The Rigveda mentions rivers such as Sindhu (Indus), Vitasta (Jhelum), Asikni (Chenab), Parushni (Ravi), Vipasha (Beas) and Shutudri (Sutlej).

The precise identification of the Vedic Sarasvati with modern or ancient river systems remains debated.

### Political organization

Society was organized around kinship groups and tribes called **Jana**.

The tribal chief was known as the **Rajan**. He was expected to protect his people, lead in warfare and perform religious functions.

His authority was not equivalent to that of the powerful hereditary monarchs who emerged in later centuries.

Two important assemblies were:

- **Sabha**: Usually associated with a smaller council of influential or senior members.
    
- **Samiti**: A broader tribal assembly involved in public affairs.
    

Other institutions, such as the **Vidatha**, are also mentioned, although their exact functions remain debated.

### Economy

The economy was predominantly **pastoral(ગ્રામ્ય જીવનને લગતું**. **Cattle** were an important measure of wealth, and disputes over cattle appear frequently in Vedic hymns.

**Agriculture** was also practiced, especially barley cultivation, but it was less prominent than in the Later Vedic period.

**Crafts**, exchange and other occupations existed, but the large-scale urban economic system characteristic of Mature Harappan cities was absent from the Rigvedic social world.

### Political System

The basic political unit was the **jana**, meaning tribe or people.
Important terms include:
- **Rajan** — chief or king
- **Sabha** — assembly
- **Samiti** — larger tribal assembly
- **Grama** — village or group
The king was not initially an absolute monarch. Tribal assemblies had influence.

---
### Society

The family was generally patriarchal, with the household forming the basic social unit.

Women participated in certain religious and intellectual activities. The Vedic tradition preserves hymns associated with women seers, although these examples do not mean that women enjoyed equal rights across society.

Social distinctions existed, but the fully developed, rigid caste structures of much later periods should not be projected backwards onto all Early Vedic communities.

Early Vedic society was organized around family and clan.
The social divisions that later became the **varna system** were still developing.

The four varnas later became:
- Brahmana
- Kshatriya
- Vaishya
- Shudra

In the earlier period, these divisions were generally less rigid than in later periods.

### Religion

Early Vedic religion focused heavily on **nature-related deities and sacrificial rituals**.

Important deities included

| Deity       | Associated with           |
| ----------- | ------------------------- |
| **Indra**   | war, storms, rain         |
| **Agni**    | fire                      |
| **Varuna**  | cosmic order and waters   |
| **Surya**   | sun                       |
| **Soma**    | sacred ritual drink/deity |
| **Vayu**    | wind                      |
| **Ushas**   | dawn                      |
| **Ashvins** | healing and protection    |
Among these, **Indra and Agni** are especially prominent in the Rigveda.

Sacrificial rituals and hymns were central religious practices. Large permanent temples and image worship were not characteristic features of the Rigvedic textual evidence.

One of the famous events described in the Rigveda is the **Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna)**, associated with King Sudas and a conflict near the Parushni (Ravi) River.

---
### Yajna — Sacrifice

A central religious practice was **Yajna**, or sacrificial ritual.
Offerings such as:

- ghee
- grains
- milk
- soma

were offered into fire.
Agni was believed to carry offerings to the gods.

## Important Concept: Rita

One major Vedic idea was **Rita**.

Rita means something like:

**cosmic order, truth, and the proper order of the universe.**
