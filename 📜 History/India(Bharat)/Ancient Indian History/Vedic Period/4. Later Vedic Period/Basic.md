During the Later Vedic Period, Vedic culture expanded eastward toward the **Ganga-Yamuna plains**.
Agriculture became much more important.
Iron technology also became increasingly important.
This contributed to:
- forest clearing
- larger settlements
- increased agriculture
- stronger kingdoms

--- 
### Political Changes

The political system gradually changed from smaller tribal chiefdoms to larger kingdoms.
The power of the **Rajan** increased.
Kingship became more hereditary and powerful.
Large royal rituals also developed, such as:

Rajasuya - A royal consecration ceremony.
Ashvamedha - Horse sacrifice associated with royal power and sovereignty.
Vajapeya - Another important royal ritual connected with prestige and authority.

---
### Social Changes
The **varna system became more structured** during the Later Vedic period.

The four varnas were:
Brahmana
↓
Priests / religious specialists

Kshatriya
↓
Rulers / warriors

Vaishya
↓
Farmers / traders / producers

Shudra
↓
Service and labour groups

However, later caste or **jati** society developed over a much longer period, so it is not correct to assume that the later complex caste system already existed fully in the early Vedic age.

----
### Position of Women

Women appear in Vedic literature in religious and intellectual contexts.
Some famous women associated with later Vedic philosophical traditions include:

- **Gargi**
- **Maitreyi**

Over time, many historians argue that women's social position became more restricted in certain areas, though Vedic society was complex and practices varied.

---
### Economy
The economy changed considerably.
#### Early Vedic economy

Mostly:

- cattle herding
- pastoralism
- limited agriculture
- barter

#### Later Vedic economy

More emphasis on:

- agriculture
- iron tools
- crafts
- trade
- permanent settlements

Barley was important early on, while rice became increasingly significant in the eastern regions.

---
### Literature of the Vedic Period

Vedic literature is much larger than just the four Vedas.

The main layers are:

Vedas
↓
Samhitas(Collections of hymns, chants, and formulas.)
↓
Brahmanas(Texts explaining sacrifices and rituals.)
↓
Aranyakas(“Forest texts” dealing with symbolic and contemplative interpretations of ritual.)
↓
Upanishads
Philosophical texts exploring deeper questions such as:
- What is reality?
- What is the self?
- What is Brahman?
- What happens after death?

Later Vedic thought increasingly focused on ideas such as:

**Brahman** = ultimate reality
**Atman** = inner self or soul
A major philosophical idea became the relationship between **Atman and Brahman**.
These ideas later became fundamental to many Hindu philosophical traditions.

**Karma and Rebirth**
Ideas of **karma**, **rebirth**, and liberation became increasingly developed in the later Vedic and Upanishadic period.

These concepts later became central not only to Hindu traditions but also influenced the religious environment in which **Buddhism and Jainism** developed.
