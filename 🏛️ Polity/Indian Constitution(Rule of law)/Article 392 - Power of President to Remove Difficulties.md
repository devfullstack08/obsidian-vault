# Article 392: Power of the President to Remove Difficulties

Article 392 is the final article of **Part XXI** of the Constitution of India, which deals with "Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions."

---

## Constitution Text

> **392. Power of the President to remove difficulties** — 
> 
> (1) The President may, for the purpose of enabling this Constitution to be functioning, and in particular for enabling the transition from the provisions of the Government of India Act, 1935, to the provisions of this Constitution, by order direct that this Constitution shall, during such period as may be specified in the order, have effect subject to such adaptations, whether by way of modification, addition or omission, as he may deem to be necessary or expedient:
> 
> *Provided that no such order shall be made after the first meeting of Parliament duly constituted under Chapter II of Part V.*
> 
> (2) Every order made under clause (1) shall be laid before Parliament.
> 
> (3) The powers conferred on the President by this article, by article 324, by clause (3) of article 367 and by article 391 shall, before the commencement of this Constitution, be exercisable by the Governor-General of the Dominion of India.

---

## Detailed Analysis & Key Points

### 1. Purpose of the Article
* During the transition from a British Dominion (under the Government of India Act 1935) to an independent Republic, many legal and administrative difficulties were anticipated.
* This article gave the President of India extraordinary, temporary powers to adapt or modify constitutional provisions to ensure a smooth transition.

### 2. Limitations on the Power
* **Time Limit:** The President's power under this article was strictly temporary. It expired once the first elected Parliament held its first meeting (which occurred in May 1952).
* **Parliamentary Accountability:** Every adaptation order made by the President under this article had to be laid before the Parliament for its review.

### 3. Pre-Commencement Exercise
* Before the formal commencement of the Constitution on January 26, 1950 (when there was no President yet), these transitional powers were exercisable by the **Governor-General of the Dominion of India** (C. Rajagopalachari).
* This allowed necessary preparatory orders to be passed before Republic Day.
