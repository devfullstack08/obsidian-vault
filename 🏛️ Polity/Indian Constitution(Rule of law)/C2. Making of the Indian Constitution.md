# Chapter 2 — Making of the Indian Constitution

## 1. Introduction

The Constitution of India was framed by the **Constituent Assembly of India**.

The Constituent Assembly:

- was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946**;
- held its first meeting on **9 December 1946**;
- adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**;
- brought most provisions into force on **26 January 1950**.

> [!summary]  
> The Indian Constitution was not written by a single person. It was prepared through debates, committee reports, expert advice and decisions made by the Constituent Assembly.

---

# 2. Demand for a Constituent Assembly

## 2.1 M. N. Roy

The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward in **1934** by **M. N. Roy**.

He was:

- a revolutionary;
- a political thinker;
- a pioneer of the communist movement in India;
- later associated with Radical Humanism.

## 2.2 Indian National Congress

In **1935**, the Indian National Congress officially demanded that the Constitution of India should be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected by Indians.

## 2.3 Jawaharlal Nehru’s declaration

In **1938**, Jawaharlal Nehru declared on behalf of the Congress:

> The Constitution of free India must be framed, without outside interference, by a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise.

## 2.4 British acceptance

The British government gradually accepted the idea.

### August Offer, 1940

For the first time, the British government recognised in principle that Indians should frame their own Constitution.

### Cripps Proposals, 1942

The Cripps Mission proposed the establishment of a constitution-making body after the Second World War.

However, the proposal was rejected because:

- India was not immediately granted independence;
- provinces were given the option to remain outside the proposed Union;
- princely states were not to be represented democratically;
- the British retained control over defence during the war.

### Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

The Cabinet Mission Plan finally provided for the formation of the Constituent Assembly.

---

# 3. Cabinet Mission, 1946

## 3.1 Members of the Cabinet Mission

The Cabinet Mission consisted of three British Cabinet ministers:

1. **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** — Secretary of State for India
2. **Sir Stafford Cripps** — President of the Board of Trade
3. **A. V. Alexander** — First Lord of the Admiralty

It arrived in India in March 1946.

## 3.2 Purpose

The mission aimed to:

- discuss the transfer of power;
- establish a constitutional framework;
- create a Constituent Assembly;
- preserve Indian unity;
- resolve differences between the Congress and Muslim League.

## 3.3 Main constitutional proposal

The mission proposed:

- a Union of India;
- a weak central government;
- provincial autonomy;
- grouping of provinces;
- a Constituent Assembly;
- an interim government.

---

# 4. Composition of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly originally had **389 members**.

## 4.1 Distribution of seats

|Category|Seats|
|---|---|
|British Indian provinces|292|
|Chief Commissioners’ provinces|4|
|Princely states|93|
|**Total**|**389**|

The four Chief Commissioners’ provinces were:

- Delhi;
- Ajmer-Merwara;
- Coorg;
- British Baluchistan.

## 4.2 After Partition

Following the partition of India in 1947, the total strength of the Constituent Assembly was reduced from **389 to 299**.

|Category after Partition|Seats|
|---|---|
|Provinces|229|
|Princely states|70|
|**Total**|**299**|

---

# 5. Method of Election

The Constituent Assembly was **partly elected and partly nominated**.

## 5.1 Representatives of provinces

Representatives of British Indian provinces were elected indirectly by members of the provincial legislative assemblies.

The election used:

- proportional representation;
- single transferable vote.

## 5.2 Community representation

Seats in each province were divided among:

- Muslims;
- Sikhs;
- General category.

Members of each community in the provincial legislature elected their own representatives.

## 5.3 Princely states

Representatives of princely states were nominated by the rulers of those states.

## 5.4 Not based on universal adult franchise

The Constituent Assembly was not directly elected by all adult citizens.

The provincial assemblies that elected its members had themselves been elected under a limited franchise.

Only a small proportion of the population had voting rights during the 1946 provincial elections.

> [!important]  
> The Constituent Assembly was not directly elected through universal adult franchise.

---

# 6. Allocation of Seats

Seats were broadly allotted according to population.

The general principle was:

> One seat for approximately every one million people.

The seats assigned to each British Indian province were divided among communities according to their population.

---

# 7. Results of the Constituent Assembly Elections

Elections to the Constituent Assembly were held in July–August 1946.

For the 296 seats assigned to British India:

|Political group|Seats|
|---|---|
|Indian National Congress|208|
|Muslim League|73|
|Others and independents|15|
|**Total**|**296**|

The Congress obtained a clear majority.

The Muslim League initially participated in the elections but later boycotted the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly.

# 8. Nature of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly was:

- indirectly elected;
- partly nominated;
- representative of provinces and princely states;
- dominated by the Congress;
- not elected through universal adult franchise;
- initially created under a British plan;
- later transformed into a sovereign body.

## 8.1 Was it representative?

Although it was not directly elected, the Assembly included representatives from:

- Hindus;
- Muslims;
- Sikhs;
- Christians;
- Parsis;
- Anglo-Indians;
- Scheduled Castes;
- Scheduled Tribes;
- women;
- princely states;
- different regions;
- different political opinions.

Prominent members included:

- Dr B. R. Ambedkar;

# 9. First Meeting of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on:

> **9 December 1946**

The Muslim League boycotted the meeting.

Only about 211 members attended the first session.

## 9.1 Temporary President

**Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** was appointed temporary President of the Assembly.

He was the oldest member.

## 9.2 Permanent President

On **11 December 1946**, **Dr Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President of the Constituent Assembly.

## 9.3 Vice-President

**H. C. Mookherjee** was elected Vice-President.

Later, **V. T. Krishnamachari** was also elected Vice-President.

## 9.4 Constitutional Adviser

**B. N. Rau** was appointed Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly.

His full name was:

> **Sir Benegal Narsing Rau**

He:

- studied constitutions of different countries;
- prepared an initial constitutional memorandum;
- advised different committees;
- prepared the preliminary draft of the Constitution;
- met international constitutional experts.

# 10. Objectives Resolution

## 10.1 Introduction

Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the **Objectives Resolution** on:

> **13 December 1946**

It was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:

> **22 January 1947**

## 10.2 Main principles

The Objectives Resolution declared that:

- India would become an independent sovereign republic;
- territories of British India, princely states and other willing territories would form a Union;
- constituent units would possess autonomy;
- all power and authority would be derived from the people;
- all citizens would receive justice, equality and freedom;
- minorities and backward groups would receive safeguards;
- the territorial integrity of India would be maintained;
- India would promote world peace and human welfare.

## 10.3 Significance

The Objectives Resolution:

- provided the philosophy of the Constitution;
- guided the Constituent Assembly;
- became the basis of the Preamble;
- expressed popular sovereignty;
- committed India to justice, liberty, equality and safeguards for minorities.

> [!exam-note]  
> The **Preamble** is based largely on the **Objectives Resolution** moved by Jawaharlal Nehru.

---

# 11. Effect of the Indian Independence Act, 1947

The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament in July 1947.

It created two independent dominions:

- India;
- Pakistan.

From **15 August 1947**, the Indian Constituent Assembly became a sovereign body.

It was no longer bound by the limitations of the Cabinet Mission Plan.

## 11.1 Two functions of the Assembly

After independence, the Constituent Assembly performed two roles:

1. Constitution-making body
2. Legislative body for the Dominion of India

When it met as a Constituent Assembly, it was presided over by:

> Dr Rajendra Prasad

When it met as the Dominion Legislature, it was presided over by:

> G. V. Mavalankar

These two functions were performed on separate days.

---

# 12. Changes Caused by Partition

The Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 accepted the partition of India.

As a result:

- representatives of areas included in Pakistan left the Indian Constituent Assembly;
- the total membership was reduced to 299;
- separate Constituent Assemblies were created for India and Pakistan;
- representatives of princely states gradually joined the Indian Assembly.

The Muslim League members belonging to territories that remained in India could participate in the Indian Constituent Assembly.

# 14. Drafting Committee

## 14.1 Formation

The Drafting Committee was formed on:

> **29 August 1947**

It originally had seven members.

## 14.2 Original members

1. **Dr B. R. Ambedkar** — Chairman
2. **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar**
3. **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**
4. **K. M. Munshi**
5. **Syed Mohammad Saadulla**
6. **B. L. Mitter**
7. **D. P. Khaitan**

## 14.3 Changes in membership

B. L. Mitter resigned due to ill health and was replaced by:

> N. Madhava Rau

D. P. Khaitan died and was replaced by:

> T. T. Krishnamachari

## 14.4 Role of Dr B. R. Ambedkar

Ambedkar:

- chaired the Drafting Committee;
- defended the Draft Constitution in the Assembly;
- explained constitutional provisions;
- answered objections and proposed amendments;
- played a central role in legal drafting;
- promoted constitutional democracy and social justice.

He is popularly called:

> **Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution**

However, the Constitution was a collective product involving:

- the Constituent Assembly;
- different committees;
- B. N. Rau;
- Drafting Committee members;
- constitutional experts;
- Assembly debates.

> [!important]  
> Dr Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, not the sole author of the Constitution.

---

# 15. Role of B. N. Rau

B. N. Rau made a major contribution as Constitutional Adviser.

He:

- studied constitutions of about 60 countries;
- prepared reports on constitutional questions;
- advised committees;
- prepared the initial draft;
- travelled to the United States, Canada, Ireland and the United Kingdom;
- consulted judges and constitutional experts.

He met Justice Felix Frankfurter and Justice Learned Hand in the United States.

He also discussed the due process clause with Justice Felix Frankfurter.

After these consultations, the phrase:

> “procedure established by law”

was preferred over the American expression:

> “due process of law”

in Article 21 as originally framed.

---

# 16. Preparation of the Draft Constitution

The constitution-making process passed through several stages.

## Stage 1: Committee reports

Different committees submitted reports on:

- Union structure;
- provincial government;
- Fundamental Rights;
- minorities;
- judiciary;
- tribal areas;
- distribution of powers.

## Stage 2: B. N. Rau’s draft

B. N. Rau prepared a preliminary draft based on committee decisions.

His draft contained approximately:

- 243 Articles;
- 13 Schedules.

## Stage 3: Drafting Committee’s first draft

The Drafting Committee examined Rau’s draft and committee reports.

It prepared a Draft Constitution, which was published in:

> February 1948

The public, provincial governments, central ministries and institutions were invited to submit suggestions.

## Stage 4: Revised draft

After considering suggestions, the Drafting Committee prepared a revised draft.

It was published in:

> October 1948

## Stage 5: Introduction in the Constituent Assembly

Dr B. R. Ambedkar introduced the Draft Constitution in the Assembly on:

> **4 November 1948**

---

# 17. Readings of the Draft Constitution

The Draft Constitution was considered in three readings.

## 17.1 First Reading

Period:

> 4 November 1948 to 9 November 1948

During this stage:

- the general philosophy was discussed;
- members examined broad constitutional principles;
- no detailed clause-by-clause voting took place.

## 17.2 Second Reading

Period:

> 15 November 1948 to 17 October 1949

This was the most detailed stage.

During this stage:

- each Article was discussed;
- amendments were proposed;
- provisions were debated clause by clause;
- amendments were accepted or rejected.

## 17.3 Third Reading

Period:

> 14 November 1949 to 26 November 1949

During this stage:

- the Constitution was discussed as a whole;
- final speeches were delivered;
- the Constitution was formally adopted.

---

# 18. Amendments to the Draft Constitution

A large number of amendments were proposed.

Commonly cited figures are:

- about **7,635 amendments** were proposed;
- approximately **2,473 amendments** were actually moved and discussed.

This shows that the Constituent Assembly did not merely approve a ready-made document.

The Draft Constitution underwent extensive:

- debate;
- revision;
- modification;
- scrutiny;
- voting.

---

# 19. Adoption of the Constitution

The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on:

> **26 November 1949**

This date is mentioned in the Preamble:

> “In our Constituent Assembly this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution.”

## 19.1 Constitution Day

Since 2015, **26 November** has been officially observed as:

> **Constitution Day** or **Samvidhan Divas**

It commemorates the adoption of the Constitution.

---

# 20. Signing of the Constitution

The members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution on:

> **24 January 1950**

A total of **284 members** signed it.

The Constitution was signed in:

- Hindi;
- English.

The handwritten copies were prepared by:

- **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** — English calligraphy;
- **Vasant Krishan Vaidya** — Hindi calligraphy.

The pages were decorated by artists from Shantiniketan under the guidance of:

> Nandalal Bose

The artwork represented different periods of Indian history and culture.

---

# 21. Date of Commencement

Most provisions of the Constitution came into force on:

> **26 January 1950**

India became a:

> Sovereign Democratic Republic

Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.

## 21.1 Why was 26 January selected?

The date was chosen to honour the **Purna Swaraj Declaration**.

On **26 January 1930**, the Indian National Congress had observed:

> Independence Day

The Lahore Session of the Congress in December 1929 had declared complete independence as its goal.

Therefore, 26 January carried symbolic importance in the freedom struggle.

---

# 22. Provisions Enforced on 26 November 1949

Although the Constitution as a whole came into effect on 26 January 1950, some provisions came into operation immediately on 26 November 1949.

These included provisions relating to:

- citizenship;
- elections;
- provisional Parliament;
- temporary and transitional arrangements;
- short title;
- commencement;
- repeal of previous constitutional laws.

Relevant Articles included:

- Articles 5 to 9;
- Article 60;
- Article 324;
- Articles 366 and 367;
- Article 379;
- Articles 380, 388 and 391;
- Articles 392 and 393.

The Constituent Assembly took:

> **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**

to complete the Constitution.

It held:

- **11 sessions**;
- meetings over approximately **165 days**;
- about **114 days** were spent considering the Draft Constitution.

# 25. Original Constitution

The Constitution adopted in 1949 originally contained:

- **395 Articles**;
- **22 Parts**;
- **8 Schedules**.

The number of Articles and Schedules has increased through constitutional amendments.

> [!note]  
> Different modern sources count Articles differently because inserted Articles may carry numbers such as 21A, 239AA and 243A.

For examinations, remember the original structure:

```
395 Articles
22 Parts
8 Schedules
```

# 27. Constitutional Sources

The framers examined the constitutional experiences of several countries.

The Constitution borrowed features but adapted them to Indian circumstances.

|Country/source|Features adopted or influenced|
|---|---|
|Government of India Act, 1935|Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, public service commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details|
|United Kingdom|Parliamentary government, cabinet system, rule of law, legislative procedure, single citizenship|
|United States|Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary, impeachment, office of Vice-President|
|Ireland|Directive Principles, nomination of members to Rajya Sabha, method of presidential election|
|Canada|Federation with a strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre, appointment of governors|
|Australia|Concurrent List, freedom of trade, joint sitting of Parliament|
|Germany|Suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency|
|Soviet Union|Fundamental Duties and ideals of social, economic and political justice|
|France|Republic and ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity|
|South Africa|Constitutional amendment procedure and election of Rajya Sabha members|
|Japan|Procedure established by law|

> [!important]  
> Borrowing constitutional provisions does not mean blind copying. The framers modified foreign features to meet Indian conditions.

# 28. Criticism of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly faced several criticisms.

## 28.1 Not a popular body

Criticism:

- it was not directly elected by the people;
- universal adult franchise was not used.

Response:

- direct elections were difficult under colonial conditions;
- members were elected by provincial legislatures;
- the Assembly contained distinguished representatives from many communities.

## 28.2 Not sovereign initially

Criticism:

- it was created under the Cabinet Mission Plan;
- its authority originated from the British government.

Response:

- after the Indian Independence Act, 1947, it became a sovereign body;
- it could modify the Cabinet Mission framework;
- it framed and adopted the Constitution in the name of the people.

## 28.3 Dominated by Congress

Criticism:

- the Congress held an overwhelming majority.

Response:

- Congress itself contained diverse ideological groups;
- many members disagreed openly with the party leadership;
- debates were not always decided on rigid party lines;
- non-Congress members also made important contributions.

## 28.4 Hindu-dominated body

Criticism:

- Partition and the Muslim League’s boycott reduced Muslim representation.

Response:

- Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Scheduled Castes and tribal communities remained represented;
- minority issues received detailed discussion;
- special safeguards were considered.

## 28.5 Lawyer-dominated body

Criticism:

Many members were lawyers, making the Constitution:

- technical;
- lengthy;
- legally complex.

Response:

Legal expertise was necessary to:

- define institutions;
- distribute powers;
- establish rights;
- draft enforceable provisions;
- resolve constitutional ambiguities.

## 28.6 Time-consuming

Criticism:

The Assembly took nearly three years.

Response:

India’s Constitution had to address:

- federalism;
- minority rights;
- social inequality;
- princely states;
- Partition;
- Fundamental Rights;
- tribal administration;
- parliamentary institutions;
- emergency powers.

The time taken was reasonable for such a large and diverse country.

---

# 29. Was the Constituent Assembly Sovereign?

## Before 15 August 1947

The Assembly was created under the Cabinet Mission Plan and operated within a British constitutional framework.

Therefore, its sovereignty was limited.

## After 15 August 1947

Following the Indian Independence Act:

- British parliamentary authority over India ended;
- the Assembly could frame any Constitution;
- it could modify earlier plans;
- it performed legislative functions;
- it became a fully sovereign body.

> [!summary]  
> The Constituent Assembly was not fully sovereign at its creation, but it became sovereign after independence.

---

# 30. Was the Constituent Assembly a Congress Body?

The Congress had a clear majority, but the Assembly should not be treated as merely a Congress party meeting.

Reasons:

- Congress represented a broad national movement;
- the party included conservatives, socialists, Gandhians and liberals;
- experts who were not prominent Congress politicians were included;
- members exercised independent judgment;
- committees involved specialists;
- debates contained genuine disagreement.

Granville Austin described the Constituent Assembly as:

> “India in microcosm”

This means that it broadly reflected India’s social and political diversity, though not perfectly.

- First official Congress demand: **1935**
- Assembly formed under: **Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946**
- Original strength: **389**
- Strength after Partition: **299**
- First meeting: **9 December 1946**
- Temporary President: **Sachchidananda Sinha**
- Permanent President: **Dr Rajendra Prasad**
- Vice-President: **H. C. Mookherjee**
- Constitutional Adviser: **B. N. Rau**
- Objectives Resolution moved by: **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- Objectives Resolution adopted: **22 January 1947**
- Drafting Committee formed: **29 August 1947**
- Drafting Committee Chairman: **Dr B. R. Ambedkar**
- Constitution adopted: **26 November 1949**
- Constitution signed: **24 January 1950**
- Constitution commenced: **26 January 1950**
- Time taken: **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**
- Original Articles: **395**
- Original Parts: **22**
- Original Schedules: **8**
- Members who signed: **284**
- Sessions: **11**
- Total expenditure: approximately **₹63.96 lakh**

# 37. Common Exam Traps

## Trap 1

> The Constituent Assembly was elected directly by universal adult franchise.

**Incorrect**

It was indirectly elected by provincial legislative assemblies.

## Trap 2

> All members of the Constituent Assembly were elected.

**Incorrect**

Representatives of princely states were nominated.

## Trap 3

> The Constitution came into force on 26 November 1949.

**Partly incorrect**

It was adopted on 26 November 1949, but most provisions came into force on 26 January 1950.

## Trap 4

> Dr Ambedkar alone wrote the Constitution.

**Incorrect**

He chaired the Drafting Committee, but the Constitution was a collective product.

## Trap 5

> The Constituent Assembly was fully sovereign from its first meeting.

**Incorrect**

It became sovereign after independence on 15 August 1947.

## Trap 6

> The Constitution was signed on 26 November 1949.

**Incorrect**

It was adopted on 26 November 1949 and signed on 24 January 1950.

## Trap 7

> The Constituent Assembly was created by the Indian Independence Act.

**Incorrect**

It was formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946.

# 38. Mains Analysis

## Why was the constitution-making process successful?

The process succeeded because of:

- experienced leadership;
- committee-based work;
- constitutional expertise;
- extensive debate;
- respect for procedure;
- willingness to compromise;
- recognition of India’s diversity;
- commitment to democracy;
- public circulation of the draft;
- ability to learn from global constitutions.

## Limitations

The process also had limitations:

- no direct universal adult election;
- Congress dominance;
- limited representation of women;
- reduced Muslim representation after Partition;
- technical and legal complexity;
- limited participation of the wider public.

## Overall assessment

Despite limitations, the Constituent Assembly created a Constitution that:

- established parliamentary democracy;
- introduced universal adult franchise;
- guaranteed Fundamental Rights;
- sought social and economic transformation;
- balanced unity with federalism;
- protected diversity;
- enabled peaceful constitutional change.

# 39. Possible UPSC/GPSC Mains Questions

## Question 1

**“The Constituent Assembly was not directly elected, yet it was broadly representative.” Discuss.**

### Answer framework

1. Explain indirect election.
2. Mention limited franchise.
3. Explain Congress dominance.
4. Mention representation of communities, regions and specialists.
5. Discuss open debates and committee system.
6. Conclude that representation was imperfect but substantial for the period.

## Question 2

**Discuss the contribution of Dr B. R. Ambedkar to the making of the Indian Constitution.**

### Answer framework

- Chairman of Drafting Committee;
- legal and constitutional expertise;
- defence of constitutional provisions;
- social justice;
- Fundamental Rights;
- constitutional remedies;
- parliamentary democracy;
- constitutional morality;
- warning against inequality.

Conclude that he was central but part of a collective process.

## Question 3

**Examine the significance of the Objectives Resolution in the framing of the Indian Constitution.**

### Answer framework

- introduced by Nehru;
- adopted in January 1947;
- declared sovereignty of the people;
- promised justice, liberty and equality;
- safeguards for minorities;
- national unity;
- basis of the Preamble;
- philosophical guide to the Constitution.

## Question 4

**Was the Constituent Assembly a sovereign body? Explain.**

### Answer framework

- created under Cabinet Mission;
- initially subject to British constitutional authority;
- Independence Act ended British control;
- became sovereign after 15 August 1947;
- performed constitution-making and legislative functions;
- adopted Constitution in the name of the people.

# 40. One-Minute Revision

> The demand for a Constituent Assembly was first made by M. N. Roy in 1934. It was established under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. Its original strength was 389, which was reduced to 299 after Partition. The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946, with Sachchidananda Sinha as temporary President. Dr Rajendra Prasad became its permanent President, while B. N. Rau served as Constitutional Adviser. Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the Objectives Resolution, which became the philosophical foundation of the Preamble. The Drafting Committee was formed on 29 August 1947 under Dr B. R. Ambedkar. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, signed on 24 January 1950 and came into force on 26 January 1950. The process took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.

# 41. Chapter Summary

The Indian Constitution emerged through:

```
Demand for a Constituent Assembly
        ↓
Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946
        ↓
Election and formation of the Assembly
        ↓
Objectives Resolution
        ↓
Committee reports
        ↓
B. N. Rau’s preliminary draft
        ↓
Drafting Committee
        ↓
Three readings and amendments
        ↓
Adoption on 26 November 1949
        ↓
Signing on 24 January 1950
        ↓
Commencement on 26 January 1950
```

> [!final-takeaway]  
> The making of the Indian Constitution was a carefully debated, committee-driven and collective process. It combined the experience of the freedom struggle, Indian social realities and useful features from world constitutions to create a democratic framework for an exceptionally diverse nation.
