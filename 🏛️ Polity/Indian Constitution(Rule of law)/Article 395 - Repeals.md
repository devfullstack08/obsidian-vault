# Article 395: Repeals(રદ કરે છે)

Article 395 is the final article of the Constitution of India, located in **Part XXII**. It formally repeals the British laws that previously governed India.

---

## Constitution Text

> **395. Repeals** — The Indian Independence Act, 1947, and the Government of India Act, 1935, together with all enactments amending or supplementing the latter Act, but not including the Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949, are hereby repealed.

---

## Detailed Analysis & Key Points

### 1. Purpose of the Article
* Before January 26, 1950, India was legally governed under British laws (primarily the Government of India Act 1935, modified by the Indian Independence Act 1947).
* To become a truly sovereign, independent republic, India had to sever its legal dependency on the British Parliament. Article 395 achieved this by formally repealing those colonial statutes.

### 2. Specific Repeals
* **Government of India Act, 1935:** This was the massive administrative framework under which British India was run and served as India's interim constitution from 1947 to 1950.
* **Indian Independence Act, 1947:** The British Act that partition-divided India and created the two dominions (India and Pakistan).
* **All amendments and supplements** to the 1935 Act.

### 3. What was NOT Repealed (The Exception)
* **Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949:** This Act was deliberately **not repealed**.
* **Reason:** This Act had abolished the jurisdiction of the British Privy Council (in London) over appeals from Indian courts and transferred that authority to the Federal Court (now the Supreme Court of India). Keeping this Act alive ensured that the Supreme Court of India maintained its absolute, final judicial authority without any legal challenge.
