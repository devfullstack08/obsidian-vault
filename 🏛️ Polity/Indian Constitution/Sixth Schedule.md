# Sixth Schedule of the Indian Constitution

The Sixth Schedule contains provisions for the administration of tribal areas in the northeastern states of India. It seeks to safeguard the rights and culture of tribal populations through local autonomy.

---

## States Covered: Acronym "AMTM"

The Sixth Schedule applies **only** to the tribal areas of four northeastern states:
1. **A**ssam
2. **M**eghalaya
3. **T**ripura
4. **M**izoram

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Arunachal Pradesh**, Manipur, Nagaland, and Sikkim are **not** covered under the Sixth Schedule. (Arunachal Pradesh is a Fifth Schedule state).

---

## Key Features & Administration

### 1. Autonomous Districts and Regions
* The tribal areas in these four states are constituted as **Autonomous Districts**.
* If there are different Scheduled Tribes in an autonomous district, the Governor can divide the district into **Autonomous Regions**.
* **Power of the Governor:** The Governor has the power to organize, reorganize, redefine boundaries, or create new autonomous districts/regions.

### 2. Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)
Each autonomous district has an **Autonomous District Council (ADC)**:
* **Composition:** Consists of not more than **30 members**.
  * **4 members** are nominated by the Governor (hold office during the pleasure of the Governor).
  * **26 members** are elected on the basis of adult franchise (term is 5 years).
* **Powers of the Council:**
  * **Legislative:** Can make laws on land, forests, water, agriculture, village administration, inheritance, marriage, and social customs (subject to the assent of the Governor).
  * **Judicial:** Can constitute village councils or courts to try suits and cases between Scheduled Tribes within the area.
  * **Regulatory:** Can establish primary schools, dispensaries, markets, fisheries, and roads.
  * **Financial:** Has the power to assess and collect land revenue and impose taxes on professions, trades, animals, and vehicles.

### 3. Exclusions of Acts of Parliament and State Legislature
* Acts of Parliament or the State Legislature do not automatically apply to autonomous districts/regions, or they apply with specified modifications and exceptions.
* In Assam, the **Governor** decides on the application of central/state laws.
* In Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram, the **President** decides on central laws, and the **Governor** decides on state laws.
