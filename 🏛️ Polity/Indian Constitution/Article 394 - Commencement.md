# Article 394: Commencement(પ્રારંભ)

Article 394 is the second article of **Part XXII** of the Constitution of India. It defines when the different provisions of the Constitution came into effect.

---

## Constitution Text

> **394. Commencement** — This article and articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393 shall come into force at once, and the remaining provisions of this Constitution shall come into force on the twenty-sixth day of January, 1950, which day is referred to in this Constitution as the commencement of this Constitution.

---

## Detailed Analysis & Key Points

### 1. Two-Stage Commencement
The Indian Constitution did not come into force entirely on a single day. It was implemented in two stages:

#### Stage 1: Immediate Effect (November 26, 1949)
A total of **16 articles** came into force immediately upon adoption of the Constitution on November 26, 1949. These dealt with urgent matters:
* **Citizenship:** Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 (needed because of partition and refugee movements).
* **Oath by the President:** Article 60.
* **Elections:** Article 324 (needed to start preparing electoral rolls).
* **Definitions & Interpretation:** Articles 366, 367.
* **Temporary & Transitional provisions:** Articles 379, 380, 388, 391, 392, 393.
* **Self-execution:** Article 394 itself had to come into force to enable the other articles.

#### Stage 2: Main Commencement (January 26, 1950)
* All remaining provisions (including Fundamental Rights, DPSP, Judiciary, Union and State Executives, etc.) came into force on **January 26, 1950**.
* This date is legally referred to as the **"Commencement of this Constitution"** and is celebrated as **Republic Day**.

### 2. Historical Significance of January 26
* The Constituent Assembly completed its draft on November 26, 1949, but waited until January 26, 1950, to bring the bulk of it into effect.
* This was done to honor the **Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence)** declaration made by the Indian National Congress on **January 26, 1930** at the Lahore Session.
