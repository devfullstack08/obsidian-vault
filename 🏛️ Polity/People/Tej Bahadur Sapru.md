**Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru** (8 December 1875 – 20 January 1949).

## Why he matters for the Constitution

He is the liberal lawyer who appears at two ends of the Indian drafting timeline.

1. **1925–1928.** He worked on the Commonwealth of India Bill with [[Annie Besant]], and he was a member of [[Motilal Nehru]]’s committee. The Nehru Report’s attempt to settle minority rights without separate electorates is the kind of settlement Sapru spent his life arguing for.
2. **1945.** He chaired the non-party **Sapru Committee**. Its report divided rights into **justiciable** and **non-justiciable**. That division is the ancestor of Fundamental Rights (Part III) and Directive Principles (Part IV).

Full events: [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#Sapru Committee Report, 1945|Sapru Committee Report]] and the [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#Nehru Report, 1928|Nehru Report]].

## The person

- A Kashmiri Pandit lawyer of Allahabad, in the same city and profession as Motilal Nehru, but a **Liberal**, not a Congress mass leader.
- He served as Law Member of the Viceroy’s Council (1920–1923). He believed India could win self-government by negotiation and a written settlement, not only by mass struggle.
- He kept trying to hold Hindus and Muslims to one constitution: in the Nehru Committee, at the Round Table Conferences, and again in the 1945 committee, when Partition was already close.
- He died in January 1949, a year before the Constitution commenced. He was not a member of the Constituent Assembly.

## What to remember

| Point | Fact |

| 1928 | Member of Motilal’s committee, not its chairman |
