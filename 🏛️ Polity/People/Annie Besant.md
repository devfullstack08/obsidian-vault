**Annie Besant** (1 October 1847 – 20 September 1933).

## Why she matters for the Constitution

She is the person behind the **Commonwealth of India Bill, 1925**, the draft that sits between Tilak’s Swaraj Bill and Motilal Nehru’s Report.

She is also the witness who connected the **1895 Swaraj Bill** with Bal Gangadhar Tilak. That is why we say the 1895 author is unknown, but the document is linked with Tilak.

Full event: [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#Commonwealth of India Bill, 1925|Commonwealth of India Bill, 1925]].

## The person

- Born in London. She came to India through the **Theosophical Society** and made Adyar, Madras, her base.
- She was not Indian by birth. She became a Congress leader and, in **1917**, the first woman president of the Indian National Congress.
- In **1916** her **Home Rule League** and Tilak’s league asked for self-government. The two leagues were separate.
- In 1925, with Indian liberals including [[Tej Bahadur Sapru]], she put a dominion constitution into the form of a bill. George Lansbury introduced it in the House of Commons on **9 December 1925**. It did not pass.
- The bill added rights that the 1895 draft had not stressed in the same way: freedom of conscience, and equality of the sexes.

## What to remember

| Point | Fact |
| ----- | ---- |
| Her constitutional document | **Commonwealth of India Bill, 1925** |
| What it asked for | Dominion status, and a bill of rights |
| Trap | She introduced the *idea* into Parliament through Lansbury. She was not a member of the Constituent Assembly. She died in **1933**, before it existed. |
