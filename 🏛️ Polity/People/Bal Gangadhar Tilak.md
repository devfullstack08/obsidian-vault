**Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (23 July 1856 – 1 August 1920), called **Lokmanya**.

## Why he matters for the Constitution

The **Swaraj Bill of 1895** (Constitution of India Bill) is the first Indian attempt to draft a constitution. Annie Besant later said the document was influenced by Tilak, who made **Swaraj** the nationalist demand. The author’s name is not known for certain. In an exam, write “linked with Tilak”, not “drafted by Tilak”.

Full event: [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#Swaraj Bill, 1895|Swaraj Bill, 1895]].

## The person

- A scholar of mathematics and law, from Maharashtra. He taught, then left teaching for public life.
- With Agarkar and Chiplunkar he was part of the new assertive politics of western India. His papers were **Kesari** (Marathi) and **The Mahratta** (English).
- He used public festivals — Ganesh Utsav and Shivaji festival — to take politics beyond the English-educated few.
- His line, **“Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it,”** is the slogan of this phase. In the 1890s, Swaraj still meant self-government inside the Empire, which is exactly what the 1895 Bill asks for.
- The British jailed him for sedition (1908–1914, Mandalay).
- In **1916** he and [[Annie Besant]] launched the **Home Rule Leagues**, again asking for self-government, not yet complete separation.
- He died on **1 August 1920**, before the Nehru Report and long before the Constituent Assembly. Gandhi called him the Maker of Modern India.
