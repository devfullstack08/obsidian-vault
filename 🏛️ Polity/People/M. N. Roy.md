**M. N. Roy** (Manabendra Nath Roy; born Narendra Nath Bhattacharya, 21 March 1887 – 25 January 1954).

## Why he matters for the Constitution

In **1934** he was the first person to put forward, in clear terms, the idea that India must have a **Constituent Assembly**: a body of Indians to frame the Constitution.

Earlier documents (the Swaraj Bill of 1895, the Commonwealth Bill of 1925, the Nehru Report of 1928) were drafts of a constitution. They were not this demand. The Congress adopted Roy’s idea officially in **1935**.

Full event: [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#M. N. Roy|Demand for a Constituent Assembly]].

## The person

- A revolutionary from Bengal. In the First World War he looked for German arms against British rule, then moved through East Asia and the United States into Mexico.
- In Mexico he became a communist. In **1920**, at Tashkent, he helped found an émigré Communist Party of India. He argued with Lenin, at the second Comintern congress, about whether communists in colonies should support nationalist leaders. His view was more hostile to the national bourgeoisie than Lenin’s.
- He returned to India, was jailed, and by the 1930s had broken with the Communist International.
- After that break he called his position **Radical Humanism**, and organised the Radical Democratic Party. The 1934 proposal belongs to this later Roy: a democrat asking for a constitution made by the people, not a party manifesto.
- In **1944** he also published a draft constitution of his own (*Constitution of Free India*). That draft is not the 1934 proposal. Exams ask about **1934**.
