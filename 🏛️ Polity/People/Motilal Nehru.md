**Motilal Nehru** (6 May 1861 – 6 February 1931).

## Why he matters for the Constitution

He chaired the committee that wrote the **Nehru Report (August 1928)**.

That report is the first detailed Indian constitution accepted by a wide group of parties. It asked for **dominion status** (self-government) and a settlement of **minority rights** through joint electorates, not separate electorates.

The British did not grant it. The demand fell on deaf ears. In December 1929 Congress let the scheme lapse and declared Purna Swaraj.

Full event: [[1. Demand for a Constituent Assembly#Nehru Report, 1928|Nehru Report, 1928]].

## The person

- A leading lawyer of Allahabad, and one of the richest Indians of his generation. Father of **Jawaharlal Nehru**.
- He began as a moderate. After the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) and the start of Non-Cooperation, he gave up his practice and joined Gandhi.
- When Gandhi suspended Non-Cooperation in 1922, Motilal and **C. R. Das** formed the **Swaraj Party** (1923) to enter the councils and obstruct them from inside. That is a different “Swaraj” from the 1895 Bill.
- Congress president twice: **Amritsar, 1919**, and **Calcutta, 1928**. The Calcutta session accepted his Report and gave Britain one year to concede dominion status.
- On **23 December 1929** he was among the leaders who met the Viceroy and failed to get a promise that the Round Table Conference would frame a dominion constitution.
- He died on **6 February 1931**. He never sat in the Constituent Assembly.

## Motilal or Jawaharlal?

| | Motilal Nehru | Jawaharlal Nehru |
| - | ------------- | ---------------- |
| Document | **Nehru Report, 1928** | Objectives Resolution, **1946**; the 1938 declaration on a Constituent Assembly |
| Demand in 1928–29 | **Dominion status** | He wanted **complete independence**, and thought dominion status was not enough |
| Role in 1928 | **Chairman** of the committee | Secretary of the same committee |

If a question says “Nehru Report”, the answer is **Motilal**.

## What to remember

| Point | Fact |
| ----- | ---- |
| Asked for | Self-government as a dominion, plus minority safeguards without separate electorates |
| Why it failed | Britain refused; Jinnah’s Fourteen Points rejected the minority formula; younger leaders wanted Purna Swaraj |
| Died | 1931 — before Karachi’s economic resolution was followed by a Constituent Assembly |
