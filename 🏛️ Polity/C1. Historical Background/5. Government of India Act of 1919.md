The Government of India Act of 1919 is also known as the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**.

- Edwin Montagu was the Secretary of State for India.
- Lord Chelmsford was the Viceroy of India.

## Main Provisions

### Dyarchy in Provinces

Provincial subjects were divided into two categories:

| Reserved Subjects | Transferred Subjects |
| ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Police            | Education            |
| Finance           | Public health        |
| Land revenue      | Local government     |
| Irrigation        | Agriculture          |
| Justice           | Industries           |

- Reserved subjects were administered by the Governor and his Executive Council.
- Transferred subjects were administered by Indian ministers responsible to the legislative council.

### Bicameralism at the Centre

The Central Legislature was divided into two Houses:

1. Council of State
2. Legislative Assembly

### Other Provisions

- Direct elections were introduced.
- Separate electorates were extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans.
- Provincial budgets were separated from the Central budget.
- A Public Service Commission was proposed.
- The Central Public Service Commission was established in 1926.
- A statutory commission was to be appointed after ten years to review the working of the Act.
- The Simon Commission was appointed in 1927 for this purpose.

## Importance

- It introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
- It introduced bicameralism at the Centre.
- It expanded direct elections.
- It increased Indian participation in administration.

## Limitations

- The Governor had extensive powers.
- Dyarchy was complicated and unsuccessful.
- The Central government remained highly authoritarian.
- Indians did not receive real responsible government.
