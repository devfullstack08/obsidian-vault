## Prelims-Focused Facts

- The Regulating Act of 1773 created the office of Governor-General of Bengal.
- Pitt’s India Act of 1784 introduced dual control.
- The Charter Act of 1833 created the office of Governor-General of India.
- The Charter Act of 1853 introduced open competition for civil services.
- The Government of India Act of 1858 transferred power from the Company to the Crown.
- The Indian Councils Act of 1909 introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
- The Government of India Act of 1919 introduced dyarchy in provinces.
- The Government of India Act of 1935 introduced provincial autonomy.
- The Indian Independence Act of 1947 ended British rule in India.

## Common Exam Traps

### Trap 1

**Governor-General of Bengal** was created by the Regulating Act of 1773.

**Governor-General of India** was created by the Charter Act of 1833.

### Trap 2

Dyarchy in the **provinces** was introduced by the Government of India Act of 1919.

Dyarchy at the **Centre** was proposed by the Government of India Act of 1935.

### Trap 3

Separate electorate for Muslims was introduced by the Indian Councils Act of 1909.

It was later extended to other communities by the Government of India Act of 1919.

### Trap 4

Provincial autonomy was introduced by the Government of India Act of 1935.

It was not introduced by the Government of India Act of 1919.

## Mains Answer Framework

### Question

Discuss the constitutional development of India during British rule.

### Introduction

The constitutional development of India was a gradual process that began with the Regulating Act of 1773 and culminated in the Indian Independence Act of 1947.

### Main Body

#### Company Rule

- Parliamentary control began in 1773.
- Administrative centralisation developed through the Charter Acts.
- The Company’s commercial role ended in 1833.
- Open competition for civil services began in 1853.

#### Crown Rule

- Direct Crown rule began in 1858.
- Indian representation gradually increased.
- Separate electorates were introduced in 1909.
- Dyarchy was introduced in 1919.
- Provincial autonomy was introduced in 1935.
- Independence was granted in 1947.

### Conclusion

The constitutional reforms introduced by the British were limited and primarily designed to protect colonial interests. However, they created administrative and legislative institutions that influenced the framing of the Constitution of independent India.
