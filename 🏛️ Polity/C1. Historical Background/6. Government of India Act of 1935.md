The Government of India Act of 1935 was the most detailed constitutional law enacted by the British for India.

Many provisions of the present Indian Constitution were influenced by this Act.

## Main Provisions

### All-India Federation

- The Act proposed an All-India Federation.
- It was to include British Indian provinces and princely states.
- The federation never came into existence because the princely states did not join.

### Provincial Autonomy

- Dyarchy in the provinces was abolished.
- Provinces were granted autonomy.
- Provincial ministers became responsible to provincial legislatures.

### Dyarchy at the Centre

- Dyarchy was proposed at the Centre.
- Federal subjects were divided into reserved and transferred subjects.
- This provision was never implemented.

### Division of Powers

Powers were divided into three lists:

1. Federal List
2. Provincial List
3. Concurrent List

Residuary powers were given to the Governor-General.

### Other Provisions

- Bicameralism was introduced in six provinces:
  - Bengal
  - Bombay
  - Madras
  - Bihar
  - Assam
  - United Provinces
- Separate electorates were extended to more communities.
- The Council of India was abolished.
- The Reserve Bank of India was established in 1935.
- A Federal Court was established in 1937.
- The Federal Public Service Commission and Provincial Public Service Commissions were provided.
- Burma was separated from India.
- Aden was separated from India.

## Importance

- It introduced provincial autonomy.
- It provided a federal structure.
- It introduced three legislative lists.
- It became an important source of the Indian Constitution.

## Provisions Borrowed by the Indian Constitution

- Federal structure
- Office of Governor
- Public Service Commissions
- Emergency provisions
- Administrative details
- Three-fold distribution of legislative powers
