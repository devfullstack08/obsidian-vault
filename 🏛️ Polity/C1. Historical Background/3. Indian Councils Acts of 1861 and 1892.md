## Indian Councils Act of 1861

### Main Provisions

- Indians were nominated to the legislative council for the first time.
- The Viceroy was authorised to nominate Indians as non-official members.
- Lord Canning nominated the Raja of Banaras, the Maharaja of Patiala and Sir Dinkar Rao.
- Legislative powers were restored to Bombay and Madras.
- The portfolio system was officially recognised.
- The Viceroy was empowered to issue ordinances during emergencies.
- An ordinance remained valid for six months.
- Legislative councils were established in Bengal, the North-Western Provinces and Punjab.

### Importance

- It introduced limited Indian representation.
- It began the process of decentralisation.
- It gave legal recognition to the portfolio system.
- It marked the beginning of representative institutions in India.

## Indian Councils Act of 1892

### Main Provisions

- The number of additional members in the Central and Provincial Legislative Councils was increased.
- Members were allowed to discuss the annual budget.
- Members could ask questions on public matters.
- An indirect and limited system of election was introduced.
- The word “election” was not officially used in the Act.

### Importance

- It introduced the principle of indirect election.
- It increased the participation of Indians in legislative councils.
- It gave members limited financial and questioning powers.
