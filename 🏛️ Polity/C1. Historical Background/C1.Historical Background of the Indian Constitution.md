## Introduction

The Constitution of India did not emerge suddenly in 1950. It developed gradually through several laws, regulations and constitutional reforms introduced during British rule.

To understand the Indian Constitution properly, it is necessary to study the major Acts passed by the British Parliament from **1773 to 1947**.

---

## 1. Regulating Act of 1773

The Regulating Act of 1773 was the first major step taken by the British government to control the activities of the East India Company in India.

### Main Provisions

- The Governor of Bengal was designated as the **Governor-General of Bengal**.
    
- Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal.
    
- An Executive Council of four members was created to assist the Governor-General.
    
- The governors of Bombay and Madras were made subordinate to the Governor-General of Bengal in matters of war and peace.
    
- A Supreme Court was established at Calcutta in 1774.
    
- The servants of the East India Company were prohibited from accepting gifts and bribes.
    
- The Court of Directors was required to report the Company’s revenue, civil and military affairs to the British government.
    

### Importance

- It was the first step towards the centralisation of administration in India.
    
- It was the first attempt by the British Parliament to control the East India Company.
    
- It laid the foundation of a central administration in India.
    

---

## 2. Amending Act of 1781

The Amending Act of 1781 is also known as the **Act of Settlement**.

It was passed to remove the defects of the Regulating Act of 1773.

### Main Provisions

- The Governor-General and his Council were exempted from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court for official acts.
    
- Revenue matters were excluded from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
    
- The Supreme Court was required to apply the personal laws of Hindus and Muslims.
    
- Appeals from provincial courts could be taken to the Governor-General-in-Council instead of the Supreme Court.
    

### Importance

- It clarified the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
    
- It reduced conflict between the Supreme Court and the Governor-General’s Council.
    

---

## 3. Pitt’s India Act of 1784

Pitt’s India Act introduced a system of **dual control** over the administration of British India.

### Main Provisions

- The commercial functions of the Company remained with the Court of Directors.
    
- Political and administrative control was given to a new body called the **Board of Control**.
    
- The British government gained greater control over the Company’s administration.
    
- The Company’s territories in India were officially called the **British possessions in India**.
    
- The Governor-General’s Council was reduced from four members to three.
    

### Dual System of Control

|Authority|Function|
|---|---|
|Court of Directors|Commercial affairs|
|Board of Control|Political and administrative affairs|

### Importance

- It distinguished between the commercial and political functions of the Company.
    
- It established the direct control of the British government over Indian administration.
    

---

## 4. Act of 1786

The Act of 1786 was passed mainly to satisfy the demands of Lord Cornwallis.

### Main Provisions

- The Governor-General was given the power to override the decisions of his Council in special circumstances.
    
- The Governor-General could also act as the Commander-in-Chief of the British forces in India.
    

---

## 5. Charter Act of 1793

### Main Provisions

- The commercial monopoly of the East India Company was extended for another twenty years.
    
- The Governor-General was given greater authority over the governors of Bombay and Madras.
    
- The salaries of the members of the Board of Control were to be paid from Indian revenues.
    
- The Governor-General was empowered to override his Council in certain situations.
    

---

## 6. Charter Act of 1813

### Main Provisions

- The East India Company’s trade monopoly in India was abolished.
    
- However, the Company retained its monopoly over trade in tea and trade with China.
    
- Christian missionaries were permitted to come to India.
    
- The British government accepted responsibility for the education of Indians.
    
- An annual amount of ₹1 lakh was provided for the promotion of education.
    
- Local governments were authorised to impose taxes.
    

### Importance

- It ended the Company’s general trade monopoly.
    
- It marked the beginning of state involvement in education.
    
- It allowed Christian missionaries to operate in India.
    

---

## 7. Charter Act of 1833

The Charter Act of 1833 was an important step towards administrative centralisation.

### Main Provisions

- The Governor-General of Bengal became the **Governor-General of India**.
    
- Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India.
    
- The legislative powers of the governors of Bombay and Madras were taken away.
    
- The Governor-General of India received exclusive legislative powers for British India.
    
- The East India Company’s commercial activities were completely abolished.
    
- The Company became a purely administrative body.
    
- A Law Commission was established.
    
- Lord Macaulay became the first Chairman of the First Law Commission.
    
- The Act declared that no Indian should be denied government employment on the basis of religion, place of birth, descent or colour.
    

### Importance

- It completed the centralisation of legislative authority.
    
- It ended the East India Company’s commercial role.
    
- It attempted to introduce equality in public employment.
    
- It led to the codification of Indian laws.
    

---

## 8. Charter Act of 1853

The Charter Act of 1853 was the last Charter Act passed by the British Parliament.

### Main Provisions

- The legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General’s Council were separated.
    
- Six additional members were added to the Council for legislative purposes.
    
- It introduced a separate legislative council known as the **Indian Legislative Council**.
    
- Open competition was introduced for recruitment to the civil services.
    
- Indians were allowed to compete for civil service positions.
    
- Local representation was introduced in the legislative council.
    
- The rule of the East India Company was extended without fixing a specific time period.
    

### Importance

- It introduced open competition for civil services.
    
- It separated legislative and executive functions.
    
- It introduced limited local representation in the legislature.
    

---

# Crown Rule: 1858–1947

After the Revolt of 1857, the British government ended the rule of the East India Company and took direct control over India.

---

## 9. Government of India Act of 1858

### Background

The Revolt of 1857 exposed the weaknesses of Company rule. As a result, the British Parliament passed the Government of India Act of 1858.

### Main Provisions

- The rule of the East India Company was abolished.
    
- The administration of India was transferred to the British Crown.
    
- The Governor-General of India was also designated as the **Viceroy of India**.
    
- Lord Canning became the first Viceroy of India.
    
- The Board of Control and Court of Directors were abolished.
    
- The office of the **Secretary of State for India** was created.
    
- The Secretary of State was assisted by a Council of India consisting of fifteen members.
    
- The Secretary of State was a member of the British Cabinet and was responsible to the British Parliament.
    

### Importance

- It ended Company rule in India.
    
- It established direct rule by the British Crown.
    
- It created a highly centralised administrative system.
    

---

## 10. Indian Councils Act of 1861

### Main Provisions

- Indians were nominated to the legislative council for the first time.
    
- The Viceroy was authorised to nominate Indians as non-official members.
    
- Lord Canning nominated the Raja of Banaras, the Maharaja of Patiala and Sir Dinkar Rao.
    
- Legislative powers were restored to Bombay and Madras.
    
- The portfolio system was officially recognised.
    
- The Viceroy was empowered to issue ordinances during emergencies.
    
- An ordinance remained valid for six months.
    
- Legislative councils were established in Bengal, the North-Western Provinces and Punjab.
    

### Importance

- It introduced limited Indian representation.
    
- It began the process of decentralisation.
    
- It gave legal recognition to the portfolio system.
    
- It marked the beginning of representative institutions in India.
    

---

## 11. Indian Councils Act of 1892

### Main Provisions

- The number of additional members in the Central and Provincial Legislative Councils was increased.
    
- Members were allowed to discuss the annual budget.
    
- Members could ask questions on public matters.
    
- An indirect and limited system of election was introduced.
    
- The word “election” was not officially used in the Act.
    

### Importance

- It introduced the principle of indirect election.
    
- It increased the participation of Indians in legislative councils.
    
- It gave members limited financial and questioning powers.
    

---

## 12. Indian Councils Act of 1909

The Indian Councils Act of 1909 is also known as the **Morley-Minto Reforms**.

- Lord Morley was the Secretary of State for India.
    
- Lord Minto was the Viceroy of India.
    

### Main Provisions

- The size of the Central and Provincial Legislative Councils was increased.
    
- The number of non-official members was increased.
    
- Official members continued to have a majority in the Central Legislative Council.
    
- Non-official members obtained a majority in Provincial Legislative Councils.
    
- Members were allowed to ask supplementary questions.
    
- Members could move resolutions on the budget and matters of public interest.
    
- Separate electorates were introduced for Muslims.
    
- Muslim voters could elect only Muslim representatives.
    
- Satyendra Prasad Sinha became the first Indian member of the Viceroy’s Executive Council.
    

### Importance

- It introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
    
- It legalised communal representation.
    
- It increased Indian participation in legislative bodies.
    

### Criticism

Separate electorates encouraged communal politics and divided people on religious grounds.

---

## 13. Government of India Act of 1919

The Government of India Act of 1919 is also known as the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**.

- Edwin Montagu was the Secretary of State for India.
    
- Lord Chelmsford was the Viceroy of India.
    

### Main Provisions

#### Dyarchy in Provinces

Provincial subjects were divided into two categories:

|Reserved Subjects|Transferred Subjects|
|---|---|
|Police|Education|
|Finance|Public health|
|Land revenue|Local government|
|Irrigation|Agriculture|
|Justice|Industries|

- Reserved subjects were administered by the Governor and his Executive Council.
    
- Transferred subjects were administered by Indian ministers responsible to the legislative council.
    

#### Bicameralism at the Centre

The Central Legislature was divided into two Houses:

1. Council of State
    
2. Legislative Assembly
    

#### Other Provisions

- Direct elections were introduced.
    
- Separate electorates were extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans.
    
- Provincial budgets were separated from the Central budget.
    
- A Public Service Commission was proposed.
    
- The Central Public Service Commission was established in 1926.
    
- A statutory commission was to be appointed after ten years to review the working of the Act.
    
- The Simon Commission was appointed in 1927 for this purpose.
    

### Importance

- It introduced dyarchy in the provinces.
    
- It introduced bicameralism at the Centre.
    
- It expanded direct elections.
    
- It increased Indian participation in administration.
    

### Limitations

- The Governor had extensive powers.
    
- Dyarchy was complicated and unsuccessful.
    
- The Central government remained highly authoritarian.
    
- Indians did not receive real responsible government.
    

---

## 14. Government of India Act of 1935

The Government of India Act of 1935 was the most detailed constitutional law enacted by the British for India.

Many provisions of the present Indian Constitution were influenced by this Act.

### Main Provisions

#### All-India Federation

- The Act proposed an All-India Federation.
    
- It was to include British Indian provinces and princely states.
    
- The federation never came into existence because the princely states did not join.
    

#### Provincial Autonomy

- Dyarchy in the provinces was abolished.
    
- Provinces were granted autonomy.
    
- Provincial ministers became responsible to provincial legislatures.
    

#### Dyarchy at the Centre

- Dyarchy was proposed at the Centre.
    
- Federal subjects were divided into reserved and transferred subjects.
    
- This provision was never implemented.
    

#### Division of Powers

Powers were divided into three lists:

1. Federal List
    
2. Provincial List
    
3. Concurrent List
    

Residuary powers were given to the Governor-General.

#### Other Provisions

- Bicameralism was introduced in six provinces:
    
    - Bengal
        
    - Bombay
        
    - Madras
        
    - Bihar
        
    - Assam
        
    - United Provinces
        
- Separate electorates were extended to more communities.
    
- The Council of India was abolished.
    
- The Reserve Bank of India was established in 1935.
    
- A Federal Court was established in 1937.
    
- The Federal Public Service Commission and Provincial Public Service Commissions were provided.
    
- Burma was separated from India.
    
- Aden was separated from India.
    

### Importance

- It introduced provincial autonomy.
    
- It provided a federal structure.
    
- It introduced three legislative lists.
    
- It became an important source of the Indian Constitution.
    

### Provisions Borrowed by the Indian Constitution

- Federal structure
    
- Office of Governor
    
- Public Service Commissions
    
- Emergency provisions
    
- Administrative details
    
- Three-fold distribution of legislative powers
    

---

## 15. Indian Independence Act of 1947

The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament on **18 July 1947**.

It came into effect on **15 August 1947**.

### Main Provisions

- British India was divided into two independent dominions:
    
    1. India
        
    2. Pakistan
        
- British rule in India ended.
    
- The office of the Viceroy was abolished.
    
- Each dominion was to have a Governor-General.
    
- The Governor-General was appointed by the British monarch on the advice of the Dominion Cabinet.
    
- The office of the Secretary of State for India was abolished.
    
- The Constituent Assemblies of India and Pakistan became sovereign legislative bodies.
    
- British control over the princely states ended.
    
- Princely states were free to join India or Pakistan.
    
- The British monarch could no longer veto Indian laws.
    
- The title “Emperor of India” was removed from the British Crown.
    
- Civil servants appointed before independence continued to receive their existing benefits.
    

### Importance

- It ended British rule in India.
    
- It made India an independent and sovereign country.
    
- It gave full legislative authority to the Constituent Assembly.
    
- It marked the beginning of constitutional government by Indians.

# Common Exam Traps

## Trap 1

**Governor-General of Bengal** was created by the Regulating Act of 1773.

**Governor-General of India** was created by the Charter Act of 1833.

## Trap 2

Dyarchy in the **provinces** was introduced by the Government of India Act of 1919.

Dyarchy at the **Centre** was proposed by the Government of India Act of 1935.

## Trap 3

Separate electorate for Muslims was introduced by the Indian Councils Act of 1909.

It was later extended to other communities by the Government of India Act of 1919.

## Trap 4

Provincial autonomy was introduced by the Government of India Act of 1935.

It was not introduced by the Government of India Act of 1919.

# Mains Answer Framework

## Question

Discuss the constitutional development of India during British rule.

## Introduction

The constitutional development of India was a gradual process that began with the Regulating Act of 1773 and culminated in the Indian Independence Act of 1947.

## Main Body

### Company Rule

- Parliamentary control began in 1773.
    
- Administrative centralisation developed through the Charter Acts.
    
- The Company’s commercial role ended in 1833.
    
- Open competition for civil services began in 1853.
    

### Crown Rule

- Direct Crown rule began in 1858.
    
- Indian representation gradually increased.
    
- Separate electorates were introduced in 1909.
    
- Dyarchy was introduced in 1919.
    
- Provincial autonomy was introduced in 1935.
    
- Independence was granted in 1947.
    

## Conclusion

The constitutional reforms introduced by the British were limited and primarily designed to protect colonial interests. However, they created administrative and legislative institutions that influenced the framing of the Constitution of independent India.
