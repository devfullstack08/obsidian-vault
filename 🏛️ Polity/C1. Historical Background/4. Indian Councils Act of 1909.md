The Indian Councils Act of 1909 is also known as the **Morley-Minto Reforms**.

- Lord Morley was the Secretary of State for India.
- Lord Minto was the Viceroy of India.

## Main Provisions

- The size of the Central and Provincial Legislative Councils was increased.
- The number of non-official members was increased.
- Official members continued to have a majority in the Central Legislative Council.
- Non-official members obtained a majority in Provincial Legislative Councils.
- Members were allowed to ask supplementary questions.
- Members could move resolutions on the budget and matters of public interest.
- Separate electorates were introduced for Muslims.
- Muslim voters could elect only Muslim representatives.
- Satyendra Prasad Sinha became the first Indian member of the Viceroy’s Executive Council.

## Importance

- It introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
- It legalised communal representation.
- It increased Indian participation in legislative bodies.

## Criticism

Separate electorates encouraged communal politics and divided people on religious grounds.
