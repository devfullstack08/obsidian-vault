Critics raised several objections against the Constituent Assembly. Each criticism has a standard reply used in exam answers.

## 1. Not a popular / directly elected body

**Criticism:** Members were not elected by universal adult franchise.

**Reply:** Adult franchise was not then practicable. The Assembly was still broadly representative of British India and the princely states. Granville Austin called it:

> “India in microcosm.”

## 2. Not sovereign at the beginning

**Criticism:** It was created by the Cabinet Mission Plan, so it was not a fully sovereign body at first.

**Reply:** After the Indian Independence Act, 1947, from **15 August 1947** it became a sovereign body. It could:

- abrogate or alter any British law;
- make any constitution it chose;
- not be dissolved by the British Parliament.

## 3. Congress-dominated

**Criticism:** Congress had an overwhelming majority (about 208 of 296 provincial seats).

**Reply:** Congress had won the 1945–46 elections. The Assembly still included the Muslim League (until Partition), independents, princely-state nominees and other groups. Debates were open and lengthy.

## 4. Hindu-dominated

**Criticism:** After Partition, the Assembly had a large Hindu majority.

**Reply:** Membership followed the communal and territorial scheme of the time. Minority members, including Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Parsis and Scheduled Castes/Tribes, took part. The Constitution itself provided minority and weaker-section safeguards.

## 5. Lawyer-dominated

**Criticism:** Too many lawyers shaped a legalistic document.

**Reply:** A written constitution needs legal skill. Non-lawyers such as Nehru, Patel, Rajendra Prasad and others also played major political roles.

## 6. Time-consuming

**Criticism:** Nearly three years was too long.

**Reply:** The country was partitioned, princely states had to be integrated, and every Article was debated. The result was a durable constitution.

> [!important]
> For Mains: do not only list criticisms. Always give the reply. The Assembly was **representative though not directly elected**, and **sovereign after 15 August 1947**, not from the first meeting on 9 December 1946.
