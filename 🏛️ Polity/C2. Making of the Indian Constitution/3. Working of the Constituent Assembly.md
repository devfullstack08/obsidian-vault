## First meeting

The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on:

> **9 December 1946**

The Muslim League boycotted the meeting.

Only about **211** members attended the first session.

## Temporary President

**Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** was appointed temporary President of the Assembly.

He was the oldest member.

## Permanent President

On **11 December 1946**, **Dr Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President of the Constituent Assembly.

## Vice-President

**H. C. Mookherjee** was elected Vice-President.

Later, **V. T. Krishnamachari** was also elected Vice-President.

## Constitutional Adviser

**B. N. Rau** (Sir Benegal Narsing Rau) was appointed Constitutional Adviser.

His work with the draft is in [[4. Committees of the Constituent Assembly]].

## Objectives Resolution

Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the **Objectives Resolution** on:

> **13 December 1946**

It was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:

> **22 January 1947**

### Main principles

The Objectives Resolution declared that:

- India would become an independent sovereign republic;
- territories of British India, princely states and other willing territories would form a Union;
- constituent units would possess autonomy;
- all power and authority would be derived from the people;
- all citizens would receive justice, equality and freedom;
- minorities and backward groups would receive safeguards;
- the territorial integrity of India would be maintained;
- India would promote world peace and human welfare.

### Significance

The Objectives Resolution:

- provided the philosophy of the Constitution;
- guided the Constituent Assembly;
- became the basis of the Preamble;
- expressed popular sovereignty;
- committed India to justice, liberty, equality and safeguards for minorities.

> [!exam-note]
> The **Preamble** is based largely on the **Objectives Resolution** moved by Jawaharlal Nehru.

## Changes by the Indian Independence Act, 1947

The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament in July 1947.

It created two independent dominions:

- India;
- Pakistan.

From **15 August 1947**, the Indian Constituent Assembly became a **sovereign** body.

It was no longer bound by the limitations of the Cabinet Mission Plan.

### Two functions of the Assembly

After independence, the Constituent Assembly performed two roles:

1. Constitution-making body
2. Legislative body for the Dominion of India

When it met as a Constituent Assembly, it was presided over by:

> Dr Rajendra Prasad

When it met as the Dominion Legislature, it was presided over by:

> G. V. Mavalankar

These two functions were performed on separate days.

### Changes caused by Partition

The Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 accepted the partition of India.

As a result:

- representatives of areas included in Pakistan left the Indian Constituent Assembly;
- the total membership was reduced to **299**;
- separate Constituent Assemblies were created for India and Pakistan;
- representatives of princely states gradually joined the Indian Assembly.

The Muslim League members belonging to territories that remained in India could participate in the Indian Constituent Assembly.
