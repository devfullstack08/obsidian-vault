The Constitution of India was framed by the **Constituent Assembly of India**.

It was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946**.

## M. N. Roy

The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward in **1934** by **M. N. Roy**.

He was:

- a revolutionary;
- a political thinker;
- a pioneer of the communist movement in India;
- later associated with Radical Humanism.

## Indian National Congress

In **1935**, the Indian National Congress officially demanded that the Constitution of India should be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected by Indians.

## Jawaharlal Nehru’s declaration

In **1938**, Jawaharlal Nehru declared on behalf of the Congress:

> The Constitution of free India must be framed, without outside interference, by a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise.

## British acceptance

The British government gradually accepted the idea.

### August Offer, 1940

For the first time, the British government recognised in principle that Indians should frame their own Constitution.

### Cripps Proposals, 1942

The Cripps Mission proposed the establishment of a constitution-making body after the Second World War.

The proposal was rejected because:

- India was not immediately granted independence;
- provinces were given the option to remain outside the proposed Union;
- princely states were not to be represented democratically;
- the British retained control over defence during the war.

### Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

The Cabinet Mission Plan finally provided for the formation of the Constituent Assembly.

The Cabinet Mission consisted of three British Cabinet ministers:

1. **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** — Secretary of State for India
2. **Sir Stafford Cripps** — President of the Board of Trade
3. **A. V. Alexander** — First Lord of the Admiralty

It arrived in India in March 1946.

It aimed to:

- discuss the transfer of power;
- establish a constitutional framework;
- create a Constituent Assembly;
- preserve Indian unity;
- resolve differences between the Congress and Muslim League.

It proposed:

- a Union of India;
- a weak central government;
- provincial autonomy;
- grouping of provinces;
- a Constituent Assembly;
- an interim government.
