The Constituent Assembly originally had **389 members**.

## Distribution of seats

| Category | Seats |
| -------- | ----- |
| British Indian provinces | 292 |
| Chief Commissioners’ provinces | 4 |
| Princely states | 93 |
| **Total** | **389** |

The four Chief Commissioners’ provinces were:

- Delhi;
- Ajmer-Merwara;
- Coorg;
- British Baluchistan.

## After Partition

Following the partition of India in 1947, the total strength of the Constituent Assembly was reduced from **389 to 299**.

| Category after Partition | Seats |
| ------------------------ | ----- |
| Provinces | 229 |
| Princely states | 70 |
| **Total** | **299** |

## Method of election

The Constituent Assembly was **partly elected and partly nominated**.

### Representatives of provinces

Representatives of British Indian provinces were elected **indirectly** by members of the provincial legislative assemblies.

The election used:

- proportional representation;
- single transferable vote.

### Community representation

Seats in each province were divided among:

- Muslims;
- Sikhs;
- General category.

Members of each community in the provincial legislature elected their own representatives.

### Princely states

Representatives of princely states were nominated by the rulers of those states.

### Not based on universal adult franchise

The Constituent Assembly was not directly elected by all adult citizens.

The provincial assemblies that elected its members had themselves been elected under a limited franchise.

Only a small proportion of the population had voting rights during the 1946 provincial elections.

> [!important]
> The Constituent Assembly was not directly elected through universal adult franchise.

## Allocation of seats

Seats were broadly allotted according to population.

The general principle was:

> One seat for approximately every one million people.

The seats assigned to each British Indian province were divided among communities according to their population.

## Results of the Constituent Assembly elections

Elections to the Constituent Assembly were held in July–August 1946.

For the **296** seats assigned to British India:

| Political group | Seats |
| --------------- | ----- |
| Indian National Congress | 208 |
| Muslim League | 73 |
| Others and independents | 15 |
| **Total** | **296** |

The Congress obtained a clear majority.

The Muslim League initially participated in the elections but later boycotted the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly.

## Nature of the Assembly

The Constituent Assembly was:

- indirectly elected;
- partly nominated;
- representative of provinces and princely states;
- dominated by the Congress;
- not elected through universal adult franchise;
- initially created under a British plan;
- later transformed into a sovereign body (from 15 August 1947).

Although it was not directly elected, the Assembly included representatives from:

- Hindus;
- Muslims;
- Sikhs;
- Christians;
- Parsis;
- Anglo-Indians;
- Scheduled Castes;
- Scheduled Tribes;
- women;
- princely states;
- different regions;
- different political opinions.

Prominent members included:

- Jawaharlal Nehru;
- Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel;
- Dr B. R. Ambedkar;
- Dr Rajendra Prasad;
- Maulana Abul Kalam Azad;
- K. M. Munshi;
- Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar;
- Gopalaswami Ayyangar;
- H. C. Mookherjee;
- Frank Anthony;
- Jaipal Singh Munda;
- Dakshayani Velayudhan;
- Hansa Mehta;
- Durgabai Deshmukh;
- Sarojini Naidu.
