The Constituent Assembly appointed several committees to examine different constitutional issues.

There were:

- major committees;
- minor committees;
- procedural committees;
- subject-specific committees.

## Purpose of the main committees

**Union Powers Committee** (Nehru): distribution of powers between the Union and provinces; subjects of the Centre; nature of Union authority.

**Union Constitution Committee** (Nehru): structure of the Union government; executive and legislative arrangements.

**Provincial Constitution Committee** (Patel): structure of provincial governments; legislatures; executive; governors and ministers.

**Advisory Committee** (Patel): Fundamental Rights; minority safeguards; tribal areas; excluded and partially excluded areas.

**States Committee** (Nehru): negotiations with princely states.

**Rules of Procedure / Steering / Finance and Staff** (Rajendra Prasad): debates, order of work, money and staff.

**Credentials Committee** (Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar): verification of members’ credentials.

**House Committee** (B. Pattabhi Sitaramayya): accommodation and facilities.

**Order of Business Committee** (K. M. Munshi): sequence of business.

**Committee on the Functions of the Constituent Assembly** (G. V. Mavalankar): constitution-making vs legislative functions.

**Ad Hoc Committee on the National Flag** (Rajendra Prasad): National Flag; adopted on **22 July 1947**.

**Special Committee to Examine the Draft Constitution** (Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar): examined parts of the draft.

## Drafting Committee

The Drafting Committee was formed on:

> **29 August 1947**

It originally had **seven** members:

1. **Dr B. R. Ambedkar** — Chairman
2. **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar**
3. **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**
4. **K. M. Munshi**
5. **Syed Mohammad Saadulla**
6. **B. L. Mitter**
7. **D. P. Khaitan**

### Changes in membership

B. L. Mitter resigned due to ill health and was replaced by **N. Madhava Rau**.

D. P. Khaitan died and was replaced by **T. T. Krishnamachari**.

### Role of Dr B. R. Ambedkar

Ambedkar:

- chaired the Drafting Committee;
- defended the Draft Constitution in the Assembly;
- explained constitutional provisions;
- answered objections and proposed amendments;
- played a central role in legal drafting;
- promoted constitutional democracy and social justice.

He is popularly called:

> **Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution**

The Constitution was still a **collective** product involving the Assembly, its committees, B. N. Rau, Drafting Committee members and the debates.

> [!important]
> Dr Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, not the sole author of the Constitution.

---

## Role of B. N. Rau

B. N. Rau was Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly.

His full name was **Sir Benegal Narsing Rau**.

He:

- studied constitutions of about **60 countries**;
- prepared reports on constitutional questions;
- advised committees;
- prepared the **preliminary draft** of the Constitution;
- travelled to the United States, Canada, Ireland and the United Kingdom;
- consulted judges and constitutional experts.

He met Justice Felix Frankfurter and Justice Learned Hand in the United States.

After discussion of the due process clause with Justice Frankfurter, the phrase:

> “procedure established by law”

was preferred over the American expression:

> “due process of law”

in Article 21 as originally framed.

His preliminary draft contained about:

- **243 Articles**;
- **13 Schedules**.

> [!exam-note]
> B. N. Rau = Constitutional Adviser. Ambedkar = Chairman of the Drafting Committee.

---

## Preparation of the Draft Constitution

1. Committees submitted reports.
2. B. N. Rau prepared the preliminary draft (about 243 Articles, 13 Schedules).
3. Drafting Committee published the first Draft Constitution in **February 1948**. The public, provincial governments, central ministries and institutions were invited to submit suggestions.
4. Revised draft published in **October 1948**.
5. Dr B. R. Ambedkar introduced the Draft Constitution on **4 November 1948**.

### Three readings

| Reading | Period | What happened |
| ------- | ------ | ------------- |
| First | 4–9 November 1948 | general principles; no clause-by-clause voting |
| Second | 15 November 1948 – 17 October 1949 | clause-by-clause debate; most detailed stage |
| Third | 14–26 November 1949 | Constitution discussed as a whole; then adopted |

About **7,635** amendments were proposed; about **2,473** were moved and discussed.

The Assembly did not merely approve a ready-made document. The draft was debated, revised and voted upon.
