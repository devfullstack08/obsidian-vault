**Parallelism** means that when two or more words, phrases, or ideas are connected, they should have the **same grammatical form**.

This makes a sentence balanced and easy to read.

For example:

- **reading, dancing, singing** ✅
- **to read, to dance, to sing** ✅
- **read, dance, sing** ✅

But mixing the forms is incorrect:

- **reading, dancing, to sing** ❌

Your example is perfect:

Incorrect:

> She likes reading, dancing, and **to sing**.

Correct:

> She likes reading, dancing, and **singing**.

### Rules to remember

1. **Items in a list should have the same form.**

Incorrect:

> He likes **swimming, to run, and cycling**.

Correct:

> He likes **swimming, running, and cycling**.

2. With **and / or / but**, keep the structure parallel.

Incorrect:

> She wants **to study and getting a job**.

Correct:

> She wants **to study and to get a job**.

Or:

> She wants **studying and getting a job**.

3. With **either...or / neither...nor**, both sides should match.

Incorrect:

> You can either **study at home** or **going to the library**.

Correct:

> You can either **study at home** or **go to the library**.

4. With **not only...but also**, keep both parts parallel.

Incorrect:

> He is not only **intelligent** but also **works hard**.

Correct:

> He is not only **intelligent** but also **hardworking**.

Or:

> He not only **studies hard** but also **works hard**.

5. Comparisons should also be parallel.

Incorrect:

> I like **swimming** more than **to run**.

Correct:

> I like **swimming** more than **running**.

A very easy trick is:

**Check the words before and after “and,” “or,” “but,” “either...or,” and “not only...but also.” If one side is an -ing form, noun, adjective, or infinitive, the other side should usually use the same form.**

For example:

- **smart and hardworking** ✅ adjective + adjective
- **reading and writing** ✅ -ing + -ing
- **to eat and to sleep** ✅ infinitive + infinitive
- **a teacher and a writer** ✅ noun + noun
