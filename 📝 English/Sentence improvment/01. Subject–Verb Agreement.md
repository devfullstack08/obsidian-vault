A singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.
> He **works** hard.  
> They **work** hard.

Each, every, either, neither = singular
Everyone, someone, nobody, anybody = singular
“One of the” + plural noun + singular verb
“A number of” = plural
“The number of” = singular

With a relative pronoun, the verb in that clause follows the **antecedent**. See [[Pronoun]].

> He is one of the students who **have** passed.  
> He is the only one of the students who **has** passed.

If the subject is generic **one**, the verb is singular, and later words stay with **one / one's / oneself**, not **he / his / himself**. See [[Pronoun]].

> One **has** to do **one's** duty.

Phrases like **along with, together with, as well as, in addition to, accompanied by, rather than, and not, no less than, like, unlike, besides, including** are prepositional. They do **not** work like **and**. The verb agrees with the **first subject**. See [[Prepositions]].

> The teacher along with the students **is** present.  
> The captain as well as the players **is** ready.  
> The manager and not the workers **is** responsible.

Either...or / neither...nor: the verb usually agrees with the nearest subject.
