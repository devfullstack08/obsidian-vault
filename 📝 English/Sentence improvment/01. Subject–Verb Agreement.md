# Subject–Verb Agreement

Subject–Verb Agreement means that the **verb must agree with the subject in number and person**.

- Singular subject → singular verb
    
- Plural subject → plural verb
    

> He **works** hard.  
> They **work** hard.

In the simple present tense:

- He / She / It → **works, goes, does, has**
    
- I / We / You / They → **work, go, do, have**
    

---

# 1. Each / Every / Either / Neither + singular noun + singular verb

When **each, every, either, neither** are followed directly by a noun, the noun is singular and the verb is singular.

> Each **student is** ready.  
> Every **boy has** a book.  
> Either **road leads** to the station.  
> Neither **answer is** correct.

### Rule

**Each / Every / Either / Neither + singular noun + singular verb**

---

# 2. Each of / Either of / Neither of + plural noun + singular verb

After **of**, we normally use a plural noun/pronoun, but the verb remains singular.

> Each of the **students is** ready.  
> Either of the **answers is** correct.  
> Neither of the **boys has** arrived.

### Structure

**Each / Either / Neither + of + plural noun/pronoun + singular verb**

---

# 3. Everyone / Everybody / Someone / Somebody / Anyone / Anybody / No one / Nobody / Everything / Something / Nothing = singular

These indefinite pronouns take singular verbs.

> Everyone **is** ready.  
> Somebody **has** called you.  
> Nobody **knows** the answer.  
> Everything **was** fine.

### SSC Trap

> Everybody **are** happy. ❌  
> Everybody **is** happy. ✅

---

# 4. One of the + plural noun + singular verb

The real subject is **one**, so the verb is singular.

> One of the boys **is** absent.  
> One of my friends **has** joined the army.  
> One of the books **was** missing.

### Structure

**One of the + plural noun + singular verb**

---

# 5. A number of + plural noun + plural verb

**A number of** means **many**.

> A number of students **are** absent.  
> A number of people **have** complained.

### Memory Trick

**A number of = many → plural verb**

---

# 6. The number of + plural noun + singular verb

Here the real subject is **number**.

> The number of students **is** increasing.  
> The number of applicants **has** doubled.

### Compare

> A number of students **are** absent.  
> The number of students **is** increasing.

---

# 7. Along with / together with / as well as / in addition to / accompanied by etc.

Words and phrases such as:

- along with
    
- together with
    
- as well as
    
- in addition to
    
- accompanied by
    
- besides
    
- including
    
- rather than
    
- and not
    
- no less than
    
- like
    
- unlike
    

do **not** make the subject plural.

The verb agrees with the **main/first subject**.

> The teacher, along with the students, **is** present.  
> The captain as well as the players **is** ready.  
> The manager and not the workers **is** responsible.

### Shortcut

**A + along with/as well as + B → verb agrees with A**

---

# 8. Either...or / Neither...nor / Not only...but also

The verb usually agrees with the **nearest subject**.

> Either Ram or his friends **are** coming.  
> Either his friends or Ram **is** coming.

> Neither the teacher nor the students **were** ready.  
> Neither the students nor the teacher **was** ready.

> Not only the teacher but also the students **are** excited.

### Shortcut

**Either A or B → verb agrees with B**

**Neither A nor B → verb agrees with B**

**Not only A but also B → verb agrees with B**

---

# 9. Two subjects joined by AND usually take a plural verb

> Ram and Shyam **are** friends.  
> Tea and coffee **are** available.

### Rule

**A and B → plural verb**

But there are important exceptions.

---

# 10. Two nouns joined by AND referring to one person or one thing take a singular verb

> The poet and philosopher **is** dead.

This means one person who was both a poet and philosopher.

Compare:

> The poet and the philosopher **are** dead.

Here there are two different people.

### Article Trick

One article:

> The poet and philosopher **is**

Two articles:

> The poet and the philosopher **are**

---

# 11. Two nouns forming one idea/unit may take a singular verb

> Bread and butter **is** my usual breakfast.  
> Rice and curry **is** his favourite meal.

The combination is treated as one unit.

---

# 12. Each / Every before two nouns joined by AND takes a singular verb

> Every boy and every girl **is** ready.  
> Each man and each woman **has** a ticket.

### Rule

**Every A and every B → singular verb**

---

# 13. Many a + singular noun + singular verb

> Many a student **has** failed because of carelessness.  
> Many a man **has** tried.

Although the meaning is plural, the grammatical structure is singular.

### Structure

**Many a + singular noun + singular verb**

---

# 14. More than one + singular noun + singular verb

> More than one student **has** failed.  
> More than one person **was** injured.

### Structure

**More than one + singular noun + singular verb**

---

# 15. Collective nouns(**single unit**)

- team
- committee
- family
- jury
- audience
- government

When the group acts as **one unit**, use a singular verb.

> The committee **has** made its decision.  
> The team **is** playing well.

When members are acting separately, a plural verb may be used, especially in British English.

> The committee **are** divided in their opinions.

For SSC questions, check whether the group is acting **as one unit or individually**.

---

# 16. Uncountable nouns take singular verbs

Examples:

- furniture
    
- information
    
- advice
    
- luggage
    
- equipment
    
- machinery
    
- knowledge
    
- bread
    

> The furniture **is** expensive.  
> This information **is** useful.  
> His luggage **was** lost.

Never write:

> The furnitures are ❌  
> The informations are ❌

---

# 17. Some nouns ending in -s are singular(singular uncountable noun)

These look plural but are grammatically singular.

Examples:

- mathematics
    
- physics
    
- economics
    
- politics
    
- news
    
- measles
    

> Mathematics **is** difficult.  
> Physics **is** interesting.  
> The news **is** shocking.

---

# 18. Some nouns are always plural

Examples:

- scissors
    
- trousers
    
- pants
    
- spectacles
    
- binoculars
    
- pliers
    
- shorts
    

> My trousers **are** new.  
> These scissors **are** sharp.

But with **a pair of**, the verb becomes singular.

> A pair of trousers **is** lying on the bed.  
> A pair of scissors **is** on the table.

---

# 19. People / police / cattle take plural verbs

> The police **are** investigating the case.  
> The cattle **are** grazing.  
> People **are** waiting outside.

Not:

> The police is ❌

---

# 20. Money / time / distance / weight as one unit take a singular verb

When a quantity is considered as a single amount, use a singular verb.

> Ten kilometres **is** a long distance.  
> Five years **is** a long time.  
> Ten thousand rupees **is** enough.  
> Fifty kilograms **is** too heavy.

But if individual units are emphasized, a plural verb may be possible.

---

# 21. Fractions / percentages depend on the noun after OF

The verb depends on the noun following **of**.

> Half of the water **is** gone.  
> Half of the students **are** absent.

> Twenty percent of the work **is** complete.  
> Twenty percent of the workers **are** absent.

### Rule

**Fraction/percentage + of + noun → verb agrees with that noun**

---

# 22. Some / most / all / none + of

The verb generally depends on the noun after **of**.

> Some of the water **is** dirty.  
> Some of the students **are** absent.

> Most of the work **is** finished.  
> Most of the workers **are** ready.

> All of the money **is** gone.  
> All of the students **are** present.

With **none**, both singular and plural may occur depending on meaning, though plural is common with countable plural nouns.

> None of the money **is** missing.  
> None of the students **are** ready.

---

# 23. Relative pronoun: who / that / which agrees with its antecedent

The verb after a relative pronoun depends on the noun the pronoun refers to.

> He is one of the students who **have** passed.

Here **who → students**, so plural verb **have**.

Compare:

> He is the only one of the students who **has** passed.

Here **who → the only one**, so singular **has**.

### SSC Shortcut

**One of the + plural noun + who + plural verb**

> He is one of the boys who **work** hard.

But:

**The only one of the + plural noun + who + singular verb**

> He is the only one of the boys who **works** hard.

---

# 24. Generic ONE remains consistent

When **one** means “a person in general,” maintain the same pronoun family.

> One **has** to do **one's** duty.  
> One should control **oneself**.  
> One should do **one's** best.

### Pattern

**one → one's → oneself**

Do not unnecessarily switch:

> One should do his duty. ❌  
> One should do one's duty. ✅

---

# 25. Gerund / infinitive / clause as a subject usually takes a singular verb

> Swimming **is** good exercise.  
> To err **is** human.  
> What he said **is** true.

The whole activity/idea is treated as one thing.

---

# 26. Titles of books, films, newspapers, countries etc. take singular verbs

Even if the title looks plural:

> The Arabian Nights **is** an interesting book.  
> Gulliver's Travels **is** a famous work.

The title represents one work.

---

# 27. There is / There are

The verb agrees with the real subject that follows.

> There **is** a book on the table.  
> There **are** five books on the table.

> There **is** some water in the bottle.

---

# 28. Here is / Here are

The verb agrees with the subject that follows.

> Here **is** your book.  
> Here **are** your books.

---

# 29. Subject separated from verb by extra words

Do not let words between the subject and verb confuse you.

> The quality of these products **is** excellent.

Subject = **quality**, not products.

> The list of candidates **is** on the table.

Subject = **list**, not candidates.

### SSC Trick

Remove the **of phrase** mentally:

> The quality ~~of these products~~ **is** excellent.

---

# 30. Noun + of + plural noun

The verb generally agrees with the noun before **of**.

> The bouquet of flowers **is** beautiful.  
> The box of chocolates **is** empty.  
> The quality of the products **is** good.

---

# 31. The + adjective can represent a whole class → plural verb

Examples:

- the rich
    
- the poor
    
- the blind
    
- the elderly
    
- the young
    

> The rich **are** not always happy.  
> The poor **need** assistance.

These expressions represent groups of people.

---

# 32. A lot of / plenty of depend on the noun after OF

> A lot of money **is** required.  
> A lot of students **are** absent.

> Plenty of water **is** available.  
> Plenty of books **are** available.

---

# 33. No + noun

Verb depends on whether the noun is singular or plural.

> No student **is** absent.  
> No students **are** absent.

---

# 34. One and a half + plural noun usually takes singular verb

> One and a half hours **is** enough.

The quantity is treated as one measurement.

---

# 35. Singular names of organizations/countries/companies usually take singular verb

> India **is** developing rapidly.  
> Microsoft **is** a technology company.

Even where a name contains plural-looking words, if it represents one organization/entity, singular agreement is normally used.

---

# 36. Person of the verb

The verb must also agree in person.

> I **am** ready.  
> He **is** ready.  
> They **are** ready.

With **or/nor**, nearest-subject agreement is normally followed:

> Either you or I **am** responsible.  
> Either I or you **are** responsible.

In formal writing, awkward combinations are often rewritten.

# High-Priority SSC Traps

1. **A number of → plural**
    
2. **The number of → singular**
    
3. **One of the + plural noun → singular verb**
    
4. **One of the students who → plural verb**
    
5. **The only one who → singular verb**
    
6. **Each of / Either of / Neither of + plural noun → singular verb**
    
7. **Along with / as well as → verb agrees with first subject**
    
8. **Either...or / neither...nor → verb agrees with nearest subject**
    
9. **Many a → singular**
    
10. **More than one → singular**
    
11. **Police / cattle / people → plural**
    
12. **News / mathematics / physics → singular**
    
13. **Percentage/fraction → depends on noun after of**
    
14. **Money/time/distance as a single unit → singular**
    
15. **Subject + of + noun → identify the actual subject before choosing the verb**
