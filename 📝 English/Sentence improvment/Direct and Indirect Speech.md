## 1. What is Direct Speech?

Direct speech gives the **exact words** spoken by a person.

Example:

> He said, “I am happy.”

The exact words are inside quotation marks.

### Structure

**Reporting Verb + “Exact Words”**

Example:

> She said, “I am tired.”

---

# 2. What is Indirect Speech?

Indirect speech reports the speaker's words **without using the exact quotation**.

Example:

> He said that he was happy.

### Direct

> He said, “I am happy.”

### Indirect

> He said that he was happy.

Important changes may happen in:

- pronouns
    
- tense
    
- time words
    
- place words
    
- reporting verb
    
- sentence structure
    

---

# 3. Reporting Verb and Reported Speech

Example:

> Rahul said, “I am busy.”

### Reporting Verb

> Rahul **said**

### Reported Speech

> “I am busy.”

This distinction is very important.

---

# 4. Main Rule

When converting direct speech into indirect speech:

1. Remove quotation marks.
    
2. Change the reporting verb if required.
    
3. Use the correct connector such as:
    
    - that
        
    - if
        
    - whether
        
    - wh-word
        
    - to
        
4. Change pronouns.
    
5. Change tense when required.
    
6. Change time/place words when required.
    
7. Change question structure into normal statement order.
    

---

# 5. Assertive / Statement Sentences

For ordinary statements:

### Direct

> He said, “I am tired.”

### Indirect

> He said **that** he was tired.

### Formula

**said + that + statement**

Example:

> She said, “I like tea.”

Indirect:

> She said that she liked tea.

---

# 6. Said vs Told

This is very important.

### Said

Use:

**said + that**

> He said that he was busy.

You normally do not put a person directly after **said**.

❌ He said me that he was busy.

✅ He said that he was busy.

---

### Told

Use:

**told + person + that**

> He told me that he was busy.

Formula:

**told + object/person + that**

Examples:

> She told him that she was tired.

> Rahul told me that he would come.

### Easy Rule

**said → no object required**

**told → object/person required**

---

# 7. Said to

Direct speech often has:

> He said to me, “I am busy.”

In indirect speech:

> He told me that he was busy.

So:

**said to + person → told + person**

Example:

> She said to him, “I am happy.”

Indirect:

> She told him that she was happy.

---

# 8. Pronoun Change Rule

A useful traditional rule is:

### First Person

Changes according to the **subject of the reporting verb**.

### Second Person

Changes according to the **object of the reporting verb**.

### Third Person

Usually remains unchanged.

---

## Example — First Person

> Rahul said, “I am tired.”

"I" refers to Rahul.

Indirect:

> Rahul said that **he** was tired.

---

## Example — Second Person

> Rahul said to me, “You are late.”

"You" refers to me.

Indirect:

> Rahul told me that **I** was late.

---

## Example — Third Person

> Rahul said, “He is honest.”

Indirect:

> Rahul said that **he** was honest.

Context decides who "he" refers to.

---

# 9. Easy Pronoun Rule

Remember:

**1st person → Subject**

**2nd person → Object**

**3rd person → No change usually**

Traditional shortcut:

**SON**

- **S** = First person changes according to **Subject**
    
- **O** = Second person changes according to **Object**
    
- **N** = Third person = **No change**

# 10. Tense Change Rule

If the reporting verb is in the **past tense**, the tense of the reported speech usually moves one step back.

|                    | present                                                                                                                                                               | past                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| simple             | Present Simple → Past Simple<br><br>V1 → V2<br><br>He said, “I play cricket.”<br>He said that he **played** cricket.                                                  | Past Simple → Past Perfect<br><br>**V2 → had + V3**<br><br>He said, “I bought a car.”<br>He said that he **had bought** a car.<br>  |
| Continuous         | Present Continuous → Past Continuous<br><br>**am/is/are + V-ing** -> **was/were + V-ing**<br><br>She said, “I am studying.”<br>She said that she **was studying**.    | Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous<br>Direct:<br><br>She said, “I was sleeping.”<br>She said that she **had been sleeping**. |
| Perfect            | Present Perfect → Past Perfect<br><br>**has/have + V3** -> **had + V3**<br><br>He said, “I have finished the work.”<br>He said that he **had finished** the work.<br> | No Change                                                                                                                           |
| Perfect Continuous | Present Perfect Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous<br><br> She said, “I have been working.”<br> She said that she **had been working**.                             | No Change                                                                                                                           |


|                        |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Will → Would           | He said, “I will come.”<br>He said that he **would come**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Shall → Should / Would | The exact change depends on meaning and person.<br><br>He said, “I shall return.”<br>He said that he **would return**.<br><br>For suggestions/questions, **shall** may become **should**.                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Can → Could            | She said, “I can swim.”<br>She said that she **could swim**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| May → Might            | He said, “I may go.”<br>He said that he **might go**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| must → must/had to     | **Must** may remain **must**, especially for rules, obligations still valid, or strong necessity.<br><br>The teacher said, “Students must wear uniforms.”<br>The teacher said that students **must** wear uniforms.<br><br>Sometimes **must → had to** when referring to past necessity.<br><br>He said, “I must leave now.”<br>He said that he **had to** leave then. |

# 25. When Tense Does NOT Change

Tense does not always change.

## Case 1: Reporting Verb is Present/Future

Direct:

> He says, “I am tired.”

Indirect:

> He says that he **is tired**.

Not:

> ❌ He says that he was tired.

Because **says** is present tense.

---

## Case 2: Universal Truth

Direct:

> The teacher said, “The Earth revolves around the Sun.”

Indirect:

> The teacher said that the Earth **revolves** around the Sun.

We don't normally change it to:

> revolved

because it is still true.

---

## Case 3: Scientific Facts / Permanent Truths

> The teacher said, “Water boils at 100°C.”

Indirect:

> The teacher said that water **boils** at 100°C.

---

# 26. Time and Place Word Changes

When context changes, these words often change.

| Direct     | Indirect                          |
| ---------- | --------------------------------- |
| now        | then                              |
| today      | that day                          |
| tonight    | that night                        |
| yesterday  | the previous day / the day before |
| tomorrow   | the next day / following day      |
| last night | the previous night                |
| last week  | the previous week                 |
| next week  | the following week                |
| ago        | before                            |
| here       | there                             |
| this       | that                              |
| these      | those                             |
| come       | go                                |

Example:

> He said, “I will come here tomorrow.”

Indirect:

> He said that he would go there the next day.

But these changes depend on context. If the place/time is still the same, a change may not be necessary in real English.

# 28. Yes/No Questions

Use:

**asked + if/whether + subject + verb**

### Direct

> He said to me, “Are you happy?”

### Indirect

> He **asked me if** I was happy.

Not:

> ❌ He asked me if was I happy.

Because indirect questions use **statement order**.

### Correct Order

**Subject + Verb**

not:

**Verb + Subject**

---

# 29. Ask That — Important Rule

Your note:

> after ask that never come

This is mostly useful for reported questions.

For questions:

❌ He asked that I was happy.

✅ He asked **if** I was happy.

✅ He asked **whether** I was happy.

But **ask + that-clause** can exist in a different meaning in formal English:

> He asked that the meeting be postponed.

Here **asked** means **requested**.

So for SSC reported-question rules, remember:

> **Asked + that ❌ for reporting a question**

Use:

**if / whether / WH-word**

# 32. Said → Asked

Your note is partly correct.

Use **asked** when the speaker is asking a **question**.

Direct:

> He said to me, “Are you ready?”

Indirect:

> He **asked** me if I was ready.

But when someone asks another person **to do something**, we may also use:

- asked
    
- requested
    
- told
    
- ordered
    
- advised
    

depending on meaning.

Example:

> He said to me, “Please help me.”

Indirect:

> He **asked me to help him**.

Here **asked** means **requested**, not asked a question.

# 35. Requests

Use:

- requested
    
- asked
    
- begged
    
- urged
    

depending on meaning.

### Direct

> He said to me, “Please help me.”

Indirect:

> He **requested me to help him**.

or more natural:

> He **asked me to help him**.

**asked/requested + object + to + V1**

---

# 36. Advice

Use:

**advised + person + to + V1**

> The doctor said to me, “Take rest.”

Indirect:

> The doctor **advised me to take rest**.

---

# 37. Prohibition

Direct:

> The teacher said, “Do not smoke.”

Indirect:

> The teacher **forbade them to smoke**.

or:

> The teacher told them not to smoke.

Important:

With **forbid**:

✅ forbade him **to go**

Not:

❌ forbade him not to go

Because **forbid itself is negative in meaning**.

# 38. Suggestions — "Let Us / Let's"

Your note is correct:

**Let us / Let's → suggested**

### Direct

> He said, “Let's go for a walk.”

Indirect:

> He **suggested that we should go for a walk**.

or:

> He suggested **going for a walk**.

### Common SSC Formula

**Let's + V1**

↓

**suggested that + subject + should + V1**

Example:

> She said, “Let's start the meeting.”

Indirect:

> She suggested that they should start the meeting.

---

# 39. Important: Suggest Structure

Don't say:

❌ He suggested me to go.

Use:

✅ He suggested **that I should go**.

or:

✅ He suggested **going**.

So:

**suggest + that-clause**

or

**suggest + V-ing**

---

# 40. "Let Me"

"Let me" is usually a request to be allowed to do something.

Direct:

> He said, “Let me go.”

Indirect:

> He requested that he might be allowed to go.

or:

> He asked to be allowed to go.

For competitive-exam grammar, you may see:

**requested + to be allowed to**

---

# 41. "Let Him / Her / Them"

Direct:

> He said, “Let him enter.”

Depending on meaning:

> He said that he should be allowed to enter.

or:

> He ordered that he be allowed to enter.

Context matters.

# 49. Reported Questions — Golden Rule

This is one of the most important SSC rules.

Direct:

> He asked, “Where are you going?”

Question order:

**are + you**

Indirect:

> He asked where **I was going**.

Statement order:

**I + was**

So remember:

## Direct Question

**Helping verb + Subject**

## Indirect Question

**Subject + Helping verb**

# 52. That vs If/Whether vs WH-Word

This is an excellent shortcut.

### Statement

Use:

**that**

> He said **that** he was tired.

### Yes/No Question

Use:

**if / whether**

> He asked **if** I was tired.

### WH Question

Use the same **WH word**.

> He asked **why** I was tired.

### Command / Request

Use:

**to + V1**

> He told me **to sit**.

# 55. Important Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1

❌ He said me that he was tired.

✅ He told me that he was tired.

✅ He said that he was tired.

---

### Mistake 2

❌ He asked that I was ready.

✅ He asked if I was ready.

---

### Mistake 3

❌ He asked where was I going.

✅ He asked where I was going.

---

### Mistake 4

❌ He suggested me to go.

✅ He suggested that I should go.

✅ He suggested going.

---

### Mistake 5

❌ He told that he was busy.

Usually:

✅ He said that he was busy.

or:

✅ He told me that he was busy.

Because **told** normally needs an object.

---

### Mistake 6

❌ He asked me that where I lived.

✅ He asked me where I lived.

Do not use **that + WH word** together.

# 63. The 10 Rules You Should Keep in Mind

### Rule 1

**Statement → that**

> He said that...

### Rule 2

**Yes/No Question → if/whether**

> He asked if...

### Rule 3

**WH Question → same WH word**

> He asked where...

### Rule 4

**No inversion in indirect questions**

❌ where was he

✅ where he was

### Rule 5

**said to → told** for statements when an object is present.

### Rule 6

**said/said to → asked** when reporting a question.

### Rule 7

**Command/request → to + V1**

> told me to go

### Rule 8

**Negative command → not to + V1**

> told me not to go

### Rule 9

**Let's / Let us → suggested**

> suggested that we should...

### Rule 10

If the reporting verb is past, usually move the reported tense one step backward.
