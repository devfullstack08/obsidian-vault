# Word Case Study: Prude (અતિ-મર્યાદાશીલ / ક્ષુલ્લક બાબતે શરમાનાર વ્યક્તિ)

A comprehensive guide to the word **Prude**, its cultural synonyms, historical idioms, and shades of meaning for competitive exams.

---

## 1. Core Definition & Usage

* **Noun:** **Prude** (/pruːd/) — A person who is, or claims to be, easily shocked by matters relating to sex, nudity, or moral propriety.
* **Adjective:** **Prudish** — Having or revealing a tendency to be easily shocked by matters relating to sex or nudity.
* **Etymology:** From French *prude* (virtuous woman), which was a back-formation of *prudefemme* (wise, virtuous woman - from Old French *prou* meaning valiant/good + *femme* meaning woman). Over time, the meaning shifted from genuine virtue to an affected, rigid show of modesty.
* **Example Sentence:**
  > *"The love scene in the novel was so ambiguous and romantic that none but a **prude** could find it objectionable."*

## 3. In-Depth Study of Key Synonyms & Idioms

### A. Mrs. Grundy (Grundyism)
* **Origin:** From Thomas Morton's 1798 play *Speed the Plough*. Mrs. Grundy is a character who never actually appears on stage, but other characters constantly worry about her judgment, asking: *"What will Mrs. Grundy say?"*
* **Meaning:** She is a figurative name for an extremely conventional, narrow-minded, or priggish person. A tendency to be overly fearful of what others might think is referred to as **grundyism**.

### B. Priggish (Prig)
* **Meaning:** A **prig** is a person who shows an inordinately zealous approach to matters of form and propriety—especially where they can show off superior knowledge of rules and protocols. 
* **Key Traits:** Prigs rely on rigid rules to resolve all questions, showing self-righteousness and little consideration for the feelings or intentions of others.

### C. Miss Prim
* **Meaning:** An idiomatic expression used to describe a woman who is excessively proper, prim, or prudish. It carries a negative connotation, suggesting that their concern with propriety is affected or artificial.
* **Usage:** Often used in the phrase *"Miss Prim and Proper"* to describe a stiff, straight-laced person.
* **Literary Connection:** The title of Natalia Sanmartín Fenollera's novel *The Awakening of Miss Prim* features Prudencia Prim, a rigid outsider who eventually learns to relax her strict rules.

### D. Holy Willie (Holy Willie's Prayer)
* **Origin:** From Robert Burns' famous 1785 satirical poem *Holy Willie's Prayer*. The poem was based on William Fisher, a real-life strict church elder in Mauchline, Scotland.
* **Meaning:** A **hypocritically pious** person. In Burns' poem, Willie prays to God, praising himself as one of the morally "chosen" people (predestination), while unintentionally exposing his own pride, lust, and greed.
* **Modern Usage:** In the Scots language, the phrase describes anyone who is humorless, ultra-religious, or hypocritical—judging everyone except themselves.

### E. Goody Two-Shoes / Goody-goody
* **Origin:** From the 1765 children's story *The History of Little Goody Two-Shoes*. The protagonist, Margery Meanwell, is a poor orphan who receives a pair of shoes and goes around behaving perfectly and doing good deeds.
* **Meaning:** Today, it is used informally to describe someone who is self-righteously or annoyingly virtuous, always trying to please authority.

## 4. Antonyms (Opposites)

* **Libertine:** A person (typically a man) who leads an unrestrained, dissolute life without moral principles (especially in sexual matters).
* **Hedonist:** A person who believes that the pursuit of pleasure is the most important thing in life.
* **Freethinker:** A person who forms opinions on the basis of reason, independent of authority, tradition, or established moral dogmas.
