## How much is there? (Scarce / Rare / Limited / Abundant / Insufficient)

| Word | Core meaning | Best used for | Example |
| ---- | ------------ | ------------- | ------- |
| Scarce(અછત / ઓછું મળે) | available in very small quantity compared with need | food, water, jobs, resources | Clean water is **scarce** in this region. |
| Rare(દુર્લભ) | not commonly found, seen, or happening | species, metals, events, diseases | Gold is relatively **rare** in the Earth’s crust. |
| Limited(મર્યાદિત) | available only in a fixed/restricted amount | time, seats, money, resources | We have **limited** time. |
| Abundant(પ્રચુર / વિપુલ) | available in a large quantity; plentiful | resources, food, wildlife, water | Iron is **abundant** in the Earth’s crust. |
| Insufficient(અપૂરતું) | not enough for a particular purpose or need | money, evidence, time, supply | The evidence was **insufficient** to prove the case. |

The easiest distinction is:

- **Scarce** = difficult to get because there isn’t much of it.
- **Rare** = uncommon.
- **Limited** = restricted in amount.
- **Insufficient** = not enough for the requirement.
- **Abundant** = more than enough / plentiful.

For example:

- Water may be **limited** in a tank.
- During a drought, water becomes **scarce**.
- If you need 100 litres but have only 50 litres, the water is **insufficient**.
- A mineral found only in a few places may be **rare**.
- If a resource exists in huge quantities, it is **abundant**.

### Synonym–antonym view

**Scarce**

- Synonyms: meagre, scanty, sparse, deficient, inadequate
- Antonyms: abundant, plentiful, ample

**Rare**

- Synonyms: uncommon, unusual, exceptional, infrequent
- Antonyms: common, ordinary, frequent

**Limited**

- Synonyms: restricted, finite, bounded
- Antonyms: unlimited, unrestricted, infinite

**Abundant**

- Synonyms: plentiful, ample, copious, profuse
- Antonyms: scarce, scanty, deficient

**Insufficient**

- Synonyms: inadequate, deficient, lacking
- Antonyms: sufficient, adequate, enough

### One important SSC trap

**Rare ≠ scarce**

A thing can be rare but not scarce for your needs, and something can be scarce without being naturally rare.

Example: During a temporary shortage, petrol may become **scarce**, but petrol itself is not something we normally call a **rare** commodity.

### Memory trick

Think:

- **R** → Rare = Rarely seen
- **S** → Scarce = Short supply
- **L** → Limited = Limit exists
- **I** → Insufficient = It is not enough
- **A** → Abundant = A lot available

For exams, remember this strongest pair:

**Scarce ↔ Abundant**

and

**Insufficient ↔ Sufficient.**
