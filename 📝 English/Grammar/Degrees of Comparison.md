
## Overview

Adjectives and adverbs can be used in three degrees of comparison:

1. Positive Degree
    
2. Comparative Degree
    
3. Superlative Degree
    

---

## 1. Positive Degree

The positive degree describes a person, place, thing, or quality without directly comparing it with another.

### Structure

```text
Subject + verb + adjective
```

### Examples

- Rahul is tall.
    
- This car is expensive.
    
- She is intelligent.
    
- He runs fast.
    

The positive degree is also used in equal comparisons.

### Equal comparison

```text
as + adjective + as
```

Examples:

- Rahul is as tall as Amit.
    
- This phone is as expensive as that phone.
    
- She is as intelligent as her sister.
    

### Unequal comparison

```text
not as/so + adjective + as
```

Examples:

- Amit is not as tall as Rahul.
    
- This phone is not as expensive as that one.
    
- He is not so strong as his brother.
    

---

## 2. Comparative Degree

The comparative degree is used to compare two people, things, places, groups, or categories.

### Structure

```text
Subject + verb + comparative adjective + than + object
```

### Examples

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    
- Gold is more expensive than silver.
    
- This book is better than that book.
    
- A train is faster than a bus.
    
- Class A is more disciplined than Class B.
    

### Main Rule

> Use the comparative degree when comparing two sides.

The two sides may be:

- two people
    
- two things
    
- two places
    
- two groups
    
- two categories
    

---

## 3. Superlative Degree

The superlative degree is used when one person or thing is compared with three or more members of a group.

It shows the highest or lowest degree of a quality.

### Structure

```text
Subject + verb + the + superlative adjective + in/of + group
```

### Examples

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    
- Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
    
- She is the most intelligent student in the group.
    
- This is the best book of the three.
    

### Main Rule

> Use the superlative degree when one person or thing is compared with all the others in a group.

---

# Comparison Between Two and More Than Two

## Comparison Between Two

Use the comparative degree.

Examples:

- Riya is taller than Priya.
    
- This road is safer than that road.
    
- Of the two brothers, Amit is the taller.
    

### Important Rule

When only two people or things are compared, use:

```text
the + comparative
```

Correct:

- Of the two brothers, Amit is the taller.
    
- Of the two options, this is the better one.
    

Incorrect:

- Of the two brothers, Amit is the tallest.
    
- Of the two options, this is the best one.
    

---

## Comparison Among Three or More

Use the superlative degree.

Examples:

- Riya is the tallest of the three sisters.
    
- Amit is the most intelligent student in the class.
    
- This is the cheapest phone in the shop.
    

---

# Formation of Comparative and Superlative Adjectives

## 1. Short Adjectives

For many one-syllable adjectives, add:

```text
-er = comparative
-est = superlative
```

|Positive|Comparative|Superlative|
|---|---|---|
|tall|taller|tallest|
|small|smaller|smallest|
|fast|faster|fastest|
|old|older|oldest|
|young|younger|youngest|

Examples:

- Amit is taller than Raj.
    
- Amit is the tallest boy in the group.
    

---

## 2. Adjectives Ending in `e`

Add:

```text
-r = comparative
-st = superlative
```

|Positive|Comparative|Superlative|
|---|---|---|
|large|larger|largest|
|wise|wiser|wisest|
|nice|nicer|nicest|

---

## 3. Adjectives Ending in Consonant-Vowel-Consonant

Double the final consonant before adding `-er` or `-est`.

|Positive|Comparative|Superlative|
|---|---|---|
|big|bigger|biggest|
|hot|hotter|hottest|
|thin|thinner|thinnest|

---

## 4. Adjectives Ending in `y`

Change `y` to `i` and add `-er` or `-est`.

|Positive|Comparative|Superlative|
|---|---|---|
|happy|happier|happiest|
|easy|easier|easiest|
|heavy|heavier|heaviest|
|busy|busier|busiest|

---

## 5. Long Adjectives

For most adjectives with two or more syllables, use:

```text
more = comparative
most = superlative
```

|Positive|Comparative|Superlative|
|---|---|---|
|beautiful|more beautiful|most beautiful|
|intelligent|more intelligent|most intelligent|
|expensive|more expensive|most expensive|
|difficult|more difficult|most difficult|

Examples:

- This dress is more beautiful than that one.
    
- This is the most beautiful dress in the shop.
    

---

# Double Comparison Errors

Do not use two comparative or superlative forms together.

Incorrect:

- more taller
    
- more better
    
- most tallest
    
- most smartest
    

Correct:

- taller
    
- better
    
- tallest
    
- smartest
    

---

# Irregular Degrees of Comparison

Some adjectives do not follow normal rules.

| Positive | Comparative     | Superlative       |
| -------- | --------------- | ----------------- |
| good     | better          | best              |
| bad      | worse           | worst             |
| little   | less            | least             |
| much     | more            | most              |
| many     | more            | most              |
| far      | farther/further | farthest/furthest |
| old      | older/elder     | oldest/eldest     |

Examples:

- This book is better than that one.
    
- This is the best book in the series.
    
- His condition is worse today.
    
- This is the worst result.
    

---

# Use of `Than`

The comparative degree usually uses `than`.

Examples:

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    
- This laptop is more expensive than that one.
    
- Gold is more valuable than silver.
    

Incorrect:

- Rahul is taller Amit.
    

Correct:

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    

---

# Use of `The`

The superlative degree usually uses `the`.

Examples:

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    
- This is the most expensive laptop.
    
- She is the best player on the team.
    

However, possessive words can replace `the`.

Examples:

- He is my best friend.
    
- This is India’s largest state by area.
    

Incorrect:

- He is my the best friend.
    

Correct:

- He is my best friend.
    

---

# Use of `Of` and `In`

## Use `of`

Use `of` before a specific group or number.

Examples:

- She is the tallest of the three girls.
    
- This is the best of all the options.
    
- He is the strongest of the four players.
    

## Use `in`

Use `in` before a place, organization, class, or larger group.

Examples:

- She is the tallest girl in the class.
    
- Mumbai is one of the largest cities in India.
    
- He is the best player in the team.
    

---

# One of the + Superlative

Use:

```text
one of the + superlative adjective + plural noun
```

Examples:

- Gujarat is one of the **most** developed **states** in India.
    
- He is one of the **best** **players** on the team.
    
- This is one of the **most** useful **books**.
    

---

# Any Other and All Other

## Comparative with `any other`

Use:

```text
comparative + than any other + singular noun
```

Examples:

- Mount Everest is higher than any other mountain in the world.
    
- Rahul is taller than any other boy in the class.
    

## Comparative with `all other`

Use:

```text
comparative + than all other + plural noun
```

Examples:

- Mount Everest is higher than all other mountains in the world.
    
- Rahul is taller than all other boys in the class.
    

---

# Difference Between `Any` and `Any Other`

Use `any other` when the subject is part of the same group.

Example:

- India is larger than any other country in South Asia.
    

India is part of South Asia.

Use `any` when the subject is not part of the group.

Example:

- India is larger than any country in Europe.
    

India is not part of Europe.

---

# Transformation of Degrees

The degree can change while the meaning remains almost the same.

## Superlative to Comparative

Superlative:

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    

Comparative:

- Rahul is taller than any other boy in the class.
    
- Rahul is taller than all other boys in the class.
    

---

## Comparative to Positive

Comparative:

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    

Positive:

- Amit is not as tall as Rahul.
    

---

## Superlative to Positive

Superlative:

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    

Positive:

- No other boy in the class is as tall as Rahul.
    

---

# Common Errors

## Error 1: Using a Superlative for Two

Incorrect:

- Of the two boys, Rahul is the tallest.
    

Correct:

- Of the two boys, Rahul is the taller.
    

---

## Error 2: Double Comparison

Incorrect:

- more better
    
- most tallest
    

Correct:

- better
    
- tallest
    

---

## Error 3: Forgetting `Than`

Incorrect:

- Rahul is taller Amit.
    

Correct:

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    

---

## Error 4: Forgetting `The`

Incorrect:

- Rahul is tallest boy in the class.
    

Correct:

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    

---

## Error 5: Incorrect Same-Group Comparison

Incorrect:

- Rahul is taller than any boy in his class.
    

This may logically include Rahul himself.

Correct:

- Rahul is taller than any other boy in his class.
    

---

## Error 6: Comparing Unrelated Things

Incorrect:

- Amit’s salary is higher than Raj.
    

This compares Amit’s salary with Raj as a person.

Correct:

- Amit’s salary is higher than Raj’s salary.
    
- Amit’s salary is higher than Raj’s.
    

---

# Special Comparative Structures

## The More..., the More...

This structure shows that one change causes or accompanies another.

Examples:

- The more you practise, the better you become.
    
- The harder you work, the more you learn.
    
- The earlier you start, the sooner you finish.
    

---

## Comparative and Comparative

This structure shows continuous change.

Examples:

- The weather is getting colder and colder.
    
- Technology is becoming more and more advanced.
    
- The problem became worse and worse.
    

---

## Words Used Before Comparatives

The following words can strengthen or reduce a comparative:

- much
    
- far
    
- a lot
    
- slightly
    
- a little
    
- considerably
    

Examples:

- This car is much faster than that one.
    
- Gold is far more expensive than silver.
    
- Rahul is slightly taller than Amit.
    
- This method is a lot better.
    

Do not normally use `very` before a comparative.

Incorrect:

- very taller
    
- very better
    

Correct:

- much taller
    
- much better
    

---

# Quick Summary

## Positive Degree

Used when there is no direct comparison.

Example:

- Rahul is tall.
    

## Comparative Degree

Used to compare two sides.

Example:

- Rahul is taller than Amit.
    

## Superlative Degree

Used when one person or thing is compared with three or more members of a group.

Example:

- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.
    

---

# Final Rule

> When a comparison is made between two people, things, groups, or categories, we generally use the comparative degree.

> When one person or thing is compared with three or more members of a group, we generally use the superlative degree.
