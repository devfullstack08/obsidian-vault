
## Basic Rule

### Active

**Subject + Verb + Object**

Example:

> She writes a letter.

### Passive

**Object + form of "be" + V3 + by + Subject**

Example:

> A letter is written by her.

So remember:

**S + V + O**

becomes

**O + be + V3 + by + S**

----

## Three Main Changes in Passive Voice / Three main rules

### Change 1: Object becomes Subject /  Interchange Subject and Object (Pronoun Changes)

In an active sentence, the Subject does the action, and the Object receives it. In a passive sentence, the **Object becomes the new Subject**, and the **Subject becomes the new Object** (often placed after the word "by").
  
Active:

> Rahul helps Mohan.

- Rahul = Subject
- helps = Verb
- Mohan = Object

Passive:

> Mohan is helped by Rahul.

So:

**Object → New Subject**

#### Pronoun Conversion Table:
When interchanging subject and object, personal pronouns change as follows:

| Active Voice (Subject) | Passive Voice (Object) |
| :--------------------- | :--------------------- |
| **I**                  | **me**                 |
| **We**                 | **us**                 |
| **You**                | **you**                |
| **He**                 | **him**                |
| **She**                | **her**                |
| **It**                 | **it**                 |
| **They**               | **them**               |
|                        |                        |

#### Example:
* **Active:** **She** (Subject) helped **him** (Object).
* **Passive:** **He** (New Subject) was helped by **her** (New Object).

### Change 2: Main Verb becomes V3 / The Main Verb is Always in V3 (Past Participle) Form

In passive voice, the main verb is **always** written in its third form (**V3 / Past Participle**), regardless of the tense of the sentence. The tense of the sentence is carried by the appropriate form of the auxiliary verb **"to be"** (am, is, are, was, were, being, been, be).
#### Formula Structure:
$$\text{Passive Voice} = \text{Subject} + \text{Form of 'to be'} + \mathbf{V3} + (\text{by} + \text{Object})$$
#### Examples of Verb Changes:
* **Play (V1) $\rightarrow$ Played (V3):**
  * *Active:* They play football. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* Football is **played** by them.
* **Eat (V1) $\rightarrow$ Eaten (V3):**
  * *Active:* She was eating an apple. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* An apple was being **eaten** by her.
* **Do (V1) $\rightarrow$ Done (V3):**
  * *Active:* We have done our duty. $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* Our duty has been **done** by us.


### 3. Tense Does Not Change / In **active-passive voice**, the **tense should remain the same**.

This is one of the most important rules.
When converting active voice into passive voice, **the tense remains the same**.

In active-passive voice, the tense does not change. If the active sentence is in present tense, the passive sentence will also be in present tense. Only the sentence structure changes.

Active sentence in present tense → Passive sentence also in present tense
Active sentence in past tense → Passive sentence also in past tense
Active sentence in future tense → Passive sentence also in future tense

Present:
Active: He writes a letter.
Passive: A letter is written by him.

Past:
Active: He wrote a letter.
Passive: A letter was written by him.

Future:
Active: He will write a letter.
Passive: A letter will be written by him.

## Some other Important  rules 

### 1. Sentences with Two Objects

Some sentences contain two objects.

Example:

> She gave me a gift.

Here:

- me = indirect object
- gift = direct object

Two passive forms are possible.

### First

> I was given a gift by her.

### Second

> A gift was given to me by her.

Another:

> The teacher taught us English.

Passive:

> We were taught English by the teacher.

or:

> English was taught to us by the teacher.


### 2. When "By" Is Not Required

The original subject is often omitted if it is:

- unknown
- obvious
- unimportant
- unnecessary

Active:

> Someone stole my phone.

Passive:

> My phone was stolen.

No need to say:

> My phone was stolen by someone.

Another:

> People speak English worldwide.

Passive:

> English is spoken worldwide.

### 3.By vs With

### By

Used for the person or agent performing the action.

> The letter was written **by John**.

### With

Used for an instrument or tool.

> The letter was written **with a pen**.

Another:

> The window was broken **by Rahul**.

Rahul = person.

> The window was broken **with a hammer**.

Hammer = tool.

### Shortcut

**By = Who?**

**With = What tool?**

### 4. Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

See [[Transitive and Intransitive Verb]].

Passive voice normally requires a verb that has an **object**.

Such verbs are called **transitive verbs**.

Example:

> She eats an apple.

Object = apple.

Passive:

> An apple is eaten by her.

But:

> He sleeps.

There is no object.

So we cannot normally write:

> ❌ Sleep is slept by him.

Common intransitive verbs:

- sleep
- arrive
- come
- go
- die
- happen
- laugh
- cry

### 5. Phrasal Verbs in Passive Voice

Do not remove the preposition or particle.

Active:

> They looked after the child.

Passive:

> The child was **looked after** by them.

---

Active:

> They called off the meeting.

Passive:

> The meeting was **called off**.

---

Active:

> Everyone laughed at him.

Passive:

> He was **laughed at**.


### 6. Passive with See, Hear, Watch, etc.

Active:

> They saw him enter the room.

Passive:

> He was seen **to enter** the room.

Active:

> I heard her sing.

Passive:

> She was heard **to sing**.

Again, **to** appears in passive.



### 7. Infinitive Passive

Active infinitive:

**to + V1**

Passive infinitive:

**to + be + V3**

Example:

> They want to complete the project.

When needed:

> The project needs **to be completed**.

Another:

> This form has to be submitted.

**to be submitted** = passive infinitive.

---

### 8. Gerund Passive

Active gerund:

**V-ing**

Passive gerund:

**being + V3**

Example:

> I dislike people criticizing me.

Passive idea:

> I dislike **being criticized**.

Another:

> He enjoys being praised.

**being praised** = passive gerund.
