
## Exam Shortcut

1. **If I were you**, not normally _If I was you_ in formal hypothetical usage.
    
2. **If + Past Simple → would + V1**
    
3. **If + had + V3 → would have + V3**
    
4. Never normally write **would** directly in the _if-clause_.
    

Example:

> ✅ If I **knew** the answer, I **would tell** you.

> ✅ If I **had known** the answer, I **would have told** you.

Those two structures cover a large part of hypothetical/imaginary-situation grammar.

-----

For **hypothetical / imaginary situations**, English mainly uses **conditionals**, **wish**, **if only**, and sometimes **as if / as though**.

The most important rule is this:

> For imaginary or unreal situations, English often uses a **past form**, even when we are talking about the **present or future**.

## 1. Present/Future Hypothetical — Second Conditional

Use this for an unreal, unlikely, or imaginary situation in the present or future.

### Structure

**If + Past Simple, would + V1**

Example:

> If I had more money, I would buy a car.

Meaning: I do not have enough money now.

Another:

> If I were rich, I would travel the world.

Meaning: I am not rich; I am imagining it.

### Important rule: "were"

For formal/standard hypothetical English, use:

> If I **were**...

not necessarily:

> If I was...

Examples:

> If I were you, I would accept the offer.

> If she were here, she would help us.

This is called the **subjunctive were**.

---

# 2. Past Hypothetical — Third Conditional

Use this for an imaginary situation in the past that **did not happen**.

### Structure

**If + had + V3, would have + V3**

Example:

> If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

Actual situation:

- I did not study hard.
    
- I did not pass.
    

Another:

> If she had left earlier, she would have caught the train.

Actual situation:

- She did not leave early.
    
- She missed the train.
    

---

# 3. Real/Possible Condition — First Conditional

This is not completely imaginary. It is a **real future possibility**.

### Structure

**If + Present Simple, will + V1**

Example:

> If it rains, I will stay home.

The rain is actually possible.

Compare:

### Possible

> If I get the job, I will move to Delhi.

### Hypothetical

> If I got the job, I would move to Delhi.

The second one sounds more uncertain or imaginary.

---

# 4. Zero Conditional — General Truth

Use this for facts and general truths.

### Structure

**If + Present Simple, Present Simple**

Example:

> If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

> If I don't sleep enough, I feel tired.

This is not hypothetical.

---

# 5. Quick Conditional Table

|Type|Situation|Structure|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|Zero|Fact/general truth|if + present, present|If you heat ice, it melts.|
|First|Real future possibility|if + present, will + V1|If it rains, I will stay home.|
|Second|Unreal/imaginary present or future|if + past, would + V1|If I were rich, I would travel.|
|Third|Unreal past|if + had + V3, would have + V3|If I had studied, I would have passed.|

# 6. "Wish" for Imaginary Situations

## Wish about the Present

Use:

**wish + Past Simple**

Example:

> I wish I had more money.

Meaning:

> I don't have much money.

Another:

> I wish I were taller.

Meaning:

> I am not taller.

Notice again:

**were** is commonly used in formal hypothetical statements.

---

# 7. Wish about the Past

Use:

**wish + had + V3**

Example:

> I wish I had studied harder.

Meaning:

> I didn't study hard, and now I regret it.

Another:

> She wishes she had gone to the party.

Meaning:

> She did not go.

---

# 8. Wish about a Future Change / Complaint

Use:

**wish + would + V1**

Example:

> I wish he would stop shouting.

> I wish it would stop raining.

Usually this expresses:

- irritation
    
- desire for change
    
- complaint
    

---

# 9. "If Only"

**If only** is similar to **wish**, but usually stronger or more emotional.

### Present

> If only I were rich!

Meaning:

> I wish I were rich.

### Past

> If only I had studied harder!

Meaning:

> I wish I had studied harder.

### Future/change

> If only he would listen!

---

# 10. "As If / As Though"

Use these when something appears to be true, although it may not be true.

### Unreal Present

Often use past tense:

> He talks as if he knew everything.

Meaning:

> He probably does not know everything.

Another:

> She acts as though she were the boss.

Meaning:

> She is not actually the boss.

---

# 11. Unreal Past with "As If"

Use:

**as if/as though + had + V3**

Example:

> He looked at me as if he had seen a ghost.

> She behaved as though nothing had happened.

---

# 12. "Would Rather"

For your own preference:

> I would rather stay home.

But when you want **another person** to do something:

### Present/Future preference

**would rather + subject + Past Simple**

Example:

> I would rather you stayed here.

Meaning:

> I want you to stay here.

Another:

> I'd rather he didn't come.

---

# 13. "It's Time"

For something that should happen now:

**It's time + subject + Past Simple**

Example:

> It's time we went home.

Although **went** is past tense, the meaning is:

> We should go home now.

Another:

> It's time you started studying.

---

# 14. Suppose / Supposing / Imagine

These are also used for hypothetical situations.

Example:

> Suppose you won ₹1 crore. What would you do?

> Imagine you were the Prime Minister. What would you change?

> Supposing you lost your phone, what would you do?

For imaginary present/future situations, we commonly use:

**past form + would**

---

# 15. Could and Might in Hypothetical Statements

You don't always need **would**.

### Could

Means ability/possibility.

> If I had more time, I could learn Spanish.

### Might

Means possibility.

> If we left now, we might catch the train.

### Would

Usually describes the expected result.

> If I had more time, I would travel more.

So:

- **would** = expected result
    
- **could** = ability/possibility
    
- **might** = uncertain possibility
    

---

# 16. Very Important Rule: Don't Normally Use "Would" After "If"

Incorrect:

> ❌ If I would have money, I would buy a car.

Correct:

> ✅ If I had money, I would buy a car.

Incorrect:

> ❌ If I would study, I would pass.

Correct:

> ✅ If I studied, I would pass.

For third conditional:

❌ If I would have studied...

✅ If I had studied...

---

# 17. Present Reality vs Hypothetical

Compare:

### Reality

> I am not rich.

### Hypothetical

> If I were rich, I would buy a big house.

The past form **were** doesn't mean past time here.

It shows:

> **distance from reality**

This is a very important grammar concept.

---

# 18. Past Reality vs Hypothetical

Reality:

> I did not study.

Hypothetical:

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

So:

### Present unreal

**Past Simple**

> If I knew...

### Past unreal

**Past Perfect**

> If I had known...

---

# 19. Mixed Conditional

Sometimes the condition is in the past, but its result is in the present.

### Structure

**If + had + V3, would + V1**

Example:

> If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.

Past reality:

> I didn't study medicine.

Present result:

> I am not a doctor.

Another:

> If he had saved more money, he would be financially secure now.

---

# 20. Reverse Mixed Conditional

Present condition + past result.

Example:

> If I were more careful, I wouldn't have made that mistake.

Meaning:

> I am generally not careful, and because of that I made the mistake.

---

# 21. Easy Memory Trick

Think of hypothetical grammar as moving **one step backward in tense**.

### Real present

> I am rich.

### Imaginary present

> If I **were** rich...

---

### Real past

> I studied.

### Imaginary past

> If I **had studied**...

So:

**Present imaginary → Past Simple**

**Past imaginary → Past Perfect**

---

# 22. Most Important Patterns to Memorize

### Imaginary Present/Future

**If + Past Simple → would + V1**

> If I were rich, I would travel.

### Imaginary Past

**If + had + V3 → would have + V3**

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

### Present Wish

**wish + Past Simple**

> I wish I were rich.

### Past Wish

**wish + had + V3**

> I wish I had studied.

### Strong Wish

**if only + same patterns**

> If only I had more time!

### Imaginary Comparison

**as if/as though + past**

> He talks as if he knew everything.
