## 1. What Is a Conditional Sentence?

A conditional sentence describes a **condition** and its **result**.

It normally contains two clauses:

- **If-clause:** Expresses the condition.
    
- **Main clause:** Expresses the result.
    

**Example:**

> If I study hard, I will pass the examination.

- Condition: If I study hard
    
- Result: I will pass the examination
    

Conditional sentences are broadly divided into two categories:

|Real / Possible Conditions|Unreal / Hypothetical Conditions|
|---|---|
|Describe facts, genuine possibilities, or uncertain real events.|Describe imaginary, contrary-to-fact, or unlikely situations.|
|Generally use ordinary present or past tense forms.|Often use past forms to express unreality.|
|If I **am** rich, I will buy a car.|If I **were** rich, I would buy a car.|
|If she **studies**, she will pass.|If she **studied**, she would pass.|
|If he **was** there yesterday, he probably saw her.|If he **had been** there yesterday, he would have seen her.|

**Key concept:** Real vs unreal describes how the speaker views the condition, not simply whether the sentence refers to the past, present, or future.

## 2. Types of Conditional Sentences — Master Table

|Type|Situation|Structure|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Zero**|General facts, truths, habits|If + Present Simple, Present Simple|If you heat ice, it melts.|
|**First**|Real or possible future|If + Present Simple, will + V1|If I study, I will pass.|
|**Second**|Imaginary, unreal, or unlikely present/future|If + Past Simple, would + V1|If I were rich, I would travel.|
|**Third**|Unreal past; something that did not happen|If + had + V3, would have + V3|If I had studied, I would have passed.|
|**Mixed (Past → Present)**|Unreal past condition with present result|If + had + V3, would + V1|If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.|
|**Mixed (Present → Past)**|Unreal present/general condition with past result|If + Past Simple, would have + V3|If I were more careful, I wouldn't have made that mistake.|

### Real Conditions

**Zero Conditional — Facts and General Truths**

> If you heat water sufficiently, it boils.

This describes a general truth.

**First Conditional — Genuine Future Possibility**

> If I get the job, I will move to Delhi.

The speaker considers getting the job a real possibility.

**Real Condition About the Past**

> If he was at the office yesterday, he probably saw Rahul.

We do not know whether he was at the office. We are discussing an actual past possibility, not imagining a different past.

### Unreal Conditions

**Second Conditional — Imaginary Present or Future**

> If I were rich, I would buy a Ferrari.

Reality: I am not rich.

> If I won the lottery tomorrow, I would travel abroad.

This is a hypothetical, unlikely future situation, not necessarily an impossible one.

**Third Conditional — Imaginary Past**

> If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

Reality: I did not study hard enough, and I did not pass.

The past cannot be changed, so we imagine a different outcome.

## 3. Why Do We Use Past Tense for Unreal Situations?

**One of the most important grammar concepts: A past form does not always express past time.**

In hypothetical grammar, the past form often shows distance from reality.

|Meaning|Form|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Real present/future|Present Simple|If I have money, I will buy a car.|
|Unreal present/future|Past Simple|If I had money, I would buy a car.|
|Unreal past|Past Perfect|If I had had money, I would have bought a car.|

Notice the final example:

- **had had** = Past Perfect of _have_.
    
- First _had_ = auxiliary verb.
    
- Second _had_ = past participle (V3).
    

### Memory Trick: Move One Step Back

**Real present/future → Unreal present/future**

Present Simple → Past Simple

> If I **know** the answer, I **will tell** you.

> If I **knew** the answer, I **would tell** you.

**Real past → Unreal past**

Past Simple → Past Perfect

> If he **was** there, he **saw** the incident. (The result is stated as following from the condition.)

> If he **had been** there, he **would have seen** the incident. (Imaginary past)

## 4. Subjunctive 'Were' — Very Important

In formal English, hypothetical present conditions commonly use **were** with all subjects.

**Structure:**

If + Subject + were + ..., Subject + would/could/might + V1

|Subject|Example|
|---|---|
|I|If I **were** rich, I would travel.|
|He|If he **were** here, he would help.|
|She|If she **were** the manager, things would change.|
|It|If it **were** possible, I would do it.|
|They|If they **were** ready, we could leave.|

**SSC Exam Trap:**

- ❌ If I was you, I would apologize.
    
- ✅ If I were you, I would apologize.
    

**Important exception:** _If I was_ is possible in informal hypothetical English, and it is entirely normal when discussing an uncertain actual past.

> If I was rude yesterday, I apologize.

This does not imagine an impossible situation. The speaker is uncertain about a real past event.

**Exam strategy:** For formal hypothetical situations, choose **were**, especially in _If I were you_.

## 5. Would vs Could vs Might

In unreal conditional sentences, the main clause can use **would, could, or might**.

|Modal|Meaning|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Would**|Expected hypothetical result|If I had money, I would buy a house.|
|**Could**|Ability or possibility|If I had more time, I could learn French.|
|**Might**|Uncertain possibility|If we left early, we might catch the train.|

The same distinction applies to unreal past conditions:

- **Would have + V3** — Expected hypothetical past result.
    
- **Could have + V3** — Past ability or possibility.
    
- **Might have + V3** — Uncertain hypothetical past possibility.
    

Examples:

> If I had trained harder, I would have won.

> If I had trained harder, I could have won.

> If I had trained harder, I might have won.

The degree of certainty changes.

## 6. Related Hypothetical Expressions

Hypothetical grammar is not restricted to _if-clauses_.

|Expression|Usage|Structure|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Wish — Present**|Unreal present desire|Wish + Past Simple|I wish I were rich.|
|**Wish — Past**|Past regret|Wish + had + V3|I wish I had studied.|
|**Wish — Change**|Complaint/desire for change|Wish + would + V1|I wish he would listen.|
|**If Only**|Strong wish or regret|Same patterns as wish|If only I had known!|
|**As if / As though**|Imaginary comparison|As if + Past Simple|He acts as if he were the boss.|
|**Would Rather**|Preference about another person's action|Would rather + subject + Past Simple|I'd rather you stayed here.|
|**It's Time**|Something should happen now|It's time + subject + Past Simple|It's time we went home.|
|**Suppose / Imagine**|Imaginary situation|Suppose + Past Simple|Suppose you won ₹1 crore. What would you do?|

### Important Distinctions

**Wish vs Hope**

- I **hope** I pass the exam. → A real future possibility.
    
- I **wish** I had passed the exam. → Regret about the past.
    

**As If — Real vs Unreal**

- He looks as if he **is** tired. → He may actually be tired.
    
- He acts as if he **were** the king. → He is not the king; the comparison is imaginary.
    

**Would Rather — Same vs Different Subject**

- I would rather **stay** home. → Bare infinitive (V1).
    
- I would rather **you stayed** home. → Past Simple for another person's desired action.

## 7. Important SSC CGL Error Detection Rules

|Rule|Incorrect for Intended Meaning|Correct|
|---|---|---|
|First conditional normally uses present tense after _if_.|If he will come, I will help him.|If he comes, I will help him.|
|Second conditional uses would + V1.|If I were rich, I will buy a car.|If I were rich, I would buy a car.|
|Third conditional uses Past Perfect after _if_.|If I would have known, I would have helped.|If I had known, I would have helped.|
|Use _were_ in formal unreal conditions.|If I was you, I would refuse.|If I were you, I would refuse.|
|After _would have_, use V3.|She would have went.|She would have gone.|
|_Wish_ for past regret takes had + V3.|I wish I studied harder last year.|I wish I had studied harder last year.|
|_Unless_ already means _if not_.|Unless you study, you will not fail. (If the intended meaning is a warning about failing.)|Unless you study, you will fail.|

**Important:** These corrections are meaning-dependent. For example, _if + will_ is sometimes grammatical when _will_ expresses willingness: "If you will wait here, I'll call the manager." Negative clauses after _unless_ can also be correct when the meaning requires them.

## 8. Advanced Rule — Conditional Inversion

In formal English, **if** can sometimes be omitted, and the auxiliary verb moves before the subject.

|Normal Conditional|Inverted Conditional|
|---|---|
|If I had known, I would have helped.|**Had I known**, I would have helped.|
|If I were you, I would refuse.|**Were I you**, I would refuse.|
|If you should need help, call me.|**Should you need** help, call me.|

These structures are especially useful for advanced sentence transformation and error-detection questions.

## 9. Quick Revision — Real vs Unreal

|Question|Real Condition|Unreal Condition|
|---|---|---|
|Is the event possible?|Treated as a genuine possibility|Imagined, unlikely, or contrary to reality|
|Present/future verb form|Present Simple|Past Simple / were|
|Future/main result|Will + V1|Would + V1|
|Unreal past condition|—|Had + V3|
|Unreal past result|—|Would have + V3|
|Example|If I study, I will pass.|If I studied, I would pass.|
|Past example|If he was there, he saw it.|If he had been there, he would have seen it.|

### Final Memory Formula

- **Zero:** Present → Present
    
- **First:** Present → Will + V1
    
- **Second:** Past → Would + V1
    
- **Third:** Had + V3 → Would have + V3
    
- **Mixed:** Past and present hypothetical forms combined
    

**Golden Rule:** Do not choose a conditional structure merely from the time mentioned in the sentence. First decide whether the speaker presents the condition as **real, possible, unlikely, or contrary to fact**.
