A **verb** is an action word. After the verb, ask **what?** or **whom?**

If you get an answer → **transitive**  
If you get no answer → **intransitive**

---

## Transitive verb

A **transitive verb** needs an **object**. The action goes from the doer to a receiver.

> She **eats** an apple.  
> He **wrote** a letter.  
> The teacher **called** me.

Ask: eats **what?** → an apple  
So **eats** is transitive.

**Subject + Verb + Object**

Only transitive verbs can normally become **passive**. See [[03. Basic Active to Passive Rule]].

> An apple is eaten by her.

---

## Intransitive verb

An **intransitive verb** has **no object**. The action stays with the doer.

> He **sleeps**.  
> They **arrived**.  
> The baby **cried**.  
> She **went** home.

Ask: sleeps **what?** → no answer  
So **sleeps** is intransitive.

**Subject + Verb**

No normal passive:

> Sleep is slept by him. ❌

Common intransitive verbs:

sleep, arrive, come, go, die, happen, laugh, cry, sit, stand, fall, wait

---

## Some verbs can be both

It depends on whether an object is there.

| Transitive (has object) | Intransitive (no object) |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| He **opened** the door. | The door **opened**. |
| She **sang** a song. | She **sang**. |
| They **changed** the plan. | The weather **changed**. |
| He **runs** a shop. | He **runs** every morning. |

---

## Easy memory

**Transitive = action + receiver**  
**Intransitive = action only, no receiver**

Ask after the verb:

**what? / whom?**  
answer = transitive  
no answer = intransitive

For confusing pairs like **rise/raise, lie/lay, fall/fell**, see [[Confusing Verb]].
