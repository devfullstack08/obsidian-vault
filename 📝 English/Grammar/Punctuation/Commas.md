
A **comma (,)** is used to separate parts of a sentence so the meaning is clear.

The most important uses are:

1. **To separate items in a list**
    

- I bought **apples, bananas, oranges, and grapes**.
    
- She is **smart, hardworking, and honest**.
    

2. **After an introductory word or phrase**
    

- **However,** I disagree.
    
- **In the morning,** we went for a walk.
    
- **After finishing the work,** he went home.
    

3. **Before conjunctions joining two complete sentences**  
    Use a comma before **and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor** when both sides are complete sentences.
    

- I wanted to go, **but** I was tired.
    
- She studied hard, **so** she passed.
    

But not usually:

- She studied hard **and passed**.  
    No comma needed because “passed” is not a separate full sentence.
    

4. **To separate extra information**
    

- My brother, **who lives in Delhi,** is a doctor.
    
- Mr Sharma, **our English teacher,** is absent today.
    

If you remove the extra information, the sentence still makes sense.

5. **When directly addressing someone**
    

- **Rahul,** come here.
    
- Please listen, **sir**.
    
- **Students,** open your books.
    

6. **With yes, no, well, etc.**
    

- **Yes,** I understand.
    
- **No,** I don’t agree.
    
- **Well,** we can try again.
    

7. **In direct speech**
    

- He said, **“I am ready.”**
    
- **“Come here,”** she said.
    

8. **To separate dates and places**
    

- September 2, 2026
    
- Surat, Gujarat, India
    

### Important point

A comma can change the meaning:

- **Let’s eat, Grandma.** ✅  
    = speaking to Grandma
    
- **Let’s eat Grandma.** 😄  
    = sounds like Grandma is the food
    

Easy memory:

**Comma = a small pause that separates ideas and prevents confusion.**
