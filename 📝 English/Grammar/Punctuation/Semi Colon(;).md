
A **semicolon** is stronger than a comma but weaker than a full stop.

Use it mainly in these situations:

1. **To join two closely related complete sentences**
    

- I was tired; I continued working.
    
- She loves reading; her brother prefers sports.
    

Both sides can stand alone as full sentences.

2. **Before words like however, therefore, nevertheless, moreover, consequently** when they connect two complete sentences
    

- I studied hard; **however,** I did not pass.
    
- He was ill; **therefore,** he stayed home.
    

Notice:  
**semicolon + however + comma**

3. **To separate complicated items in a list** when the items already contain commas
    

- We visited **Surat, Gujarat; Mumbai, Maharashtra; and Jaipur, Rajasthan.**
    

Without semicolons, the list could be confusing.

### Semicolon vs comma

❌ I was tired, I continued working.  
This is a **comma splice**.

✅ I was tired; I continued working.  
✅ I was tired, but I continued working.  
✅ I was tired. I continued working.

### Easy memory

**Semicolon = connects two complete, closely related thoughts.**

A useful test is:

> **Sentence 1; Sentence 2**

If both parts can be complete sentences by themselves, a semicolon may be correct.
