
A **hyphen (-)** joins words or parts of words together. It is different from a dash.

The most important uses are:

1. **Compound adjectives before a noun**
    

- a **five-year-old** boy
    
- a **well-known** actor
    
- a **high-quality** product
    
- a **ten-minute** break
    

But usually not after the noun:

- The actor is **well known**.
    
- The boy is **five years old**.
    

2. **Numbers from 21 to 99**
    

- twenty-one
    
- thirty-five
    
- ninety-nine
    

3. **Fractions used as words**
    

- one-third
    
- two-thirds
    
- three-fourths
    

4. **Some prefixes**, especially to avoid confusion
    

- ex-president
    
- self-control
    
- all-inclusive
    
- non-English
    
- re-enter
    

5. **To avoid an awkward or unclear word**
    

- re-cover = cover again
    
- recover = get better
    
- re-sign = sign again
    
- resign = leave a job
    

### Very important rule

When **number + unit** is used before a noun, the unit stays singular:

- a **10-year-old** boy ✅
    
- a **10-years-old** boy ❌
    
- a **five-day** trip ✅
    
- a **five-days** trip ❌
    

But:

- The trip lasted **five days**.
    
- He is **10 years old**.
    

Easy memory:

**Hyphen = joins words that work together as one idea.**
