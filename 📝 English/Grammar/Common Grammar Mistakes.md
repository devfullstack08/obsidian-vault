
### Rules 1. He, she, It  -> s, es

**he, she, it, or a singular noun** -> s, es
**I, You, We. They  plural noun** -> without s, es

| Incorrect            | Correct                |     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | --- |
| He go to school.     | He goes to school.     |     |
| She do not like tea. | She does not like tea. |     |

| Correct                                    |
| ------------------------------------------ |
| I/You/We/They **play** every day.          |
| He/She **plays** every day.                |
| Riya **watches** television every evening. |

### Rules 5. With words like senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, we use to, not than.

Because **senior** already has a comparative meaning: **higher in age, position, rank, or experience**.

| Word | Correct Preposition |
|---|---|
| senior | to |
| junior | to |
| superior | to |
| inferior | to |
| prior | to |

| Incorrect                               | Correct                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| He is senior than me.                   | He is senior to me.                   |
| She is junior than me.                  | She is junior to me.                  |
| This product is superior than that one. | This product is superior to that one. |
| This quality is inferior than that one. | This quality is inferior to that one. |
| This event happened prior than that.    | This event happened prior to that.    |

But with normal comparative adjectives, use “than”

| Word | Correct Sentence |
|---|---|
| older | He is older than me. |
| taller | He is taller than me. |
| smarter | She is smarter than me. |
| better | This is better than that. |

### Rules 6. When used Many, Much and a lot of 

Because **many** is used with **countable plural nouns**.

| Word     | Use with                       | Example                             |
| -------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| many     | countable plural nouns         | many books, many people, many tasks |
| much     | uncountable nouns              | much work, much water, much time    |
| a lot of | countable and uncountable both | a lot of books, a lot of work       |

| Incorrect          | Correct               |
| ------------------ | --------------------- |
| I have many works. | I have a lot of work. |
| I have much works. | I have much work.     |
| I have many work.  | I have many tasks.    |

### Rule 7. have and having.

When **have = own/possess**, we normally do **not** use it in continuous form:

| Meaning of have               | Correct Form | Example                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------- |
| own / possess                 | have         | I have a car.          |
| eat / drink / experience      | having       | I am having lunch.     |
| face / experience a situation | having       | I am having a problem. |

| Incorrect                   | Correct               |
| --------------------------- | --------------------- |
| I am having a car.          | I have a car.         |
| She is having two brothers. | She has two brothers. |
| He is having a house.       | He has a house.       |

| Correct Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I am having lunch. | I am eating lunch. |
| I am having tea. | I am drinking tea. |
| I am having a good time. | I am enjoying. |
| I am having a problem. | I am facing a problem. |

### Rules 8.  Explain something to someone.

Because **explain** does not normally take a person directly as the object.
explain + something + to + someone

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| Can you explain me? | Can you explain it to me? |
| Please explain me this topic. | Please explain this topic to me. |
| He explained me the rule. | He explained the rule to me. |
| Teacher explained us grammar. | The teacher explained grammar to us. |
But this is correct:  Can you explain yourself?

### Rule 11: Facts and Universal Truths

When we write facts, universal truths, scientific facts, habits, or general truths, we usually use the **simple present tense**.

Simple present tense means:

Subject + V1 / V1+s/es

## Examples

The sun rises in the east.
Water boils at 100°C.
The Earth revolves around the Sun.
When a plastic comb is rubbed on dry hair, it acquires a static charge.

### Rules 12: Affect vs. Effect

Affect = verb = to influence (Action)
Effect = noun = result or consequence(End result)

| Incorrect                       | Correct                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| The weather effected the match. | The weather affected the match. |
| The medicine had a good affect. | The medicine had a good effect. |
| This will have no affect on me. | This will have no effect on me. |
| We reached to Delhi.            | We reached Delhi.               |
| They reached at the station.    | They reached the station.       |
