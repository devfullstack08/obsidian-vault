
Common correlative conjunctions are:

- **either … or** — _You can either call me or text me._
- **neither … nor** — _She is neither tired nor hungry._
- **both … and** — _He is both smart and hardworking._
- **not only … but also** — _She is not only kind but also helpful._
- **whether … or** — _I don’t know whether he will come or stay home._
- **rather … than** — _I would rather walk than drive._


### Rule 1. Subject- verb agreement - the verb usually agrees with the subject nearest to the verb.

| Incorrect                                              | Correct                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Neither the teacher nor the students **is** working.   | Neither the teacher nor the students **are** working. |
| Either the players or the captain **are** responsible. | Either the players or the captain **is** responsible. |

Why?

- **students** is nearest to the verb → plural → **are**
- **captain** is nearest to the verb → singular → **is**

So the easy rule is:

**Either A or B + verb → the verb agrees with B, the nearer subject.**
