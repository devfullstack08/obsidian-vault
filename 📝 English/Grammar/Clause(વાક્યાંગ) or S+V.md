A **clause** is a group of words that contains a **subject + verb**.
Example:

> **She sings.**

- Subject → **She**
    
- Verb → **sings**
    
- Therefore, it is a **clause**.
    

Another example:

> **because she was tired**

- Subject → **she**
    
- Verb → **was**
    
- Therefore, it is also a **clause**.
    

---

# Why Clauses Are Important

Clauses are one of the most important parts of English grammar because they help us understand how sentences are formed.

Clauses are useful for:

- Sentence structure
    
- Subject and verb identification
    
- Simple sentences
    
- Compound sentences
    
- Complex sentences
    
- Conjunctions
    
- Relative pronouns
    
- Direct and indirect speech
    
- Active and passive voice
    
- Error detection
    
- Sentence improvement
    
- Sentence transformation
    
- Competitive examinations
    

---

# Clause vs Phrase

The easiest way to differentiate between a **clause** and a **phrase** is to check for a subject and verb.

## Clause

A clause normally contains:

> **Subject + Verb**

Example:

> **when he arrived**

- Subject → **he**
    
- Verb → **arrived**
    

Therefore:

> **when he arrived = Clause**

---

## Phrase

A phrase does not normally contain a complete **subject + verb** combination.

Example:

> **in the morning**

There is:

- no subject
    
- no verb
    

Therefore:

> **in the morning = Phrase**

---

## Easy Rule

> **Clause = Subject + Verb**

> **Phrase = No complete Subject + Verb combination**

---

# Main Types of Clauses

There are two basic categories:

1. **Independent Clause**
    
2. **Dependent Clause**
    

Dependent clauses can further be divided into:

1. Noun Clause
    
2. Adjective Clause
    
3. Adverb Clause
    

---

# 1. Independent Clause

An **independent clause** expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a complete sentence.

Structure:

> **Subject + Verb + Complete Meaning**

Example:

> **I went home.**

- Subject → I
    
- Verb → went
    
- Complete meaning → Yes
    

Therefore:

> **I went home = Independent Clause**

Other examples:

> She is studying.

> Rahul plays cricket.

> They completed the project.

---

# 2. Dependent Clause

A **dependent clause** contains a subject and verb, but it does not express a complete meaning by itself.

It depends on another clause.

Example:

> **because I was tired**

- Subject → I
    
- Verb → was
    
- Complete meaning → No
    

If we only say:

> Because I was tired.

The listener may ask:

> What happened because you were tired?

So we need an independent clause.

Complete sentence:

> **I went home because I was tired.**

Independent Clause:

> I went home.

Dependent Clause:

> because I was tired.

---

# Three Main Types of Dependent Clauses

## 1. Noun Clause

A **noun clause** performs the function of a noun in a sentence.

It can work as:

- Subject
    
- Object
    
- Complement
    

Example:

> **What he said** was true.

Clause:

> What he said

It functions as the **subject** of the sentence.

Another example:

> I know **that he is honest**.

Ask:

> I know **what?**

Answer:

> that he is honest

Therefore:

> **that he is honest = Noun Clause**

---

## Common Noun Clause Words

Noun clauses often begin with:

- that
    
- what
    
- whatever
    
- who
    
- whoever
    
- whom
    
- whose
    
- which
    
- whether
    
- if
    
- why
    
- how
    
- when
    
- where
    

Examples:

> I know **what he wants**.

> She asked **whether I was ready**.

> **Whoever wins the match** will receive the trophy.

---

# 2. Adjective Clause

An **adjective clause** describes or gives additional information about a noun or pronoun.

It works like an adjective.

Example:

> The man **who is standing there** is my uncle.

Main noun:

> the man

Clause:

> who is standing there

The clause describes **the man**.

Therefore:

> **who is standing there = Adjective Clause**

---

## Common Relative Words

Adjective clauses commonly begin with:

- who
    
- whom
    
- whose
    
- which
    
- that
    
- where
    
- when
    

Examples:

> The girl **who won the competition** is my sister.

> This is the book **that I bought yesterday**.

> I visited the village **where my grandfather was born**.

---

## Easy Trick

Ask:

> **Which one? What kind? Which person/thing?**

If the clause describes a noun, it is usually an **adjective clause**.

---

# 3. Adverb Clause

An **adverb clause** gives additional information about:

- time
    
- reason
    
- condition
    
- place
    
- purpose
    
- result
    
- comparison
    
- concession
    

It usually modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb.

Example:

> I stayed home **because it was raining**.

Clause:

> because it was raining

It tells us **why** I stayed home.

Therefore:

> **because it was raining = Adverb Clause of Reason**

---

# Types of Adverb Clauses

## Adverb Clause of Time

Common words:

- when
    
- while
    
- before
    
- after
    
- until
    
- since
    
- as soon as
    

Example:

> Call me **when you arrive**.

---

## Adverb Clause of Reason

Common words:

- because
    
- since
    
- as
    

Example:

> I stayed home **because I was sick**.

---

## Adverb Clause of Condition

Common words:

- if
    
- unless
    
- provided that
    
- as long as
    

Example:

> **If you work hard**, you will succeed.

---

## Adverb Clause of Purpose

Common words:

- so that
    
- in order that
    

Example:

> He studied hard **so that he could pass the exam**.

---

## Adverb Clause of Concession

Common words:

- although
    
- though
    
- even though
    

Example:

> **Although he was tired**, he continued working.

---

## Adverb Clause of Place

Common words:

- where
    
- wherever
    

Example:

> Sit **where you feel comfortable**.

---

## Adverb Clause of Comparison

Common words:

- than
    
- as...as
    

Example:

> She is taller **than I am**.

# How to Identify a Clause

Use these steps.

## Step 1 — Find the Verb

Example:

> when Rahul arrived

Verb:

> arrived

---

## Step 2 — Find Who Performs or Experiences the Verb

Ask:

> Who arrived?

Answer:

> Rahul

So:

- Subject → Rahul
    
- Verb → arrived
    

Therefore:

> **when Rahul arrived = Clause**

---

## Step 3 — Check Whether the Meaning Is Complete

> Rahul arrived.

Complete meaning → Yes

Therefore:

> Independent Clause

But:

> when Rahul arrived

Incomplete meaning → Yes

Therefore:

> Dependent Clause

---

# Exam Tricks

## Trick 1

First search for:

> **Subject + Verb**

If there is no subject-verb combination, it is probably a phrase.

---

## Trick 2

If the clause works like a **noun**, it is a:

> **Noun Clause**

Example:

> I know **what he wants**.

Ask:

> I know what?

Answer:

> what he wants

---

## Trick 3

If the clause describes a **noun**, it is an:

> **Adjective Clause**

Example:

> The man **who helped me** is a doctor.

The clause describes:

> man

---

## Trick 4

If the clause answers:

- When?
    
- Why?
    
- Where?
    
- How?
    
- Under what condition?
    
- For what purpose?
    

It is usually an:

> **Adverb Clause**

Example:

> I stayed inside **because it was raining**.

Ask:

> Why did I stay inside?

Answer:

> because it was raining

Therefore:

> Adverb Clause

# Competitive Exam Example

### Question

Identify the type of clause:

> I know **that he is honest**.

Options:

A. Adjective Clause  
B. Adverb Clause  
C. Noun Clause  
D. Independent Clause

Answer:

> **C. Noun Clause**

Reason:

Ask:

> I know **what?**

Answer:

> that he is honest

The clause acts as the object of **know**.

---

# Another Exam Example

> The boy **who won the race** is my brother.

The clause:

> who won the race

describes:

> the boy

Therefore:

> **Adjective Clause**

---

# Another Exam Example

> I will call you **when I reach home**.

Ask:

> When will I call you?

Answer:

> when I reach home

Therefore:

> **Adverb Clause of Time**

# Important Difference

## Independent Clause

> She went home.

Complete meaning.

## Dependent Clause

> because she was tired

Incomplete meaning.

Together:

> She went home **because she was tired**.

Structure:

> **Independent Clause + Dependent Clause**

---

# Simple Formula

Remember:

> **Clause = Subject + Verb**

Then:

> **Complete meaning = Independent Clause**

> **Incomplete meaning = Dependent Clause**

For dependent clauses:

> **Acts as noun → Noun Clause**

> **Describes noun → Adjective Clause**

> **Tells when/why/how/where/condition → Adverb Clause**
