**Parallelism** means that when two or more words, phrases, or ideas are connected, they should have the **same grammatical form**.

**Check the words before and after “and,” “or,” “but,” “either...or,” 
“not only...but also.” 
If one side is an -ing form, noun, adjective, or infinitive, the other side should usually use the same form.**

This makes a sentence balanced and easy to read.

For example:
- **smart and hardworking** ✅ adjective + adjective
- **reading and writing** ✅ -ing + -ing
- **to eat and to sleep** ✅ infinitive + infinitive
- **a teacher and a writer** ✅ noun + noun

- **reading, dancing, singing** ✅
- **to read, to dance, to sing** ✅
- **read, dance, sing** ✅

But mixing the forms is incorrect:
- **reading, dancing, to sing** ❌

| Incorrect                                                                                      | Correct                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| He likes **swimming, to run, and cycling**.<br>(items in a list)                               | He likes **swimming, running, and cycling**.                                                                                  |
| She wants **to study and getting a job**.<br>(and / or / but)                                  | She wants **to study and to get a job**.<br><br>She wants **studying and getting a job**.                                     |
| You can either **study at home** or **going to the library**.<br>(either...or / neither...nor) | You can either **study at home** or **go to the library**.                                                                    |
| He is not only **intelligent** but also **works hard**.<br>(not only...but also)               | He is not only **intelligent** but also **hardworking**.<br><br><br>He not only **studies hard** but also **works hard**.<br> |
| I like **swimming** more than **to run**.<br>(Comparisons)                                     | I like **swimming** more than **running**.                                                                                    |
