### Difference Between Homonyms, Homophones, and Homographs

|Term|Same Sound?|Same Spelling?|Different Meaning?|
|---|---|---|---|
|Homonyms|Sometimes|Sometimes|Yes|
|Homophones|Yes|Usually no|Yes|
|Homographs|Not always|Yes|Yes|
|Heteronyms|No|Yes|Yes|

**Homonyms**

- Same spelling **and** pronunciation, but different meanings: **bank, bat, bark, match**
- **Homophones** → same sound, different spelling/meaning: **sea/see, right/write**
- **Homographs** → same spelling, different meanings: **lead, tear, bow**
- **Heteronyms** → same spelling but different pronunciation and meaning: **lead /liːd/ vs lead /led/**


### Homonym

Words with the same spelling or pronunciation but different meanings.

Example:

> **bank** = financial institution / side of a river

### Homophone

Words with the same pronunciation but different meanings and usually different spellings.

Example:

> **see / sea**

### Homograph

Words with the same spelling but different meanings.

Example:

> **bat** = animal / sports equipment

### Heteronym

Words with the same spelling but different pronunciations and meanings.

Example:

> **lead** /liːd/ and **lead** /led/


----

Homonym vs Polysemy

This is a more advanced distinction.

### Homonym

Two meanings are considered separate or unrelated.

Example:

**bat**

- flying mammal
    
- sports equipment
    

### Polysemy

One word has several **related meanings**.

Example:

**head**

- head of a person
    
- head of a department
    
- head of a table
    

The meanings are related through the idea of being at the top or in charge.

For everyday grammar study, you usually do not need to separate these strictly, but the distinction is useful in linguistics.
