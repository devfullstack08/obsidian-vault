### The normal order is:
Subject + helping verb + main verb

---
### But after a negative expression at the beginning:
Negative expression + helping verb + subject + main verb
Negative first → auxiliary first

Inversion happens mainly when the negative expression is **moved to the beginning**.

**Normal:** I had never seen such a beautiful place.  
**Inversion:** **Never had I seen** such a beautiful place.

**Normal:** He not only speaks English, but he also speaks French.
**Inversion**: **Not only does he speak** English, but he also speaks French.

**Normal:** He rarely visits us.
**Inversion**: Rarely does he visit us.

**Normal:** She never complained.
**Inversion**: Never did she complain.

| Beginning expression       | Example                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Never**                  | Never **have I seen** this before.                      |
| **Rarely**                 | Rarely **does he visit** us.                            |
| **Seldom**                 | Seldom **do we get** such an opportunity.               |
| **Hardly**                 | Hardly **had I reached** home when it started raining.  |
| **Scarcely**               | Scarcely **had she arrived** when the meeting began.    |
| **No sooner**              | No sooner **had he arrived** than it started raining.   |
| **Not only**               | Not only **did he help** me, but he also gave me money. |
| **Under no circumstances** | Under no circumstances **should you open** this door.   |
| **At no time**             | At no time **did he admit** his mistake.                |
| **Little**                 | Little **did I know** what would happen next.           |

#### What if there is no helping verb?
Then use **do / does / did**, just like a question.

**Normal:** He rarely visits his hometown.
**Inversion**: Rarely does he visit his hometown.

**Normal:** He never told me the truth.
**Inversion**: Never did he tell me the truth.


#### Not only — one special point
When only the first clause starts with **Not only**, invert the first clause, not necessarily the second:

Not only did he pass the exam, but he also topped the class.
