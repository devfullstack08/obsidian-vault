### 1. The Many Deaths of Nora Dalmasso
#### Río Cuarto, Córdoba, Argentina — 2006

- The case concerns the unsolved killing of **Nora Dalmasso**, a 51-year-old woman from a wealthy and socially prominent family in Río Cuarto, Argentina.
- Nora lived with her family in **Villa Golf**, an affluent gated neighbourhood in Río Cuarto.
- Her husband, **Marcelo Macarrón**, was a well-known traumatologist and golfer.
- The case is explored in the documentary series:
  - **The Many Deaths of Nora Dalmasso**
  - It is also commonly discussed under the question: **Who Killed Nora Dalmasso?**

#### Discovery of the body

- Nora was killed on **25 November 2006**.

- On **26 November 2006**, a neighbour found her body in her daughter’s bedroom.

- She was found partially or completely unclothed, with the fabric belt of a bathrobe tied around her neck.

- The autopsy determined that she died from **asphyxiation caused by strangulation**.

- There were no clear signs of forced entry or robbery, leading investigators to believe that she may have known the person who entered the house.

#### Problems with the investigation

- The crime scene was not properly protected.

- Relatives, neighbours, police officers and other people entered the house shortly after the body was discovered.

- Important evidence may have been contaminated or lost.

- Investigators initially failed to take Nora’s fingerprints and later had to exhume her body for further examination.

- The investigation was affected by:
  - Conflicting forensic theories
  - Media sensationalism
  - Leaked crime-scene photographs
  - Political pressure
  - Rumours about Nora’s personal life
  - Frequent changes in investigators and prosecutors

- Much of the media coverage focused on Nora’s alleged relationships rather than on preserving evidence and identifying her killer.
#### Major suspects and accused persons

##### 1. Rafael Magnasco

- Rafael Magnasco was a former Córdoba provincial security adviser.
- Rumours connected him romantically to Nora.
- He voluntarily provided a DNA sample and was briefly treated as a suspect.
- The accusation against him was later dismissed.

##### 2. Guillermo Albarracín

- Guillermo Albarracín was a close friend of Marcelo Macarrón.
- Albarracín admitted that he had previously had an intimate relationship with Nora.
- However, he stated that he was in Uruguay with Marcelo when the killing occurred.
- He was investigated but was not convicted or formally established as Nora’s killer.

##### 3. Gastón Zárate

- Gastón Zárate was a painter who had worked at the family’s home.
- He was arrested in February 2007 after a witness implicated him.
- Local residents protested his arrest in what became known as the **Perejilazo**, arguing that police were attempting to blame a poor worker without sufficient evidence.
- Zárate was released shortly afterwards and was ultimately cleared.

##### 4. Facundo Macarrón

- Facundo Macarrón was Nora’s son.
- In June 2007, prosecutors accused him of aggravated sexual abuse and homicide.
- The accusation relied partly on disputed DNA evidence and questions surrounding his alibi.
- His defence argued that the presence of his DNA in the family home was unsurprising because he lived there.
- The charges against Facundo were dismissed in 2012–2013, and he was cleared.
##### 5. Marcelo Macarrón

- Marcelo Macarrón was Nora’s husband.
- At the time of the killing, he was attending a golf tournament in Punta del Este, Uruguay.
- Prosecutors later alleged that he had hired someone to kill Nora.
- The prosecution suggested possible financial or marital motives.
- Marcelo went on trial in 2022 on a charge involving aggravated homicide allegedly committed through payment or promise of payment.
- In July 2022, a jury acquitted him because the prosecution had not presented sufficient evidence.
- His acquittal meant that no member of Nora’s family had been convicted of her killing.

#### New DNA suspect: Roberto Bárzola

- In December 2024, investigators identified **Roberto Marcos Bárzola** as a new suspect.

- Bárzola had reportedly worked at the Macarrón home shortly before Nora’s death, polishing wooden floors.

- Prosecutors stated that his DNA matched:
  - Genetic material found on the bathrobe belt used to strangle Nora
  - A hair recovered from Nora’s body

- Bárzola was accused of sexual assault followed by homicide.

- However, prosecutors faced a major legal difficulty because the ordinary limitation period for prosecuting the crime may have expired.

- Investigators have continued examining whether the case can proceed under legal obligations connected with investigating violence against women.

- The identification of Bárzola does not by itself constitute a conviction. He must still be treated as an accused or suspected person unless a court establishes guilt.

#### Political and institutional influence

- The case involved powerful families, government officials, police officers and intense political attention.

- Political figures publicly promised that the crime would be solved, while police and judicial officials faced pressure over the lack of progress.

- Several officials resigned or were removed during the investigation.

- Allegations of political interference, class privilege and institutional failure became central themes in public discussion of the case. 

#### Important quotation

> “When politics comes in through the door, justice goes out the window.”

- This quotation expresses the idea that political pressure can interfere with an impartial investigation.

#### Final legal position

- Nora Dalmasso’s husband and son were both accused at different stages but were ultimately cleared.

- Guillermo Albarracín, Rafael Magnasco and Gastón Zárate were also investigated without being convicted.

- Roberto Bárzola was identified as a new suspect in 2024 through DNA evidence.

- Therefore, the safest conclusion is:

> Nora Dalmasso’s murder produced numerous suspects and accusations, but no person has received a final criminal conviction for killing her.

#### Main significance of the case

- The case became one of Argentina’s most controversial criminal investigations because of:
  - Mishandling of the crime scene
  - Weak and changing theories
  - Wrongful or unsupported accusations
  - Political pressure
  - Class divisions
  - Sensational media coverage
  - Victim-blaming focused on Nora’s private life
