### 2. Amy Bradley Is Missing
#### Caribbean Sea — 24 March 1998

- **Amy Lynn Bradley** was a 23-year-old American woman from Virginia, United States.

- She disappeared during a family cruise aboard Royal Caribbean’s ship:
  - **Rhapsody of the Seas**

- The cruise began in:
  - **San Juan, Puerto Rico**
  - on 21 March 1998

- The ship then travelled to Aruba.

- On 23 March, it departed Aruba and sailed toward:
  - **Curaçao, Netherlands Antilles**

- Amy disappeared during the early morning of **24 March 1998**, while the ship was approaching Curaçao. 
#### Before her disappearance

- Amy was travelling with:
  - Her father, Ron Bradley
  - Her mother, Iva Bradley
  - Her brother, Brad Bradley

- On the night of 23 March, Amy and Brad attended a party and danced at the ship’s nightclub.

- Amy was seen dancing with **Alister “Yellow” Douglas**, a musician in the ship’s band.

- Amy and her brother later returned to the family cabin.

- According to the family, Amy’s father saw her resting or sleeping on a chair on the cabin balcony at approximately **5:15–5:30 a.m.**

- At around **6:00 a.m.**, he discovered that she was missing.

- Her cigarettes and lighter were also reportedly gone.

#### Search aboard the ship

- Amy’s family immediately began searching for her.

- They asked the cruise staff to prevent passengers from leaving the ship until Amy was found.

- However, passengers were permitted to disembark after the ship reached Curaçao.

- A search of the ship and surrounding waters failed to find her.

- The Netherlands Antilles Coast Guard conducted an aerial and maritime search, but no confirmed trace of Amy was discovered.

#### Main theories

##### Theory 1: Accidental fall overboard

- One possibility is that Amy accidentally fell from the ship.

- However:
  - She was a trained lifeguard and a strong swimmer.
  - Her family believed she was afraid of open water.
  - No body or confirmed evidence of a fall was recovered.

- An accidental fall remains possible but has never been proven.

##### Theory 2: Suicide

- Some authorities initially considered whether Amy had deliberately gone overboard.

- Her family strongly rejected this theory.

- No confirmed evidence has established that she died by suicide.

##### Theory 3: Abduction or human trafficking

- Amy’s family believes she may have been abducted from the ship and taken ashore.

- This theory is partly based on:
  - Alleged sightings after her disappearance
  - Claims that some crew members paid unusual attention to her
  - The delay in restricting passengers from leaving the ship

- No court or official investigation has proven that she was trafficked.

#### Alister “Yellow” Douglas

- Alister Douglas was a musician who was seen dancing with Amy before she disappeared.

- Some members of Amy’s family considered his behaviour suspicious.

- He was questioned by investigators.

- He denied involvement, and no evidence has established that he was responsible for Amy’s disappearance.

- Therefore, he should not be described as her kidnapper or killer.

#### Reported sightings

Several people later claimed to have seen Amy, but none of these sightings has been conclusively verified.

##### Curaçao taxi-driver report

- A taxi driver claimed that a young woman resembling Amy approached him near the port and asked to use a telephone.

- The report was not independently confirmed.

##### Canadian tourist’s report

- A Canadian tourist later claimed to have seen a distressed woman resembling Amy on a beach in Curaçao.

- He said the woman appeared to be accompanied by two men.

- This sighting was never conclusively verified.

##### U.S. Navy serviceman’s account

- A former U.S. Navy serviceman stated that he encountered a woman in a Curaçao brothel who identified herself as Amy Bradley and said she was being held against her will.

- He did not report the incident until years later.

- Authorities could not conclusively confirm the account.

##### Photograph of a woman called “Jas”

- In 2005, Amy’s family received photographs from an adult-services website showing a woman using the name “Jas.”

- The woman resembled Amy, and some facial experts reportedly considered a connection possible.

- However, the woman’s identity was never conclusively established as Amy.

#### FBI investigation

- The FBI continues to list Amy Lynn Bradley as missing.

- She has several distinguishing tattoos, including:
  - A Tasmanian Devil spinning a basketball
  - A sun tattoo on her lower back
  - A Chinese symbol on her ankle
  - A gecko tattoo near her navel

- The FBI has published age-progressed images to show what Amy might look like today. 
#### Current status

- Amy Bradley has never been found.

- No body has been recovered.

- No person has been charged with causing her disappearance.

- Reported sightings and theories involving an accident, suicide, abduction or trafficking remain unconfirmed.

> The disappearance of Amy Lynn Bradley remains an open and unresolved missing-person case.

### 3. The Many Deaths of Nora Dalmasso
#### Río Cuarto, Córdoba, Argentina — 2006

- The case concerns the unsolved killing of **Nora Dalmasso**, a 51-year-old woman from a wealthy and socially prominent family in Río Cuarto, Argentina.
- Nora lived with her family in **Villa Golf**, an affluent gated neighbourhood in Río Cuarto.
- Her husband, **Marcelo Macarrón**, was a well-known traumatologist and golfer.
- The case is explored in the documentary series:
  - **The Many Deaths of Nora Dalmasso**
  - It is also commonly discussed under the question: **Who Killed Nora Dalmasso?**

#### Discovery of the body

- Nora was killed on **25 November 2006**.

- On **26 November 2006**, a neighbour found her body in her daughter’s bedroom.

- She was found partially or completely unclothed, with the fabric belt of a bathrobe tied around her neck.

- The autopsy determined that she died from **asphyxiation caused by strangulation**.

- There were no clear signs of forced entry or robbery, leading investigators to believe that she may have known the person who entered the house.

#### Problems with the investigation

- The crime scene was not properly protected.

- Relatives, neighbours, police officers and other people entered the house shortly after the body was discovered.

- Important evidence may have been contaminated or lost.

- Investigators initially failed to take Nora’s fingerprints and later had to exhume her body for further examination.

- The investigation was affected by:
  - Conflicting forensic theories
  - Media sensationalism
  - Leaked crime-scene photographs
  - Political pressure
  - Rumours about Nora’s personal life
  - Frequent changes in investigators and prosecutors

- Much of the media coverage focused on Nora’s alleged relationships rather than on preserving evidence and identifying her killer.
#### Major suspects and accused persons

##### 1. Rafael Magnasco

- Rafael Magnasco was a former Córdoba provincial security adviser.
- Rumours connected him romantically to Nora.
- He voluntarily provided a DNA sample and was briefly treated as a suspect.
- The accusation against him was later dismissed.

##### 2. Guillermo Albarracín

- Guillermo Albarracín was a close friend of Marcelo Macarrón.
- Albarracín admitted that he had previously had an intimate relationship with Nora.
- However, he stated that he was in Uruguay with Marcelo when the killing occurred.
- He was investigated but was not convicted or formally established as Nora’s killer.

##### 3. Gastón Zárate

- Gastón Zárate was a painter who had worked at the family’s home.
- He was arrested in February 2007 after a witness implicated him.
- Local residents protested his arrest in what became known as the **Perejilazo**, arguing that police were attempting to blame a poor worker without sufficient evidence.
- Zárate was released shortly afterwards and was ultimately cleared.

##### 4. Facundo Macarrón

- Facundo Macarrón was Nora’s son.
- In June 2007, prosecutors accused him of aggravated sexual abuse and homicide.
- The accusation relied partly on disputed DNA evidence and questions surrounding his alibi.
- His defence argued that the presence of his DNA in the family home was unsurprising because he lived there.
- The charges against Facundo were dismissed in 2012–2013, and he was cleared.
##### 5. Marcelo Macarrón

- Marcelo Macarrón was Nora’s husband.
- At the time of the killing, he was attending a golf tournament in Punta del Este, Uruguay.
- Prosecutors later alleged that he had hired someone to kill Nora.
- The prosecution suggested possible financial or marital motives.
- Marcelo went on trial in 2022 on a charge involving aggravated homicide allegedly committed through payment or promise of payment.
- In July 2022, a jury acquitted him because the prosecution had not presented sufficient evidence.
- His acquittal meant that no member of Nora’s family had been convicted of her killing.

#### New DNA suspect: Roberto Bárzola

- In December 2024, investigators identified **Roberto Marcos Bárzola** as a new suspect.

- Bárzola had reportedly worked at the Macarrón home shortly before Nora’s death, polishing wooden floors.

- Prosecutors stated that his DNA matched:
  - Genetic material found on the bathrobe belt used to strangle Nora
  - A hair recovered from Nora’s body

- Bárzola was accused of sexual assault followed by homicide.

- However, prosecutors faced a major legal difficulty because the ordinary limitation period for prosecuting the crime may have expired.

- Investigators have continued examining whether the case can proceed under legal obligations connected with investigating violence against women.

- The identification of Bárzola does not by itself constitute a conviction. He must still be treated as an accused or suspected person unless a court establishes guilt.

#### Political and institutional influence

- The case involved powerful families, government officials, police officers and intense political attention.

- Political figures publicly promised that the crime would be solved, while police and judicial officials faced pressure over the lack of progress.

- Several officials resigned or were removed during the investigation.

- Allegations of political interference, class privilege and institutional failure became central themes in public discussion of the case. 

#### Important quotation

> “When politics comes in through the door, justice goes out the window.”

- This quotation expresses the idea that political pressure can interfere with an impartial investigation.

#### Final legal position

- Nora Dalmasso’s husband and son were both accused at different stages but were ultimately cleared.

- Guillermo Albarracín, Rafael Magnasco and Gastón Zárate were also investigated without being convicted.

- Roberto Bárzola was identified as a new suspect in 2024 through DNA evidence.

- Therefore, the safest conclusion is:

> Nora Dalmasso’s murder produced numerous suspects and accusations, but no person has received a final criminal conviction for killing her.

#### Main significance of the case

- The case became one of Argentina’s most controversial criminal investigations because of:
  - Mishandling of the crime scene
  - Weak and changing theories
  - Wrongful or unsupported accusations
  - Political pressure
  - Class divisions
  - Sensational media coverage
  - Victim-blaming focused on Nora’s private life
  
### 4. The Disappearance of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH370
#### 8 March 2014

- Malaysia Airlines Flight **MH370** was a Boeing 777 travelling from:
  - **Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia**
  - to **Beijing, China**

- The aircraft took off from Kuala Lumpur shortly after **12:40 a.m. local time** on 8 March 2014.

- There were **239 people on board**:
  - 227 passengers
  - 12 crew members

- The aircraft disappeared from civilian air-traffic-control radar after its final communication with Malaysian controllers.

- Its last recorded radio message was:

> “Good night, Malaysian Three Seven Zero.”

- The aircraft’s transponder stopped transmitting shortly afterwards.

- Military radar later indicated that the aircraft had turned away from its planned route, crossed the Malay Peninsula and continued northwest over the Strait of Malacca.

- Satellite communication data suggested that the aircraft remained airborne for several more hours and probably travelled south towards the **southern Indian Ocean**. 

#### What probably happened

- Investigators believe the aircraft eventually ran out of fuel and entered the Indian Ocean.

- However, the main wreckage, flight-data recorder and cockpit-voice recorder have not been recovered.

- Because the aircraft has not been found, investigators have not been able to determine:
  - The exact crash location
  - The exact cause of the disappearance
  - Who controlled the aircraft after it changed course
  - Whether the diversion was intentional
  - What happened inside the cabin and cockpit

#### Debris

- Several pieces of aircraft debris were later found on islands and coastlines in the western Indian Ocean.

- A wing component called a **flaperon**, found on Réunion Island in 2015, was confirmed as coming from MH370.

- Other recovered pieces were classified as confirmed, almost certain or highly likely to have come from the aircraft.

- The debris supports the conclusion that MH370 ended in the Indian Ocean, but it does not prove why the aircraft disappeared. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

#### Main theories

##### Theory 1: Deliberate action by the pilot

- One theory suggests that Captain **Zaharie Ahmad Shah** deliberately diverted the aircraft.

- Supporters of this theory point to:
  - The aircraft’s controlled change of direction
  - The disabling or loss of communication systems
  - The route across the Malay Peninsula
  - Flight-simulator data recovered from the captain’s home computer

- However, investigators did not establish a clear motive.

- The official investigation did not prove that the captain deliberately caused the disappearance.

- Therefore, write:

> Pilot involvement is considered a possible theory, but it has not been proven.

- Do not write:

> The pilot did it.

##### Theory 2: Hijacking

- Another theory is that one or more passengers or crew members hijacked the aircraft.

- Investigators examined the passengers’ identities and backgrounds.

- Two passengers were travelling on stolen passports, but investigators found no evidence linking them to terrorism.

- No terrorist organisation produced credible evidence proving responsibility for MH370.

- There is no confirmed evidence that passengers or outside hijackers took control of the aircraft.

##### Theory 3: Military interception or accidental shoot-down

- Some theories claim that MH370 may have been intercepted or accidentally shot down by military forces.

- Variations of this theory suggest that the aircraft:
  - Entered restricted airspace
  - Was carrying sensitive cargo
  - Was mistaken for a threat
  - Was secretly intercepted

- No verified radar, weapons, wreckage or official evidence has established that MH370 was shot down.

- This remains a speculative theory and should not be presented as fact.

##### Other theories

Other proposed explanations include:

- An onboard fire
- Electrical failure
- Sudden decompression
- Cabin hypoxia, causing everyone to become unconscious
- A struggle inside the cockpit
- Mechanical failure
- A combination of technical failure and human intervention

None of these explanations has been conclusively proved.

#### Search operation

- Malaysia, Australia and China coordinated a major search of the southern Indian Ocean.

- The initial underwater search continued until January 2017.

- Ocean Infinity conducted another search in 2018 without finding the main wreckage.

- Further search operations were undertaken in 2025–2026, but they also did not locate the aircraft.

- MH370 therefore remains missing, although searches and scientific analyses have greatly narrowed the most likely region.

#### Connection with Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17

- Approximately four months after MH370 disappeared, another Malaysia Airlines Boeing 777 was destroyed.

- Malaysia Airlines Flight **MH17** was travelling from:
  - Amsterdam, Netherlands
  - to Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

- On **17 July 2014**, MH17 was shot down over eastern Ukraine.

- All **298 people on board** were killed:
  - 283 passengers
  - 15 crew members

- Therefore, write **298 people**, not “around 300.”

- MH17 and MH370 were separate events:
  - MH370 disappeared for an unknown reason.
  - MH17 was shot down by a surface-to-air missile during the conflict in eastern Ukraine. 

#### Final conclusion

- MH370 disappeared after changing course and continuing to fly for several hours.

- Satellite evidence indicates that it probably ended in the southern Indian Ocean.

- Some debris has been recovered, but the main wreckage and flight recorders remain missing.

- Theories involving the pilot, hijacking, mechanical failure, fire, hypoxia and military interception have been proposed.

- None of these theories has been conclusively proven.

> The disappearance of MH370 remains one of the greatest mysteries in modern aviation history.

### 5. The 2012 Berlin Darkroom Murders
#### Berlin, Germany

- The **2012 Berlin Darkroom Murders** were a series of poisonings committed against men in Berlin’s gay community.

- The case is also known as:
  - The **Darkroom Killer case**
  - The **Berlin Nightlife Killer case**

- The offender was publicly identified as **Dirk P.**, following German privacy conventions that often abbreviate surnames.

- Dirk P. was a former nurse and, at the time of the crimes, was training to become a primary-school teacher.

#### Crimes

- Between **26 April and 15 May 2012**, Dirk P. poisoned four men.

- He secretly gave his victims excessive quantities of **GHB**, also known as:
  - Liquid ecstasy
  - K.O. drops
  - A date-rape drug

- Three victims died, while a fourth man survived after receiving medical assistance.

- The three fatal attacks occurred in different places:
  1. One victim was killed in his apartment.
  2. One victim was killed in the darkroom of a gay bar in Berlin-Friedrichshain.
  3. Another victim was killed in his apartment.

- Therefore, it is not completely accurate to say that all three were “darkroom murders.” The name came from the killing that occurred in the darkroom.

- Dirk P. robbed the victims after poisoning them, taking items such as money, mobile phones and bank cards.

- His total financial gain was reportedly less than **€500**.

#### Investigation and Arrest

- Police initially had difficulty connecting the deaths because some appeared to be accidental drug overdoses.

- The crimes were linked after investigators recognised similarities in the victims’ deaths and robberies.

- Dirk P. used one victim’s stolen bank card to purchase a train ticket.

- CCTV footage and the stolen-card transaction helped investigators identify him.

- He was arrested on **26 May 2012**.

#### Trial and Sentence

- Dirk P. admitted administering GHB to the victims but claimed that he had not intended to kill them.

- The court rejected his explanation.

- On **28 June 2013**, the Berlin Regional Court convicted him of:
  - Three counts of murder
  - One count of attempted murder

- He was sentenced to **life imprisonment**.

- The court also determined that there was a **particular severity of guilt**.

- This meant that an ordinary release on parole after 15 years would not apply. He was expected to serve at least about 25 years.

- The court regarded **greed** as the main legal motive because Dirk P. robbed the victims, although the judge also stated that he appeared to enjoy exercising power over them. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

#### Death

- Dirk P. was considered at high risk of suicide while in custody.

- On **28 March 2014**, he died by suicide in the prison hospital at Berlin-Plötzensee.

- His death occurred approximately **nine months after he received the life sentence**.

- He was 39 years old at the time of his death. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

#### Final Outcome

- Dirk P. murdered three men and attempted to murder a fourth by secretly poisoning them with GHB.

- His crimes became known internationally as the **Berlin Darkroom Murders** or the **Nightlife Killer case**.

- In 2024, the case became the subject of the documentary series **Crime Scene Berlin: Nightlife Killer**.

### 6. The Sheena Bora Case
#### Mumbai, India — 2012

- The case concerns the disappearance and alleged murder of **Sheena Bora**, a 25-year-old woman living and working in Mumbai.

- Sheena was last seen on **24 April 2012** in Mumbai.

- At that time, Sheena was publicly introduced as the younger sister of media executive **Indrani Mukerjea**.

- It was later revealed that Sheena was actually **Indrani Mukerjea’s daughter**, not her sister.

- The case became widely known through the Netflix documentary series:
  - **The Indrani Mukerjea Story: Buried Truth**. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

#### Indrani Mukerjea

- Indrani Mukerjea was a media executive and a co-founder of **INX Media**.

- Her husband at the time was media executive **Peter Mukerjea**.

- Indrani was arrested by Mumbai Police on **25 August 2015**, more than three years after Sheena disappeared.

- She was accused of involvement in Sheena’s abduction, murder and the disposal of her body.

- Indrani has denied murdering Sheena, and the criminal trial remains ongoing. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

#### Sheena’s disappearance

- According to the prosecution, Sheena was allegedly abducted and killed on 24 April 2012.

- The prosecution alleges that she was strangled inside a vehicle.

- Her body was allegedly taken to a forested area in Raigad district, where it was burned and disposed of.

- After Sheena disappeared, messages were reportedly sent from her phone suggesting that she had ended her relationship with Rahul and moved abroad.

- No immediate missing-person complaint was registered, despite Rahul repeatedly questioning her disappearance.

#### Main accused

The principal accused in the case included:

1. **Indrani Mukerjea**
   - Sheena’s biological mother.

2. **Sanjeev Khanna**
   - Indrani’s former husband.
   - He is the father of Indrani’s younger daughter, **Vidhie Mukerjea**.
   - He was accused of participating in the alleged abduction and killing.

3. **Shyamvar Rai**
   - Indrani’s former driver.
   - His arrest in an unrelated weapons case in August 2015 led police to reopen the Sheena Bora case.
   - He later became an approver and testified for the prosecution.

4. **Peter Mukerjea**
   - Indrani’s then-husband and Sheena’s stepfather.
   - He was arrested in November 2015 and accused of participating in the alleged conspiracy.
   - He has denied involvement. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

#### Family relationships

- **Sheena Bora** and **Mikhail Bora** were Indrani Mukerjea’s children.

- They were raised by Indrani’s parents and were publicly presented as Indrani’s younger siblings.

- **Siddhartha Das** stated that he was the biological father of Sheena and Mikhail and testified as Sheena’s father in court. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

- Indrani later disputed that account and made a different claim regarding Sheena’s conception. That claim is **Indrani’s personal allegation and has not been judicially established as fact**.

- Indrani and Sanjeev Khanna had a daughter named **Vidhie Mukerjea**.

- Peter Mukerjea had two sons from his earlier marriage:
  1. Rahul Mukerjea
  2. Rabin Mukerjea

#### Relationship between Sheena and Rahul

- Sheena was in a romantic relationship with **Rahul Mukerjea**, Peter Mukerjea’s son from an earlier marriage.

- They were not biologically related.

- Because Indrani had publicly introduced Sheena as her sister, Sheena appeared socially to be Rahul’s step-aunt, although she was actually his stepsister through their parents’ marriage.

- Rahul told investigators that he and Sheena planned to marry.

- The prosecution alleged that Indrani disapproved of their relationship and feared that her concealed relationship with Sheena would become public. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

#### Discovery of the case

- In August 2015, police arrested driver Shyamvar Rai in an unrelated case involving an illegal weapon.

- During questioning, Rai allegedly revealed information about Sheena’s killing.

- Police then arrested Indrani Mukerjea and Sanjeev Khanna.

- Peter Mukerjea was arrested later in November 2015.

- Investigators recovered remains from Raigad that the prosecution identified as belonging to Sheena.

#### Charges

- The accused faced charges including:
  - Murder
  - Kidnapping
  - Criminal conspiracy
  - Destruction of evidence
  - Giving false information

- Indrani and Sanjeev were also charged with allegedly conspiring to kill Mikhail Bora, although Mikhail survived. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

#### Bail

- In May 2022, the Supreme Court granted bail to Indrani Mukerjea after she had spent nearly seven years in custody.

- Bail did **not** mean that she was found innocent.

- It meant that she could remain outside prison while the trial continued, subject to court conditions.

- As of 2026, she remained an accused person and the trial was still continuing. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

#### Important legal position

- Indrani Mukerjea, Sanjeev Khanna and Peter Mukerjea have been accused in the case.

- They should not be described as convicted murderers unless a court delivers a final conviction.

- Therefore, the legally correct wording is:

> Indrani Mukerjea is accused of conspiring to abduct and murder her daughter, Sheena Bora.

- Do not write:

> Indrani Mukerjea killed her daughter.
   
### 7. The Disappearance of Lucie Blackman  
#### Tokyo, Japan

- Lucie Blackman was a **21-year-old British woman** and former British Airways flight attendant.
- She moved to Tokyo in May 2000 and worked as a hostess at the Casablanca nightclub in the Roppongi district.
- On **1 July 2000**, Lucie went out with a customer and disappeared.
- Her family travelled to Japan and launched a major public campaign to find her.
- Police later focused their investigation on **Joji Obara**, a wealthy Japanese businessman and property developer.
- During searches connected to Obara, investigators discovered hundreds of videotapes showing him sexually assaulting unconscious or heavily drugged women.
- The recordings and other evidence indicated that Obara had repeatedly drugged and sexually assaulted women over several years.
- Lucie’s dismembered remains were discovered on **9 February 2001** in a seaside cave near one of Obara’s properties.
- Because her remains were badly decomposed, investigators could not conclusively establish the exact cause of her death.

#### Charges against Joji Obara

- Obara was prosecuted in connection with:
  1. The abduction and death of Lucie Blackman
  2. The rape and death of Australian woman **Carita Ridgway**
  3. The rape of eight other women

#### 2007 District Court Judgment

- On **24 April 2007**, the Tokyo District Court convicted Obara of:
  - Raping eight women
  - Raping and causing the death of Carita Ridgway

- He was sentenced to **life imprisonment**.

- However, the court acquitted him of all charges relating to Lucie Blackman because it found insufficient direct forensic evidence proving that he had drugged, raped or caused her death.

#### Appeal

- Prosecutors appealed the acquittal relating to Lucie Blackman in 2007.

- The appeal trial began before the Tokyo High Court on **25 March 2008**.

- On **16 December 2008**, the Tokyo High Court found Obara guilty of:
  - Abducting Lucie Blackman
  - Dismembering her body
  - Illegally disposing of and abandoning her remains

- However, the court still did not convict him of causing Lucie’s death or raping her because the available evidence could not conclusively prove those charges.

- The High Court upheld his existing sentence of **life imprisonment**.

#### Final Outcome

- Joji Obara remained sentenced to life imprisonment for the rape of several women, the death of Carita Ridgway, and offences involving the abduction and disposal of Lucie Blackman’s body.

- The case became internationally known because of the seriousness of the crimes, the large number of suspected victims and criticism of the initial police investigation.


### 8. House of Secrets: The Burari Deaths

- On **1 July 2018**, 11 members of the same family were found dead in their home in Sant Nagar, Burari, Delhi.
- The victims included **seven women and four males**, including two teenage boys.
- Ten were found hanging, while the elderly grandmother was found dead in a separate room.
- Police discovered diaries containing spiritual instructions that closely matched the condition in which the bodies were found.
- Lalit Chundawat reportedly believed that the spirit of his deceased father communicated through him.
- The family appeared to believe that the father’s spirit would save them during the ritual.
- Investigators found no convincing evidence of an outside killer.
- The deaths were officially treated as a ritualistic mass suicide associated with shared delusional beliefs, although the family may not have expected to die.

### 9. Elisa and Marcela
#### A Real Historical Love Story — Spain, 1901

- **Elisa Sánchez Loriga** and **Marcela Gracia Ibeas** were two Spanish women who worked as teachers in Galicia, Spain.

- They met while studying to become teachers and later developed a romantic relationship.

- At that time, marriage between two women was not legally permitted.

#### Their plan to marry

- Elisa adopted a male identity and presented herself as a man named:

  - **Mario Sánchez**

- She cut her hair, wore men’s clothing and created a false personal history.

- Elisa, as “Mario,” was baptised by a Catholic priest.

- On **8 June 1901**, Elisa and Marcela were married in the Church of Saint George in:

  - **A Coruña, Galicia, Spain**

- Their marriage is considered the first documented same-sex marriage in Spain.

#### Discovery of their identity

- After the wedding, people became suspicious of “Mario.”

- Newspapers exposed that Mario was actually Elisa.

- The case became known in the press as:

> “A marriage without a man.”

- Elisa and Marcela faced public humiliation, social hostility and legal action.

#### Escape to Portugal

- The couple fled from Spain to **Porto, Portugal**.

- They were arrested there after Spanish authorities requested their extradition.

- A Portuguese court later released them, and they avoided being sent back to Spain.

- Marcela gave birth to a daughter named **Enriqueta** during this period.

#### Life in Argentina

- In **1902**, Elisa, Marcela and Marcela’s daughter travelled to Argentina.

- They changed their names:

  - Elisa used the name **María Sánchez**
  - Marcela used the name **Carmen Noya**

- They settled in or around Buenos Aires Province.

- Elisa later married a Danish immigrant named **Christian Jensen**, apparently as part of an effort to secure financial and social stability.

- Jensen later became suspicious of Elisa’s identity and tried to have the marriage annulled.

- Historical research indicates that Elisa and Marcela continued living together in Argentina for a significant period. 

#### Was their marriage annulled(રદ કરાયેલ)?

- The 1901 marriage certificate of Elisa and Marcela was reportedly never formally annulled.

- However, the marriage occurred through Elisa’s false male identity and was not legally recognised as a same-sex marriage under the law of that period.

- It remains an important historical precedent in the history of LGBTQ+ rights in Spain. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

#### Elisa & Marcela film

- Their story inspired the Spanish film:

  - **Elisa & Marcela**

- The film was released in **2019** and was directed by **Isabel Coixet**.

- It is based on real historical events, although some scenes and details were dramatised.

#### Final conclusion

- Elisa and Marcela were two Spanish teachers who challenged the social and legal restrictions of their time.

- Elisa assumed the identity of a man so that they could marry in a Catholic church in 1901.

- After their identities were exposed, they fled to Portugal and later moved to Argentina.

> Their story is remembered as one of the earliest documented attempts by two women to marry in modern European history.
