
### 1. The Sheena Bora Case
#### Mumbai, India — 2012

- The case concerns the disappearance and alleged murder of **Sheena Bora**, a 25-year-old woman living and working in Mumbai.

- Sheena was last seen on **24 April 2012** in Mumbai.

- At that time, Sheena was publicly introduced as the younger sister of media executive **Indrani Mukerjea**.

- It was later revealed that Sheena was actually **Indrani Mukerjea’s daughter**, not her sister.

- The case became widely known through the Netflix documentary series:
  - **The Indrani Mukerjea Story: Buried Truth**. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

#### Indrani Mukerjea

- Indrani Mukerjea was a media executive and a co-founder of **INX Media**.

- Her husband at the time was media executive **Peter Mukerjea**.

- Indrani was arrested by Mumbai Police on **25 August 2015**, more than three years after Sheena disappeared.

- She was accused of involvement in Sheena’s abduction, murder and the disposal of her body.

- Indrani has denied murdering Sheena, and the criminal trial remains ongoing. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

#### Sheena’s disappearance

- According to the prosecution, Sheena was allegedly abducted and killed on 24 April 2012.

- The prosecution alleges that she was strangled inside a vehicle.

- Her body was allegedly taken to a forested area in Raigad district, where it was burned and disposed of.

- After Sheena disappeared, messages were reportedly sent from her phone suggesting that she had ended her relationship with Rahul and moved abroad.

- No immediate missing-person complaint was registered, despite Rahul repeatedly questioning her disappearance.

#### Main accused

The principal accused in the case included:

1. **Indrani Mukerjea**
   - Sheena’s biological mother.

2. **Sanjeev Khanna**
   - Indrani’s former husband.
   - He is the father of Indrani’s younger daughter, **Vidhie Mukerjea**.
   - He was accused of participating in the alleged abduction and killing.

3. **Shyamvar Rai**
   - Indrani’s former driver.
   - His arrest in an unrelated weapons case in August 2015 led police to reopen the Sheena Bora case.
   - He later became an approver and testified for the prosecution.

4. **Peter Mukerjea**
   - Indrani’s then-husband and Sheena’s stepfather.
   - He was arrested in November 2015 and accused of participating in the alleged conspiracy.
   - He has denied involvement. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

#### Family relationships

- **Sheena Bora** and **Mikhail Bora** were Indrani Mukerjea’s children.

- They were raised by Indrani’s parents and were publicly presented as Indrani’s younger siblings.

- **Siddhartha Das** stated that he was the biological father of Sheena and Mikhail and testified as Sheena’s father in court. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

- Indrani later disputed that account and made a different claim regarding Sheena’s conception. That claim is **Indrani’s personal allegation and has not been judicially established as fact**.

- Indrani and Sanjeev Khanna had a daughter named **Vidhie Mukerjea**.

- Peter Mukerjea had two sons from his earlier marriage:
  1. Rahul Mukerjea
  2. Rabin Mukerjea

#### Relationship between Sheena and Rahul

- Sheena was in a romantic relationship with **Rahul Mukerjea**, Peter Mukerjea’s son from an earlier marriage.

- They were not biologically related.

- Because Indrani had publicly introduced Sheena as her sister, Sheena appeared socially to be Rahul’s step-aunt, although she was actually his stepsister through their parents’ marriage.

- Rahul told investigators that he and Sheena planned to marry.

- The prosecution alleged that Indrani disapproved of their relationship and feared that her concealed relationship with Sheena would become public. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

#### Discovery of the case

- In August 2015, police arrested driver Shyamvar Rai in an unrelated case involving an illegal weapon.

- During questioning, Rai allegedly revealed information about Sheena’s killing.

- Police then arrested Indrani Mukerjea and Sanjeev Khanna.

- Peter Mukerjea was arrested later in November 2015.

- Investigators recovered remains from Raigad that the prosecution identified as belonging to Sheena.

#### Charges

- The accused faced charges including:
  - Murder
  - Kidnapping
  - Criminal conspiracy
  - Destruction of evidence
  - Giving false information

- Indrani and Sanjeev were also charged with allegedly conspiring to kill Mikhail Bora, although Mikhail survived. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

#### Bail

- In May 2022, the Supreme Court granted bail to Indrani Mukerjea after she had spent nearly seven years in custody.

- Bail did **not** mean that she was found innocent.

- It meant that she could remain outside prison while the trial continued, subject to court conditions.

- As of 2026, she remained an accused person and the trial was still continuing. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

#### Important legal position

- Indrani Mukerjea, Sanjeev Khanna and Peter Mukerjea have been accused in the case.

- They should not be described as convicted murderers unless a court delivers a final conviction.

- Therefore, the legally correct wording is:

> Indrani Mukerjea is accused of conspiring to abduct and murder her daughter, Sheena Bora.

- Do not write:

> Indrani Mukerjea killed her daughter.

### 2. House of Secrets: The Burari Deaths

- On **1 July 2018**, 11 members of the same family were found dead in their home in Sant Nagar, Burari, Delhi.
- The victims included **seven women and four males**, including two teenage boys.
- Ten were found hanging, while the elderly grandmother was found dead in a separate room.
- Police discovered diaries containing spiritual instructions that closely matched the condition in which the bodies were found.
- Lalit Chundawat reportedly believed that the spirit of his deceased father communicated through him.
- The family appeared to believe that the father’s spirit would save them during the ritual.
- Investigators found no convincing evidence of an outside killer.
- The deaths were officially treated as a ritualistic mass suicide associated with shared delusional beliefs, although the family may not have expected to die.


### 3. Curry and cyanide / Koodathayi / Jolly Joseph case(2002-2016)

The case concerns **six deaths in the Ponnamattam family in Koodathayi, Kozhikode, Kerala, between 2002 and 2016**. Jolly Joseph was arrested in 2019 and became the prime accused. Police alleged that cyanide was used in several of the deaths.

1. **Annamma Thomas — 2002** (Jolly's mother-in-law. Her death was initially treated as a natural/medical death and there was no post-mortem examination at the time.)
2. **Tom Thomas — 2008**(Jolly's father-in-law. His death also did not initially trigger a homicide investigation.)
3. **Roy Thomas — 2011**(Jolly's first husband. This death became especially important because a post-mortem and viscera examination detected cyanide. A forensic expert later testified in court that Roy's death was due to cyanide poisoning.)
4. **Mathew Manjadiyil — 2014**(A relative who had reportedly raised questions after Roy's death. His death was later included among the alleged killings.)
5. **Alphine Shaju — 2014** (Shaju's young daughter. Her death was initially believed to have resulted from choking. It was later investigated as part of the same series.)
6.  **Sily Shaju — 2016**(Shaju's wife. Her death was also later investigated as an alleged poisoning.)

Jolly later married **Shaju in 2017**.

#### About the NIT story

Jolly reportedly told relatives and others that she worked as a lecturer/professor at **NIT Calicut**, but NIT officials later said she had **never been employed there, either permanently or temporarily**. Reports also described her presenting herself publicly as an NIT lecturer.

#### How did the investigation actually start?
It wasn't simply that the siblings suddenly decided in 2017 that all six deaths were murders.

Suspicion grew around **Roy Thomas's death and a property/will dispute**. Roy's brother **Rojo Thomas** pursued information and complained to police about an allegedly forged will connected with family property. Police eventually began a detailed investigation, exhumed remains, and arrested Jolly and others in October 2019.
#### Cyanide evidence: this part needs careful wording
The strongest direct forensic evidence concerns **Roy Thomas**.

Cyanide was detected during the investigation of Roy's death, and in 2025 a forensic expert testified that the post-mortem and viscera findings established cyanide poisoning as the cause of Roy's death.

For four exhumed victims — **Annamma, Tom, Mathew and Alphine** — later testing at the Central Forensic Science Laboratory found **no detectable cyanide**.

#### M. S. Mathew and cyanide procurement(પ્રાપ્તિ)

Police alleged that **M. S. Mathew**, who worked in connection with a jewellery business, helped obtain cyanide for Jolly, and that **Praji Kumar**, a goldsmith, was involved further along the alleged procurement chain. Both became accused in parts of the case.

A serial killer does not have to possess exactly the same motive for every victim. The term generally concerns **multiple killings occurring as separate events over time**, rather than requiring one identical motive throughout.

However, there is another legal reason to be cautious: **Jolly Joseph has not yet been finally convicted of all six murders**.

The media and investigators commonly refer to the Koodathayi matter as a serial-murder case, but Jolly Joseph remains an accused while the criminal trials continue.
#### What was the alleged motive?

This is actually one of the most interesting parts of the case.

It is probably too simplistic to reduce the prosecution theory to only:

> money

or only:

> property

or only:

> relationship.

Different alleged deaths appear to fit different possible objectives.

The prosecution has alleged a significant **property motive**, including an allegedly forged will concerning the Ponnamattam family's property. Charges in Roy Thomas's case included murder, conspiracy and document forgery.

But the overall prosecution narrative has also involved alleged attempts to remove people perceived as obstacles, establish control within the family, and eventually create circumstances in which Jolly could marry Shaju.

That does **not mean psychology has established a single proven motive**. Motive is something the prosecution tries to infer from evidence; the court must decide what has actually been proved.

The prosecution theory appears to involve a combination of property/control and personal relationship objectives rather than one simple motive, but the final judicial findings have not yet been made.

#### The alleged forged will is very important

It became an important evidentiary and investigative thread. In June **2026**, the Kerala High Court allowed the prosecution to examine an expert who had given an opinion regarding documents including the **allegedly forged will**. This confirms that the will/property evidence remains relevant to the ongoing proceedings.

By early 2023 charges had been framed/read in the **Roy Thomas murder case**, and the Kerala High Court on **6 March 2023** refused to interfere with the trial court's dismissal of Jolly's discharge petition. That date is associated with an important High Court order, not simply the beginning date of the entire six-case trial.

The cases are separate criminal prosecutions connected to the six deaths.

The proceedings have continued for years. In July 2025, forensic testimony regarding Roy Thomas was still being recorded.
And in June **2026**, the Kerala High Court was still deciding procedural/evidentiary questions connected to the case.

So as of **August 2026**, the safe summary is:
The Koodathayi prosecutions remain ongoing; there has not yet been a final judgment establishing Jolly Joseph's guilt in all six alleged murders.

#### Why this case is important from a criminal-investigation perspective

This case is less interesting because of cyanide itself and more interesting because of the **investigative failure-and-reconstruction problem**.

Several deaths occurred years apart.

Each death, looked at individually, could initially appear explainable.

There were limited post-mortems.

Physical evidence degraded over time.

Family relationships created natural trust.

Then a later inconsistency — particularly surrounding Roy's death and the property/will dispute — caused investigators to look backward.

The case therefore demonstrates an important principle:

> **A pattern may become visible only when previously isolated events are examined together.**

But investigators also have to avoid the opposite danger: once they suspect a pattern, they must not automatically assume that **every earlier death must have been murder**. That is why forensic evidence, witness testimony, documentary evidence and circumstantial evidence all matter.
