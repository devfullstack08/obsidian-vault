### Subjunctive “were”

When we talk about an **imaginary, unreal, impossible, or hypothetical situation in the present**, we often use **were** after **if**, even with:

**I / he / she / it**

Examples:

> If I **were** rich, I would travel the world.  
> If he **were** here, he would help us.  
> If she **were** the manager, things would be different.  
> If it **were** possible, I would do it.

Normally we say:

> I **was** tired.  
> He **was** late.  
> She **was** angry.

But in an unreal condition:

> If I **were** you... ✅  
> If he **were** the king... ✅  
> If she **were** my sister... ✅

### Why “were”?

Because the speaker is not talking about a real fact. The speaker is **imagining a different reality**.

Take this sentence:

> If I were you, I would not accept that offer.

I am **not you**. It is impossible for me literally to become you. So this is hypothetical.

That is why SSC-style formal grammar expects:

> If I **were** you... ✅

not

> If I **was** you... ❌

### Compare real vs unreal

Look at this difference carefully:

> If he **was** at the office yesterday, he probably saw Rahul.

Here we do not know whether he was there. It is a **real possibility about the past**.

But:

> If he **were** here now, he would help us.

He is **not here**. We are imagining the situation.

So:

**was = possible/actual past situation**
**were = unreal/hypothetical situation**

### Most important structure

For present unreal conditions:

**If + subject + past form, subject + would/could/might + V1**

Example:

> If I **were** rich, I **would buy** a house.

Meaning: I am not rich now.

> If she **knew** the answer, she **would tell** us.

Meaning: She probably does not know the answer.

> If they **had** more money, they **could travel** abroad.

Meaning: They do not have enough money now.

Notice something important:

Only **be** has the special form **were** for all persons.

We say:

> If I **were**...  
> If he **were**...  
> If she **were**...  
> If it **were**...

But other verbs simply use their normal past form:

> If I **knew**...  
> If he **had**...  
> If she **worked**...  
> If they **lived**...

### Very common examples to memorize

> If I **were** you, I would apologize.
> If I **were** in your place, I would study harder.
> If he **were** honest, people would trust him.
> If she **were** taller, she could become a model.
> If I **were** a bird, I would fly across the ocean.
> If I **were** the Prime Minister, I would change the law.
> If he **were** my brother, I would help him.
> If today **were** Sunday, we would not have to work.
> If this **were** true, we would have a serious problem.
> If there **were** more time, I would explain everything.

### “Wish” also uses this rule

With **wish**, when you wish the present situation were different, formal grammar also prefers **were**.

> I wish I **were** rich.

Reality: I am not rich.

> She wishes she **were** taller.

Reality: She is not taller.

> I wish he **were** here.

Reality: He is not here.

> I wish it **were** easier.

Reality: It is not easy.

A very useful memory link:

**If I were...**  
**I wish I were...**

Both describe a reality that is different from the actual situation.

### “As if / as though”

You may also see **were** after **as if** or **as though** when something is unreal.

> He behaves as if he **were** the boss.

Reality: He is not the boss.

> She talks as though she **were** an expert.

Reality: She is probably not an expert.

> He spends money as if he **were** a millionaire.

Reality: He is not a millionaire.

### Important exam trap

Question:

> If I ___ you, I would accept the job.

A. am  
B. was  
C. were  
D. have been

Answer:

**C. were**

Because this is an unreal/hypothetical condition.

Another:

> If he ___ more careful, he would make fewer mistakes.

A. is  
B. was  
C. were  
D. has been

**were**

> If he **were** more careful, he would make fewer mistakes.

### But don’t use “were” everywhere after if

This is extremely important.

If the condition is real or possible, use normal tense rules.

> If he **is** tired, he will sleep. ✅

Not:

> If he were tired, he will sleep. ❌

Real possibility:

> If it **rains** tomorrow, we will stay home.

Hypothetical:

> If it **were** raining now, we would stay home.

Real:

> If she **is** at home, call her.

Hypothetical:

> If she **were** at home, she would answer the phone.

### Easy reality test

Whenever you see an **if-clause**, ask:

**Is the speaker talking about a real possibility, or imagining something contrary to reality?**

If real:

> If I **am** free tomorrow, I will come.

If imaginary:

> If I **were** free now, I would come with you.

Notice the meaning:

> If I were free now...

implies:

**I am not free now.**

### One more powerful trick

When you see:

**would + V1** in the main clause,

there is a good chance the if-clause is hypothetical.

> If I **were** rich, I **would buy** a Ferrari.

> If he **knew** her number, he **would call** her.

> If she **were** here, she **would help** us.

So memorize:

**IF + hypothetical past → WOULD + V1**

Especially:

**If I/he/she/it WERE → would + V1**

### SSC memory formula

Keep this in your mind until the exam:

**Reality says WAS. Imagination says WERE.**

Example:

> Yesterday I **was** tired.  
> If I **were** stronger, I could work longer.

And the golden sentence:

> **If I were you, I would...**
