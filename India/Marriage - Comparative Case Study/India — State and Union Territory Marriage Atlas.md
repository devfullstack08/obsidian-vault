# India — State and Union Territory Marriage Atlas

> [!warning] How to read this atlas
> State borders do not define a single marriage culture. Each entry gives **representative examples**, not an official or exhaustive state ritual. Religion, caste, tribe, language, locality and family can matter more than state.

Back to [[Marriage - Master Case Study]]. See also [[Religion-wise Marriage Rituals]].

## North India

### Himachal Pradesh

Pahari Hindu weddings may include deity/family blessings, *barat*, sacred-fire rites, feasting and distinctive local music. Kinnauri and other communities have their own kinship histories and ceremonial dress. Contemporary ceremonies often combine local caps, shawls and instruments with mainstream Hindu sequences.

### Punjab

Among Sikhs, the centre is **Anand Karaj**: *ardaas*, four *lavan* sung while the couple circles Guru Granth Sahib, concluding hymns and *langar*. Punjabi Hindu and Muslim families use their respective religious rites, while *roka*, mehendi, jaggo, music, *milni* and reception are widely shared regional celebrations.

### Haryana

Representative Hindu ceremonies include engagement, *ban/baan*, turmeric, *ghurchari*, *barat*, sacred-fire rites and *vidai*. Village, gotra and clan exogamy have historically shaped spouse selection. Urban weddings increasingly resemble broader Delhi–NCR celebrations.

### Uttarakhand

Kumaoni and Garhwali weddings commonly include family-deity worship, turmeric, processions, priest-led rites and folk music. Kumaoni symbols such as *pichora* and large *nath* can mark bridal identity. Community practices differ in the hills and plains.

### Uttar Pradesh

Awadhi, Braj, Bhojpuri and western-UP weddings vary widely. Hindu patterns may include *tilak*, haldi, *barat*, *jaimala*, *kanyadan*, fire rites and *vidai*. Muslim weddings centre on *nikah*, *mahr*, witnesses and feast; regional songs, sehra and processions surround the contract.

### Rajasthan

Representative Rajput and other Hindu customs include *tilak*, *pithi*, *ban*, groom’s procession, *toran*, fire rites and clan-deity blessings, accompanied by *maand* and folk songs. Historical elite marriage often created political alliances; contemporary practice is far more diverse and legally governed.

## West and Central India

### Gujarat

Customs can include *chandlo matli*, *gol dhana*, *ganesh sthapana*, *grah shanti*, *pithi*, *mameru*, *varghodo*, *jaimala*, fire rites, *saptapadi*, *kansar* and *vidai*. Gujarati Muslim, Christian, Jain, tribal and Parsi communities follow distinct cores with regional food, dress and music.

### Maharashtra

In many Maharashtrian Hindu weddings: *sakharpuda*, *kelvan*, halad, *seemant pujan*, the **antarpat** cloth, *mangalashtaka*, exchange of garlands, *kanyadan*, *lajahoma* and *saptapadi*. The couple may wear mundavalya. Buddhist, Muslim, Christian and other ceremonies differ.

### Goa

Goan Catholic weddings commonly include church preparation, nuptial Mass or service, vows, rings, civil documentation and reception; *roce* (coconut-milk anointing) is a regional pre-wedding custom among many Catholics. Hindu Goan communities use their own temple/household and Vedic-customary rites.

### Madhya Pradesh

Mainstream Hindu, Muslim, Jain and other rites coexist with Gond, Bhil, Baiga and additional Adivasi traditions. Tribal ceremonies may foreground clan negotiation, community dancing, bride-service/gifts or local deities; there is no single “tribal wedding.”

### Chhattisgarh

Regional Hindu patterns coexist with Gond, Muria, Oraon and other community customs. Village feasts, ancestor/local-deity acknowledgement, music and dance can be central. Practices must be identified by the specific community rather than generalised as Chhattisgarhi.

## East India

### Bihar

Maithil, Bhojpuri and Magahi traditions include engagement or *tilak*, turmeric, *matkor*, *barat*, priest-led rites, *sindoor-daan*, fire rites and *vidai*. Maithil ceremonies can have elaborate lineage and ritual conventions. Muslim communities centre the marriage on *nikah*.

### Jharkhand

Santhal, Munda, Ho, Oraon and other communities maintain distinct clan-exogamy rules, negotiation, community dancing, rice-beer/feast customs and ancestral or village recognition, alongside Hindu, Muslim and Christian weddings. Conversion and urbanisation have produced blended forms.

### Odisha

Odia Hindu weddings can include *nirbandha*, turmeric, *barajatri*, *kanyadan*, *homa*, *saptapadi* and post-wedding games. Jagannath devotion and local customs may influence ceremony. Numerous Adivasi communities have separate systems.

### West Bengal

In many Bengali Hindu weddings the bride may arrive on a *piri*, cover her face with betel leaves, perform *shubho drishti*, exchange garlands, participate in *sampradan*, fire offerings and *sindoor-daan*. Bengali Muslim marriages centre on *nikah* and *mahr*; regional food, music and dress surround it.

## South India

### Andhra Pradesh

Telugu Hindu sequences often include *nischitartham*, turmeric, *snatakam*, *kashi yatra*, *jeelakarra-bellam* placed on the couple’s heads, *mangalasutra dharana*, *talambralu* (rice shower) and *saptapadi*. Exact rites differ sharply by caste and sect.

### Telangana

Telugu rites such as *jeelakarra-bellam*, *talambralu* and mangalasutra may appear with Telangana songs and food. Muslim *nikah* traditions are prominent, especially in Hyderabad, where Deccani customs can surround the contract and *walima*.

### Karnataka

Kannada Hindu customs vary among regions and communities; examples include *nischaya tamulam*, turmeric, *kashi yatre*, *muhurtha*, *dhare*, mangalasutra and *saptapadi*. Kodava, Tuluva, Lingayat, Muslim, Christian and Jain communities have distinctive forms.

### Tamil Nadu

Tamil Hindu weddings may include *nichayathartham*, *vratham*, *janavasam*, *kashi yatra*, exchange of garlands, *oonjal*, *kanyadanam*, tying the **tali**, fire rites and *saptapadi*. The Self-Respect marriage tradition permits non-priestly, equality-oriented solemnisation under state law when legal conditions are met.

### Kerala

Malayali Hindu forms are often shorter than northern Vedic-style weddings; tying a *tali*, garlands, giving cloth and family blessings may be central depending on community. Nair matrilineal history, Namboodiri rites, Syrian Christian church weddings, Mappila Muslim *nikah* and tribal customs create deep diversity.

### Arunachal Pradesh

Dozens of peoples—including Nyishi, Adi, Apatani, Monpa and others—have different marriage exchanges, feasts, clan rules and religious forms. Buddhist Monpa ceremonies, indigenous Donyi-Polo-related customs and Christian weddings coexist. Always name the community.

Meitei Hindu ceremonies may include formal family negotiations, community observances, sacred rites and characteristic dress; Meitei marriage culture also reflects indigenous traditions. Naga, Kuki-Zo, Muslim/Pangal and Christian communities follow other forms.

### Meghalaya

Khasi, Jaintia and Garo societies are well known for matrilineal descent, though their rules differ. Weddings may be Christian, indigenous or blended; descent and inheritance through women do not automatically mean women hold all public authority. Residence and clan exogamy are important analytical topics.

### Mizoram

Most contemporary Mizo weddings are Christian, commonly involving church vows, prayer, rings, witnesses and feast. Earlier customary bride-price and clan negotiations changed under Christianity, law, education and urban life; customary exchange may survive in modified form.

### Nagaland

Naga peoples have distinct clan-exogamy, negotiations, feasts, textiles and former customary practices. Today Christian church weddings are widespread, often combined with community dress and customary family exchange. “Naga wedding” is therefore a family of traditions, not one rite.

### Sikkim

Nepali Hindu, Bhutia and Lepcha communities contribute different forms. Buddhist ceremonies may involve lama-led prayers and blessings; Nepali Hindu weddings may include fire rites and *sindoor*; Christian and civil ceremonies also occur.

### Tripura

Bengali Hindu and Muslim rites coexist with Tripuri and other indigenous customs. Community feasting, local deities, textiles, negotiation and dance may accompany the union. Specific terms vary by group.

## Union Territories

### Andaman and Nicobar Islands

The settler population follows diverse mainland Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh and civil forms. Indigenous peoples have separate social systems, but sensitive or protected communities should not be reduced to tourism-style ritual descriptions without reliable ethnography and ethical context.

### Chandigarh

Punjabi Sikh and Hindu forms are common, alongside interregional and civil weddings. *Anand Karaj*, Hindu customary rites and Special Marriage Act ceremonies are all present.

### Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu

Gujarati, tribal, Hindu, Muslim and Catholic traditions overlap. Coastal Catholic influence is more visible in Daman and Diu; Warli, Kokna and other communities contribute distinct kinship and festive practices.

### Delhi (NCT)

Delhi is a national convergence zone: North Indian Hindu weddings, *nikah*, church weddings, *Anand Karaj*, Jain and civil ceremonies, and hybrid interregional events. Event-industry trends often spread nationally from metropolitan centres.

### Jammu and Kashmir

Kashmiri Muslim weddings centre on *nikah* and may include elaborate regional feasts such as *wazwan*. Kashmiri Pandit weddings include customary/Vedic rites and regional practices; Jammu’s Dogra traditions differ. Ladakh is treated separately as a UT.

### Ladakh

Tibetan Buddhist communities may seek astrological timing and lama-led blessings, with ceremonial scarves, feasting and local music; Muslim communities follow *nikah* with Ladakhi/Balti cultural elements. Historical kinship practices should not be assumed to describe every contemporary family.

### Lakshadweep

Predominantly Muslim island communities centre marriage on *nikah*, consent, *mahr* and witnesses, surrounded by island-specific matrilineal/kinship histories, dress, songs and feasts. Local practice differs from mainland stereotypes.

### Puducherry

Tamil Hindu, Christian and Muslim rites predominate, with some French civil/cultural legacy. Church weddings, tali-centred Hindu rites, *nikah* and civil registration coexist across its separated regions.

## Comparative regional patterns

- **North:** clan/village exogamy has historically been influential; processional and fire-rite forms are common among Hindus.
- **South:** some communities permit or historically preferred certain cousin/uncle–niece relationships; tali/mangalasutra often has strong symbolic importance.
- **Northeast:** enormous ethnic diversity, relatively weaker “one pan-Indian wedding” pattern, and significant Christian–customary blending.
- **West:** mercantile, Parsi, Jain, Hindu, Muslim, Catholic and tribal networks produce plural forms.
- **Metropolitan India:** interregional ceremony design, civil registration and compressed or hybrid weddings are increasingly visible.

These are tendencies—not rules.
