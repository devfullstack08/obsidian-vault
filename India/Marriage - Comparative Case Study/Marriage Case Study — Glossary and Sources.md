# Glossary and Sources

Back to [[Marriage - Master Case Study]].

## Glossary

- **Affinal kin:** relatives created through marriage.
- **Consanguineal kin:** blood/descent relatives.
- **Endogamy:** marriage within a specified group.
- **Exogamy:** marriage outside a specified group, such as clan/gotra.
- **Hypergamy:** marriage into a group of higher status.
- **Hypogamy:** marriage into a group of lower status.
- **Monogamy:** one spouse at a time.
- **Polygyny:** one man with multiple wives.
- **Polyandry:** one woman with multiple husbands.
- **Patrilineal/matrilineal:** descent traced primarily through father’s/mother’s line.
- **Patrilocal/matrilocal/neolocal:** residence near husband’s kin/wife’s kin/in a new independent household.
- **Bridewealth:** marriage-related transfer from groom’s side to bride’s kin in some systems.
- **Dowry:** marriage-connected transfer commonly from bride’s side; coercive demand is illegal in India.
- **Mahr:** mandatory Islamic marital entitlement from groom to bride.
- **Liminality:** transitional state between old and new social identities.
- **Solemnisation:** performance of acts legally/religiously constituting marriage.
- **Registration:** official recording; its relationship to solemnisation varies by law.

## Source hierarchy used

1. Current statutory text and court decisions for law.
2. Official denominational/religious guidance for doctrinal rites.
3. Peer-reviewed anthropology/history for interpretation and variation.
4. Government cultural documentation for regional examples.
5. Community accounts for lived detail, labelled as such.

## Academic starting points

- [Allendorf & Pandian — The Decline of Arranged Marriage?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5362258/): national change and continuity, spouse choice and regional variation.
- [Oxford Research Archive — variations in South Indian Hindu marriage ritual](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A708c69b3-b4e7-44c9-9027-1bb3b97f76c6): evidence that caste and region alter ritual form.
- [University of Edinburgh — The Female Bridegroom](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/the-female-bridegroom-a-comparative-study-of-life-crisis-rituals-/): life-cycle ritual, kinship and South India/Sri Lanka.
- [Oxford Academic — marriage ritual and politics in medieval Rajasthan](https://academic.oup.com/book/35022/chapter-abstract/298844736): alliance, honour, gifts and historical context.
- [IGNOU eGyanKosh — Life Cycle Rituals](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/21739/1/Unit-28.pdf): anthropological framing of Hindu marriage complexity.

## Research cautions

- A tourism page can describe clothing or performance but is weak evidence for legal validity.
- A priest’s explanation may represent one sect, lineage or local school.
- Historical ethnographies may preserve colonial bias and should be cross-checked with contemporary community voices.
- Search-engine summaries and wedding-industry blogs should not be treated as final authority.
- Exact legal claims should be rechecked against current statutes and judgments on the date of use.
