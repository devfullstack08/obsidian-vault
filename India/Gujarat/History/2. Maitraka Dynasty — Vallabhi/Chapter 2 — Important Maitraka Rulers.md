
You do not need to memorize every Maitraka ruler equally.

Focus on the important ones.

### 1. Bhatarka

- Founder
    
- Former Gupta military governor
    
- Used title **Senapati**
    

### 2. Dharasena I

- Successor of Bhatarka
    
- Also used the title **Senapati**
    

### 3. Dronasimha

He adopted the title:

> **Maharaja**

This is significant because it shows increasing political independence.

### 4. Dhruvasena I

His period is associated with the religious importance of Vallabhi.

The famous **Jain council at Vallabhi** is traditionally associated with this broad Maitraka period.

### 5. Guhasena

Guhasena is important because inscriptions suggest a further move away from nominal Gupta allegiance.

He stopped using expressions implying subordination to a superior imperial ruler. 

### 6. Dharasena II

He adopted more prestigious sovereign titles, reflecting increasing Maitraka power.

### 7. Shiladitya I

Also known as:

> **Dharmaditya**

He is one of the most important Maitraka rulers.

The Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** visited western India during the 7th century and described the Vallabhi region and its rulers in favourable terms.

### 8. Dhruvasena II

Also known as:

> **Baladitya**

He is politically important because he was connected through marriage with **Harshavardhana's family**.

### 9. Dharasena IV

A powerful ruler who assumed grand imperial titles such as:

- Maharajadhiraja
    
- Parameshvara
    
- Chakravartin
    

The Sanskrit poet **Bhatti** is traditionally associated with his court. 

### 10. Later Shiladityas

A succession of rulers named Shiladitya continued Maitraka rule.

The last known ruler is generally identified as:

> **Shiladitya VI**

By the late 8th century, Maitraka rule disappeared.

## Maitraka Political Expansion

Initially the Maitrakas were mainly based in:

> **Saurashtra**

Over time their territory expanded into other parts of Gujarat and adjoining regions.

Evidence suggests Maitraka influence reached:

- north Gujarat
    
- central Gujarat
    
- parts of Malwa
    
- areas around Ujjain at certain points
    

A copper-plate grant associated with Kharagraha I has been used as evidence for Maitraka influence extending toward Ujjain. 

So do not imagine the Maitrakas as rulers of only one coastal city.

At their height they were a major western Indian power.

---

## Maitrakas and Harshavardhana

This is an important political relationship.

During the 7th century, **Harshavardhana of Kanauj** became one of northern India's strongest rulers.

The Maitrakas came under Harsha's influence or suzerainty for a period, but retained considerable local autonomy. After Harsha's death in **647 CE**, Maitraka independence strengthened again. 

A marriage alliance also linked the Maitrakas with Harsha's family.

### Memory line

> **Maitrakas were influenced by Harsha, but they were not permanently absorbed into his kingdom.**

---

## Administration

The Maitraka kingdom had a structured administrative system.

The kingdom was divided into territorial units.

Sources mention administrative units such as:

> **Vishaya**

Officials associated with administration included officers such as:

- Rashtrapati
    
- Amatya
    
- village-level officials
    

The lowest important local unit was the:

> **Grama — village**

with local officials such as the **Gramakuta**. 


A simple model:

```text
King
↓
Provincial / regional officers
↓
Vishaya
↓
Local administration
↓
Grama / Village
```

## Copper-Plate Grants and Inscriptions

One of the most important sources for Maitraka history is:

> **Copper-plate land grants**

These grants provide information about:

- kings
    
- genealogy
    
- titles
    
- administration
    
- land grants
    
- villages
    
- taxation
    
- religion
    
- officials
    

This is why our knowledge of the Maitrakas is relatively detailed compared with many other regional dynasties.

For exam questions:

> **Maitraka history → inscriptions + copper-plate charters**

## Vallabhi as an Educational Centre

This is one of the most important chapters.

Vallabhi became one of ancient India's major centres of higher learning.

It is frequently compared with:

> **Nalanda**

But remember:

> **Nalanda → Bihar**  
> **Vallabhi → Gujarat**

Vallabhi attracted students and scholars from different regions.

Subjects likely included:

- Buddhist philosophy
    
- logic
    
- grammar
    
- political studies
    
- administration
    
- religious texts
    

Chinese Buddhist travellers recorded Vallabhi's reputation as an important centre of scholarship.

---

## Xuanzang and Vallabhi

The famous Chinese Buddhist monk and traveller:

> **Xuanzang / Hiuen Tsang**

visited India in the 7th century.

His travels lasted approximately from 629 to 645 CE. 

He visited western India and provided valuable information about Vallabhi.

His accounts suggest Vallabhi was:

- prosperous
    
- important commercially
    
- associated with Buddhist monasteries
    
- intellectually active
    

He also described a Maitraka ruler, commonly associated with **Shiladitya**, positively. 


> **Xuanzang → Harsha + Nalanda + Vallabhi**

---

## I-Tsing and Vallabhi

Another Chinese Buddhist traveller:

> **I-Tsing / Yijing**

also referred to Vallabhi's reputation for learning.

By the later 7th century, Vallabhi was still regarded as a major scholarly centre. 

Two Buddhist scholars traditionally associated with Vallabhi were:

- **Gunamati**
    
- **Sthiramati**
    

---

## Jainism and Vallabhi

Vallabhi was extremely important in the history of **Jainism**.

A major Jain council is traditionally associated with Vallabhi.

Its importance lies in the preservation and systematization of Jain canonical traditions.

Therefore remember:

> **Vallabhi = Buddhism + Jainism + higher learning**

Do not think Vallabhi was exclusively Buddhist.

---

## Religion under the Maitrakas

Most Maitraka rulers were associated primarily with:

> **Shaivism**

Their coins and inscriptions sometimes contain Shaiva symbols such as:

- **Trishula**
    
- **Nandi**
    

However, the dynasty was religiously tolerant.

Different rulers and grants supported:

- Shaivism
    
- Vaishnavism
    
- Buddhism
    
- Jainism
    
- Surya worship
    

One ruler, **Dharapatt(a)**, is particularly associated with **Sun worship**.
### Important conclusion

> Maitraka rule shows **religious pluralism**, not religious uniformity.

## Maitraka Coinage

Maitraka coins were influenced by earlier:

- Western Kshatrapa coinage
    
- Gupta coinage
    

Common features included:

- ruler's head/profile
    
- Brahmi legends
    
- religious symbols
    

The **trident** became particularly important and reflected Shaiva associations. 

### Memory line

> **Maitraka coin → Trishula → Shaivism**

## Economy and Trade

Vallabhi benefited enormously from Gujarat's location.

Gujarat had access to:

> **Arabian Sea trade**

The Maitraka kingdom was connected with:

- western Indian ports
    
- inland trade routes
    
- Malwa
    
- Rajasthan
    
- northern India
    
- overseas commerce
    

Agriculture remained important, but urban centres and trade also supported the kingdom.

Copper-plate grants indicate the importance of:

- villages
    
- land revenue
    
- agricultural land
    
- religious land grants
    

---

## Society and Culture

The Maitraka age was culturally significant because Gujarat became a meeting point of:

- Sanskritic culture
    
- Jain scholarship
    
- Buddhist scholarship
    
- Brahmanical traditions
    
- merchant communities
    

Sanskrit was important in inscriptions and elite literary culture.

Prakrit and regional languages also continued to develop.

Vallabhi's educational importance helped make Saurashtra one of western India's major cultural regions.

---
## Architecture and Temples

Numerous temples and religious remains associated with the Maitraka period have been identified, especially in Saurashtra.

The period contributed to the gradual development of western Indian temple architecture.

Important architectural themes include:

- early structural temples
    
- Shaiva shrines
    
- Surya worship
    
- Buddhist establishments
    
- Jain religious centres
    

More than a hundred temple remains have been associated broadly with the Maitraka period, particularly along parts of coastal Saurashtra.
