
Bhima I is one of the **most important early Solanki/Chaulukya rulers of Gujarat**. His reign is especially important because of the **Somnath raid of Mahmud of Ghazni**, the recovery of Chaulukya power afterward, and major architectural developments.

### 1. Accession

Bhima I succeeded **Durlabharaja** around **1022 CE**.

He was Durlabharaja’s nephew and the son of **Nagaraja**.

> **Durlabharaja → Bhima I**

He ruled from **Anahilapataka (Patan)**.

---

## 2. Mahmud of Ghazni and Somnath — 1025–26 CE

The most famous event of Bhima I’s reign was the invasion of Gujarat by **Mahmud of Ghazni**.

Mahmud entered Gujarat and attacked the **Somnath temple** in Saurashtra around **1025–26 CE**. Gujarat government historical material also places the raid in **1026 during Bhima I’s reign**. 


> **Mahmud of Ghazni → Somnath → Bhima I**

### What did Bhima do?

When Mahmud invaded, Bhima did not fight a decisive battle with him at Somnath. Historical accounts indicate that Bhima withdrew toward **Kanthkot in Kutch**, while Mahmud proceeded to Somnath. After Mahmud left Gujarat, Bhima re-established Chaulukya authority.

So avoid writing:

> “Bhima defeated Mahmud of Ghazni.”

That is **not correct**.

Better:

> **During Bhima I’s reign, Mahmud of Ghazni raided Somnath; after Mahmud withdrew, Bhima restored Chaulukya authority in Gujarat.**

---

# 3. Was Somnath rebuilt?

Traditions associate the restoration of Somnath after Mahmud’s raid with Bhima I and other regional rulers.

For exam purposes, the safest point is:

> **Somnath was restored after Mahmud’s raid during the wider political recovery under Bhima I.**

Avoid overly precise claims about a single person rebuilding the entire temple unless the source/question specifically gives that tradition.

---

# 4. Bhima I and the Paramaras

Bhima I also had conflicts with the **Paramaras**, who controlled regions adjoining Gujarat.

He fought Paramara chiefs in areas such as **Arbuda**, the Mount Abu region. Sources describe him suppressing revolts among Paramara chiefs who had earlier functioned as Chaulukya feudatories.

This shows that Bhima’s reign was not only about the Ghaznavid invasion.

After the crisis, he continued expanding and consolidating Chaulukya power.

---

# 5. Bhima I and King Bhoja of Malwa

Another important contemporary was:

> **Bhoja of the Paramara dynasty of Malwa**

Bhoja was one of the most famous rulers of medieval western/central India.

Later traditions describe Bhima I cooperating with the Kalachuri ruler **Lakshmi-Karna** against Bhoja around the final years of Bhoja’s reign.

However, the exact details are uncertain because later chronicles exaggerate the victories of their own rulers.

> **Bhima I ↔ contemporary/rival of Paramara Bhoja**

---

# 6. Modhera Sun Temple

One of the greatest monuments associated with Bhima I’s period is:

> **Sun Temple of Modhera**

It is located in present-day **Mehsana district**.

According to Gujarat Tourism, the temple was built around **1026–27 CE during the reign of Bhima I**. 

It is dedicated to:

> **Surya — the Sun God**

### Important parts

The temple complex includes:

- **Surya Kund**
    
- **Sabha Mandapa**
    
- main shrine / Gudha Mandapa
    

The Surya Kund contains numerous small shrines; Gujarat Tourism notes **108 shrines** around the kund. 

### Memory line

> **Bhima I = Modhera Sun Temple**

---

# 7. Vimal Vasahi — Mount Abu

Another extremely important monument from Bhima I’s period is:

> **Vimal Vasahi Jain Temple**

It is part of the famous **Dilwara Jain Temples at Mount Abu**.

It was built by:

> **Vimal Shah**

who was a minister associated with Bhima I’s court.

The temple is dedicated to:

> **Adinatha / Rishabhanatha**, the first Jain Tirthankara.

The Dilwara temple’s own historical account associates Vimal Vasahi with Vimal Shah, minister of Bhima of the Chaulukya dynasty. ([Seth Shri Kalyanji Parmanandji Pedhi -](https://www.dilwarajaintemple.org/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Dilwara Jain Temple, Mount Abu"))

So remember:

> **Bhima I → Minister Vimal Shah → Vimal Vasahi → Mount Abu**

---

# 8. Rani ki Vav

Bhima I is also directly connected to one of Gujarat’s most famous monuments:

> **Rani ki Vav — Patan**

It was commissioned by his queen:

> **Udayamati**

in memory of Bhima I.

Therefore:

> **Bhima I → Queen Udayamati → Rani ki Vav**

Do not confuse this with Modhera:

> **Modhera Sun Temple → reign of Bhima I**  
> **Rani ki Vav → Udayamati, in memory of Bhima I**

---

# 9. Why Bhima I’s reign is important

His reign demonstrates something important about Solanki Gujarat.

He faced a major external invasion early in his reign:

```text
Mahmud invades
↓
Somnath raided
↓
Bhima withdraws temporarily
↓
Mahmud leaves Gujarat
↓
Bhima restores Chaulukya authority
↓
Kingdom survives and continues expanding
```

So the Ghaznavid raid **did not destroy the Solanki dynasty**.

Bhima continued ruling for decades afterward.

---

# 10. Architecture under Bhima I

Bhima I’s reign belongs to one of the major phases in the development of western Indian temple architecture.

Three names should immediately come to mind:

> **Modhera Sun Temple**  
> **Vimal Vasahi**  
> **Rani ki Vav**

These monuments show the flourishing of the architectural tradition later described as **Maru-Gurjara / Solanki architecture**.

---

# 11. Religion during his reign

Bhima himself belonged to the wider Brahmanical royal tradition, while his administration also included major Jain patrons such as **Vimal Shah**.

This is important because Solanki Gujarat saw strong interaction between:

- Shaivism
    
- Vaishnavism
    
- Surya worship
    
- Jainism
    

Therefore, the Solanki period should not be understood as religiously uniform.

---

# 12. End of Bhima I’s Reign

Bhima I ruled for roughly four decades and died/left the throne around:

> **1064 CE**

He was succeeded by his son:

> **Karna**

So:

```text
Durlabharaja
↓
Bhima I
1022–1064
↓
Karna
1064–1092
```

### Trap 1

**Who defeated Mahmud of Ghazni at Somnath?**

Do not answer:

> Bhima I

Bhima was the ruler of Gujarat during the raid, but Mahmud successfully raided Somnath and then left Gujarat. ([Girsomnath](https://girsomnath.nic.in/about-district/history/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "History | District Gir Somnath, Government of Gujarat | India"))
