
These three rulers form the **transition period between Mularaja I and Bhima I**.  **Durlabharaja is the most important of the three**.

### 1. Chamundaraja — c. 996–1008 CE

**Chamundaraja** was the son and successor of **Mularaja I** and ruled from **Anahilapataka (Patan)**.

Remember:

> **Mularaja founded the dynasty → Chamundaraja continued it**

Unlike Mularaja, Chamundaraja is not remembered for creating a large new empire. His reign was mainly important for **maintaining the kingdom established by his father**.

Some later literary traditions say that toward the end of his reign he withdrew from active kingship and went on pilgrimage. These details come largely from later chronicles, so treat them cautiously rather than as unquestionable facts. 

### Family connection

Chamundaraja had sons including:

- **Vallabharaja**
    
- **Durlabharaja**
    
- Nagaraja
    

This becomes important because two of his sons ruled one after another. 

---

# 2. Vallabharaja — 1008 CE

**Vallabharaja** succeeded Chamundaraja.

His reign was extremely short — **less than one year**. 

According to later historical traditions, Vallabharaja went on a military expedition but contracted **smallpox** and died during the campaign. 

Therefore:

> **Vallabharaja = very short reign + death from smallpox**

Because his reign was so short, he does not have the same political importance as Mularaja, Durlabharaja, or Bhima I.

### Exam trap

Do not accidentally remove Vallabharaja from the succession sequence:

```text
Chamundaraja
      ↓
Vallabharaja
      ↓
Durlabharaja
```

Some genealogical records themselves omitted him because of his extremely brief reign, but other Chaulukya records include him.

---

# 3. Durlabharaja — 1008–1022 CE

After Vallabharaja died, his brother:

> **Durlabharaja**

became king.

He ruled approximately from **1008 to 1022 CE**, with Anahilapataka as his capital. 

Durlabharaja is considerably more important politically than his immediate predecessors.

## 4. Conquest of Lata

One of Durlabharaja's major achievements was his campaign into:

> **Lata**

Ancient/medieval **Lata** broadly refers to the region of **southern Gujarat**, especially areas around the lower Narmada and adjoining south Gujarat.

Historical reconstruction suggests that Durlabharaja successfully extended Chaulukya power into the Lata region.

This matters because control over southern Gujarat could provide access to commercially important routes and regions.

So remember:

> **Durlabharaja → Lata campaign**

---

# 5. Durlabharaja and Anahilapataka

Durlabharaja continued ruling from:

> **Anahilapataka / Patan**

Later literary traditions credit him with construction activities in the capital, including a reservoir known as:

> **Durlabha Sarovara**

and temples associated with his reign. 

For GPSC, however, don't spend too much time memorizing every building attributed by later texts to him.

His political importance is greater:

> **He strengthened the kingdom before Bhima I inherited it.**

---

# 6. Succession to Bhima I

Durlabharaja apparently had no son who succeeded him.

The throne therefore passed to his nephew:

> **Bhima I**

Bhima was the son of Durlabharaja's brother **Nagaraja**.

This gives you the family line:

```text
Mularaja I
   ↓
Chamundaraja
   ├───────────────┬───────────────┐
   ↓               ↓               ↓
Vallabharaja   Durlabharaja     Nagaraja
                                   ↓
                                 Bhima I
```

So after Durlabharaja:

> **Bhima I became king in c. 1022 CE.** 

---

# 7. Why this transition period matters

Think of the first four Solanki rulers like this:

> **Mularaja → creates the kingdom**  
> **Chamundaraja → maintains it**  
> **Vallabharaja → very brief interruption**  
> **Durlabharaja → strengthens/expands toward Lata**  
> **Bhima I → enters the major historical phase**

This makes the chronology much easier than memorizing isolated names.

# 8.  Quick Table

|Ruler|Approx. Reign|Main point|
|---|--:|---|
|**Chamundaraja**|996–1008|Son of Mularaja; continued Solanki rule|
|**Vallabharaja**|1008|Very short reign; reportedly died of smallpox|
|**Durlabharaja**|1008–1022|Important Lata campaign; strengthened kingdom|
|**Bhima I**|from 1022|Next major Solanki ruler|

The ruler sequence and approximate dates are supported by the standard Chaulukya chronology.
