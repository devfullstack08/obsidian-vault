When India became independent on **15 August 1947**, the Khasi, Jaintia and Garo Hills did not immediately form a separate state.

Instead, they remained part of **Assam**.

However, many tribal leaders wanted greater control over their own administration, language, customs and political affairs.

The demand for a separate hill state became stronger during the late 1950s and early 1960s.

A major development was the formation of the **All Party Hill Leaders' Conference (APHLC)** on 6 July 1960.

Under leaders including Williamson A. Sangma, the movement pursued statehood through constitutional and largely peaceful political methods.

## **Formation of Meghalaya — Timeline**

1. **1947 — Independence**
	- Khasi, Jaintia and Garo Hills remained within Assam.
	
2. **1960 — Hill State Movement**
	- APHLC emerged as a major organisation advocating separate statehood.
	
3. **1969 — Assam Reorganisation (Meghalaya) Act**
	- Parliament provided for the creation of an autonomous state within Assam.
	
4. **2 April 1970 — Autonomous State**
	- Meghalaya became an autonomous state within Assam, inaugurated by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi.
	
5. **1971 — North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act**
	- Parliament enacted legislation providing for full statehood.
	
6. **21 January 1972 — Full Statehood**
	- Meghalaya became a separate state of India, with Shillong as its capital.

Sources: Government of Meghalaya and the North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971
