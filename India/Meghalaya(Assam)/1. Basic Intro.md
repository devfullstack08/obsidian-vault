The name **Meghalaya** comes from Sanskrit:
- **Megha** = Cloud
- **Alaya** = Abode or home
Therefore, Meghalaya means **“Abode of Clouds.”**

Its mountainous landscape receives abundant rainfall, especially around Sohra (Cherrapunji) and Mawsynram.

## Who lived there before the British arrived?

1. Khasi
	- Traditionally lived in the Khasi Hills. Their political organisation included local chieftainships, and their society preserves important matrilineal traditions.

2. Jaintia (Pnar)
	- Associated with the Jaintia Hills and the historical Jaintia kingdom, which extended into areas of present-day Bangladesh.
3. Garo (A'chik)
	- Traditionally lived in the Garo Hills, with their own village institutions, customary practices and matrilineal clan traditions.

An interesting feature is that many Khasi, Jaintia and Garo families follow **matrilineal descent**: children belong to their mother's clan, and certain inheritance rights pass through women. This does not necessarily mean that women hold all political authority.

## How did the British enter Meghalaya?

After the British expanded their power in Assam following the **Treaty of Yandabo (1826)**, they wanted better transport connections between Assam and the Surma Valley.

They sought to construct a road through Khasi territory.

At first, Khasi chief **U Tirot Sing** negotiated with the British. However, disputes over territorial authority and British expansion led to armed resistance in 1829.

The conflict became known as the **Anglo-Khasi War (1829–1833)**.

Tirot Sing was eventually captured and imprisoned in Dhaka, where he died in 1835.

The Jaintia and Garo Hills also witnessed resistance to British rule.

## Three important freedom fighters of Meghalaya

1. U Tirot Sing (Khasi resistance, 1829–1833)
2. U Kiang Nangbah (Jaintia uprising, 1862)
3. Pa Togan Sangma (Garo resistance, 1872)

These leaders became symbols of the region's resistance to colonial expansion. U Kiang Nangbah was executed by the British on 30 December 1862, and Pa Togan Sangma is remembered for resisting British forces in the Garo Hills.
