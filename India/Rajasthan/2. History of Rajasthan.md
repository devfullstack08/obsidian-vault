## **1. Introduction — Physical Features of Rajasthan**

Rajasthan is not entirely a desert. Although the **Thar Desert** occupies a large part of western Rajasthan, the state also contains ancient mountains, fertile plains, rocky plateaus and deep river valleys.

These variations in terrain are known as **physiography** or _physical relief_.

Rajasthan is divided into **four major physiographic regions**, according to the Government of Rajasthan.

**Physical Divisions of Rajasthan**

1. Western Sandy Plains — Thar Desert

2. Aravalli Mountain Region

3. Eastern Plains

4. Southeastern Plateau — Hadoti

The most important geographical feature is the **Aravalli Range**, which runs in a southwest–northeast direction across Rajasthan.

It separates two broad regions:

- **Northwest:** Predominantly dry, sandy and arid terrain.
    
- **Southeast:** Generally better-watered terrain with hills, plains and plateaus.
    

The Rajasthan State Biodiversity Board describes this broad division as approximately 60% northwest and 40% southeast of the range.

## **3. Western Sandy Plains — Thar Desert (पश्चिमी रेतीला मैदान)**

The Western Sandy Plains form the **largest physical region of Rajasthan**, occupying roughly three-fifths of the state's area.

The region is associated with the Indian portion of the Thar Desert.

### **Location**

Found mainly in western and northwestern Rajasthan, including the geographical areas around:

- Jaisalmer
    
- Barmer
    
- Bikaner
    
- Jodhpur
    
- Churu
    
- Nagaur
    
- Sri Ganganagar and Hanumangarh

The region also extends toward the semi-arid Shekhawati belt.

### **Major characteristics**

|Feature|Explanation|
|---|---|
|Relief|Sand dunes, sandy plains, rocky surfaces and low uplands|
|Climate|Arid to semi-arid|
|Rainfall|Generally low and highly variable|
|Vegetation|Sparse, drought-resistant grasses, shrubs and trees|
|Settlement|Often concentrated near reliable water sources|
|Landforms|Dunes, interdunal depressions, playas and rocky exposures|

### **Important desert landforms**

1. **Barchan Dunes (बरखान)**
	- Crescent-shaped dunes formed by wind moving sand under relatively consistent wind directions and limited sand supply. Their horns generally point downwind.
2. **Longitudinal Dunes**
	- Long, narrow dunes extending broadly along the net direction of sand transport.

3. **Playas**
	- Low-lying, often enclosed depressions where water may collect temporarily and evaporate, leaving behind salts or fine sediments.

### **Important subregions**

**Marusthali:** The drier western desert core, especially associated with Jaisalmer and Barmer.

**Bangar or Bagar:** Semi-arid tracts lying farther east of the most intensely desertified area.

**Shekhawati:** A semi-arid belt associated with Jhunjhunu, Sikar and neighbouring areas.

**Luni Basin:** Southwestern Rajasthan, where the Luni drainage system influences the landscape.

These are geographical and cultural subregions, not separate states or administrative divisions.

### **Why is western Rajasthan so dry?**

Three factors are especially important:

1. The **Aravalli Range is broadly aligned with the southwest monsoon winds**, so it does not force strong, widespread orographic rainfall on the western side.
    
2. The region is far from the main sources of moisture, and monsoon circulation weakens inland.
    
3. High temperatures and evaporation contribute to arid conditions.

**Important distinction:** The Aravallis are not a continuous, high mountain wall creating a simple rain-shadow desert. Their alignment, modest elevation and regional monsoon dynamics all matter.

### **Concept for UPSC Mains**

Desert geography influences Rajasthan's settlements, agriculture, transport, drinking-water requirements, pastoral traditions and water-conservation practices.

Detailed desert climate, soils and irrigation will be studied in Chapters 3, 4 and 18.

## **4. Aravalli Mountain Region (अरावली पर्वतीय प्रदेश)**

The Aravalli Range is one of India's oldest geological mountain systems.

It forms a discontinuous chain of hills and ridges, extending broadly from the Delhi region through Rajasthan toward Gujarat.

### **Key characteristics**

|Feature|Information|
|---|---|
|Orientation|Southwest to northeast|
|Geological character|Very old, highly eroded mountain system|
|Highest peak|Guru Shikhar|
|Highest peak elevation|1,722 metres|
|Location of Guru Shikhar|Mount Abu, Sirohi district|
|Major role|Major physical divide of Rajasthan|

The **Rajasthan State Biodiversity Board** confirms Guru Shikhar's elevation and the Aravalli range's importance in separating Rajasthan's northwestern and southeastern landscapes.

### **Three broad sections of the Aravallis**

|Section|Important characteristics|
|---|---|
|Northern Aravallis|Lower, discontinuous hills around Alwar, Jaipur, Khetri and adjoining areas|
|Central Aravallis|Hills and valleys of the Ajmer–Beawar–Rajsamand belt|
|Southern Aravallis|Higher, more rugged hills associated with Udaipur, Sirohi and Mount Abu|

### **Important peaks and passes**

- **Guru Shikhar:** Highest peak in Rajasthan and the Aravalli Range.
    
- **Ser:** A prominent high peak in the Mount Abu region.
    
- **Dilwara:** Another important peak in the Mount Abu hills.
    
- **Taragarh:** Hill associated with Ajmer's historic fort.
    
- **Haldighati:** A mountain pass in the Aravalli region near Rajsamand, historically significant for the Battle of Haldighati (1576).

### **Why are the Aravallis important?**

**Geography:** They divide Rajasthan into contrasting climatic and physical regions.

**Drainage:** Hills and ridges form watersheds from which streams flow in different directions.

**Natural resources:** The ancient rocks contain important mineral deposits.

**Ecology:** They support forests, wildlife habitats and ecological connectivity.

**History:** Their hill passes and fortified sites influenced political and military history.

**Environmental concern:** Mining, land degradation and settlement pressures make conservation important.

**Exam trap:** The Himalayas are geologically young fold mountains. The Aravallis are much older and have experienced extensive erosion.

## **5. Eastern Plains (पूर्वी मैदान)**

The Eastern Plains lie mainly to the northeast, east and southeast of the Aravalli hills.

The Rajasthan Foundation estimates their area at approximately **23.3% of the state**.

Rajasthan Foundation

### **Important geographical areas**

The eastern plain region includes areas around Bharatpur, Dausa, Jaipur, Tonk and parts of Ajmer, Bhilwara and Chittorgarh, depending on the physiographic classification used.

### **Characteristics**

- Comparatively flatter relief, interrupted by low hills and valleys.
    
- Extensive agricultural land in suitable areas.
    
- Important river-created plains and valleys.
    
- Generally better conditions for settlement and cultivation than the western desert core.

### **Important subdivisions**

**Banas Plain:** A major plain associated with the Banas river system and its tributaries.

**Chambal Basin:** Eastern and southeastern areas associated with the Chambal River, including eroded landscapes.

**Bharatpur Plain:** Low-lying northeastern plains, part of a wider alluvial landscape.

### **What are Chambal ravines?**

Ravines are deep, narrow channels formed when running water progressively cuts into soil and sediment.

The Chambal landscape contains extensive **gully and ravine erosion**, creating rugged, dissected terrain called _badlands_.

**UPSC geography concept:** Ravine formation is strongly related to water erosion, surface runoff, vulnerable soils and land-use conditions.

Do not confuse the generally flatter Eastern Plains with the rocky Southeastern Plateau, although the Chambal drainage connects both regions.

## **6. Southeastern Plateau — Hadoti Region (दक्षिण-पूर्वी पठार)**

The southeastern part of Rajasthan contains a region commonly called the **Hadoti (Harauti) Plateau**.

It is associated especially with:

- Kota
    
- Bundi
    
- Baran
    
- Jhalawar

These names describe the broad historical-geographical region; individual physiographic boundaries are not identical to district boundaries.

### **Major characteristics**

|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|Landform|Elevated tablelands, escarpments, valleys and undulating surfaces|
|Geological influence|Vindhyan rock formations and extensions of the Malwa plateau|
|Drainage|Strongly associated with the Chambal river system|
|Agriculture|Significant cultivation in suitable plateau and valley areas|
|Regional distinction|Less dominated by sand dunes than western Rajasthan|

### **What is a plateau?**

A **plateau** is an elevated area of land that is relatively level or gently undulating compared with surrounding areas.

The Hadoti region includes plateau surfaces and deeply incised valleys.

It is not one uniformly flat elevated surface.

### **Relation with the Malwa Plateau**

The Malwa Plateau extends mainly across Madhya Pradesh, but its geological and geographical influence continues into southeastern Rajasthan, especially around Jhalawar.

Some parts are associated with basaltic rocks, while other parts of Hadoti contain important Vindhyan sedimentary formations.

**Important:** Do not assume the entire Hadoti Plateau is made of black basaltic rock. Its geological composition varies.

## **8. Understand the relationship between geography and human life**

This is the kind of explanation that helps in UPSC and GPSC Mains.

**Physical Relief**

Desert / Mountain / Plain / Plateau

**Influences climate and drainage**

**Affects water availability and soils**

**Shapes agriculture, settlement and livelihoods**

For example, in the western sandy region, limited water availability has historically encouraged water conservation, pastoral livelihoods and settlement around dependable water sources.

In the eastern plains, landforms and greater access to water have generally supported denser settlement and more intensive agriculture.

In the Aravalli hills, rugged terrain has shaped transport routes, resource use and the historical location of forts.

These relationships are tendencies, not universal rules. Irrigation, technology and infrastructure can significantly modify geographical constraints.

## **9. Important geographical terms**

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
