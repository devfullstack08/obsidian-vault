**Rajasthan is India's largest state by geographical area**, situated in the northwestern part of the country.

The name Rajasthan means **“Land of Kings”** (_Raja_ = King; _Sthan_ = Place). Historically, much of the region was called **Rajputana**, referring to the princely states associated with Rajput rulers, although its history also includes many other kingdoms and communities.

Rajasthan is important because it combines distinctive physical geography with a long history of kingdoms, desert trade, architecture and cultural traditions.

### The two geographical features that explain Rajasthan

1. **Thar Desert**
	- Dominates western Rajasthan. Important for aridity(infertility), dunes, water scarcity, pastoralism and desert adaptation.
2. **Aravalli Range**
	- Runs generally southwest–northeast and forms an important geographical divide. Guru Shikhar is its highest peak.

Rajasthan was formed through **seven stages of integration between 1948 and 1956**, rather than in a single event.

### Historical background — From Rajputana to Rajasthan

Before independence, Rajasthan did not exist in its present form as one unified administrative state.

The region contained numerous princely states and chiefships, including Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Kota and Bundi.

Much of the region was administered under the British Indian political arrangement known as the **Rajputana Agency**.

After Indian independence in 1947, these states were gradually integrated into the Indian Union.

#### Seven stages of Rajasthan's integration (1948–1956)

1. **18 March 1948 — Matsya Union**
    
    Alwar, Bharatpur, Dholpur and Karauli formed the first union. Sources sometimes distinguish the agreement date (17 March) from the inauguration (18 March).
    
2. **25 March 1948 — Rajasthan Union**
    
    Nine states, including Kota, Bundi, Tonk, Jhalawar, Banswara and Dungarpur, formed a larger union.
    
3. **18 April 1948 — United Rajasthan**
    
    Mewar (Udaipur) joined the Rajasthan Union.
    
4. **30 March 1949 — Greater Rajasthan**
    
    Jaipur, Jodhpur, Bikaner and Jaisalmer joined. The integration was inaugurated by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
    
5. **15 May 1949 — United Greater Rajasthan**
    
    Matsya Union merged with Greater Rajasthan.
    
6. **26 January 1950 — Rajasthan**
    
    Most of Sirohi was incorporated into the state, marking another important stage of consolidation.
    
7. **1 November 1956 — Reorganised Rajasthan**
    
    Ajmer, Abu Road and Sunel Tappa were incorporated through the States Reorganisation process, establishing the state's present broad territorial shape.
