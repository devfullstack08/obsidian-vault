Visakhapatnam is called the **City of Destiny** primarily due to a visionary prediction made in the 1930s by Dr. C.R. Reddy, the former Vice-Chancellor of Andhra University. 

### Origin of the Name

Dr. C.R. Reddy coined the phrase when he made the bold decision to relocate Andhra University from Bejawada (now Vijayawada) to Visakhapatnam. At the time, Visakhapatnam was merely a small, little-known fishing village called **Waltair**, while other cities like Guntur and Rajahmundry were more developed educational and cultural hubs.  Dr. Reddy believed that this coastal village was destined to become the **intellectual capital** and a major metropolis of the future. 

### Fulfillment of the Destiny

The title stuck because the city's subsequent growth remarkably fulfilled this prophecy:

- **Industrial Hub**: It transformed from a quiet village into one of India's largest industrial centers, hosting major entities like the Visakhapatnam Steel Plant (inaugurated by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in 1970, who further popularized the title) and the oldest shipyard in India. 
    
- **Strategic Importance**: It became the headquarters of the Eastern Naval Command and home to India's only natural harbor on the east coast. 
    
- **Cosmopolitan Growth**: Often called "India in a Microcosm," it grew into a diverse, conflict-free city that attracted people from all over the country, becoming a top contributor to India's GDP.
