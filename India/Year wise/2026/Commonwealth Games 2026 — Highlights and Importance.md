# Commonwealth Games 2026 — Highlights and Importance

> [!abstract] Core summary
> The 23rd Commonwealth Games were held in Glasgow, Scotland, from **23 July to 2 August 2026**. Australia topped the medal table. India finished **fourth with 39 medals: 13 gold, 17 silver and 9 bronze**. Glasgow 2026 was especially important because it introduced a compact, lower-risk hosting model, delivered the largest integrated Para-sport programme in Commonwealth Games history, and formally handed the Games to **Ahmedabad, host of the Centenary Commonwealth Games in 2030**.

## 2. How did the Games move to Glasgow?

The 2026 Commonwealth Games were originally awarded to the Australian state of **Victoria**, with events planned across several regional cities. In July 2023, the Victorian government withdrew because projected costs had risen sharply.

The cancellation created an institutional crisis. It raised several questions:

- Were the Commonwealth Games becoming too expensive to host?
- Was it necessary to construct new stadiums and an athletes’ village?
- Were projected economic benefits being exaggerated?
- Could smaller Commonwealth nations ever afford to host the event?
- Would the 2026 edition take place at all?

Glasgow, which had successfully hosted the 2014 Games, eventually stepped in. It proposed reusing existing facilities and delivering a smaller programme. Glasgow was officially confirmed as host in October 2024.

### The Glasgow model

- Only four major existing venue clusters were used.
- Venues were concentrated within roughly an eight-mile corridor.
- Athletes and officials stayed in hotels instead of a newly constructed village.
- The programme was limited to ten sports.
- Road events and sports requiring additional venues were excluded.
- The plan sought to avoid a major new public-funding burden for delivery.
- Organisers projected more than £100 million in inward investment and over £150 million in regional economic value.

> **Victoria’s withdrawal represented the crisis; Glasgow’s compact model represented a practical response.**

## 3. Sports programme

The ten sports were:

1. **3x3 Basketball and 3x3 Wheelchair Basketball**
2. **Artistic Gymnastics**
3. **Athletics and Para Athletics**
4. **Bowls and Para Bowls**
5. **Boxing**
6. **Judo**
7. **Netball**
8. **Swimming and Para Swimming**
9. **Track Cycling and Para Track Cycling**
10. **Weightlifting and Para Powerlifting**

### Major sports that were not included

- Shooting
- Wrestling
- Badminton
- Hockey
- Cricket
- Table Tennis
- Squash
- Rugby Sevens
- Road Cycling

Several omitted sports—especially shooting, wrestling, badminton, hockey and table tennis—have traditionally contributed heavily to India’s medal total. This makes a direct comparison with Birmingham 2022 misleading unless the change in programme is considered.

## 6. Major Indian highlights

### 6.1 Boxing’s historic campaign

All ten members of India’s boxing contingent won medals: **seven gold and three silver**. Boxing was India’s most successful discipline by gold medals.

Gold medallists:

- Preeti Pawar—Women’s 54kg
- Jaismine Lamboria—Women’s 57kg
- Sakshi Chaudhary—Women’s 51kg
- Priya Ghanghas—Women’s 60kg
- Arundhati Choudhary—Women’s 70kg
- Sachin Siwach—Men’s 60kg
- Ankush Panghal—Men’s 80kg

Silver medallists:

- Jadumani Singh—Men’s 55kg
- Lovlina Borgohain—Women’s 75kg
- Narender Berwal—Men’s +90kg

### 6.2 Mirabai Chanu’s gold-medal hat-trick

Mirabai Chanu won the women’s 48kg weightlifting title. It was her third successive Commonwealth Games gold medal, confirming her status as one of India’s most consistent international weightlifters.

### 6.3 Gulveer Singh’s double-track breakthrough

Gulveer Singh won:

- Silver in the men’s 10,000m
- Bronze in the men’s 5,000m

He became the first Indian athlete to win two track medals at a single Commonwealth Games.

### 6.4 Tejaswin Shankar’s decathlon milestone

Tejaswin Shankar won bronze in the men’s decathlon. It was India’s first Commonwealth Games medal in the decathlon and represented progress in multi-event athletics.

### 6.5 Para-athletes’ contribution

India’s Para-athletes made a substantial contribution:

- Sharmila Dhankar—Women’s Shot Put F57 gold
- Dilip Gavit—Men’s 100m T47 gold
- Soman Rana—Men’s Shot Put F57 gold
- Mohammed Basil—Men’s 100m T47 silver
- Shubham Juyal—Men’s Shot Put F57 silver
- Shilpa K. Shyla—Women’s Shot Put F57 bronze
- Jhandu Kumar—Para Powerlifting bronze

### 6.6 Two gold medals in judo

- Asmita Dey—Women’s 48kg gold
- Harsh Singh—Men’s 60kg gold
- Yamini Mourya—Women’s 57kg silver
- Unnati Sharma—Women’s 63kg bronze

The results demonstrated India’s emerging depth in a sport that has not traditionally produced a large Commonwealth medal haul.

### 6.7 Neeraj Chopra’s silver

Neeraj Chopra won silver in the men’s javelin throw. Although he did not win gold, the result extended his record of reaching the podium at major international championships.

### 6.8 Women powered the campaign

According to the Indian Olympic Association, women athletes won **eight of India’s thirteen gold medals**. Their performance in boxing, weightlifting, judo and Para Athletics demonstrated the growing strength of women’s elite sport in India.

## 8. Major international highlights

### 8.1 Australia’s dominance

Australia topped the table with 70 gold and 171 total medals. Its success demonstrated exceptional depth across swimming, athletics, cycling, gymnastics and Para disciplines.

### 8.2 Emma Finucane won four gold medals

Welsh track cyclist **Emma Finucane** became the first sprint cyclist to win four track gold medals at one Commonwealth Games:

- Team Sprint
- Individual Sprint
- 1,000m Time Trial
- Keirin

### 8.3 Return of the Commonwealth Mile

The historic Mile returned to the Commonwealth athletics programme for the first time since 1966. Scotland’s Josh Kerr won in front of the home crowd, producing one of the defining moments of the Games.

### 8.4 New Zealand won netball gold

New Zealand defeated Jamaica in the final and won its first Commonwealth netball title since Delhi 2010. Australia failed to reach the gold-medal match for the first time in Commonwealth Games netball history.

### 8.5 Historic medals for smaller nations

Cyprus, Tonga, Guernsey, Norfolk Island and the Isle of Man recorded rare or historic medals. Matthew Bostock of the Isle of Man won the final gold medal of Glasgow 2026 in the men’s points race.

### 8.6 Oldest Commonwealth Games champion

Australia’s **Louise Hoskins**, aged 76, won the women’s B6–B8 Para Bowls pairs title with Serena Bonnell. She became the oldest gold medallist in Commonwealth Games history.

## 9. A historic Games for Para sport

Glasgow 2026 featured:

- Six fully integrated Para sports
- A record **47 Para medal events**
- The largest integrated Para-sport programme in Commonwealth Games history
- Expanded Para Track Cycling and Para Swimming programmes
- Para athletes competing within the same schedule, venues, medal table and public narrative as other athletes

This integration is one of the Commonwealth Games’ strongest distinctive features. It gives Para athletes greater visibility, encourages investment in accessible facilities and coaching, and avoids treating disability sport as a separate side event.

## 10. Why were the 2026 Commonwealth Games important?

### 10.1 They rescued the 2026 edition

After Victoria withdrew, the future of the Games was uncertain. Glasgow demonstrated that an edition could be organised within a short planning period using an established sporting city.

### 10.2 They offered a new compact hosting model

Glasgow showed that a multi-sport event could be staged without constructing an expensive athletes’ village or numerous new venues. This lowered financial and operational risk.

### 10.3 They reopened the debate over cost and legacy

The success of a sporting event should not be measured only through medals, spectators and ceremonies. It should also be evaluated through:

- Responsible use of public money
- Reuse of existing facilities
- Long-term community access to sport
- Tourism and investment
- Environmental impact
- Post-Games maintenance costs
- Transparent auditing of promised economic benefits

### 10.4 They advanced Para-sport inclusion

The record Para programme created visibility, role models, classification expertise and a stronger argument for funding disability sport.

### 10.5 They gave smaller nations a global platform

Many smaller Commonwealth nations have limited opportunities to win medals at the Olympics. The Commonwealth Games provide them with a realistic elite stage, international recognition and a pathway for athlete development.

### 10.6 They supported Olympic preparation

Athletes gained experience of an international multi-sport environment, major finals, media attention and competition pressure. The Games therefore served as an important step within the Olympic cycle.

### 10.7 They increased the visibility of women’s sport

Women’s boxing, cycling, judo, weightlifting and netball produced major stories. For India, women winning eight of thirteen gold medals was particularly significant.

## 11. Why were the Games important for India?

### Fourth place despite an unfavourable programme

India’s 39-medal total was lower than its 61 medals at Birmingham 2022. However, Glasgow excluded several Indian strongholds. Fourth place therefore demonstrated adaptability within a very different programme.

### Diversification into boxing and athletics

India did not rely on shooting, wrestling or badminton:

- Boxing produced ten medals from ten competitors.
- Athletics and Para Athletics produced sixteen medals.
- Judo produced four medals.
- India achieved firsts in decathlon and distance-track events.

This diversification matters for Olympic strategy because athletics contains a large number of medal events.

### Growth of India’s Para-sport system

Multiple Para-athletics medals strengthened the case for continued investment in:

- Talent identification
- Accessible training centres
- Specialised coaching
- Sports science
- Classification support
- Competition exposure
- High-quality prosthetics and equipment

### Administrative preparation for Ahmedabad 2030

Glasgow acted as a learning laboratory for Indian sports administrators in:

- Venue clustering
- Athlete transport
- Accessibility
- Volunteer management
- Security
- Broadcasting
- Sustainability
- Cultural presentation
- Post-event legacy planning

## 12. Special importance for Gujarat—Ahmedabad 2030

**Ahmedabad—officially styled Amdavad by Commonwealth Sport—has been confirmed as host of the 2030 Centenary Commonwealth Games.** The first Games took place in Hamilton, Canada, in 1930, making the Ahmedabad edition the movement’s hundredth-anniversary event.

### Glasgow-to-Ahmedabad handover

At the closing ceremony on 2 August 2026, Glasgow handed the Commonwealth Games to Ahmedabad. This was not merely ceremonial; it marked the beginning of a four-year delivery cycle.

### Expected structure of Ahmedabad 2030

- A larger programme of 15–17 sports
- Initial confirmed programme including Athletics and Para Athletics, Swimming and Para Swimming, Table Tennis and Para Table Tennis, Bowls and Para Bowls, Weightlifting and Para Powerlifting, Artistic Gymnastics, Netball and Boxing
- India’s first Commonwealth Games since Delhi 2010
- Global presentation of Gujarat’s culture, including Garba and dhol traditions
- A major test of India’s capacity to organise large multi-sport events

### Opportunities for Gujarat

- International tourism
- Sports-management and event-industry employment
- Grassroots academies
- Improved public transport
- Accessible public infrastructure
- Global branding of Gujarat’s culture and economy
- Experience relevant to future Olympic ambitions

### Risks and concerns

- Cost overruns
- Underused stadiums or “white elephants”
- Displacement and land acquisition
- Security expenditure
- Heat, air quality and climate resilience
- Event spending at the expense of grassroots sport
- Weak financial transparency
- Promised infrastructure that does not benefit ordinary citizens after the Games

> [!important] Standard for success
> Ahmedabad 2030 should not be judged by how grand it looks. It should be judged by how effectively it uses existing infrastructure, how transparent its costs are, how accessible the event is, and what long-term benefits remain for athletes and residents.

## 13. Political and historical dimension

The Commonwealth Games originated within the British imperial system. The 1930 event was known as the British Empire Games. Its name changed as colonies became independent and the Commonwealth evolved.

### Supportive interpretation

The modern Games can provide:

- A shared sporting network
- Cultural exchange
- Opportunities for smaller countries
- Athlete-development pathways
- Leadership in integrated Para sport
- Cooperation among diverse nations

### Critical interpretation

Critics point to:

- The Games’ colonial origins
- The symbolic role of the British monarch
- Questions about relevance beside the Olympics and World Championships
- High hosting costs and declining bidder interest
- Uneven quality of competition because several leading global sporting powers are outside the Commonwealth
- The risk of celebrating “Commonwealth unity” without honestly confronting imperial violence

### Balanced assessment

The Games have a colonial historical origin, but their contemporary value should be judged by modern governance, equality, sustainability, athlete opportunity and the treatment of host communities.

## 14. Glasgow’s success and limitations

### Achievements

- Delivered the Games on a short planning cycle
- Brought together 74 teams and over 3,000 athletes
- Used existing venues efficiently
- Attracted approximately 434,000 ticket holders, with additional participation at fan sites
- Staged 215 medal events
- Delivered a record integrated Para programme
- Created a bridge to Ahmedabad 2030

### Limitations

- Only ten sports meant many athletes had no opportunity to compete.
- The absence of an athletes’ village may have reduced the traditional shared multi-sport experience.
- The smaller programme made historical medal comparisons difficult.
- Glasgow solved the immediate 2026 problem but not the long-term need for sustainable future hosts.
- Projected economic benefits still require independent post-event assessment.
