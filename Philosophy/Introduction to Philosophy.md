# Introduction to Philosophy: The Three Pillars

To understand any philosophical system (called a *Darshana* in Indian Philosophy), it is best to analyze it through its three fundamental pillars: **Epistemology**, **Metaphysics**, and **Ethics**.

You are absolutely correct! These three pillars form the foundation of any philosophical inquiry.

---

## 1. Epistemology (Theory of Knowledge)
* **Sanskrit Term:** *Pramana-shastra* (પ્રમાણશાસ્ત્ર)
* **Core Question:** *How do we know what we know?*
* **What it covers:**
  * It investigates the source, nature, validity, and limits of knowledge.
  * Before asserting what is real (Metaphysics), a philosopher must establish how they know it is real.
* **Key Concepts:**
  * **Pramana (Sources of Knowledge):** The means of obtaining valid knowledge. Major pramanas include:
    * *Pratyaksha* (Perception) – Direct sensory experience (e.g., seeing smoke).
    * *Anumana* (Inference) – Logical reasoning based on prior observation (e.g., inferring fire from smoke).
    * *Shabda* (Verbal Testimony) – The words of a reliable authority or scripture.
  * **Pramanya (Validity of Knowledge):** How do we verify if a belief is actually true?

---

## 2. Metaphysics (Theory of Reality)
* **Sanskrit Term:** *Tattva-shastra* (તત્વશાસ્ત્ર)
* **Core Question:** *What is the ultimate nature of reality?*
* **What it covers:**
  * The study of existence, being, and what exists beyond the physical world.
* **Key Concepts:**
  * **God (*Ishvara*):** Does a supreme creator/ruler exist? Or can the universe be explained purely by natural/material laws?
  * **Soul/Self (*Atman* / *Jiva*):** Is there a permanent conscious entity? Or is consciousness just a temporary state of the body (as in Charvaka) or a stream of changing elements (as in Buddhism)?
  * **Cosmos/World (*Jagat*):** How did the universe come into being? Is the physical world real, or is it an illusion (*Maya*)?
  * **Causation:** How do cause and effect operate in reality?

---

## 3. Ethics (Moral Philosophy)
* **Sanskrit Term:** *Nitishastra* (નીતિશાસ્ત્ર) / *Dharma* (ધર્મ)
* **Core Question:** *How should we live? What is good conduct?*
* **What it covers:**
  * The study of moral values, duties, right vs. wrong behavior, and the ultimate purpose of life.
* **Key Concepts:**
  * **Karma:** Do actions have moral consequences? Is there an invisible moral law that governs future births?
  * **Purusharthas (Human Goals):** The goals of human life:
    * *Dharma* (Moral duty/righteousness)
    * *Artha* (Material wealth/security)
    * *Kama* (Sensory pleasure/desire)
    * *Moksha* (Liberation from suffering/rebirth) – The ultimate ethical goal for most Indian schools.
  * **Path of Life:** How does one achieve the ultimate goal? (e.g., Charvaka's Hedonism, Buddhism's Eightfold Path, or Vedanta's path of knowledge).

---

## Summary: Analyzing any Philosophy

| Pillar           | Focus          | Key Question        | Example (Charvaka School)                                                             |
| :--------------- | :------------- | :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Epistemology** | Knowledge      | How do we know?     | Only direct **Perception** (*Pratyaksha*) is valid. Rejects inference and testimony.  |
| **Metaphysics**  | Reality        | What is real?       | Only **Matter** is real. Rejects God, soul, heaven, and rebirth.                      |
| **Ethics**       | Action & Goals | How should we live? | Practical **Hedonism** (enjoy life, avoid pain) because there is no life after death. |
