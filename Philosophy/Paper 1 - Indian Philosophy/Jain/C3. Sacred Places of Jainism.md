# Chapter 3: Sacred & Holy Places of Jainism (જૈન તીર્થધામો)

In Jainism, sacred places of pilgrimage are called **Tirthas** (તીર્થ). Unlike other religions, Jain pilgrimages are not seen as places where God resides, but as **Moksha-Bhumis** (lands of liberation) where ascetics achieved ultimate freedom, or places of outstanding spiritual and architectural heritage.

---

## 1. Classification of Jain Tirthas

Jain sacred places are broadly classified into four categories:

1. **Siddha-Kshetra:** Places where Tirthankaras or omniscient saints (*Kevalins*) attained Moksha.
2. **Kalyanaka-Kshetra:** Places associated with the five auspicious events (*Kalyanakas*) of a Tirthankara’s life (Birth, Renunciation, Omniscience, etc.).
3. **Atishaya-Kshetra:** Historical sites where miracles or highly inspiring events occurred.
4. **Kala-Kshetra:** Places celebrated for their extraordinary artistic, cultural, and architectural heritage.

---

## 2. Major Jain Sacred Sites: In-Depth & Current Details

### A. Sammed Shikharji (Parasnath Hills, Jharkhand)
* **Status:** The holiest *Siddha-Kshetra* for both Shvetambara and Digambara sects.
* **Significance:** 20 out of 24 Tirthankaras attained Nirvana here.
* **Recent Current Affairs (2022-2023 Controversy):**
  * **The Issue:** The Jharkhand State Government issued a notification designating the Parasnath Hills as an eco-tourism destination.
  * **The Protest:** The Jain community launched massive nationwide protests. They argued that declaring it a "tourist spot" would lead to the commercialization, consumption of alcohol, non-vegetarian food, and littering, violating the spiritual sanctity of their holiest land.
  * **Outcome:** In January 2023, the Central Government intervened and directed the state government to halt all eco-tourism and tourism activities on the hill, preserving its status as a sacred pilgrimage site.

---

### B. Palitana (Shatrunjaya Hills, Bhavnagar, Gujarat)
* **Status:** The premier *Siddha-Kshetra* for Shvetambaras.
* **Significance:** Dedicated to the 1st Tirthankara, **Lord Rishabhadeva (Adinatha)**. Jains believe millions of souls, including Rishabhadeva’s son Pundarika, achieved liberation here.
* **Unique Features:**
  * It is the **largest temple city in the world**, featuring over 863 marble-carved temples on a single hill.
  * No one (including priests) is allowed to stay on the hill overnight; everyone must descend before sunset.
* **Current Status (World's First Vegetarian City):**
  * In **2014**, the Gujarat government declared Palitana a **completely meat-free zone**, banning the sale of meat, fish, eggs, and animal slaughter legally within the city limits.
  * **Recent issues (2022-2023):** Massive protests occurred due to the vandalism of a sacred shrine (Charan Paduka) and reports of illegal mining/liquor sales on the hills. Jains successfully demanded stricter security and protection of the entire hill range.

---

### C. Pawapuri (Nalanda District, Bihar)
* **Status:** A major *Siddha-Kshetra*.
* **Significance:** The place where **Lord Mahavira** (24th Tirthankara) delivered his last sermon and attained Nirvana in 527 BCE.
* **The Jal Mandir:** A famous temple built in the middle of a lotus-filled water tank. Jains believe that when Mahavira attained Nirvana, there was a rush to collect his ashes, which created a massive pit that eventually filled with water and became the lake.

---

### D. Mount Girnar (Junagadh, Gujarat)
* **Status:** A sacred *Siddha-Kshetra* for both sects.
* **Significance:** Dedicated to **Lord Neminatha** (22nd Tirthankara), who renounced worldly life, practiced severe penance, and attained Nirvana on the 5th peak of this mountain.
* **Unique Features:** Accessible by climbing around 10,000 stone steps. It is a symbol of inter-faith harmony, hosting Hindu shrines alongside Jain temples on different peaks.

---

### E. Shravanabelagola (Hassan District, Karnataka)
* **Status:** The most important *Digambara* holy place in South India.
* **Significance:** Houses the colossal **57-foot monolithic statue of Lord Gommateshwara (Bahubali)**, carved out of a single block of granite in 981 CE by Chamundaraya (minister of the Ganga Dynasty).
* **Current Festival (Mahamastakabhisheka):**
  * A grand festival held **once every 12 years** where the giant statue is anointed with milk, sugarcane juice, saffron, turmeric, and gold coins. The last festival was in 2018; the next will be in **2030**.

---

### F. Dilwara Temples (Mount Abu, Rajasthan)
* **Status:** Outstanding *Kala-Kshetra* (Architectural Marvel).
* **Significance:** A complex of 5 marble temples built between the 11th and 13th centuries CE.
* **Unique Features:** Renowned worldwide for their incredibly intricate marble carvings on ceilings, pillars, and arches, which look almost transparent. The temples are dedicated to Rishabhadeva and Neminatha.

---

### G. Ranakpur Jain Temple (Pali District, Rajasthan)
* **Status:** Architectural and spiritual marvel.
* **Significance:** Dedicated to Lord Rishabhadeva. Built in the 15th century by a Jain businessman, Dharna Shah, with support from Rana Kumbha.
* **Unique Features:** Built in light-colored marble, it has over **1,444 uniquely carved pillars**, and no two pillars are alike. The design allows natural light and breeze to flow through the entire three-story complex.
