# Buddhism

Buddhism is a major heterodox (*Nastika*) school of Indian philosophy founded by **Gautama Buddha** (6th Century BCE). It rejects the authority of the Vedas and the existence of a permanent soul (*Anatta*) or a creator God, focusing instead on the eradication of suffering (*Dukkha*).

---

## 1. Conception of Reality (*Sat* in Hinayana Buddhism)

In the realistic schools of **Hinayana Buddhism** (such as Sarvastivada / Vaibhashika and Sautrantika), reality is defined and compared using the following Sanskrit dictum:

> **"Arthakriya-karitvam lakshanam sat"** (અર્થક્રિયાકારિત્વં લક્ષણં સત્ / अर्थक्रियाकारित्वं लक्षणं सत्)
> 
> * **Literal Meaning:** *"Reality is characterized by causal efficiency (practical utility)."*

### Key Analysis of the Definition:
* Under this view, a thing is real **only if it has the power to produce an effect**.
* If something cannot perform any action or produce any result (e.g., a sky-flower or the horns of a hare), it is considered unreal/non-existent.
* **Link to Kshanikavada (Momentariness):** 
  * Because reality is causally efficient, it must be constantly changing. If a thing remained permanent and unchanging, it could not produce different effects at different times.
  * Therefore, everything exists only for a single moment (*Kshana*), produces its effect in the next moment, and then perishes. Reality is a stream of continuous change.

## 2. Core Doctrines of Buddhism

### A. Four Noble Truths (Arya Satyani)
1. **Dukkha:** Life is full of suffering.
2. **Dukkha Samudaya (Pratityasamutpada):** There is a cause of suffering (dependent origination).
3. **Dukkha Nirodha:** Suffering can be ended (Nirvana).
4. **Dukkha Nirodha Marga:** There is a path to end suffering (Ashtangika Marga / Eightfold Path).

### B. Pratityasamutpada (Dependent Origination)
* The foundation of Buddha's teachings: *"This being, that arises."* Everything in the universe is dependent on causes and conditions. Nothing is independent or eternal.

### C. Kshanikavada (Doctrine of Momentariness)
* Since everything is dependent on causes, everything is impermanent (*Anicca*) and changing every fraction of a second.

### D. Anatmavada (Doctrine of No-Soul)
* Buddhism rejects a permanent, eternal soul (*Atman*). The "self" is just a collection of five changing aggregates (*Skandhas*): form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness.

## 3. Major Sects of Buddhism

1. **Hinayana (Theravada):**
   * Realistic and individualistic. Focuses on personal liberation (*Arhat* ship).
   * Believes in the objective reality of the physical world based on **cusal efficiency** (*Arthakriya-karitvam*).
2. **Mahayana:**
   * Idealistic and altruistic. Focuses on the liberation of all beings (*Bodhisattva* ideal).
   * Developed into schools like **Madhyamika (Shunyavada)** and **Yogachara (Vijnanavada)**, which viewed the world as ultimately empty of independent nature (*Shunya*) or as pure consciousness.
