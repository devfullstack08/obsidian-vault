Ajivika was an ancient Indian religious and philosophical movement that developed around the 6th–5th century BCE, during the time of Gautama Buddha and Mahavira.

It was one of the important **Shramana traditions** of ancient India, alongside Buddhism and Jainism.
### 1. Founder of Ajivika

**Makkhali Gosala** (Maskarin Gosala) is traditionally regarded as the most important teacher and leader of the Ajivika movement.

He was a contemporary of Gautama Buddha and Mahavira.
### 2. Main philosophy of Ajivika

The central doctrine of Ajivika philosophy was Niyativada (नियतिवाद), meaning fatalism or absolute determinism.

According to this philosophy:

- Everything in the universe is controlled by Niyati (fate or destiny).
- Human beings cannot fundamentally change their predetermined destiny.
- Events such as birth, death, happiness, and suffering occur according to a fixed cosmic order.
- Liberation is attained through a predetermined process rather than through individual effort alone.

For example, according to Ajivika philosophy, if something is destined to happen, human effort cannot ultimately prevent it.

### 3. Other important features

- **Ascetic life**: Ajivika monks practiced strict discipline and renunciation, sometimes living without clothes.
- **Rebirth**: They believed that souls pass through a fixed cycle of births and deaths.
- **Karma**: Unlike Buddhism and Jainism, Ajivikas did not believe that personal actions could change the predetermined course of rebirth.
- **Religious texts**: Their original scriptures have not survived. Most knowledge of Ajivikas comes from Buddhist and Jain writings.
