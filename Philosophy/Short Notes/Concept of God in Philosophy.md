# The Concept of God in Philosophy

In philosophy, the definition and understanding of "God" vary significantly depending on the school of thought, culture, and era. Generally, the concept of God is analyzed under two primary views:

1. **A Personal Creator (*Ishvara* / *Srishti-karta*):** A supreme personal deity who created the universe, governs it, and administers moral justice (rewards/punishments).
2. **Impersonal Supreme Reality / Absolute Perfection (*Brahman* / *Siddha*):** An ultimate, all-pervading reality or the state of absolute spiritual perfection that any consciousness can achieve.

---

## 1. Major Philosophical Classifications

| Philosophical View | Definition | Indian Philosophy Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monotheism** | Belief in a single, personal, supreme creator God. | **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** (believes in *Ishvara* as the efficient cause of the world), **Yoga** (views *Ishvara* as a special Purusha). |
| **Polytheism** | Belief in multiple gods/deities representing different forces of nature. | **Vedic Religion** (worshipping Indra, Agni, Varuna, etc.). |
| **Monism / Pantheism** | God and the universe are one. Ultimate reality is a single, non-dual, impersonal principle. | **Advaita Vedanta** (Brahman is the only reality, without attributes - *Nirguna*). |
| **Nontheism** | Rejection of a creator God, but belief in the moral law of Karma, soul, and spiritual liberation. | **Jainism** and **Buddhism**. |
| **Atheism** | Complete rejection of God, soul, karma, and any spiritual reality. | **Charvaka** (materialist school). |

---

## 2. The Indian Context: *Astika* vs. *Nastika*

In Western philosophy, a person who does not believe in God is an atheist. However, in Indian philosophy:
* **Astika (Orthodox):** Means one who accepts the authority of the **Vedas**.
* **Nastika (Heterodox):** Means one who rejects the authority of the **Vedas**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Belief in the Vedas is not the same as belief in God.**
> * **Sankhya** and **Mimamsa** are *Astika* (Vedic) schools, but they traditionally do **not** believe in a creator God.
> * **Jainism** and **Buddhism** are *Nastika* (Non-Vedic) and do not believe in a creator God, but they firmly believe in spiritual liberation (*Moksha* / *Nirvana*).
