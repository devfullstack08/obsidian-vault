# Couplets, Quotes and Satire

This note compiles all kinds of interesting couplets, poetry, political/social satires, and quotes. Each entry includes the original text (Hindi/Devanagari), transliterated text, English translation, authorship, and a brief commentary or explanation.

---

## 1. On Authority and Conformism

### Original & Transliteration:
* **Hindi:**
  राजा बोला रात है,
  रानी बोली रात है,
  मंत्री बोला रात है,
  संतरी बोला रात है,
  यह तो सुबह-सुबह की बात है!
* **Transliterated:**
  *"Raja bola raat hai, rani boli raat hai, mantri bola raat hai, santri bola raat hai... yeh toh subah-subah ki baat hai"*
  *(User variation: "Raja bola sham he rani boli sham eto e subha subha ki bat he")*

* **Author:** **Gorakh Pandey** (गोरख पांडेय) — a revolutionary Hindi poet known for political satire.
* **English Translation:**
  *"The King said it is night, the Queen said it is night, the Minister said it is night, the Sentry said it is night... and mind you, this is a conversation taking place in the early morning!"*
* **Philosophical & Political Meaning:**
  This is a powerful satire on **authoritarianism** and **blind conformism**. It shows how power dictates "truth." Even if it is broad daylight (morning), if the ruler (King) declares it is night, the sycophants (Queen, Minister, Sentry) will agree to please the ruler. It warns against the death of independent thinking and critical questioning in society.

## 2. On the Mundane Nature of Time and Life

### Original & Transliteration:
* **Hindi:**
  सुबह होती है, शाम होती है,
  उम्र यूँ ही तमाम होती है।
* **Transliterated:**
  *"Subah hoti hai, shaam hoti hai, umra yun hi tamam hoti hai."*

* **Author:** **Mir Babar Ali Anees** (मीर अनीस) — a legendary 19th-century Urdu poet.
* **English Translation:**
  *"Morning arrives, evening falls; and in this repetitive cycle, life passes away."*
* **Philosophical Meaning:**
  This couplet captures the **existential monotony** of human existence. It describes how time slips away quietly. We wake up in the morning, sleep at night, and repeat the cycle daily. Before we realize it, our entire life has spent itself in mundane routines without us ever achieving deeper realization or spiritual awakening. It is a call to rise above the automated cycle of daily life.

## 3. On the Illusion of Goals and Destinations

### Original & Transliteration:
* **Hindi:**
  धोखा है फ़रेब है मंज़िल का हर ख़याल,
  सच पूछो तो सारा सफ़र वापसी का है यार।
* **Transliterated:**
  *"Dhokha hai, fareb hai, manjil ka har khayal; sach pucho toh sara safar wapsi ka hai yaar."*

* **Author:** **Jaun Elia** (जौन एलिया) — one of the most famous modern Marxist-Existentialist Urdu poets.
* **English Translation:**
  *"Every thought of the destination is a deceit, an illusion; if you ask the truth, the entire journey is actually a return."*
* **Philosophical Meaning:**
  This is a deeply **existentialist and mystical (Sufi/Vedantic)** thought. Elia critiques our constant obsession with "destinations" (wealth, success, endpoints). He asserts that the idea of a fixed destination is a delusion because life has no static endpoint. In reality, life is a cycle where the journey is a **return**—either a return to the nothingness (death) from which we came, or a spiritual return (*Wapsi*) to our original source (the supreme consciousness).

## 4. On Courage, Patriotism, and Resolve

### Original & Transliteration:
* **Hindi:**
  द्वंद्व कहाँ तक पाला जाए,
  युद्ध कहाँ तक टाला जाए,
  तू भी है राणा का वंशज,
  फेंक जहाँ तक भाला जाए।

  दोनों ओर लिखा हो भारत,
  सिक्का वही उछाला जाए,
  इस बिगड़े पड़ोसी को,
  अब फिर शीशे में ढाला जाए।
* **Transliterated:**
  *"Dwandwa kahan tak pala jaaye,
  Yuddh kahan tak tala jaaye,
  Tu bhi hai Rana ka vanshaj,
  Fenk jahan tak bhala jaaye.

  Dono aur likha ho Bharat,
  Sikka wahi uchhala jaaye,
  Is bigde padosi ko,
  Ab fir sheeshe mein dhala jaaye."*

* **Author:** **Wahid Ali Wahid** (वाहिद अली वाहिद) — a contemporary Hindi poet.
* **English Translation:**
  *"How long should conflict be nurtured? How long should war be avoided?
  You too are a descendant of Maharana Pratap; throw the javelin as far as it can go!
  Let 'Bharat' be written on both sides; toss only such a coin!
  And let this misbehaved neighbor be put in their place once again."*
* **Meaning & Context:**
  This is a famous patriotic poem celebrating courage, strength, and national pride. The first part inspires the reader to summon inner strength, referencing the legendary warrior king of Mewar, Maharana Pratap (Rana). It went viral globally when Indian athlete Neeraj Chopra won the Olympic Gold in Javelin Throw. The second part expresses absolute conviction in India's victory (with "Bharat" written on both sides of the coin) and advocates for taking a firm stance against hostile neighbors.
