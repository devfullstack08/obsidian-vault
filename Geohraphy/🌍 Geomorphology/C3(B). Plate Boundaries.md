
# Introduction

Tectonic plates are constantly moving.

However,

the most important geological activities do **not** occur in the middle of plates.

They occur where two plates meet.

These meeting zones are called

> Plate Boundaries.

Almost all

- Earthquakes
- Volcanoes
- Mountain building
- Ocean trenches
- Rift valleys

occur along these boundaries.

---

# Types of Plate Boundaries

There are three major types.

```
                 Plate Boundaries

                        │

        ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐

        │               │               │

 Divergent        Convergent       Transform

Constructive      Destructive     Conservative
```

---

# 1. Divergent Plate Boundary

Definition

A divergent boundary is a boundary where two tectonic plates move away from each other.

```
← ← ←       → → →

Plate      Plate

     ↑

   Magma
```

This boundary is also called

> Constructive Plate Boundary

because new crust is continuously formed.

---

# Formation Process

Step 1

Mantle convection rises.

↓

Step 2

Lithosphere cracks.

↓

Step 3

Magma rises.

↓

Step 4

Basalt erupts.

↓

Step 5

New crust forms.

↓

Step 6

Plates move farther apart.

# Divergent Boundary on Land

```
  \______/

```

```

```

# 2. Convergent Plate Boundary

Definition

A convergent boundary is a boundary where two plates move towards each other.

```
→ →     ← ←
```

This boundary is called

> Destructive Plate Boundary

because crust is destroyed.

---

# Why Does One Plate Sink?

Density determines which plate sinks.

Oceanic Crust

↓

Basalt

↓

Heavy

Continental Crust

↓

Granite

↓

Light

Therefore,

the denser plate always subducts.

---

# Subduction

Definition

The process in which one plate sinks beneath another plate is called

> Subduction.

```

         \

```

Subduction is responsible for

✔ Deep earthquakes

✔ Volcanoes

✔ Ocean trenches

# Why Volcanoes Form?

As the oceanic plate sinks,

temperature increases.

↓

Water lowers melting point.

↓

Mantle melts.

↓

Magma rises.

↓

Volcanoes form.

```
Subduction

↓

Melting

↓

Magma

↓

Volcano
```

# Formation of Himalayas

Approximately

50 million years ago

Indian Plate moved northwards.

↓

Tethys Sea disappeared.

↓

Collision occurred.

↓

Sediments compressed.

↓

Fold Mountains formed.

↓

Himalayas

Even today,

Indian Plate continues moving

about

5 cm/year.

Therefore,

the Himalayas continue rising slowly.

---

# Why No Volcanoes?

Both continental plates are light.

Neither subducts deeply.

Without deep melting,

large volcanoes do not form.

# Chapter Summary

✔ Plate boundaries are regions where tectonic plates interact.

✔ Divergent boundaries create new crust.

✔ Convergent boundaries destroy crust through subduction.

✔ Transform boundaries involve horizontal sliding.

✔ Ocean-Ocean convergence forms island arcs and trenches.

✔ Ocean-Continent convergence forms volcanic mountain ranges like the Andes.

✔ Continent-Continent convergence forms fold mountains like the Himalayas.

✔ Benioff Zone is the zone of deep earthquakes along subduction zones.

✔ Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur along plate boundaries.
