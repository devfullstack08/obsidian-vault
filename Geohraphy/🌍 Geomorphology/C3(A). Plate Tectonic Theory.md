
# Why Was Plate Tectonic Theory Needed?

By the 1960s, geology had made remarkable progress.

Scientists had:

✔ Wegener's Continental Drift Theory

✔ Arthur Holmes' Convection Current Theory

✔ Harry Hess' Sea-Floor Spreading Theory

✔ Vine & Matthews' Palaeomagnetism

Yet several important questions remained unanswered.

---

# Unanswered Questions

Scientists still wanted to know:

- Why do earthquakes occur only in certain regions?
- Why are volcanoes concentrated in specific belts?
- How are fold mountains formed?
- Why do continents and oceans move together?
- Why is Earth's crust broken into different pieces?

These questions could not be fully answered by previous theories.

A new theory was needed.

---

# Birth of Plate Tectonic Theory

During the 1960s,

many scientists combined discoveries from:

- Geology
- Geophysics
- Oceanography
- Seismology
- Palaeomagnetism

The result was

> Plate Tectonic Theory

Today,

it is the most accepted theory explaining the dynamic nature of the Earth.

---

it did not reject earlier theories.

# J. T. Wilson

John Tuzo Wilson introduced the term

> Plate

He explained that the Earth's outer shell is not one continuous piece.

Instead,

it is broken into many rigid blocks.

These rigid blocks are called

> Tectonic Plates.

# Lithosphere

Definition

The Lithosphere is the rigid outer layer of the Earth.

It consists of:

- Entire crust
- Uppermost solid mantle

```
Crust

+

Upper Solid Mantle

↓

Lithosphere
```

Characteristics

✔ Hard

✔ Rigid

✔ Brittle

✔ Broken into plates

Average Thickness

Approximately

50–200 km

---

# Asthenosphere

Below the Lithosphere lies

> Asthenosphere

Definition

The Asthenosphere is a weak, semi-molten, plastic layer of the upper mantle.

Characteristics

✔ Hot

✔ Soft

✔ Plastic

✔ Partially molten

✔ Capable of slow flow

---

# Difference Between Lithosphere and Asthenosphere

| Lithosphere | Asthenosphere |
|--------------|---------------|
| Hard | Soft |
| Rigid | Plastic |
| Broken into plates | Continuous layer |
| Moves as plates | Allows movement |
| Crust + Upper Mantle | Upper Mantle only |

---

# Why Can Plates Move?

The Lithosphere is rigid.

But it floats over the soft Asthenosphere.

Think of it like

```
Ice floating on water.
```

Similarly,

```
Lithosphere

──────────────

Floating

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Asthenosphere
```

The Asthenosphere slowly flows because of mantle convection.

This movement carries the plates.

---

# Mantle Convection

Heat from Earth's core creates convection currents.

```
Cold Material

↓

↓

↓

Hot Material

↑

↑

↑
```

These convection currents slowly drag tectonic plates.

Thus,

Mantle Convection is the driving force of Plate Tectonic Theory.

---

# What is a Plate?

Definition

A plate is a large rigid section of the Lithosphere that moves over the Asthenosphere.

Each plate moves independently.

Some plates carry continents.

Some carry oceans.

Some carry both.

---

# Types of Plates

According to composition,

there are three types.

---

## 1. Oceanic Plate

Contains only oceanic crust.

Main Rock

Basalt

Examples

- Pacific Plate
- Nazca Plate
- Cocos Plate

Characteristics

✔ Thin

✔ Dense

✔ Basaltic

---

## 2. Continental Plate

Contains only continental crust.

Main Rock

Granite

Characteristics

✔ Thick

✔ Less Dense

✔ Granitic

Example

Few purely continental plates exist.

---

## 3. Mixed Plate

Contains both

- Oceanic crust
- Continental crust

Examples

- Indian Plate
- Eurasian Plate
- African Plate
- North American Plate
- South American Plate

Most major plates belong to this category.

# Why is Oceanic Crust Denser?

Oceanic crust is made of

Basalt

↓

Rich in Iron & Magnesium

↓

Higher Density

Continental crust is made of

Granite

↓

Rich in Silica & Aluminium

↓

Lower Density

This density difference explains why oceanic plates sink beneath continental plates.
