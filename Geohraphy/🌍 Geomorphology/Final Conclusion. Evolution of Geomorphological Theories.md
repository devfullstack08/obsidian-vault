
## The Story of Earth's Surface

The understanding of the Earth's surface evolved gradually through the contributions of many scientists. Each theory solved some questions but also left new ones unanswered. Over time, these theories collectively led to the development of the modern Plate Tectonic Theory.

The sequence of scientific development is as follows:

```
Alfred Wegener (1912)

↓

Continental Drift Theory

↓

Arthur Holmes (1928)

↓

Convection Current Theory

↓

Harry Hess (1962)

↓

Sea-Floor Spreading Theory

↓

Vine & Matthews (1963)

↓

Palaeomagnetism

↓

J. T. Wilson (1965)

↓

Plate Tectonic Theory

↓

Modern GPS & Satellite Evidence
```

Today, Plate Tectonic Theory is regarded as the most comprehensive explanation for the formation and evolution of the Earth's major landforms.

# Golden Revision Sheet

Remember these five points:

1. Continents were once joined together as **Pangaea**.
2. Mantle convection provides the driving force for plate movement.
3. New oceanic crust forms at **Mid-Ocean Ridges**.
4. Old oceanic crust is destroyed at **Subduction Zones**.
5. Plate interactions create earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountains.

# Frequently Confused Concepts

| Don't Confuse | Correct Understanding |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Magma vs Lava | Magma is inside the Earth; Lava is outside. |
| Focus vs Epicentre | Focus is inside the Earth; Epicentre is on the surface. |
| Lithosphere vs Asthenosphere | Lithosphere is rigid; Asthenosphere is plastic. |
| Magnitude vs Intensity | Magnitude = Energy; Intensity = Damage. |
| P-Waves vs S-Waves | P passes through solids & liquids; S passes only through solids. |
| Oceanic vs Continental Crust | Oceanic = Basalt, denser; Continental = Granite, lighter. |

# Key Takeaway

> **The Earth's surface is dynamic, not static. Continents, oceans, mountains, and volcanoes are continuously shaped by the movement and interaction of tectonic plates driven by mantle convection. Plate Tectonic Theory unifies the earlier theories and remains the most accepted explanation for the evolution of the Earth's surface.**
