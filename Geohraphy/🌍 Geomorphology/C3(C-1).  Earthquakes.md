
# Introduction

An earthquake is one of the most destructive natural hazards on Earth.

Every day, thousands of earthquakes occur around the world.

Most are too weak to be felt, while a few are powerful enough to destroy cities.

Earthquakes are not random events.

Almost all major earthquakes occur along tectonic plate boundaries.

Therefore, Plate Tectonic Theory provides the best explanation for earthquakes.

---

# Definition

An earthquake is the sudden shaking or vibration of the Earth's surface caused by the sudden release of energy inside the Earth.

This released energy travels in the form of seismic waves.

---

# Why Do Earthquakes Occur?

The Earth's lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates.

These plates are constantly moving.

However,

their movement is not smooth.

Due to friction,

plates become locked.

Stress continues to build over time.

Eventually,

the rocks break suddenly.

The stored energy is released.

This sudden release produces an earthquake.

# Focus (Hypocentre)

Definition

The point inside the Earth where the earthquake originates is called the **Focus** or **Hypocentre**.

This is where energy is first released.

```
Earth Surface

---------------------------

          ▲

     Epicentre

---------------------------

          ●

       Focus
```

---

# Epicentre

Definition

The point on the Earth's surface directly above the Focus is called the **Epicentre**.

Maximum destruction generally occurs near the epicentre.

---

# Difference Between Focus and Epicentre

| Focus | Epicentre |
|--------|-----------|
| Inside Earth | On Earth's Surface |
| Origin of Earthquake | Point directly above Focus |
| Energy Released | Maximum Damage Nearby |

---

# Fault

Definition

A fracture or crack in the Earth's crust along which movement occurs is called a **Fault**.

Earthquakes commonly occur along faults.

Examples

- San Andreas Fault
- Himalayan Fault
- North Anatolian Fault

---

# Elastic Rebound Theory

Proposed by

**H. F. Reid**

After the 1906 San Francisco earthquake.

Explanation

Rocks behave elastically.

Stress builds due to plate movement.

When the stress exceeds the strength of rocks,

they suddenly break and return to their original position.

This rebound releases energy.

This theory explains the occurrence of tectonic earthquakes.

---

# Types of Earthquakes

## Based on Cause

### 1. Tectonic Earthquake

Most common type.

Caused by movement of tectonic plates.

Examples

- Himalayan Earthquakes
- Japan Earthquakes

---

### 2. Volcanic Earthquake

Occurs due to movement of magma during volcanic eruptions.

Usually less destructive.

Example

Mt. Etna

Mt. Fuji

---

### 3. Collapse Earthquake

Occurs due to collapse of underground caves or mines.

Very local.

---

### 4. Explosion Earthquake

Caused by

- Nuclear explosions
- Mining blasts
- Human activities

**Artificial earthquakes**.

# Seismic Waves

When an earthquake occurs,

energy travels through the Earth as seismic waves.

There are three major types.

```
Seismic Waves

│

├── P Waves

├── S Waves

└── Surface Waves
```

---

# 1. Primary Waves (P-Waves)

Definition

The fastest seismic waves.

Characteristics

✔ First to arrive.

✔ Travel through solids, liquids, and gases.

✔ Compressional (Longitudinal) waves.

Speed

Approximately

6–8 km/s in the crust.

---

# Memory Trick

**P = Primary = Fastest**

---

# 2. Secondary Waves (S-Waves)

Definition

The second seismic waves to arrive.

Characteristics

✔ Slower than P-waves.

✔ Travel only through solids.

✔ Transverse waves.

✔ Cannot travel through liquids.

Importance

Because S-waves cannot travel through the liquid outer core, scientists concluded that the Earth's outer core is liquid.

---

# Memory Trick

**S = Solid Only**

---

# 3. Surface Waves (L-Waves)

Definition

Waves that travel along the Earth's surface.

Characteristics

✔ Slowest.

✔ Largest amplitude.

✔ Cause maximum destruction.

Two Types

- Love Waves
- Rayleigh Waves

---

# Comparison of Seismic Waves

| Property | P-Wave | S-Wave | Surface Wave |
|-----------|--------|--------|--------------|
| Speed | Fastest | Medium | Slowest |
| Medium | Solid, Liquid, Gas | Solid Only | Surface Only |
| Damage | Least | Moderate | Maximum |

# Measuring Earthquakes

## Magnitude

Magnitude measures

> Total energy released.

Earlier

Richter Scale

Today

Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw)

is scientifically preferred for large earthquakes.

Magnitude is logarithmic.

Increase by

1 magnitude

↓

Energy increases approximately **32 times**.

---

# Intensity

Intensity measures

> Damage caused at a particular place.

Measured by

Modified Mercalli Intensity Scale (MMI)

Intensity depends on

- Distance from epicentre
- Building quality
- Local geology

Different places may experience different intensities during the same earthquake.

---

# Difference Between Magnitude and Intensity

| Magnitude | Intensity |
|------------|-----------|
| Energy Released | Damage Observed |
| Single Value | Varies from Place to Place |
| Richter / Moment Magnitude | Mercalli Scale |

| Zone III | Moderate |

# Why is India Earthquake-Prone?

The Indian Plate is continuously moving northwards.

It collides with the Eurasian Plate.

Stress builds up.

Frequent earthquakes occur.
