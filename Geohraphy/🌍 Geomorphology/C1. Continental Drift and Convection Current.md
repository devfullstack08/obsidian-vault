# Chapter 1 : Continental Drift Theory

---

# Why do we study Continental Drift Theory?

Before the 20th century, scientists believed that:

- Continents and oceans were fixed.
- Mountains were permanent.
- Earth's surface never changed.

However, many observations did not support this idea.

Scientists noticed:

- South America and Africa fit together.
- Same fossils found on different continents.
- Same rocks on opposite sides of oceans.
- Evidence of glaciers in tropical regions.

These questions led Alfred Wegener to propose a revolutionary theory.

## Contribution

Alfred Wegener was the **first scientist to propose that continents move**.

According to him,

> "All continents were once joined together as a single landmass and later drifted apart."

This idea became known as the **Continental Drift Theory**.

# Wegener's Assumption

Initially,

```
Entire Earth

↓

One Supercontinent

↓

Pangaea

↓

Surrounded by Panthalassa
```

During the **Mid-Mesozoic Era**, Pangaea broke into smaller continents.

These continents slowly drifted to their present positions.

---

# Four Major Evidences

---

# 1. Matching Coastlines
(Zig-Zag Fit)

```
South America

        ))))

        ((((

Africa
```

The eastern coastline of South America closely matches the western coastline of Africa.

This is called

> Jig-saw Fit.

### Example

South America ↔ Africa

---

# 2. Similarity of Rocks

Scientists found:

- Same rock age
- Same rock composition
- Same mountain structures

on opposite sides of the Atlantic Ocean.

Example

Brazil ↔ West Africa

This suggests they were once connected.

---

# 3. Fossil Evidence

Identical fossils were found on continents separated by oceans.

Important Fossils

| Fossil | Found In |
|----------|-----------|
| Mesosaurus | South America & Africa |
| Glossopteris | India, Australia, Africa, Antarctica |
| Lystrosaurus | India, Antarctica, Africa |
| Cynognathus | South America & Africa |

Question:

How could the same animal exist on continents separated by thousands of kilometres?

Answer:

They were once joined.

---

# 4. Palaeoclimatic Evidence

(Past Climate)

Evidence of ancient glaciers was found in

- India
- South Africa
- Australia
- South America

These areas are presently tropical.

Question:

How could glaciers exist in tropical regions?

Answer:

These continents were once located near the South Pole.

# Driving Force According to Wegener

Wegener proposed that continents drifted because of:

- Gravitational pull of the Sun
- Gravitational pull of the Moon
- Tidal forces

However,

he could not scientifically explain **how these forces were strong enough to move continents**.

---

# Limitations of Continental Drift Theory

## 1. No Proper Driving Force

The gravitational pull of the Sun and Moon cannot explain continental movement.

Scientists rejected this explanation.

---

## 2. SIAL Floating over SIMA

Wegener believed

```
Continents (SIAL)

↓

Floating over

↓

Ocean Floor (SIMA)
```

Modern geology shows that this explanation is incorrect.

Instead,

both continental and oceanic crust are parts of rigid tectonic plates.

---

## 3. No Explanation for Ocean Floor

The theory could not explain:

- Mid-Oceanic Ridges
- Trenches
- Oceanic Crust
- Ocean Floor Formation

---

## 4. Mountain Formation

It failed to explain:

- Fold Mountains
- Earthquakes
- Volcanoes

---

# Why was the Theory Important?

Although many parts were incorrect,

Wegener proved one revolutionary idea:

> Continents are not fixed.

This completely changed the study of Earth.

His theory became the foundation for:

```
Continental Drift

↓

Arthur Holmes

↓

Convection Current Theory

↓

Sea-Floor Spreading

↓

Plate Tectonic Theory
```

---

# Arthur Holmes (1928)

Wegener could not explain the driving force.

Arthur Holmes solved this problem.

He proposed

## Convection Current Theory

Heat from Earth's interior produces convection currents inside the mantle.

```
Hot Mantle

↑

↓

Cold Mantle

↓

Convection Current
```

These currents slowly move the Earth's crust.

Although Holmes' theory was incomplete,

it later became the basis of Plate Tectonic Theory.
