## What exactly is a star?

A **star is a massive, self-luminous sphere of extremely hot gas/plasma held together by its own gravity**.
The energy of a normal star is generated mainly by **nuclear fusion** in its core.
The simplest reaction to remember is:
- **Hydrogen -> Helium + Energy**
Most stars spend the majority of their lives converting hydrogen into helium. NASA describes main-sequence stars as stars stably undergoing this hydrogen fusion.

### Stars produce their own light.
Planets normally do **not** produce visible light of their own; they mainly reflect light from their star.
So:
**Sun → Star ✅**  
**Earth → Planet ❌ Star**  
**Moon → Natural satellite ❌ Star**

Stars consist predominantly of:

| Element          | Importance                                                                          |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hydrogen**     | Main fuel of most stars                                                             |
| Helium           | Second most abundant                                                                |
| Heavier elements | Small fraction; astronomers collectively call elements heavier than helium “metals” |

**The Sun is also a star. This is one of the most fundamental facts. **
- Sun = Our Nearest star 
- The Sun looks much larger and brighter than other stars simply because it is enormously closer to Earth.
- The Sun is approximately **4.6 billion years old** and is presently a **main-sequence star**. NASA estimates it will remain in this stage for roughly another 5 billion years.

### Why does a star not collapse?
Two opposing forces operate inside a normal star:

| Inward force                                                  | Outward force                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| - Gravity<br>- Gravity tries to compress the star inward.<br> | - Energy generated in the hot interior produces outward pressure. |
Therefore: Gravity inward <--> Pressure outward
When the two balance: **Hydrostatic Equilibrium**
NASA describes stellar life as this continuous struggle between gravity pulling inward and internal pressure pushing outward.

**G = Gravity → Goes inward**
**P = Pressure → Pushes outward**
As long as the balance exists → **stable star**.
When nuclear fuel begins running out → balance changes → the star evolves.

### How is a star born?

- Nebula -> Protostar -> Main Sequence Star
- Molecular cloud / Nebula -> (gravity)Dense clump -> Protostar -> core heats -> Hydrogen fusion begins -> Main Sequence Star

Stage 1 — Nebula / Molecular Cloud
- Stars originate inside enormous clouds consisting of: **gas + dust**
- These are often called:**molecular clouds**.or, in a broader descriptive sense, **stellar nurseries**.
- Gravity causes denser regions of the cloud to contract. NASA notes that stars form in large molecular clouds, where dense pockets eventually collapse under gravity.
- Famous example: **Eagle Nebula — Pillars of Creation**. It contains active star-forming regions.

Protostar 
- As gas and dust collapse: 
- Gravity compresses the material →
- temperature increases →
- a dense hot object begins forming.
- This young developing object is called a:Protostar
- A protostar is not yet a normal main-sequence star.
- At first, much of its energy comes from **gravitational contraction**, rather than stable hydrogen fusion. Eventually its core becomes sufficiently hot and dense for fusion to begin.

Birth of the actual star
- When the core becomes hot enough: H -> He
- Hydrogen fusion begins.
- The star becomes a: Main Sequence Star
- This is essentially the **adult/stable phase** of a star.
- ⭐ About **90% of stars** are main-sequence stars. (as per NASA)


> Which factor primarily determines the life and fate of a star?  = Mass 
> Mass determines, to a large degree:
- temperature
- luminosity
- fuel consumption rate
- lifetime
- evolution
- final fate
NASA emphasizes that stellar evolutionary paths differ primarily because of how much mass the star contains. 

### Very important paradox

- You might think: Larger star = more fuel = lives longer. ❌ Not necessarily.
- Massive stars have more fuel, **but they consume that fuel dramatically faster**.
- Therefore: 
	- **More massive star**-> **generally shorter lifetime**
- and 
	- **Low-mass star** → **generally longer lifetime**
- Some very low-mass stars can theoretically survive for **trillions of years**, whereas the most massive stars can complete their lives in only a few million years.
- Think of two cars:
	- **Small engine → slowly consumes fuel → long life**
	- **Huge racing engine → consumes fuel rapidly → short life**

### Main Sequence

The **main sequence** is the longest major stage of a normal star's life.
- During this phase:
	- **H -> He**
- occurs in the core.
- Our Sun is currently here.

### What happens when hydrogen begins running out?

- Eventually the star's core hydrogen becomes depleted.
- Then:
	- fusion support changes ↓
	- gravity begins compressing the core ↑
	- temperature rises ↑
	- outer layers expand ↑

- What happens next depends principally on the **mass of the star**.
- This gives us two major evolutionary paths.

### Complete life cycle of a Sun-like star

- Memorize: Nebula -> Protostar -> Main Sequence ->  Red\ Giant ->  Planetary Nebula -> White Dwarf
- Eventually, over an unimaginably long time:
	- **White Dwarf** -> **Black Dwarf**

but **black dwarfs are theoretical** because the Universe is not old enough for white dwarfs to have cooled that far yet.
