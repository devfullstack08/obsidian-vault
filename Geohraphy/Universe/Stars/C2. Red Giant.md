**Main Sequence** = Most normal hydrogen-fusing stars.
**Giants** = Large, luminous evolved stars.
**Supergiants** = Extremely large/luminous evolved stars.
**White Dwarfs** = Hot but very small and relatively faint.

### Red Giant
When a Sun-like star exhausts hydrogen in its core:
- core contracts
- outer layers expand
- surface becomes cooler
- star becomes enormous

It becomes a: **Red Giant**
#### Important trap 
**Red doesn't mean the whole star has less energy.**
The surface is cooler, but because the star becomes extremely large, its total luminosity can be enormous.

#### Why can a red giant be bright if it is cooler?
Because stellar luminosity depends strongly on:
**temperature AND surface area/size**.
A red giant has a **cooler surface** but an enormous radius.
Therefore it can still be extremely luminous.
This is another common conceptual trap.

### What will happen to our Sun?

- The Sun will eventually become a: **Red Giant**
- and later: **Planetary Nebula → White Dwarf**
- It will **not** end as a normal core-collapse supernova and will **not** become a stellar black hole because it is not massive enough.

> Will the Sun become a black hole? ❌ **No.**


### Planetary Nebula

- After the red giant stage, a Sun-like star can expel its outer layers.
- The expanding glowing shell of gas is called a: **Planetary Nebula**
#### Important
Despite its name:
❌ it has essentially nothing to do with planets.
The name comes from historical telescope observations where some appeared somewhat planet-like.

### White Dwarf

- What remains at the centre is the hot, dense stellar core: **White Dwarf**
- Approximately **Earth-sized**, even though they contain a substantial fraction of a star's former mass.
They no longer maintain ordinary hydrogen fusion in their cores and gradually cool. NASA describes a white dwarf as an approximately Earth-sized stellar remnant that cools over billions of years.

### Chandrasekhar Limit ⭐⭐⭐

The maximum stable mass of a typical white dwarf is approximately: **1.4M.**
where:  M. = Mass of Sun


Named after:
### ⭐ Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar

Indian-American astrophysicist and Nobel laureate.
Remember:
**Chandrasekhar Limit → White Dwarf**

Do not confuse it with ordinary neutron-star mass limits.

### Black Dwarf

Eventually a white dwarf should cool enough to become a: **Black Dwarf **
**No black dwarf is expected to exist yet.**
Why?
The Universe is not old enough for a white dwarf to cool completely to this stage.
