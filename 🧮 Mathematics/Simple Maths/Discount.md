# Discount (વળતર)

Discount is a sub-topic of Profit & Loss that deals with **Marked Price (MP)** (also known as List Price) and promotional offers.

---

## 1. Core Concepts & Formulas

* **Marked Price (MP):** The price printed or tagged on an article (before discount).
* **Discount (D):** The reduction offered on the Marked Price.
  $$Discount = MP - SP$$
  $$Discount\% = \left( \frac{Discount}{MP} \times 100 \right)\%$$
* **Selling Price (SP):** The final price paid after deducting the discount.
  $$SP = MP \times \left( \frac{100 - Discount\%}{100} \right)$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Discount is **always calculated on the Marked Price (MP)**, while profit and loss are calculated on the Cost Price (CP).

---

## 2. Ultimate Exam Shortcuts & Formulas

### A. The CP-MP Relationship Formula (Golden Formula)
This is the most powerful formula to solve questions connecting Cost Price, Marked Price, Profit%, and Discount%:

$$\frac{\text{Marked Price (MP)}}{\text{Cost Price (CP)}} = \frac{100 + Profit\%}{100 - Discount\%}$$

* *If there is a loss:*
  $$\frac{\text{MP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{100 - Loss\%}{100 - Discount\%}$$

> **Example:** A shopkeeper marks his goods in such a way that after allowing a discount of $10\%$, he still makes a profit of $20\%$. If the cost price of the article is ₹$300$, find its marked price.
> * Given: $Discount\% = 10$, $Profit\% = 20$, $CP = 300$
> * Using the formula:
>   $$\frac{MP}{300} = \frac{100 + 20}{100 - 10} = \frac{120}{90} = \frac{4}{3}$$
>   $$MP = \frac{4}{3} \times 300 = \mathbf{₹400}$$
> * **Answer:** The marked price is **₹400**. (Solved without finding the SP!)

---

### B. Successive Discounts (Equivalent Single Discount)
When a shop offers two successive discounts of $x\%$ and $y\%$, the equivalent single discount is:

$$\text{Equivalent Discount} = \left( x + y - \frac{xy}{100} \right)\%$$

* **For Three Successive Discounts ($x\%, y\%, z\%$):**
  It is much faster to use the **Fraction / Ratio Method**:
  1. Convert the percentages to fractions (e.g. $10\% = 1/10$, $20\% = 1/5$).
  2. Write initial values (MP) and final values (SP):
     * $10\%$ discount $\rightarrow$ MP is $10$, SP is $9$.
     * $20\%$ discount $\rightarrow$ MP is $5$, SP is $4$.
  3. Multiply the MP column and the SP column to get the overall ratio, then find the percentage change.

## 3. Solved Practice Questions

### Question 1 (Equivalent of Three Successive Discounts)
Find the single discount equivalent to three successive discounts of $10\%$, $20\%$, and $25\%$.
* **Solution (using the Fraction Method):**
  * $10\% = \frac{1}{10} \implies$ MP is $10$, SP is $9$.
  * $20\% = \frac{1}{5} \implies$ MP is $5$, SP is $4$.
  * $25\% = \frac{1}{4} \implies$ MP is $4$, SP is $3$.
  * Let's align and multiply (we can cross-cancel like fractions):
    * MP column: $10 \times 5 \times 4 = 200$
    * SP column: $9 \times 4 \times 3 = 108$
  * Simplified Ratio (divide both by 4):
    * MP: $50$, SP: $27$
  * Equivalent Discount $= \frac{\text{Change in value}}{\text{Initial MP}} \times 100 = \frac{50 - 27}{50} \times 100 = \frac{23}{50} \times 100 = \mathbf{46\%}$.
  * **Answer:** **$46\%$** single discount.

### Question 2 (Free Items Trick)
A shopkeeper offers a scheme: *"Buy 4, Get 1 Free."* What is the effective discount percentage?
* **Solution:**
  * Total items given to customer $= 4 + 1 = 5$ (This represents the Marked Price of 5 items).
  * Free item given $= 1$ (This represents the Discount value).
  * Effective Discount $\% = \frac{\text{Free Items}}{\text{Total Items}} \times 100 = \frac{1}{5} \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$.
