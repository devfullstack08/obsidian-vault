> Digestion is the process of breaking down complex, insoluble food into simple, soluble substances that can be absorbed and utilized by the body.

- Complex carbohydrates → Simple sugars
- Proteins → Amino acids
- Fats → Fatty acids + Monoglycerides

## 2. Organs and Functions

| Organ           | Important function                                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mouth           | Chewing; saliva begins starch digestion                |
| Oesophagus      | Moves food through peristalsis                         |
| Stomach         | HCl and pepsin begin significant protein digestion     |
| Small intestine | Completes most chemical digestion; absorbs nutrients   |
| Large intestine | Absorbs remaining water and electrolytes; forms faeces |
| Rectum          | Temporarily stores faeces                              |
| Anus            | Eliminates faeces (egestion)                           |

### Important Accessory Organs

- Liver: Largest internal organ and largest gland; produces bile.
    
- Gallbladder: Stores and concentrates bile.
    
- Pancreas: Mixed gland (exocrine + endocrine); secretes digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the small intestine.
    
- Salivary glands: Three major pairs — parotid, submandibular and sublingual.
    

## 3. Digestive Juices and Enzymes

| Juice / Enzyme             | Source                             | Function                                       |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Salivary amylase (Ptyalin) | Salivary glands                    | Starch → Maltose and shorter carbohydrates     |
| HCl                        | Stomach                            | Acidic environment; activates pepsinogen       |
| Pepsin                     | Stomach                            | Proteins → Smaller peptides                    |
| Gastric lipase             | Stomach                            | Begins limited fat digestion                   |
| Bile                       | Liver                              | Emulsifies fats; contains no digestive enzymes |
| Pancreatic amylase         | Pancreas                           | Starch → Smaller sugars                        |
| Trypsin                    | Pancreas (secreted as trypsinogen) | Digests proteins/peptides                      |
| Pancreatic lipase          | Pancreas                           | Triglycerides → Fatty acids + Monoglycerides   |
| Maltase                    | Small intestine                    | Maltose → Glucose                              |
| Sucrase                    | Small intestine                    | Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose                   |
| Lactase                    | Small intestine                    | Lactose → Glucose + Galactose                  |
| Peptidases                 | Small intestine                    | Peptides → Amino acids                         |

Remember: Bile is not an enzyme. It helps fat digestion by breaking large fat globules into smaller droplets (emulsification).

## 4. Small Intestine — Most Important Organ

![Villi in the small intestine — Science Learning Hub](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/JDBdN-QUUeK8vTxJ25DvjBxDK1eadoDFegN1GQ6oXCUYP29zoGhVtuZqBrTzzwcr_YfLmjme7pqRayWzCiyIazzyM5ajN1sS2SOZIWobnsZ1fw_cnB_VedldlcZCXIDovuqYuUJFNfJvaGfv2X6qjgFZgu97dVq_MslOZq2z1oY?purpose=inline)

The small intestine has three parts:

1. Duodenum: Receives bile and pancreatic juice.
    
2. Jejunum: Major site of nutrient absorption.
    
3. Ileum: Absorbs remaining nutrients, especially vitamin B12 and bile salts.
    

- Villi: Finger-like projections that increase absorptive surface area.
    
- Microvilli: Microscopic projections on intestinal cells.
    
- Blood capillaries: Absorb glucose, amino acids and many other nutrients.
    
- Lacteals: Lymphatic vessels that absorb most dietary long-chain fats after packaging into chylomicrons.
    

Exam fact: Most digestion and nutrient absorption occur in the small intestine, not the stomach.

## 5. Important Biological Processes

|Process|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Ingestion|Taking food into the body|
|Digestion|Breaking complex food into simpler molecules|
|Absorption|Movement of digested nutrients into blood or lymph|
|Assimilation|Utilization of absorbed nutrients by body cells|
|Egestion|Removal of undigested waste through the anus|

Peristalsis: Wave-like muscular contractions that move food through the digestive tract.

## 6. Important pH Values

|Location|Approximate pH|
|---|---|
|Saliva|6.2–7.6|
|Stomach|1.5–3.5|
|Small intestine|Approximately 6–8|

- Stomach: Acidic because of hydrochloric acid.
    
- Small intestine: Bicarbonate helps neutralize acidic chyme arriving from the stomach.

## 9. Final 30-Second Revision

Mouth → Salivary amylase → Starch digestion begins

Stomach → HCl + Pepsin → Protein digestion begins

Liver → Produces bile → Fat emulsification

Gallbladder → Stores bile

Pancreas → Amylase + Proteases + Lipase

Small intestine → Major digestion and nutrient absorption

Large intestine → Water/electrolyte absorption; faeces formation

Rectum / Anus → Storage and egestion

2. Longest digestive tract organ = Small intestine
    
3. Protein-digesting stomach enzyme = Pepsin
    
4. Bile = No digestive enzyme
