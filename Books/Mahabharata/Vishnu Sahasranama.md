
**“Vishnu Sahasranama”** means **“the thousand names of Lord Vishnu.”**

In Sanskrit:

**Vishnu** = Lord Vishnu, the preserver and protector in Hindu tradition  
**Sahasra** = thousand  
**Nama** = names

So, **Vishnu Sahasranama = 1000 names of Vishnu**.

It is not just a list of names. Each name describes a quality, power, form, or divine nature of Vishnu, such as protector, creator, destroyer of evil, source of peace, and the supreme reality.

## Where did Vishnu Sahasranama come from?

The Vishnu Sahasranama comes from the **Mahabharata**, one of the greatest Hindu eepics. It appears in the **Anushasana Parva**,(13) which is a section of the Mahabharata.

The scene happens after the great Kurukshetra war.

Bhishma Pitamaha, the great elder of the Kuru family, is lying on a bed of arrows. He is waiting for the right time to leave his body because he had the boon(વરદાન) of choosing the time of his death.

At that time, **Yudhishthira**, the eldest Pandava, is deeply troubled. He has seen so much death and destruction in the war, and he wants to know the highest truth of life.

So he asks Bhishma important spiritual questions.

## Who told Vishnu Sahasranama?

**Bhishma Pitamaha told it to Yudhishthira.**

But Lord Krishna was also present there. In Hindu belief, Krishna is an avatar of Vishnu himself, so the thousand names of Vishnu were spoken in the presence of Vishnu’s own incarnation.

That makes the moment very sacred.

## What did Yudhishthira ask?

Yudhishthira asked Bhishma questions like:

Who is the supreme God?  
What is the highest goal of life?  
By praising whom can a person get peace and freedom?  
What is the best dharma?  
By chanting whose name can a person be freed from suffering?

Bhishma answered that the highest worship is to praise **Lord Vishnu**, the supreme being, by chanting his thousand names.

Then Bhishma recited the **Vishnu Sahasranama**.

## Why is it important?

Vishnu Sahasranama is important because it is believed to bring:

Peace of mind  
Protection from fear and negativity  
Strength during difficult times  
Devotion and faith  
Spiritual growth  
Relief from stress and confusion  
Blessings of Lord Vishnu

Many people chant it daily, especially in the morning or evening. Some listen to it instead of chanting. Both are considered beneficial when done with respect and devotion.

## What are some famous names in it?

Some well-known names from Vishnu Sahasranama are:

**Vishvam** — He who is the universe  
**Vishnuh** — He who pervades everything  
**Narayana** — The refuge of all beings  
**Madhava** — Consort of Lakshmi; source of sweetness and wisdom  
**Govinda** — Protector of cows, earth, and souls  
**Achyuta** — One who never falls or changes  
**Ananta** — Endless, infinite  
**Keshava** — Slayer of demon Keshi; also one with beautiful hair  
**Damodara** — The one who was tied by the waist by mother Yashoda  
**Hari** — One who removes sins and suffering

Each name has a deeper philosophical meaning.

## Is it only for Vishnu devotees?

Mostly, it is chanted by devotees of Vishnu, Krishna, Rama, and Narayana. But many Hindus from different traditions chant it because it is seen as a universal prayer for peace, protection, and spiritual clarity.

Even Adi Shankaracharya, a great philosopher of Advaita Vedanta, wrote a famous commentary on Vishnu Sahasranama.

## How is it usually chanted?

Traditionally, it begins with introductory verses, then meditation verses, then the 1000 names, and finally the closing prayer called **Phala Shruti**, which explains the benefits of chanting it.

A common opening idea is to meditate on Lord Vishnu as peaceful, reclining on the serpent Adishesha, with lotus-like eyes, and as the Lord of the universe.

## What is the main message?

The main message of Vishnu Sahasranama is:

God has infinite qualities.  
The divine is present everywhere.  
Remembering God’s names purifies the mind.  
Faith, devotion, and dharma help a person overcome suffering.  
The highest refuge is the supreme reality, called Vishnu or Narayana.
