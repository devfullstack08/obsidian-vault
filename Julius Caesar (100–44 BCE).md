## 1. Rise to Power

- 60 BCE: Formed the First Triumvirate (informal political alliance) with Pompey and Crassus.
    
- 59 BCE: Became Consul of Rome.
    
- 58–50 BCE: Conquered much of Gaul (modern-day France and surrounding regions).
    
- 55 and 54 BCE: Led Roman military expeditions to Britain.

## 2. Civil War and Dictatorship

- 49 BCE: Crossed the Rubicon River with his army, triggering a civil war against Pompey's faction.
    
- 48 BCE: Defeated Pompey at the **Battle of Pharsalus**.
    
- 46 BCE: Became dictator for ten years.
    
- 44 BCE: Appointed Dictator Perpetuo (dictator for life).

## 3. Important Reforms

- Introduced the Julian Calendar (45 BCE), establishing a 365-day year with leap-year rules.
    
- Expanded Roman citizenship to selected provincial communities and individuals.
    
- Introduced land settlement and administrative reforms.
    
- Enlarged the Roman Senate.

## 4. Assassination

- Date: 15 March 44 BCE, known as the **Ides of March**.
    
- Place: Theatre of Pompey complex, Rome.
    
- Assassins: A group of senators, including **Brutus** and **Cassius**.
    
- Reason: They feared Caesar's concentration of power would destroy the Roman Republic.
## 5. Historical Significance

- Caesar played a major role in ending the Roman Republic's traditional political system.
    
- His assassination triggered further civil wars.
    
- His adopted heir Octavian, later known as Augustus, became Rome's first emperor in 27 BCE.
    
- Important: Julius Caesar was not Rome's first emperor; Augustus was.

Julius Caesar's famous quote: “Veni, Vidi, Vici.”
Meaning: “I came, I saw, I conquered.”

- Who said it? Julius Caesar
    
- When? 47 BCE
    
- After which battle? **Battle of Zela** (in present-day Turkey)
    
- Against whom? Pharnaces II, King of Pontus
